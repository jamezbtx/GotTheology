Passage

John 4

Verses 27–42 · ESV · Voices

The disciples return, startled that He talks with a woman, and stay silent. She leaves her jar, testifies in town, and people come. Jesus refuses their food: His food is the Father’s will. The fields are white; sower and reaper rejoice together. Many Samaritans believe because of her word, then many more because of His, and they confess Him Savior of the world.

John 4:27

27Just then his disciples came back. They marveled that he was talking with a woman, but no one said, “What do you seek?” or, “Why are you talking with her?”

Arminian

Marvel, then silence. They still have a map of whom rabbis address. Grace is already ahead of their manners.

Arminian church-life: disciples can be saved and still startled by Jesus’ company. The restraint from questioning is at least wisdom; the marvel shows how much they have to learn from the well.

Reformed

Just then: the timing keeps the disclosure (v. 26) between Jesus and the woman. Their marvel is social; their silence is mercy.

Reformed note: the Lord does not wait for His team’s comfort. He talks with whom He will, and they are not yet ready to interrogate it.

John 4:28

28So the woman left her water jar and went away into town and said to the people,

Arminian

The jar stays. The errand that brought her is outrun by the news. She goes to people, not away from them.

Arminian mission: imperfect theology can still run. Leaving the jar is a picture of a thirst reordered; speech to the town is already worship in truth beginning to overflow.

Reformed

Abandoned jar: narrative sermon. The well’s original purpose yields to witness.

Reformed sending: the one who was sought (v. 23) becomes a seeker of her neighbors. Christ’s word creates a messenger before it finishes her catechism.

John 4:29

29“Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did. Can this be the Christ?”

Arminian

Come, see—echo of John’s Gospel invitations. She leads with being known, then a question, not a lecture: Can this be the Christ?

Arminian testimony: honest wonder evangelizes. She does not claim to have solved Messiah; she invites inspection of the Man who told her her life. That is enough to start a town walking.

Reformed

All that I ever did: the exposing word has become good news. Can this be—the question is open and loaded.

Reformed witness: she repeats what He did, not a system. The Christ-question is the right one, and it is already His work in her mouth.

John 4:30

30They went out of the town and were coming to him.

Arminian

They went, they were coming: a town in motion toward Jesus. Her question was not a dead end.

Arminian hope: people can still come when invited. Fields white (v. 35) looks like this dusty road filling with Samaritans on the way to a tired Jew at a well.

Reformed

Outgoing and coming: John lets the harvest be visible before Jesus names it.

Reformed gathering: the Father-seeking of v. 23 now has a column of people. What she asked, they do; what He willed, they enact.

John 4:31

31Meanwhile the disciples were urging him, saying, “Rabbi, eat.”

Arminian

Meanwhile: two hungers. They urge lunch; He is mid-harvest.

Arminian contrast: care for Jesus’ body is not wrong, and still can miss His work. Disciples will need their eyes lifted from bread to fields.

Reformed

Rabbi, eat: ordinary love, small horizon.

Reformed irony: they went to buy food (v. 8) and return to feed a Lord whose food is elsewhere. The lesson is about to be the Father’s will.

John 4:32

32But he said to them, “I have food to eat that you do not know about.”

Arminian

Unknown food: they will mishear it as a secret snack, as she misheard living water.

Arminian pattern: Jesus keeps offering a different nourishment than His people first imagine. Knowing this food is part of learning Him.

Reformed

Food they do not know: knowledge gap again, as in 4:10.

Reformed unveiling: the Son’s sustenance is hidden from even close disciples until He speaks it. Union with the Father’s will is His meal.

John 4:33

33So the disciples said to one another, “Has anyone brought him something to eat?”

Arminian

They confer horizontally and stay in the cupboard. The woman is bringing a town; they are checking catering.

Arminian gentleness: John is not cruel to them. Literalism is the Gospel’s running joke because grace keeps translating it.

Reformed

Has anyone brought: they cannot yet hear “food” as vocation.

Reformed dullness: even the Twelve need the well’s lesson. Knowing Christ includes knowing what feeds Him.

John 4:34

34Jesus said to them, “My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to accomplish his work.

Arminian

Food = the Sender’s will done and finished. The well conversation was not a delay of lunch; it was lunch.

Arminian obedience: Jesus’ satisfaction is the accomplished work of the Father. Disciples are invited into that appetite—doing sent-will, not merely admiring it.

Reformed

Calvin: He calls it his food to do the Father’s will. Sent and accomplish: mission with a completion.

Reformed Sonship: the Sender’s will is the Son’s life. Harvest in Samaria is not extra credit; it is the work given Him to finish.

John 4:35

35Do you not say, ‘There are yet four months, then comes the harvest’? Look, I tell you, lift up your eyes, and see that the fields are white for harvest.

Arminian

Proverbial delay versus present white fields. Lift up your eyes—Wesley saw the Samaritans coming. The harvest is not later; it is walking toward them.

Arminian urgency: waiting four months is disobedience when the field is already pale. The gospel’s whoever is showing up in real time; eyes must be lifted.

Reformed

Four months is farm-sense; white now is kingdom-sense. Look, I tell you.

Reformed timing: the hour that is now (v. 23) has a visible crop. God has ripened a people the disciples did not plant this morning.

John 4:36

36Already the one who reaps is receiving wages and gathering fruit for eternal life, so that sower and reaper may rejoice together.

Arminian

Already: wages and eternal fruit now. Sower and reaper share one joy, not a rivalry of credits.

Arminian teamwork: some speak, some gather, God gives the life. Rejoicing together is the Baptist’s joy (3:29) extended to a whole crew.

Reformed

Wages already, fruit for eternal life, joint joy. The economy is God’s.

Reformed harvest: eternal life as crop means this is not mere enthusiasm. The Father pays and gathers; human roles are assigned so they can be glad together.

John 4:37

37For here the saying holds true, ‘One sows and another reaps.’

Arminian

Division of labor without division of church. Here the proverb is gospel: you may reap what you did not start.

Arminian humility: today’s convert may be yesterday’s unknown sowing. Grace lets us enter others’ labor without pretending we were first.

Reformed

The saying holds here: God chains servants across time.

Reformed providence: sowing and reaping are distinct gifts (as in 1 Cor 3). No reaper may boast as if the field began with his sickle.

John 4:38

38I sent you to reap that for which you did not labor. Others have labored, and you have entered into their labor.”

Arminian

I sent you: their reaping is assignment, not luck. Others—prophets, Baptist, the woman minutes ago—labored first.

Arminian sending: being sent to reap is gift and task. Entering others’ labor means the Samaritans’ faith will not be a disciples’ trophy.

Reformed

I sent: the Son is the sender of reapers into a field others worked.

Reformed grace in ministry: unearned harvest. They enter labor God has already been doing through others, including this noon’s witness.

John 4:39

39Many Samaritans from that town believed in him because of the woman’s testimony, “He told me all that I ever did.”

Arminian

Many believed because of her testimony. A five-husband history became the sentence God used.

Arminian confidence in witness: God uses told-truth about a life. Belief can begin on a neighbor’s word; v. 42 will deepen it without despising this beginning.

Reformed

Because of the woman’s testimony: means of faith named. Many, not all, believed.

Reformed means: the Spirit can hitch saving faith to a short, true story. The content is still Him—He told me—not her pedigree.

John 4:40

40So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them, and he stayed there two days.

Arminian

They ask Him to stay; He stays two days. The One who “had to” pass through now has to linger because they want Him.

Arminian welcome: remaining is mercy. Two days of the Word in a Samaritan town is the love of 3:16 with a guest-room.

Reformed

Asked, and He stayed. The necessity of v. 4 blossoms into two days of presence.

Reformed dwelling: the Word tabernacles where He is asked, and the asking itself is fruit of the work He already did at the well.

John 4:41

41And many more believed because of his word.

Arminian

Many more, because of His word. Her testimony opened; His word confirmed and increased.

Arminian order: hearing Christ Himself is better, and the first hearing was not fake. Faith grows as the object of faith speaks.

Reformed

His word is the higher cause of more faith. The increase is attributed to what He said.

Reformed efficacy: the Son’s speech creates believers. Two days of word explain “many more” better than two days of atmosphere.

John 4:42

42They said to the woman, “It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is indeed the Savior of the world.”

Arminian

Not ungrateful to her: they have now heard for themselves. Savior of the world—Wesley: not of the Jews only. Samaria names the wide title first in this Gospel’s narrative.

Classical Arminians receive this confession as the point of 3:16 on the ground. World is not a hint; it is the town’s conclusion after hearing Him. The Savior is for more than one mountain’s people.

Reformed

Calvin: they learned Messiah’s office extends to the whole world. Heard for ourselves, we know: faith’s certainty after the Word.

Reformed width without slogan: Savior of the world is John’s high title, and it does not cancel 3:3–8 or 3:27. The One who saves the world does so as people hear and believe; Samaria is proof the field was never only Judea.

Key contrast Shared ground: they know Jesus as Savior of the world by hearing Him, not by remaining under secondhand report. Arminians typically take the title as sincere universal saving intent now arriving in Samaria. Reformed readers typically take it as Messiah’s worldwide office—nations included—without treating “world” as a guarantee that every person will believe.

Arminian Notable voices

“My meat is to do the will of him that sent me. Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields—the Samaritans who were coming. This is indeed the Christ, the Saviour of the world—not of the Jews only.”
