Passage

John 16

Verses 1–15 · ESV · Voices

Jesus warns the Eleven so they will not fall away: synagogue expulsion and killing done as service to God, because the persecutors know neither the Father nor Him. It is to their advantage that He goes, for then He will send the Helper, who convicts the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment, and who guides them into all the truth, glorifying the Son.

John 16:1

1“I have said all these things to you to keep you from falling away.

Arminian

The farewell is pastoral: spoken so they will not fall away when hatred comes. Forewarning is mercy.

Arminian care: falling away is a real danger Jesus takes seriously. Grace that keeps also uses remembered words; they must hold what He said.

Reformed

He speaks to preserve them. The point of the discourse is perseverance through scandal.

Reformed comfort: the Shepherd prepares His own. The words themselves are a means by which He keeps them from apostasy.

John 16:2

2They will put you out of the synagogues. Indeed, the hour is coming when whoever kills you will think he is offering service to God.

Arminian

Excommunication, then killing as liturgy. Zeal without knowledge can baptize murder as worship.

Arminian warning: religious sincerity is not the same as knowing God. People can be culpable while feeling devout.

Reformed

The hour includes pious violence. Service-to-God language does not make the deed holy.

Reformed sobriety: ignorance of the Father (v. 3) can wear vestments. The church should not be shocked when hatred sounds devout.

John 16:3

3And they will do these things because they have not known the Father, nor me.

Arminian

The cause is not knowing. Hatred of the messengers tracks ignorance of the Sender and the Son.

Classical Arminians: this not-knowing is culpable; light has come. The gospel still calls persecutors to know the Father in the Son.

Reformed

Not known: relational, not a trivia gap. Persecution is fruit of that ignorance.

Reformed note: knowing the Father is given in the Son. Where that knowledge is withheld or refused, violence against His people follows.

John 16:4

4But I have said these things to you, that when their hour comes you may remember that I told them to you. I did not say these things to you from the beginning, because I was with you.

Arminian

Memory is the aim. He withheld the worst while He was physically with them.

Arminian discipleship: remember when the hour comes. Foreknowledge of trial is meant to steady faith, not to freeze it.

Reformed

Their hour will come, and His words will be waiting. Timing of disclosure is pastoral.

Reformed providence: He rations hard truth. Presence then, memory now, are both His care.

John 16:5

5But now I am going to him who sent me, and none of you asks me, ‘Where are you going?’

Arminian

Going to the Sender should have drawn a where, but sorrow has swallowed the question (v. 6).

Arminian reading: they are responsible hearers whose grief has narrowed them. He still goes for their good.

Reformed

The departure is to the Sender. Their silence is noted, not condemned as final.

Reformed direction: the mission’s next movement is upward. Their unasked question does not halt it.

John 16:6

6But because I have said these things to you, sorrow has filled your heart.

Arminian

Sorrow filled—not a passing mood. The Helper’s coming will have to meet a full heart of grief.

Arminian honesty: Jesus names their feeling without scorning it. Grief can coexist with a faith that still needs courage.

Reformed

Filled hearts: the diagnosis before the advantage of v. 7.

Reformed pastoral: He interprets their interior life. The Spirit is promised to the sorrowful, not to the already unshaken.

John 16:7

7Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you.

Arminian

Advantage in absence: the Helper will not come unless He goes, and if He goes, He sends Him. Wesley stressed expedience for the church.

Arminian hope: the Spirit is Christ’s gift to them, not a vague force. Going is for their good; the sending is personal and promised.

Reformed

Truth against their sorrow: His going is their gain because the Helper comes only then, and Jesus sends Him.

Reformed gift: Pentecost is the Son’s act from the Father’s side. The church’s Helper is not self-summoned.

John 16:8

8And when he comes, he will convict the world concerning sin and righteousness and judgment:

Arminian

The Helper’s first named work toward the world is conviction—sin, righteousness, judgment. Not flattery; exposure that can save.

Classical Arminians hear a real work of grace toward the world. Conviction is sincere; people may yield or resist, but the Spirit truly addresses the world, not a cipher.

Reformed

When He comes: conviction is the Spirit’s office in the world, Calvin said, through the gospel preached.

Reformed accent: this is Christ’s victory applied. The world is not left unaddressed; the Spirit convicts as the Son’s witness, effectual for those given to Him and exposing for those who will not believe.

Key contrast Shared: the Spirit convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment. Arminians typically describe that conviction as grace toward the world that can be received or refused. Reformed readers typically describe it as the gospel’s Spirit-powered exposure—savingly effectual in the elect, and a real verdict on remaining unbelief.

John 16:9

9concerning sin, because they do not believe in me;

Arminian

The head sin is unbelief in Jesus. Wesley called it the grand damning sin. Conviction aims here first.

Arminian evangelism: the Spirit names not-believing as sin so that people may yet believe. The diagnosis is an invitation, not a locked door.

Reformed

Sin specified: they do not believe in me. Calvin: principal sin and source of others.

Reformed focus: unbelief is not a small gap in data. The Spirit’s conviction of the world centers on refusal of the Son.

John 16:10

10concerning righteousness, because I go to the Father, and you will see me no longer;

Arminian

Righteousness is interpreted by His going to the Father—vindication of the crucified. They will not see Him, yet the Spirit will preach that going as righteousness.

Arminian gospel: the world’s measure of Jesus is reversed. Conviction of righteousness means the cross was not failure; hearers may trust the Vindicated One.

Reformed

Going to the Father is the public proof of His righteousness once sight is gone.

Reformed vindication: the Spirit convinces the world that the departed Christ is the Righteous One. Ascension interprets Golgotha.

John 16:11

11concerning judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.

Arminian

Judgment: the ruler is already judged. Conviction includes announcing the devil’s lost case.

Arminian courage: the world’s prince is not the last court. The Spirit’s work frees people to switch allegiance while the verdict stands.

Reformed

The ruler is judged—perfect tense in force. The Spirit applies that judgment to the world.

Reformed triumph: conviction of judgment is not pending maybe. Christ’s cross has judged the tyrant; the Spirit makes that fact inescapable.

John 16:12

12“I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now.

Arminian

More truth, later capacity. He will not crush them with what they cannot bear tonight.

Arminian pacing: revelation is pedagogical. The Spirit (v. 13) will continue what they cannot carry yet; they remain learners, not finished repositories.

Reformed

Cannot bear now: limitation He honors. Many things remain His to say.

Reformed patience: the Spirit’s coming is also a curriculum. What they cannot bear, He will not dump; He will send a Guide.

John 16:13

13When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth, for he will not speak on his own authority, but whatever he hears he will speak, and he will declare to you the things that are to come.

Arminian

Spirit of truth, guide into all the truth, speaking what He hears, declaring what is to come. The Eleven are promised guidance, not autonomous inspiration.

Arminian hearing: the Spirit’s guidance is real and bound to what He hears from Christ. The church still listens; guidance is not a license to ignore the Son’s words.

Reformed

He will guide—future, personal, comprehensive of the truth they need, not a rival canon from nowhere.

Reformed origin: the Spirit does not freelance. What He hears, He speaks. Apostolic truth is Christ’s, delivered by the Spirit.

John 16:14

14He will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and declare it to you.

Arminian

The Spirit’s metric is Jesus’ glory. He takes what is Christ’s and declares it—no separate celebrity.

Arminian test of spirits: does this glorify the Son? The Helper’s ministry is Christ-centered, and the church is meant to receive that declaration.

Reformed

Glorify me: the Spirit’s mission statement. Taking and declaring what is Christ’s.

Reformed rule: pneumatology serves Christology. Where the Son is not glorified, the claim to the Helper is already suspect.

John 16:15

15All that the Father has is mine; therefore I said that he will take what is mine and declare it to you.

Arminian

Father’s all is the Son’s; therefore the Spirit’s declaring is Trinitarian, not a third gospel.

Arminian worship: Father, Son, and Spirit share this giving. What is declared to the church is the fullness of God in the Son.

Reformed

The therefore binds the Trinity to the church’s hearing. All the Father has is the Son’s; the Spirit takes from that and declares.

Reformed fullness: no deficit in the Son. The Spirit does not supplement a half-Christ; He distributes what is already His.

Historic words on the Helper who convicts the world and guides the church—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside John 16:1–15.

Arminian Notable voices

“It is expedient for you that I go away… If I go, I will send him to you. He will convince the world of sin, because they believe not on me. This is the grand damning sin. Of righteousness, because I go to my Father… Of judgment, because the prince of this world is judged.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 16:7–11)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Spirit will convince the world of sin, because they do not believe in Christ… This is the principal sin, and the source of all others. He will convince of righteousness, because I go to the Father; and of judgment, because the prince of this world is already judged. The Spirit does this by the preaching of the Gospel.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 16:8–11)
