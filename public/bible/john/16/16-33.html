Passage

John 16

Verses 16–33 · WEB · Voices

A little while: they will not see Him, then see Him. Sorrow will turn to joy like birth pangs to a child. In that day they will ask the Father in His name; the Father Himself loves them. He came from the Father and returns. Their new confidence is met with a question and a prediction of scattering—yet He is not alone, and in Him they may have peace: in the world tribulation, but He has overcome the world.

John 16:16

16“A little while, and you will not see me. Again a little while, and you will see me.”

Arminian

Two little whiles: absence, then sight. The cross-and-resurrection clock is hidden in a riddle they cannot yet parse.

The absence is authentic and short. He prepares them for a night that will not be the last word.

Reformed

Little while / little while: His timetable, not theirs.

The Lord of hours speaks in compressed time. Seeing and not-seeing are both under His “little.”

John 16:17

17Some of his disciples therefore said to one another, “What is this that he says to us, ‘A little while, and you won’t see me, and again a little while, and you will see me;’ and, ‘Because I go to the Father’?”

Arminian

They quote Him to each other and still do not understand. Horizontal processing of a vertical departure.

Confusion is allowed. They stay in the conversation; that is better than leaving.

Reformed

Some said to one another: the riddle is shared, not solved.

Going to the Father is the clause they cannot yet fit to “see me.” The resurrection will parse it.

John 16:18

18They said therefore, “What is this that he says, ‘A little while’? We don’t know what he is saying.”

Arminian

They fixate on the phrase. Not knowing is confessed.

“We don’t know” is a disciple sentence. He will answer the want (v. 19) before He rebukes the scatter (v. 32).

Reformed

The little while is the sticking point. They admit ignorance.

John records the blank look so that later readers will see how resurrection light had to be given, not guessed.

John 16:19

19Therefore Jesus perceived that they wanted to ask him, and he said to them, “Do you inquire among yourselves concerning this, that I said, ‘A little while, and you won’t see me, and again a little while, and you will see me’?

Arminian

He knows the unasked question and voices it. Omniscience here is pastoral, not a trap.

The Lord answers what we cannot quite form. Wanting to ask is already met.

Reformed

He knew, so He said. The interior of the Eleven is not private from Him.

The Word reads His own. The following explanation is gift to a desire He named for them.

John 16:20

20Most certainly I tell you that you will weep and lament, but the world will rejoice. You will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will be turned into joy.

Arminian

Weep, lament, world’s party—then sorrow turned, not merely ended. Wesley heard the turn as joy.

The world’s rejoice is temporary. Grief over the cross will become joy; the turn is His work, and they will enter it.

Reformed

Calvin located the turn in the resurrection. World-rejoice is the wrong feast.

God writes joy where they will write funeral. The truly, truly binds the conversion of sorrow to His act.

John 16:21

21A woman, when she gives birth, has sorrow because her time has come. But when she has delivered the child, she doesn’t remember the anguish any more, for the joy that a human being is born into the world.

Arminian

Birth is the metaphor: hour of sorrow, then a human being, and anguish recedes before joy.

The cross is labor, not stillbirth. What comes out of the hour is life the world can meet.

Reformed

Hour, delivery, joy over a born human. The analogy is creational and precise.

The anguish is not pointless. A world gets a new-born joy because the hour came.

John 16:22

22Therefore you now have sorrow, but I will see you again, and your heart will rejoice, and no one will take your joy away from you.

Arminian

I will see you again—His seeing, not only theirs. Their joy cannot be taken away.

Resurrection joy is not mood. No one takes it; they are still commanded later to abide, but the joy’s source is His seeing them.

Reformed

He will see them: subject is Jesus. Hearts will rejoice; the joy is theft-proof.

The joy is located in His return-to-them. Enemies can take comforts; they cannot steal this seeing.

John 16:23

23“In that day you will ask me no questions. Most certainly I tell you, whatever you may ask of the Father in my name, he will give it to you.

Arminian

That day relocates asking: to the Father in the Son’s name, with a truly-truly promise of giving.

The name is not a charm; it is access. Ask—the command assumes they may, and the Father gives.

Reformed

Ask the Father in my name: new covenant access. He will give.

The day of resurrection/Spirit opens asking that is heard because of the Son, not because of better technique.

John 16:24

24Until now, you have asked nothing in my name. Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be made full.

Arminian

Until now: a new key. Ask, receive, full joy. Wesley joined this to the sorrow-joy thread.

Classical Arminians: asking is a genuine act of faith God answers. Fullness of joy is the purpose, not leftover scraps.

Reformed

Asked nothing in my name—yet. The imperative creates a new practice.

That joy may be full. Prayer in the name is a means of the joy He promised in v. 22.

John 16:25

25“I have spoken these things to you in figures of speech. But the time is coming when I will no more speak to you in figures of speech, but will tell you plainly about the Father.

Arminian

Figures now, plain speech coming. The Father is the subject of the clearer telling.

He moves them from riddle to plainness. The hour of the Spirit/resurrection will not make Him a different Lord, only a clearer one.

Reformed

Figures / plainly: two modes, one Christ. The hour is His.

Plain speech about the Father is the fruit of the finished hour, not of their cleverness tonight.

John 16:26

26In that day you will ask in my name; and I don’t say to you that I will pray to the Father for you,

Arminian

Not a denial of intercession (cf. 17; Rom 8) so much as a denial that they are distanced. They will ask in His name.

You are not stuck in a waiting room while Jesus files papers. Name-access is communion.

Reformed

He will not speak as if they lacked access. Name-asking is itself the path.

The High Priest is not unemployed; the point is the Father’s open welcome (v. 27), not a cold chain of clerks.

John 16:27

27for the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me, and have believed that I came from God.

Arminian

The Father himself loves you—because they have loved and believed the Sent One. Love and faith are named as the relational because.

This because is not wages that bought the Father, but the living bond He delights in. Loving and believing Jesus is how people stand in that love; the invitation to such faith remains sincere.

Reformed

Calvin: the Father himself loves you. The because marks those who love and believe the Son as the people of that love.

The Father’s love for the Son’s people is prior in the Gospel (3:16; 17:23–24) and here is traced in their love and faith as evidence and bond, not as an independent cause that created God’s heart.

Key contrast The Father himself loves those who love and believe the Son. Arminian readers find a living condition of trust. Reformed readers hear the Father’s own love displayed in and under the faith He has already been forming in them.

John 16:28

28I came from the Father and have come into the world. Again, I leave the world and go to the Father.”

Arminian

The whole itinerary in one sentence: from Father, into world, leaving, going to Father.

Origin and return interpret everything between. Believing He came from God (v. 27, 30) is believing this itinerary.

Reformed

Mission summary: descent and ascent. The world is a stop He now leaves.

The Sender and the destination are the same Father. History is that going.

John 16:29

29His disciples said to him, “Behold, now you are speaking plainly, and using no figures of speech.

Arminian

They think the hour of plainness has already arrived. The disciples’ “Ah, now” shows overconfident timing.

We often declare we understand just before we scatter. He will question this now (v. 31).

Reformed

They announce the curriculum complete. Plain speech, they say.

The figures are barely dry. Their certainty is a setup for v. 32.

John 16:30

30Now we know that you know all things, and don’t need for anyone to question you. By this we believe that you came from God.”

Arminian

They ground belief in His knowing-all, including unasked questions. The why of faith is right-directioned and premature.

This is belief, thin and about to be tested. He does not say they never believed; He asks if they do now, then predicts the scatter.

Reformed

Knowledge of His omniscience becomes their stated reason for believing His origin.

True as far as it goes. The next sentences will measure this confession against an hour, not a seminar.

John 16:31

31Jesus answered them, “Do you now believe?

Arminian

A question, not a congratulations. Now is the word under pressure.

Present faith must become remaining faith. The question is an invitation to humility, not a sneer.

Reformed

Do you now believe?—the now is fragile.

He does not deny all faith; He refuses their timetable of arrival. The hour will tell.

John 16:32

32Behold, the time is coming, yes, and has now come, that you will be scattered, everyone to his own place, and you will leave me alone. Yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me.

Arminian

Scatter, home, leaving Him alone—yet He is not alone. Their coming failure is predicted without canceling the Father’s presence with the Son.

Believers can scatter. Restoration is coming (ch. 21), and the prediction is meant to be remembered as mercy, not merely shame.

Reformed

The hour has come: scattering is imminent. Alone as to them; not alone as to the Father.

The Son’s communion with the Father holds when disciples do not. Their leaving does not un-Son Him.

John 16:33

33I have told you these things, that in me you may have peace. In the world you have trouble; but cheer up! I have overcome the world.”

Arminian

Purpose: peace in Him. Location: tribulation in the world. Command: take heart. Ground: He has overcome. Wesley: be of good cheer.

Peace is in a person, not in a calm decade. They will have trouble and may still take heart because His overcoming is announced as fact to be trusted.

Reformed

Calvin: victory is already ours in Christ though we wrestle under the cross. In me / in the world: two realms.

I have overcome—perfected conquest. Take heart is not pep; it is union with the Victor while tribulation is still honest.

Key contrast Common confession: tribulation in the world, peace in Christ, because He has overcome. Arminians typically press take-heart as an earnest call to trust the Victor. Reformed readers typically press the perfect tense: the overcoming is accomplished, and their peace is participation in that finished victory.
