Passage

John 13

Verses 31–38 · ESV · Voices

When Judas has gone, Jesus says now the Son of Man is glorified, and God in Him. He will be with them only a little longer; they cannot follow now. A new commandment: love one another as He has loved them; by this all will know they are His disciples. Peter asks why he cannot follow now and vows his life. Jesus answers with the rooster: the denier before dawn.

John 13:31

31When he had gone out, Jesus said, “Now is the Son of Man glorified, and God is glorified in him.

Arminian

Gone out, now glorified. The night of v. 30 is, in His mouth, the now of glory.

Arminian now: Judas’s exit starts the public path to the cross, and Jesus calls that glorifying. Glory is not only later shine; it is obedient love visible.

Reformed

Now: after the traitor’s leaving, glory language.

Reformed now: the hour of 12:23 and 13:1 speaks. God glorified in the Son of Man includes this handing-over.

John 13:32

32If God is glorified in him, God will also glorify him in himself, and glorify him at once.

Arminian

If / will / at once: the Father’s answering glory is not delayed to a remote age only.

Arminian soon: at once includes resurrection and the name above every name, pledged to the One walking toward Friday.

Reformed

God will glorify him in himself, at once.

Reformed exchange: the Son’s glorifying of God is met by God’s glorifying of the Son. At once is the Father’s speed.

John 13:33

33Little children, yet a little while I am with you. You will seek me, and just as I said to the Jews, so now I also say to you, ‘Where I am going you cannot come.’

Arminian

Little children—tender, after glory-talk. A little while; you cannot come where I am going, the same line once said to “the Jews.”

Arminian ache: even His own cannot follow this hour. 14:1–3 will promise a later taking; tonight’s cannot is real.

Reformed

Little children / cannot come. The restriction is for this going.

Reformed distinction: later they will follow (v. 36). The cannot is timed to the unique hour of atonement.

John 13:34

34A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another.

Arminian

New commandment: love one another as I have loved you. New because the measure is now His own love, heading to the basin and the cross. Wesley and Calvin both mark the example.

Arminian ethic: this is command, not mood. As I have loved you is the standard grace enables in a people who must actually love.

Reformed

Calvin: new because He sets His own example. Just as I have loved you.

Reformed newness: Leviticus already said love neighbor; the newness is the as of the crucified Lord as the community’s law.

Key contrast Shared: the new command is mutual love measured by Jesus’ love. Arminians typically emphasize the enabled duty of the as. Reformed readers typically emphasize that His prior love is both pattern and power of theirs.

John 13:35

35By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another.”

Arminian

The world’s knowledge of their discipleship hangs on if you have love. Apologetics here is a common life.

Arminian public: all people will know—or not. The if is the church’s visible test, sincerely laid on them.

Reformed

By this all will know / if you have love.

Reformed mark: doctrine without this love fails the Lord’s own ID badge. The if is the appointed evidence, not a rival gospel.

John 13:36

36Simon Peter said to him, “Lord, where are you going?” Jesus answered him, “Where I am going you cannot follow me now, but you will follow afterward.”

Arminian

Where? Cannot now, will afterward. Peter’s story is already a now and a later (21:19).

Arminian hope: the cannot is not a never. Afterward includes restoration after the rooster. Following is still the calling.

Reformed

Cannot now / will afterward: Peter’s two tenses.

Reformed pastoral: the Lord writes Peter’s martyrdom into afterward while forbidding tonight’s heroics.

John 13:37

37Peter said to him, “Lord, why can I not follow you now? I will lay down my life for you.”

Arminian

Why not now, plus a vow of his life. The will is loud and sincere and about to fail.

Arminian Peter: zeal is not the same as ability. He needs the washing he almost refused and the Spirit not yet given; the vow without that is a trap.

Reformed

Why can I not / I will lay down my life. Rash confidence, Calvin said.

Reformed reverse: the Shepherd will lay down His life for Peter, not first the other way. The vow inverts the gospel.

John 13:38

38Jesus answered, “Will you lay down your life for me? Truly, truly, I say to you, the rooster will not crow till you have denied me three times.

Arminian

Will you?—then the rooster and three denials before dawn. Foreknowledge is mercy that will hurt.

Arminian warning: a real disciple can deny. The prediction is meant to be remembered when he weeps, so that afterward (v. 36) still stands. Falling is not treated as impossible, nor as the last word.

Reformed

Will you lay down your life for me? The question unmasks the vow. Three times before the crow.

Reformed mercy in the wound: Jesus knows the denials and still calls him little child, still promises afterward. Peter’s collapse is certain tonight; it is not the end of being His.

Historic words on the new commandment and a disciple’s vowed life—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside John 13:31–38.

Arminian Notable voices

“A new commandment I give unto you, that ye love one another; as I have loved you. By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples. Wilt thou lay down thy life for my sake? The cock shall not crow, till thou hast denied me thrice.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 13:34–38)

Reformed Notable voices

“The commandment is called new, because he sets before them his own example. By this love they will be known as his. Peter’s rash confidence is reproved: he will deny him thrice before the cock crows.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 13:34–38)
