Passage

John 13

Verses 18–30 · WEB · Voices

He is not speaking of all of them. Scripture must be fulfilled: he who ate bread lifted his heel. He tells them before it happens so they will believe. The one who receives whom He sends receives Him and the Sender. Troubled in spirit, He discloses that one will betray Him. The beloved disciple, at Peter’s nod, asks who. Jesus dips the morsel, gives it to Judas; Satan enters him. “What you do, do quickly.” Judas goes out. It was night.

John 13:18

18I don’t speak concerning all of you. I know whom I have chosen; but that the Scripture may be fulfilled, ‘He who eats bread with me has lifted up his heel against me.’

Arminian

Not all; I know whom I have chosen. Psalm 41 must be fulfilled in a table-companion’s heel. Wesley noted the not-all.

Chosen here is the Eleven in contrast to Judas—His knowing is not confusion. Fulfillment does not make Judas a victim without a heel of his own; lifting the heel is still his act.

Reformed

Calvin: I know whom I have chosen. Scripture must be fulfilled.

Jesus’ knowledge of His chosen and Scripture’s must sit together with Judas’s treachery. The heel is guilty and written.

Key contrast Jesus knows His chosen; Scripture is fulfilled in the betrayer at bread. Arminians typically refuse to let fulfillment erase Judas’s agency. Reformed commentators often refuse to let agency erase that Jesus knew and Scripture stood.

John 13:19

19From now on, I tell you before it happens, that when it happens, you may believe that I am he.

Arminian

Told beforehand so that when it happens they may believe that I am. Betrayal itself becomes a faith-argument.

He wants their believing to survive the scandal. The I am is the point, even of this wound.

Reformed

He tells them before it happens, so that when it happens they may believe that I am he.

The treachery will confirm, not cancel, His identity for those who remember this sentence.

John 13:20

20Most certainly I tell you, he who receives whomever I send, receives me; and he who receives me, receives him who sent me.”

Arminian

Whoever receives the sent receives Jesus and the Father. Mission is personal identification.

After a betrayal warning, a reception promise. The church’s sent ones still carry this whoever of welcome.

Reformed

Truly, truly: chained reception—sent / me / Sender.

To receive His messenger is to receive the chain of God. Judas’s going out does not void this for the others He will send.

John 13:21

21When Jesus had said this, he was troubled in spirit, and testified, “Most certainly I tell you that one of you will betray me.”

Arminian

Troubled in spirit, then the testimony: one of you. Love to the end includes this distress.

Jesus is no stoic. The betrayal of a friend wrenches Him, and He still tells the truth.

Reformed

Jesus is troubled in spirit and testifies with a solemn “most certainly.” One of you—the circle is the wound.

The Word made flesh is troubled. Disclosure is costly and necessary.

John 13:22

22The disciples looked at one another, perplexed about whom he spoke.

Arminian

Looks around the table, uncertainty. No one points first at Judas.

They examine one another, even themselves (in the Synoptics). Uncertainty is healthier than “not me.”

Reformed

Uncertain of whom: the secret is not obvious to flesh.

The betrayer is still in their fellowship-sight as one of them.

John 13:23

23One of his disciples, whom Jesus loved, was at the table, leaning against Jesus’ chest.

Arminian

The beloved, at His side. Love named as the disciple’s identity in this Gospel.

Reclining at His side is John’s picture of the share Peter feared to lose. Intimacy is gift.

Reformed

Whom Jesus loved: the narrator’s name for himself or his figure.

The side-place will be the asking-place (v. 25). Love here is Jesus’ love.

John 13:24

24Simon Peter therefore beckoned to him, and said to him, “Tell us who it is of whom he speaks.”

Arminian

Peter prompts the one closer. Hierarchy of access at a table.

We often need a brother to ask the question we cannot. Peter’s motion is not cowardice only; it is using the beloved’s place.

Reformed

Motioned to ask: Peter still leading from a seat not the bosom.

Two disciples, one question, the Lord’s answer with a morsel.

John 13:25

25He, leaning back, as he was, on Jesus’ chest, asked him, “Lord, who is it?”

Arminian

Leaning back against Jesus, Lord, who is it? The question is whispered from contact.

Who-is-it is asked from against-Him, not from a cold row.

Reformed

Leaning back: the physical of 13:23 becomes speech.

The beloved puts Peter’s question into the Lord’s ear.

John 13:26

26Jesus therefore answered, “It is he to whom I will give this piece of bread when I have dipped it.” So when he had dipped the piece of bread, he gave it to Judas, the son of Simon Iscariot.

Arminian

The sign is a dipped morsel given. The honored gesture of giving the morsel identifies the betrayer. He gives bread to the heel-lifter.

The last gift before the night is food from Jesus’ hand. Judas receives bread and will not receive the Lord.

Reformed

Morsel dipped and given: the named man is Judas son of Simon.

No riddle left for the beloved. The gift of bread and the gift of identity fall on the same man.

John 13:27

27After the piece of bread, then Satan entered into him. Then Jesus said to him, “What you do, do quickly.”

Arminian

After the morsel, Satan entered. Then Jesus’ do quickly. Agency is layered: Satan, Judas, and Jesus’ command to hasten.

Entering does not erase Judas’s going-to-do. He is not excused as occupied housing; he is told to do what he is going to do, and he will walk out.

Reformed

Calvin: already the devil’s, now given up. Do quickly is not permission as approval; it is sovereign haste over a chosen evil.

Satan enters, and the hour moves at Jesus’ word. Darkness does not seize the timetable.

John 13:28

28Now nobody at the table knew why he said this to him.

Arminian

The table does not understand do quickly. Even with the morsel, they miss.

Spiritual crisis can sit in a room of friends who are still thinking about groceries (v. 29).

Reformed

No one knew why. The beloved may know who, not yet why the quickly.

Jesus and Judas share a meaning the others do not.

John 13:29

29For some thought, because Judas had the money box, that Jesus said to him, “Buy what things we need for the feast,” or that he should give something to the poor.

Arminian

Moneybag logic: feast or poor. They interpret in the only ledger they have.

Good guesses (charity, feast) can be completely wrong. We still baptize others’ exits with innocent errands.

Reformed

Treasurer explanations. Feast / poor: reasonable and false.

The thief of 12:6 is thought to be on a mercy run. Night will correct the minutes.

John 13:30

30Therefore having received that morsel, he went out immediately. It was night.

Arminian

Wesley and Calvin both felt the night. Light has come; a man walks into dark with bread from Jesus still in the story.

Going out is chosen motion into night. The hour of 13:1 is now Judas’s hour too, and the door was not locked.

Reformed

Calvin: a fit time for the work of darkness. Immediately went out.

The word “night” means more than the time on the clock. John 1’s darkness, 3:19’s loved dark, here walks on two feet.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

On the betrayer at the table and the night he entered, a few classic voices from each tradition.

Arminian Notable voices

“I speak not of you all; I know whom I have chosen. He it is to whom I shall give a sop. After the sop, Satan entered into him. He then having received the sop went immediately out: and it was night.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 13:18, 26–30)

Reformed Notable voices

“I know whom I have chosen. The Scripture must be fulfilled. Satan entered into him: he was already the devil’s, and now is given up. It was night: a fit time for the work of darkness.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 13:18, 27, 30)
