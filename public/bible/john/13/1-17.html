Passage

John 13

Verses 1–17 · ESV · Voices

Before the feast, Jesus knows His hour has come to depart to the Father. Having loved His own who were in the world, He loved them to the end. During supper, knowing the Father had given all things into His hands, He washes the disciples’ feet, including the coming betrayer. Peter resists, then overcorrects. Unless He washes them, they have no share with Him. He interprets the act: a servant is not greater than his master; they will be blessed if they do these things.

John 13:1

1Now before the Feast of the Passover, when Jesus knew that his hour had come to depart out of this world to the Father, having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.

Arminian

He knew the hour; He loved His own who were in the world to the end—to the uttermost, through the end. Wesley lingered on that constancy.

Arminian belonging: His own are loved all the way. The world is where they still are; the love does not wait for them to get out first.

Reformed

Calvin: love constant and invincible, to the end. Hour known, departure to the Father, His own loved.

Reformed tenacity: this verse hangs over Judas’s plot and Peter’s fall. The end will test the love; the love will outlast the test.

John 13:2

2During supper, when the devil had already put it into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon’s son, to betray him,

Arminian

Supper and a devil-put thought already in Judas. The basin will be set down in a room where betrayal is already conceived.

Arminian grief: influence is real and Judas’s heart is active. He is not a puppet only; the putting-in meets a willing interior (v. 27 will show a further entering).

Reformed

Already: the plot is in motion before the towel. Devil and heart are both named.

Reformed dark: Jesus washes feet in a room Satan has already visited. Love to the end includes a traitor at table.

John 13:3

3Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he had come from God and was going back to God,

Arminian

Knowing: all things in His hands, from God, back to God. The stoop is not identity-loss; it is identity-in-action.

Arminian lordship: the One with all things in hand picks up a towel. Greatness expresses itself as service they can imitate (v. 15).

Reformed

Given all things / come from / going back: full consciousness of deity’s errand.

Reformed paradox: omnipotence stoops. The hands that hold all things wash dirty ones.

John 13:4

4rose from supper. He laid aside his outer garments, and taking a towel, tied it around his waist.

Arminian

Rose, laid aside, took, tied. The verbs are incarnation in miniature.

Arminian sight: watch the Lord undress for work we thought beneath rabbis. The example will be this sequence, not a lecture only.

Reformed

Laid aside outer garments: the visual of 1:14 in a room.

Reformed sign: before the cross’s laying-down, a towel. He interprets His death with a basin.

John 13:5

5Then he poured water into a basin and began to wash the disciples’ feet and to wipe them with the towel that was wrapped around him.

Arminian

Poured, began to wash, wipe. Feet of the Twelve, betrayer included unless John later excepts—and he does not.

Arminian shock: He washes people who will fail Him. Grace serves before it is repaid, and often after it will not be.

Reformed

Began to wash: the Lord on the floor.

Reformed lowliness: this is the God of v. 3. The basin is theology.

John 13:6

6He came to Simon Peter, who said to him, “Lord, do you wash my feet?”

Arminian

Peter names the scandal: Lord, my feet. The question is reverence misfired.

Arminian Peter: we still try to protect Jesus from His own gospel. The protest sounds humble and refuses the gift.

Reformed

Do you wash my feet?: correct title, wrong refusal.

Reformed irony: the one about to be told he has no share is the one policing the Lord’s hands.

John 13:7

7Jesus answered him, “What I am doing you do not understand now, but afterward you will understand.”

Arminian

Now / afterward. The cross will interpret the bowl.

Arminian patience: they may receive what they do not yet understand. Afterward is resurrection-and-Spirit sight.

Reformed

Not now, afterward: He does not wait on their theory.

Reformed pedagogy: union’s rites often precede comprehension. Peter must submit in the dark.

John 13:8

8Peter said to him, “You shall never wash my feet.” Jesus answered him, “If I do not wash you, you have no share with me.”

Arminian

Never vs. no share. Wesley: if I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. Cleansing by Jesus is not optional courtesy.

Classical Arminians: share-with-me is the whole salvation-bond. Refusing His washing is refusing Him; the offer of washing is as serious as the threat of no share.

Reformed

Calvin: we must be cleansed by Him or we are no part of His. Never is Peter’s piety; no share is Jesus’ line.

Reformed necessity: washing is Christ’s act. Peter’s never would write him out of the inheritance.

Key contrast Shared: without Jesus’ washing, no share with Him. Arminians typically hear a real warning to a disciple who might refuse the gift. Reformed readers typically hear that union depends on Christ’s cleansing, which He will not let Peter’s pride veto.

John 13:9

9Simon Peter said to him, “Lord, not my feet only but also my hands and my head!”

Arminian

From never to all-over. Peter still wants to manage the volume of grace.

Arminian swing: zeal without understanding overcorrects. Jesus will scale it back to the wash they need.

Reformed

Hands and head: more water as more love.

Reformed excess: Peter’s all-in is still Peter’s program. The Lord will define the bath.

John 13:10

10Jesus said to him, “The one who has bathed does not need to wash, except for his feet, but is completely clean. And you are clean, but not every one of you.”

Arminian

Bathed / feet: once-clean people still need the smaller wash. You are clean, but not every one—Judas already in view.

Arminian two-level: justification’s bath and daily feet. Clean is real; not-every-one is also real. Remaining dirt on a clean person is not the same as never having bathed.

Reformed

Completely clean, except feet. You are clean, but not all.

Reformed already: the Eleven’s cleanliness is His verdict (15:3). The exception is the one whose heart the devil already holds.

John 13:11

11For he knew who was to betray him; that was why he said, “Not all of you are clean.”

Arminian

He knew. Not all clean is targeted knowledge, not a vague scare.

Arminian tragedy: knowing does not stop the washing of the betrayer’s feet. Love to the end includes this knowledge.

Reformed

Knew who would betray: the aside interprets v. 10.

Reformed omniscience: the unclean one is not a surprise guest. The sentence was precise.

John 13:12

12When he had washed their feet and put on his outer garments and resumed his place, he said to them, “Do you understand what I have done to you?

Arminian

Garments back on, place resumed, then the question. Understanding is now the assignment.

Arminian teaching: the sign is not self-explanatory enough; He will spell the ethic. We still need both the act and the word.

Reformed

Resumed his place: Lord again, having been servant.

Reformed sequence: humiliation, then interpretation. Do you understand is a classroom in a dining room.

John 13:13

13You call me Teacher and Lord, and you are right, for so I am.

Arminian

Teacher and Lord—they are right. The titles stay; the towel reinterpreted them.

Arminian authority: He does not cease to be Lord by washing. Example-ethics still have a Lord.

Reformed

So I am: the titles are true.

Reformed identity: servant-work did not cancel Lordship. It defined it.

John 13:14

14If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another’s feet.

Arminian

If I, then you ought. The ought is as thick as the titles.

Arminian duty: enabled imitation. Footwashing as posture toward one another is not optional for people who call Him Lord.

Reformed

Ought: the logic of the greater to the lesser.

Reformed obligation: the church’s mutual service is deduced from His. No one outranks this basin.

John 13:15

15For I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you.

Arminian

Example: do just as I have done. Wesley: I have given you an example.

Arminian pattern: just as is the measure. Creative avoidance of serving one another is already not doing this.

Reformed

Calvin: an example of humility. Just as I have done.

Reformed gift of pattern: He gave an example, not only an experience. The church is to copy the stoop.

John 13:16

16Truly, truly, I say to you, a servant is not greater than his master, nor is a messenger greater than the one who sent him.

Arminian

Truly, truly: servant < master, messenger < sender. Status-escape is closed.

Arminian missionary ethic: sent ones do not outgrow the Sender’s towel. 15:20 will reuse this for persecution.

Reformed

Not greater: two comparisons, one point.

Reformed order: messengers remain under the sent-Lord. Greatness in this house runs downward.

John 13:17

17If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them.

Arminian

Know / if you do: blessing is in the doing. Wesley: happy are ye if ye do them.

Arminian beatitude: knowledge without doing is not the blessed condition. Grace that teaches also enables the doing it blesses.

Reformed

If you know / if you do: two ifs, blessing on the second.

Reformed practice: understood towels still have to hit feet. Blessing is promised to doers in this school.

Historic words on love to the end and the Lord who washes feet—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside John 13:1–17.

Arminian Notable voices

“Having loved his own, he loved them to the end. If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you. If ye know these things, happy are ye if ye do them.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 13:1, 8, 15, 17)

Reformed Notable voices

“He loved them to the end: his love is constant and invincible. Unless I wash you, you have no part with me: we must be cleansed by him or we are no part of his. He sets before them an example of humility.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 13:1, 8, 15)
