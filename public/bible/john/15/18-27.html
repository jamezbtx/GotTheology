Passage

John 15

Verses 18–27 · WEB · Voices

Jesus warns the disciples that the world will hate them because it hated him first. They are not of the world, since he chose them out of it, and a servant is not greater than his master. Those who reject Jesus have no excuse, because he spoke and did works no one else did, yet they hated him without cause, fulfilling Scripture. The Spirit of truth, whom Jesus will send from the Father, will bear witness about him, and the disciples will bear witness too. Both traditions draw comfort and responsibility from the passage. They differ on what “I chose you out of the world” implies and on the guilt of those who saw and heard.

John 15:18

18If the world hates you, you know that it has hated me before it hated you.

Arminian

If the world hates you, know that it hated me first. Arminian readers see suffering as the shared lot of all who follow Christ faithfully.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the world’s hatred as evidence of the believer’s union with Christ.

John 15:19

19If you were of the world, the world would love its own. But because you are not of the world, since I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you.

Arminian

You are not of the world, but I chose you out of the world; therefore the world hates you. Arminian readers often read this as Christ’s choice of the disciples for apostleship and witness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a choosing that separates people from the world, consistent with personal election to salvation.

Key contrast Both see Christ’s choosing setting the disciples apart from the world. Arminian readers see a choice of the apostles for their mission. Reformed readers see it as reflecting God’s election of his people out of the world.

John 15:20

20Remember the word that I said to you: ‘A servant is not greater than his lord.’ If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you. If they kept my word, they will also keep yours.

Arminian

A servant is not greater than his master. If they persecuted me, they will persecute you. Arminian readers see a call to endure faithfully.

Reformed

Reformed readers see suffering as part of God’s purpose in conforming believers to Christ.

John 15:21

21But they will do all these things to you for my name’s sake, because they don’t know him who sent me.

Arminian

They will do all this on account of my name, because they do not know him who sent me. Arminian readers see ignorance of God as willful, rooted in refusing the light.

Reformed

Reformed readers see spiritual blindness as the condition of the unregenerate.

John 15:22

22If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have had sin; but now they have no excuse for their sin.

Arminian

If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have sin; but now they have no excuse. Arminian readers see accountability proportioned to light received. Greater revelation means greater responsibility.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sin made inexcusable by rejecting Christ, though all are already guilty in Adam. Here “sin” means the specific sin of rejecting him.

John 15:23

23He who hates me, hates my Father also.

Arminian

Whoever hates me hates my Father also. Both traditions see the unity of Father and Son. Arminian readers stress that rejecting Christ is rejecting God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s deity implied in this identification.

John 15:24

24If I hadn’t done among them the works which no one else did, they wouldn’t have had sin. But now they have seen and also hated both me and my Father.

Arminian

If I had not done among them the works no one else did, they would not be guilty of sin. Arminian readers see clear evidence that left them without excuse.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that evidence does not produce faith apart from grace, while it does increase guilt.

John 15:25

25But this happened so that the word may be fulfilled which was written in their law, ‘They hated me without a cause.’

Arminian

“They hated me without a cause.” Arminian readers see hatred as a free choice, foreseen in Scripture.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s word fulfilled even in human rebellion, without removing human guilt.

John 15:26

26“When the Counselor has come, whom I will send to you from the Father, the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father, he will testify about me.

Arminian

When the Helper comes, the Spirit of truth who proceeds from the Father, he will bear witness about me. Arminian readers see the Spirit witnessing to the world, convicting and drawing people to Christ (16:8).

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit’s witness as effective in the hearts of the elect. The verse also figures in the Western teaching that the Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son.

John 15:27

27You will also testify, because you have been with me from the beginning.

Arminian

You also will bear witness, because you have been with me from the beginning. Arminian readers see the church’s witness joined with the Spirit’s to call all people.

Reformed

Reformed readers see apostolic witness as the foundation of the church and the means of gathering God’s people.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Arminian and Reformed writers of the past on the world’s hatred and the Helper who proceeds from the Father and bears witness, in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“When the Comforter is come, whom I will send from the Father, the Spirit of truth, who proceedeth from the Father, he shall testify of me—The Spirit’s coming, and being sent by our Lord from the Father, to testify of him, are personal characters, and plainly distinguish him from the Father and the Son; and his title as the Spirit of truth, together with his proceeding from the Father, can agree to none but a Divine person.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 15:26)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Spirit is said to testify of Christ, because he retains and fixes our faith on him alone, that we may not seek elsewhere any part of our salvation. When he says that he will send him from the Father, and, again, that he proceedeth from the Father, he does so in order to increase the weight of his authority; for the testimony of the Spirit would not be sufficient against attacks so powerful if we were not convinced that he proceedeth from God.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 15:26–27)
