Passage

John 17

Verses 1–5 · WEB

Jesus begins his high priestly prayer as his hour arrives. He asks the Father to glorify the Son so that the Son may glorify him. The Father has given the Son authority over all flesh to give eternal life to all whom the Father has given him. Eternal life is to know the only true God and Jesus Christ whom he sent. Jesus has finished the work given him and asks to be glorified with the glory he had with the Father before the world existed. Verse 2 contributes to the debate over election.

John 17:1

1Jesus said these things, then lifting up his eyes to heaven, he said, “Father, the time has come. Glorify your Son, that your Son may also glorify you;

Arminian

Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify you. Arminian readers see the cross as the hour of God’s glory revealed to the world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the hour appointed in God’s eternal plan, now arriving.

John 17:2

2even as you gave him authority over all flesh, so he will give eternal life to all whom you have given him.

Arminian

You have given him authority over all flesh, to give eternal life to all whom you have given him. Arminian readers see Christ’s authority over all people, with eternal life given to those who come to him in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a distinction between Christ’s authority over all flesh and his gift of eternal life to those the Father gave him. They see that distinction as pointing to election.

Key contrast Both see Christ’s universal authority and his gift of eternal life. Arminian readers see the gift going to all who believe. Reformed readers see it going to a particular people given by the Father.

John 17:3

3This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ.

Arminian

This is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent. Arminian readers see eternal life as a personal relationship open to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see knowledge of God as the essence of salvation, given by grace.

John 17:4

4I glorified you on the earth. I have accomplished the work which you have given me to do.

Arminian

I glorified you on earth, having accomplished the work you gave me to do. Arminian readers see Christ’s obedient completion of his mission.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s finished work as securing salvation.

John 17:5

5Now, Father, glorify me with your own self with the glory which I had with you before the world existed.

Arminian

Glorify me with the glory I had with you before the world existed. Both traditions see a clear statement of the Son’s eternal pre-existence. Arminian readers see the Son returning to his glory.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s eternal deity and his exaltation after humiliation.
