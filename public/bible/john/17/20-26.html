Passage

John 17

Verses 20–26 · WEB

Jesus extends his prayer to all who will believe through the apostles’ word. He prays that they may all be one, as the Father and Son are one, so that the world may believe the Father sent him. He has given them his glory, so that the world may know the Father loved them as he loved the Son. He asks that those the Father has given him may be with him to see his glory. The world has not known the Father, but Jesus has made his name known and will continue to do so, so that the Father’s love may be in them. Both traditions draw deeply from this prayer.

John 17:20

20“Not for these only do I pray, but for those also who will believe in me through their word,

Arminian

I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word. Arminian readers see Jesus praying for all future believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ praying for all his elect, including those not yet born.

John 17:21

21that they may all be one; even as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be one in us; that the world may believe that you sent me.

Arminian

That they may all be one, so that the world may believe that you have sent me. Arminian readers see “that the world may believe” as showing Christ’s desire for the salvation of the world as a whole.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the world believing as the fruit of the church’s unity and witness, as God gathers his people from the world.

Key contrast Both see Christ praying that the world will believe. Arminian readers see his desire for the salvation of every person. Reformed readers see him desiring that people from the world will be brought to faith through the church’s witness.

John 17:22

22The glory which you have given me, I have given to them, that they may be one, even as we are one,

Arminian

The glory you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one. Arminian readers see glory shared with believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers sharing in Christ’s glory through union with him.

John 17:23

23I in them, and you in me, that they may be perfected into one, that the world may know that you sent me and loved them, even as you loved me.

Arminian

I in them and you in me, that the world may know that you sent me and loved them. Arminian readers see the church’s unity as a witness to the world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Father’s love for his people as the same love he has for the Son.

John 17:24

24Father, I desire that they also whom you have given me be with me where I am, that they may see my glory which you have given me, for you loved me before the foundation of the world.

Arminian

Father, I desire that they also, whom you have given me, may be with me where I am. Arminian readers see Christ’s desire for believers to share his glory.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s will for his people, which the Father will certainly grant.

John 17:25

25Righteous Father, the world hasn’t known you, but I knew you; and these knew that you sent me.

Arminian

O righteous Father, the world has not known you, but I have known you. Arminian readers see the world’s ignorance of God as something the gospel addresses.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the contrast between the world and those given to Christ.

John 17:26

26I made known to them your name, and will make it known; that the love with which you loved me may be in them, and I in them.”

Arminian

I made known to them your name, and I will continue to make it known, that the love with which you have loved me may be in them. Arminian readers see Christ’s continuing revelation of God’s love to believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s ongoing work ensuring that his people experience the Father’s love.
