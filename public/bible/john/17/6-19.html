Passage

John 17

Verses 6–19 · WEB · Voices

Jesus prays for his disciples, the people the Father gave him out of the world. They have kept God’s word and believed that Jesus came from the Father. Jesus says he is praying for them, not for the world, and asks the Father to keep them in his name. He kept them while he was with them, and none was lost except the son of destruction, so that Scripture might be fulfilled. He does not ask that they be taken out of the world but kept from the evil one, and sanctified in the truth. Both traditions see Christ’s care for his own here. They differ on the scope of his prayer and on what Judas’s loss means.

John 17:6

6“I revealed your name to the people whom you have given me out of the world. They were yours, and you have given them to me. They have kept your word.

Arminian

I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world. Arminian readers see the disciples as those who received Christ’s revelation and were given to him as they believed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a people given to Christ by the Father before their response, a key element of election.

Key contrast Both see the disciples as given to Christ. Arminian readers see that giving connected with their faith. Reformed readers see it as the Father’s prior gift that leads to their faith.

John 17:7

7Now they have known that all things whatever you have given me are from you,

Arminian

Now they know that everything you have given me is from you. Arminian readers see faith growing in understanding.

Reformed

Reformed readers see revelation producing true knowledge in those given to Christ.

John 17:8

8for the words which you have given me I have given to them; and they received them, and knew for sure that I came from you. They have believed that you sent me.

Arminian

They have received your words and believed that you sent me. Arminian readers see the disciples’ genuine reception of Christ’s words.

Reformed

Reformed readers see reception and faith as the fruit of the Father’s gift.

John 17:9

9I pray for them. I don’t pray for the world, but for those whom you have given me, for they are yours.

Arminian

I am praying for them. I am not praying for the world but for those you have given me. Arminian readers see Jesus focusing this prayer on his disciples without implying that he does not love the world. He prays for the world in verses 21 and 23.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s high priestly intercession as particular, for those the Father gave him. They connect this with a definite atonement.

Key contrast Both see Jesus praying here for his own. Arminian readers see this as a focus for this moment, noting that he prays for the world’s belief in verse 21. Reformed readers see his intercession, and so his saving work, as particular to those given him.

John 17:10

10All things that are mine are yours, and yours are mine, and I am glorified in them.

Arminian

All mine are yours, and yours are mine, and I am glorified in them. Arminian readers see the shared care of Father and Son.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers as the shared possession of Father and Son.

John 17:11

11I am no more in the world, but these are in the world, and I am coming to you. Holy Father, keep them through your name which you have given me, that they may be one, even as we are.

Arminian

Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me. Arminian readers see Jesus asking the Father to keep believers, which the Father does as they remain in him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s prayer as effective, guaranteeing that the Father will keep them.

John 17:12

12While I was with them in the world, I kept them in your name. I have kept those whom you have given me. None of them is lost except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled.

Arminian

While I was with them, I kept them; not one has been lost except the son of destruction. Arminian readers see Judas as one who was truly among those kept and was lost.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Judas as never truly given to Christ in the saving sense, his loss fulfilling Scripture.

Key contrast Both see Judas as the one exception. Arminian readers see someone truly among the kept who was lost, showing that falling away is possible. Reformed readers see someone who was never truly among those given to Christ for salvation.

John 17:13

13But now I come to you, and I say these things in the world, that they may have my joy made full in themselves.

Arminian

I speak these things in the world, that they may have my joy fulfilled in them. Arminian readers see joy as the experience of believers who remain in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see joy as the fruit of union with Christ.

John 17:14

14I have given them your word. The world hated them because they are not of the world, even as I am not of the world.

Arminian

I have given them your word, and the world has hated them. Arminian readers see the cost of following Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the separation between the world and those given to Christ.

John 17:15

15I pray not that you would take them from the world, but that you would keep them from the evil one.

Arminian

I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from the evil one. Arminian readers see protection that believers must also seek.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s keeping power answering Christ’s prayer.

John 17:16

16They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world.

Arminian

They are not of the world, just as I am not of the world. Arminian readers see a new identity in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers belonging to Christ rather than the world.

John 17:17

17Sanctify them in your truth. Your word is truth.

Arminian

Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth. Wesleyan readers see sanctification as a real work of holiness that can reach entire sanctification.

Reformed

Reformed readers see progressive sanctification through Scripture.

John 17:18

18As you sent me into the world, even so I have sent them into the world.

Arminian

As you sent me into the world, so I have sent them. Arminian readers see the church’s mission to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see mission as the means by which Christ gathers his people.

John 17:19

19For their sakes I sanctify myself, that they themselves also may be sanctified in truth.

Arminian

For their sake I consecrate myself, that they also may be sanctified in truth. Arminian readers see Christ’s self-offering making sanctification possible for believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ consecrating himself as sacrifice specifically for those the Father gave him.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Those given out of the world, and on the prayer that is—and is not—for the world: older Arminian and Reformed writers, each in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“I pray not for the world—Not in these petitions, which are adapted to the state of believers only. (He prays for the world at John 17:21, 23, that they may believe—That they may know God hath sent him.) This no more proves that our Lord did not pray for the world, both before and afterward, than his praying for the apostles alone, John 17:6–19, proves that he did not pray for them also which shall believe through their word, John 17:20. They were thine—By creation, and by descent from Abraham.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 17:6–19, especially 17:6, 9)

Reformed Notable voices

“He openly declares that he does not pray for the world, because he has no solicitude but about his own flock, which he received from the hand of the Father. Christ expressly declares that they who are given to him belong to the Father; and it is certain that they are given so as to believe, and that faith flows from this act of giving.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 17:6–19, especially 17:9)
