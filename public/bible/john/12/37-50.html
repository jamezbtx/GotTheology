Passage

John 12

Verses 37–50 · WEB · Voices

Despite all his signs, the people did not believe in Jesus. John explains this by quoting Isaiah: “Lord, who has believed our report?” and “He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart.” They could not believe. Yet many rulers believed secretly, loving human praise more than God’s. Jesus then cries out that whoever believes in him believes in the one who sent him. He came as light so that whoever believes may not remain in darkness. He came not to judge the world but to save it, though his word will judge those who reject it. Verses 39–40 are among the strongest statements of divine hardening in the Gospels, and the two traditions read them differently.

John 12:37

37But though he had done so many signs before them, yet they didn’t believe in him,

Arminian

Though he had done so many signs, they still did not believe. Arminian readers see unbelief as a willful refusal of abundant evidence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that miracles do not produce faith without the Spirit’s work.

John 12:38

38that the word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled, which he spoke: “Lord, who has believed our report? To whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?”

Arminian

So that the word of Isaiah might be fulfilled: “Lord, who has believed what he heard from us?” Arminian readers see Scripture foretelling unbelief that God foreknew, not caused.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s word fulfilled in Israel’s unbelief, within his sovereign plan.

John 12:39

39For this cause they couldn’t believe, for Isaiah said again:

Arminian

Therefore they could not believe, because Isaiah said again. Arminian readers see judicial hardening that follows persistent refusal. They could not believe because they had already refused.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereign hardening, in which he justly withholds grace from those who reject him.

Key contrast Both see a real inability to believe. Arminian readers see it as God’s judicial response to people’s prior, persistent refusal. Reformed readers see God’s sovereign hardening of sinners who are already in rebellion, fulfilling his purpose.

John 12:40

40“He has blinded their eyes and he hardened their heart, lest they should see with their eyes, and perceive with their heart, and would turn, and I would heal them.”

Arminian

“He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart.” Arminian readers see God confirming the choice people have made, as with Pharaoh.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God actively hardening, in judgment, those who are already sinful, which they connect with Romans 9:18.

Key contrast Both see God’s judgment in the hardening. Arminian readers see God confirming people’s chosen rejection. Reformed readers see God’s sovereign, just hardening, connected with Romans 9:18.

John 12:41

41Isaiah said these things when he saw his glory, and spoke of him.

Arminian

Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory and spoke of him. Both traditions see Isaiah’s vision of the Lord (Isaiah 6) applied to Christ, which affirms his deity.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the pre-incarnate Son as the Lord whom Isaiah saw.

John 12:42

42Nevertheless, even many of the rulers believed in him, but because of the Pharisees they didn’t confess it, so that they wouldn’t be put out of the synagogue,

Arminian

Many even of the authorities believed in him, but did not confess it for fear of the Pharisees. Arminian readers see faith that is real but weak, at risk without confession.

Reformed

Reformed readers question whether such faith was genuine, since true faith confesses Christ (Romans 10:9).

John 12:43

43for they loved men’s praise more than God’s praise.

Arminian

They loved the glory that comes from man more than the glory that comes from God. Arminian readers see misplaced love hindering faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the heart’s disordered loves as the root of unbelief.

John 12:44

44Jesus cried out and said, “Whoever believes in me, believes not in me, but in him who sent me.

Arminian

Whoever believes in me believes not in me but in him who sent me. Arminian readers see faith in Christ as faith in the Father.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the unity of Father and Son in the object of faith.

John 12:45

45He who sees me sees him who sent me.

Arminian

Whoever sees me sees him who sent me. Both traditions see Christ revealing the Father. Arminian readers stress that God is truly known in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son as the perfect image of the Father.

John 12:46

46I have come as a light into the world, that whoever believes in me may not remain in the darkness.

Arminian

I have come into the world as light, so that whoever believes in me may not remain in darkness. Arminian readers see “whoever believes” as an open invitation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see light offered to all and received by those God enables.

John 12:47

47If anyone listens to my sayings and doesn’t believe, I don’t judge him. For I came not to judge the world, but to save the world.

Arminian

I did not come to judge the world but to save the world. Arminian readers see God’s saving intention for the whole world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the primary purpose of Christ’s first coming as salvation, with judgment following rejection.

John 12:48

48He who rejects me, and doesn’t receive my sayings, has one who judges him. The word that I spoke will judge him in the last day.

Arminian

The word I have spoken will judge him on the last day. Arminian readers see judgment based on response to the light received.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s word as the standard of final judgment.

John 12:49

49For I spoke not from myself, but the Father who sent me gave me a commandment, what I should say and what I should speak.

Arminian

I have not spoken on my own authority; the Father commanded me. Arminian readers see the Son’s faithful obedience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s obedience within the covenant of redemption.

John 12:50

50I know that his commandment is eternal life. The things therefore which I speak, even as the Father has said to me, so I speak.”

Arminian

His commandment is eternal life. What I say, I say as the Father told me. Arminian readers see the Father’s word offering life to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Father’s word as the means of giving eternal life.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What older interpreters in each camp said about unbelief after signs and Isaiah’s hardening.

Arminian Notable voices

“They could not believe—they were so hardened by their own prejudices, and by the just judgment of God. He hath blinded their eyes—not as though God were the author of their unbelief, but by withdrawing his Spirit after they had long resisted it, and so giving them up to their own hearts.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 12:39–40)

Reformed Notable voices

“They could not believe, because Isaiah had foretold this blinding. God blinds the minds of the reprobate; yet they are justly punished for their unbelief, because the whole blame lies with themselves. Signs had been set before their eyes, and they refused to behold the glory of the Son of God.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 12:37–40)
