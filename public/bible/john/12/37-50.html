Passage

John 12

Verses 37–50 · ESV · Voices

Though Jesus had done so many signs, they still did not believe, that Isaiah’s word might be fulfilled. Therefore they could not believe: God has blinded eyes and hardened hearts. Isaiah said this because he saw His glory. Nevertheless many authorities believed but would not confess, loving human glory. Jesus cries out: believing Him is believing the Sender; He came as light to save, not to judge; the word He has spoken will judge the rejecter; the Father’s commandment is eternal life.

John 12:37

37Though he had done so many signs before them, they still did not believe in him,

Arminian

So many signs, still unbelief. The problem is not thin evidence. Before them names public light.

Arminian tragedy: they could have believed; they still did not. 15:24’s unique works sit here as refused. Culpable seeing-and-not-believing is the first word, before Isaiah is quoted.

Reformed

So many signs / still did not believe. Calvin: signs set before their eyes, glory refused.

Reformed diagnosis: miracle-count does not create faith. Unbelief after abundance is the fact Isaiah will interpret, not erase.

John 12:38

38so that the word spoken by the prophet Isaiah might be fulfilled: “Lord, who has believed what he heard from us, and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?”

Arminian

So that Isaiah 53:1 might be fulfilled: who has believed? to whom revealed? The servant-song already asked this of rejected proclamation.

Arminian fulfillment: Scripture is not a script that forced their no. It names a pattern of refused hearing. The arm was revealed in the signs; the who-has-believed is lament, not a shrug.

Reformed

Might be fulfilled: John’s so that is theological, not a shrug at statistics.

Reformed Scripture: unbelief does not catch God off guard. Isaiah’s question is the church’s, and it is fulfilled in this refusal of the Arm.

John 12:39

39Therefore they could not believe. For again Isaiah said,

Arminian

Therefore they could not. Wesley: so hardened by their own prejudices and by just judgment. Could not sits after would not (v. 37).

Classical Arminians refuse to make could not the first cause, as if they never had light. Judicial inability follows persistent refusal; God giving them up is judgment, not the denial that a sincere call had stood in the signs.

Reformed

Could not believe: Calvin hears Isaiah’s prediction of blinding, and still locates blame with them.

Reformed therefore: the cannot is real, predicted, and just. It does not make the signs a feint; it says unbelief has reached a God-given inability.

Key contrast Shared: they could not believe, as Isaiah said, after they did not believe. Arminians typically take the cannot as judicial hardening after long resistance of light. Reformed readers typically take it as God’s sovereign, just blinding of the reprobate, with the whole blame still theirs. Neither side should flatten the verse into ‘God blinds innocents’ or ‘Isaiah does not mean God.’

John 12:40

40“He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart, lest they see with their eyes, and understand with their heart, and turn, and I would heal them.”

Arminian

He has blinded… hardened… lest they see, understand, turn, and I would heal. The He is God (Isa 6); the lest is terrible. Wesley: not as though God were the author of their unbelief, but by withdrawing His Spirit after long resistance.

Arminian reading: healing was the would of a God who heals turners. Hardening is God handing people to the dark they loved (3:19), withdrawing striving. The verse is judgment on refuse-light, not a claim that turning was never wanted.

Reformed

He has blinded and hardened: God is the subject in Isaiah’s throne-room commission. Lest they turn and be healed is the awful purpose clause of judicial hardening.

Reformed reading: this is more than permission. God blinds, and they are guilty. The signs remain true; the hearts are judicially shut so that the glory Isaiah saw (v. 41) is refused in the very moment it stands among them.

Key contrast Do not slogan. Shared: God is involved in this blinding; they do not see, understand, or turn; healing is what turning would meet. Arminians typically describe withdrawal of Spirit after resistance. Reformed readers typically describe God’s active, just hardening of those who will not believe—without calling the signs insincere.

John 12:41

41Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory and spoke of him.

Arminian

Isaiah saw His glory—John reads Isaiah 6 as the glory of Jesus. The hard text is christological, not a detour from the Son.

Arminian worship: the Jesus they will not believe is the glory Isaiah saw. Unbelief is refusal of that glory, not a small mistake about a rabbi.

Reformed

Saw his glory and spoke of him: the Son is the LORD of Isaiah’s vision.

Reformed Christology: hardening and glory belong to the same Him. Rejecting Jesus is rejecting the glory the prophet saw.

John 12:42

42Nevertheless, many even of the authorities believed in him, but for fear of the Pharisees they did not confess it, so that they would not be put out of the synagogue;

Arminian

Nevertheless: the could not of v. 39 is not a simple zero. Many authorities believed, and would not confess, fearing synagogue expulsion.

Arminian complexity: belief without confession is a real, failed response. Hardening is not the only story in the city; cowardice is another. The nevertheless keeps human agency on the page beside Isaiah.

Reformed

Nevertheless many believed… but did not confess. Fear of people, loss of synagogue.

Reformed nevertheless: God’s blinding (vv. 39–40) does not mean no one believed. It means the public, stubborn unbelief of the many is interpreted by Isaiah; secret, unconfessing faith is another sickness (v. 43).

John 12:43

43for they loved the glory that comes from man more than the glory that comes from God.

Arminian

The because of silence: they loved human glory more than God’s. Isaiah saw His glory (v. 41); they preferred a different glory.

Arminian moral: this is a love-problem, like 3:19. They could have confessed; they loved the wrong honor. The call to come to the light still addresses such love.

Reformed

Loved man’s glory more than God’s. The authorities’ belief is diagnosed as mis-love.

Reformed exposure: unconfessing faith is glory-management. The glory Isaiah saw is outranked by the synagogue’s opinion.

John 12:44

44And Jesus cried out and said, “Whoever believes in me, believes not in me but in him who sent me.

Arminian

Cried out: public, after the narrator’s verdict. Whoever believes in me believes in the Sender.

Arminian whoever: the cry is still invitation. Hardening (vv. 39–40) has not canceled the preached whoever. Believing Jesus is believing God.

Reformed

Cried out: the public word continues. Believing Him is believing the Sender.

Reformed unity: there is no faith in the Father that bypasses the Son. The whoever is the same gospel, now shouted into a hardened city.

John 12:45

45And whoever sees me sees him who sent me.

Arminian

Seeing Jesus is seeing the Sender—14:9 already in public. The signs they would not believe were the Father on display.

Arminian seeing: the invitation is to look at this Jesus as God-with-us, not a lesser messenger.

Reformed

Whoever sees me sees the Sender.

Reformed identity: unbelief in the Son is unbelief in the Father of Isaiah 6. Sight of Jesus is sight of God.

John 12:46

46I have come into the world as light, so that whoever believes in me may not remain in darkness.

Arminian

Came as light, purpose: whoever believes may not remain in darkness. Remaining is the danger; believing is the exit.

Arminian so that: He came for this. Darkness is leavable by faith in Him. The whoever is as wide as the world He entered.

Reformed

Light into the world, so that believers not remain in dark.

Reformed purpose: remaining in darkness is the default He interrupts. The so that is saving intent located in believing union with the Light.

John 12:47

47If anyone hears my words and does not keep them, I do not judge him; for I did not come to judge the world but to save the world.

Arminian

Hearing without keeping: He does not judge that person now. Mission: not to judge the world but to save it—echo of 3:17.

Arminian world: save the world is still His errand after vv. 37–40. Present non-judging is mercy’s hour; v. 48 will not make the words toothless.

Reformed

I did not come to judge the world but to save the world. Present mission is salvation.

Reformed hour: judgment is not the incarnation’s first errand. Saving the world and Isaiah’s hardening coexist in John without canceling either.

John 12:48

48The one who rejects me and does not receive my words has a judge; the word that I have spoken will judge him on the last day.

Arminian

Rejecting Him / not receiving His words: a judge waits—the spoken word, last day. Deferred judgment is not canceled judgment.

Arminian accountability: the same words that could save will judge. Hearing and not keeping (v. 47) is not a safe middle. The last day takes the sermon seriously.

Reformed

The word that I have spoken will judge him. The rejecter already has a judge on file.

Reformed last day: Christ’s speech is eschatological now. Unbelief does not outrun the sentence it heard.

John 12:49

49For I have not spoken on my own authority, but the Father who sent me has himself given me a commandment—what to say and what to speak.

Arminian

Not His own authority: the Father commanded the speech. Rejecting the words is rejecting the Father’s commandment.

Arminian weight: this is why the whoever of v. 44 is not a private Jesus-cult. The commandment behind the cry is the Father’s.

Reformed

The Father has himself given me a commandment—what to say and speak.

Reformed origin: every public word in this chapter is mandated speech. Hardening against it is hardening against the Sender’s order.

John 12:50

50And I know that his commandment is eternal life. What I say, therefore, I say as the Father has told me.”

Arminian

The commandment is eternal life. Therefore He says what the Father told Him. The public ministry closes on life, not on a sneer.

Arminian close: even after could not believe, the last word from His mouth in this discourse is eternal life as the Father’s command. The offer remains as wide as the commandment is life.

Reformed

His commandment is eternal life. Therefore: the Son’s speech is that life, as the Father told Him.

Reformed close: Isaiah’s glory, the world’s unbelief, and the cry of light end in this: what He says is the Father’s life-command. The Judge of v. 48 is also this Life.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on unbelief after signs and Isaiah’s hardening—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside John 12:37–50.

Arminian Notable voices

“They could not believe—they were so hardened by their own prejudices, and by the just judgment of God. He hath blinded their eyes—not as though God were the author of their unbelief, but by withdrawing his Spirit after they had long resisted it, and so giving them up to their own hearts.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 12:39–40)

Reformed Notable voices

“They could not believe, because Isaiah had foretold this blinding. God blinds the minds of the reprobate; yet they are justly punished for their unbelief, because the whole blame lies with themselves. Signs had been set before their eyes, and they refused to behold the glory of the Son of God.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 12:37–40)
