Passage

John 12

Verses 20–36 · WEB · Voices

Some Greeks ask to see Jesus, and he announces that the hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified. A grain of wheat must die to bear much fruit, and whoever loves his life loses it. Jesus’ soul is troubled, but he will not ask to be saved from this hour; he came for it. A voice from heaven answers his prayer to glorify the Father’s name. Now is the judgment of this world and the casting out of its ruler, and when he is lifted up, Jesus will draw all people to himself. He urges the crowd to believe in the light while they have it. Verse 32 is a key text in the debate over the Father’s drawing in 6:44.

John 12:20

20Now there were certain Greeks among those who went up to worship at the feast.

Arminian

Some Greeks were among those who went up to worship. Arminian readers see Gentiles seeking Jesus as a sign that the gospel is for all nations.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the arrival of the Greeks as the sign that Christ’s hour, when he will gather the nations, has come.

John 12:21

21Therefore, these came to Philip, who was from Bethsaida of Galilee, and asked him, saying, “Sir, we want to see Jesus.”

Arminian

“Sir, we wish to see Jesus.” Arminian readers see genuine seeking, which grace has awakened.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Gentiles drawn to Christ, the beginning of the gathering of the other sheep (10:16).

John 12:22

22Philip came and told Andrew, and in turn, Andrew came with Philip, and they told Jesus.

Arminian

Philip told Andrew, and they told Jesus. Arminian readers see the disciples bringing seekers to Jesus.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the role of human messengers in bringing people to Christ.

John 12:23

23Jesus answered them, “The time has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.

Arminian

The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified. Arminian readers see the cross as the moment that opens salvation to the world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the hour appointed by the Father now arriving.

John 12:24

24Most certainly I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains by itself alone. But if it dies, it bears much fruit.

Arminian

Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit. Arminian readers see Christ’s death producing a great harvest available to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s death certainly producing fruit, the people he came to save.

John 12:25

25He who loves his life will lose it. He who hates his life in this world will keep it to eternal life.

Arminian

Whoever loves his life loses it, and whoever hates his life in this world will keep it for eternal life. Arminian readers see a real choice set before every disciple.

Reformed

Reformed readers see self-denial as the mark of those who belong to Christ.

John 12:26

26If anyone serves me, let him follow me. Where I am, there my servant will also be. If anyone serves me, the Father will honor him.

Arminian

If anyone serves me, he must follow me, and where I am, there will my servant be also. Arminian readers see the call to follow as ongoing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise that Christ’s servants will be with him.

John 12:27

27“Now my soul is troubled. What shall I say? ‘Father, save me from this time’? But I came to this time for this cause.

Arminian

Now is my soul troubled. Shall I say, Father, save me from this hour? No, for this purpose I have come. Arminian readers see Christ’s real human anguish and free submission.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s true humanity and his obedience to the Father’s eternal plan.

John 12:28

28Father, glorify your name!” Then a voice came out of the sky, saying, “I have both glorified it and will glorify it again.”

Arminian

“Father, glorify your name.” A voice came: “I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.” Arminian readers see the Father affirming the Son’s mission.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s glory as the final purpose of redemption.

John 12:29

29Therefore the multitude who stood by and heard it said that it had thundered. Others said, “An angel has spoken to him.”

Arminian

The crowd said it had thundered; others said an angel had spoken. Arminian readers see varied responses to the same revelation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that people need spiritual sight to perceive God’s voice.

John 12:30

30Jesus answered, “This voice hasn’t come for my sake, but for your sakes.

Arminian

This voice has come for your sake, not mine. Arminian readers see God giving evidence to help people believe.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s testimony given for the sake of his people.

John 12:31

31Now is the judgment of this world. Now the prince of this world will be cast out.

Arminian

Now is the judgment of this world; now will the ruler of this world be cast out. Arminian readers see the cross defeating Satan for the whole world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the cross as the decisive victory over Satan, freeing Christ’s people from his power.

John 12:32

32And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself.”

Arminian

And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself. Arminian readers see this as the key to 6:44. The Father’s drawing reaches all people through the cross, enabling everyone to respond.

They see “all people” as every person without exception.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the context of the Greeks (v20) and read “all people” as all kinds of people, Gentiles as well as Jews. They see the drawing as effectual, as in 6:44.

They hold that this is consistent with effectual grace for the elect from every nation.

Key contrast Both see the cross drawing people to Christ. Arminian readers see “all people” as everyone without exception, with a drawing that can be resisted. Reformed readers see it as all kinds of people, prompted by the Greeks’ arrival, and the drawing as effectual for those drawn.

John 12:33

33But he said this, signifying by what kind of death he should die.

Arminian

He said this to show by what kind of death he was going to die. Arminian readers see the lifting up as the cross.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s death foreknown and appointed.

John 12:34

34The multitude answered him, “We have heard out of the law that the Christ remains forever. How do you say, ‘The Son of Man must be lifted up’? Who is this Son of Man?”

Arminian

“We have heard from the Law that the Christ remains forever.” Arminian readers see the crowd’s confusion about a suffering Messiah.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the inability of natural understanding to grasp the cross.

John 12:35

35Jesus therefore said to them, “Yet a little while the light is with you. Walk while you have the light, that darkness doesn’t overtake you. He who walks in the darkness doesn’t know where he is going.

Arminian

The light is among you for a little while longer. Walk while you have the light. Arminian readers see an urgent call to respond before the opportunity passes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the seriousness of the present moment of grace.

John 12:36

36While you have the light, believe in the light, that you may become children of light.” Jesus said these things, and he departed and hid himself from them.

Arminian

While you have the light, believe in the light, that you may become sons of light. Arminian readers see a genuine invitation to every hearer to believe and become God’s children.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the command to believe as sincere, addressed to all, and used by God to call his people.

Voices from both camps

Classic comment on the grain of wheat and the lifted Son who draws, from both sides of the debate.

Arminian Notable voices

“Lifted up from the earth—This is a Hebraism which signifies dying. Death in general is all that is usually imported.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 12:32)

Reformed Notable voices

“Christ, being lifted up on the cross, shall gather all men to himself, in order that he may raise them from earth to heaven. The cross will be, as it were, a chariot, by which he shall raise all men, along with himself, to his Father.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 12:32)
