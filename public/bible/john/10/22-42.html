Passage

John 10

Verses 22–42 · WEB

At the Feast of Dedication, the Jews press Jesus to say plainly whether he is the Christ. He replies that he has told them and that his works bear witness, but they do not believe because they are not among his sheep. His sheep hear his voice and follow him. He gives them eternal life, they will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of his hand or the Father’s. He and the Father are one. When they try to stone him for blasphemy, he appeals to Scripture and his works, then withdraws beyond the Jordan, where many believe. Verses 26–29 are a key text in the debate over election and eternal security.

John 10:22

22It was the Feast of the Dedication at Jerusalem.

Arminian

It was the Feast of Dedication in Jerusalem. Arminian readers note the setting, recalling God’s deliverance in the time of the Maccabees.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus as the true temple, revealed at a feast celebrating the temple’s rededication.

John 10:23

23It was winter, and Jesus was walking in the temple, in Solomon’s porch.

Arminian

It was winter, and Jesus was walking in Solomon’s colonnade. Arminian readers see John’s eyewitness detail.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the historical setting of a decisive confrontation.

John 10:24

24The Jews therefore came around him and said to him, “How long will you hold us in suspense? If you are the Christ, tell us plainly.”

Arminian

“If you are the Christ, tell us plainly.” Arminian readers see a demand that reflects resistance more than openness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see unbelief that would not accept any answer.

John 10:25

25Jesus answered them, “I told you, and you don’t believe. The works that I do in my Father’s name, these testify about me.

Arminian

I told you, and you do not believe. The works I do bear witness. Arminian readers see sufficient evidence refused.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that evidence alone does not produce faith.

John 10:26

26But you don’t believe, because you are not of my sheep, as I told you.

Arminian

You do not believe because you are not among my sheep. Arminian readers see “not my sheep” as describing their character and settled unbelief, not a decree that made belief impossible.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the order of the verse: not being a sheep is the reason for unbelief, not the result. Being a sheep comes first.

Key contrast Both see unbelief connected to not being Christ’s sheep. Arminian readers see “not my sheep” as describing persistent unbelief. Reformed readers see belonging to the sheep as prior to and the cause of believing.

John 10:27

27My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me.

Arminian

My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me. Arminian readers stress that the sheep are those who keep hearing and following, so the description is ongoing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the marks of those who belong to Christ, which flow from his knowledge of them.

John 10:28

28I give eternal life to them. They will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of my hand.

Arminian

I give them eternal life, and they will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of my hand. Arminian readers affirm that no outside power can snatch believers from Christ. They hold that this does not stop a believer from walking away by ceasing to follow (v27).

Reformed

Reformed readers see an absolute promise of eternal security. Those who receive eternal life will never perish, and no one, including the believer, can remove them.

Key contrast Both affirm that no one can snatch Christ’s sheep from his hand. Arminian readers see protection from outside forces for those who keep following. Reformed readers see an unconditional guarantee that Christ’s sheep will never perish.

John 10:29

29My Father who has given them to me is greater than all. No one is able to snatch them out of my Father’s hand.

Arminian

My Father, who has given them to me, is greater than all, and no one is able to snatch them out of the Father’s hand. Arminian readers see the Father’s power protecting those who remain his sheep.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the double grip of Father and Son, which makes the believer’s security certain.

John 10:30

30I and the Father are one.”

Arminian

I and the Father are one. Both traditions see a profound claim to unity with the Father. Arminian readers see that unity guaranteeing the Father’s and Son’s shared care for believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a statement of the Son’s deity, the ground of the believer’s security.

John 10:31

31Therefore the Jews took up stones again to stone him.

Arminian

The Jews picked up stones again to stone him. Arminian readers see violent rejection of Jesus’ claim.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that they understood him to claim deity.

John 10:32

32Jesus answered them, “I have shown you many good works from my Father. For which of those works do you stone me?”

Arminian

For which good work are you going to stone me? Arminian readers see Jesus appealing to the evidence of his works.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus exposing the irrationality of unbelief.

John 10:33

33The Jews answered him, “We don’t stone you for a good work, but for blasphemy, because you, being a man, make yourself God.”

Arminian

“You, being a man, make yourself God.” Both traditions see Jesus’ opponents recognizing his claim to deity. Arminian readers note that they understood him clearly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the charge as confirming that Jesus claimed equality with God.

John 10:34

34Jesus answered them, “Isn’t it written in your law, ‘I said, you are gods’?

Arminian

“Is it not written in your Law, ‘I said, you are gods’?” Arminian readers see Jesus arguing from Psalm 82.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an argument from lesser to greater, not a denial of his unique deity.

John 10:35

35If he called them gods, to whom the word of God came (and the Scripture can’t be broken),

Arminian

If he called them gods to whom the word of God came, and Scripture cannot be broken. Both traditions see “Scripture cannot be broken” as a strong statement of Scripture’s authority.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the verse as a key support for the infallibility of Scripture.

John 10:36

36do you say of him whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world, ‘You blaspheme,’ because I said, ‘I am the Son of God’?

Arminian

Do you say of him whom the Father consecrated and sent, “You are blaspheming”? Arminian readers see Christ’s unique sending.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son consecrated for his mission.

John 10:37

37If I don’t do the works of my Father, don’t believe me.

Arminian

If I am not doing the works of my Father, do not believe me. Arminian readers see Jesus inviting examination of the evidence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the works as testimony to the Son’s divine mission.

John 10:38

38But if I do them, though you don’t believe me, believe the works, that you may know and believe that the Father is in me, and I in the Father.”

Arminian

Believe the works, that you may know that the Father is in me and I am in the Father. Arminian readers see Jesus urging faith even from those resisting him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the works pointing to the mutual indwelling of Father and Son.

John 10:39

39They sought again to seize him, and he went out of their hand.

Arminian

They sought to arrest him, but he escaped. Arminian readers see that his hour had not yet come.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereign timing protecting Jesus until his appointed hour.

John 10:40

40He went away again beyond the Jordan into the place where John was baptizing at first, and he stayed there.

Arminian

He went away again across the Jordan. Arminian readers see Jesus withdrawing from hostility.

Reformed

Reformed readers see providence guiding his movements.

John 10:41

41Many came to him. They said, “John indeed did no sign, but everything that John said about this man is true.”

Arminian

Many came to him and said, “Everything John said about this man was true.” Arminian readers see John’s witness bearing fruit.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God using the testimony of his servants to bring people to faith.

John 10:42

42Many believed in him there.

Arminian

Many believed in him there. Arminian readers see people responding in faith when the evidence is weighed honestly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God gathering his sheep, in contrast with the unbelief in Jerusalem.
