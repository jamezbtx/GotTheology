Passage

John 10

Verses 1–21 · WEB

Jesus describes himself as the door of the sheep and the good shepherd. Thieves climb in another way, but the shepherd enters by the door, calls his own sheep by name, and leads them out, and they follow because they know his voice. Jesus is the door: whoever enters through him will be saved. He came so that the sheep may have abundant life. As the good shepherd, he lays down his life for the sheep, knows his own and is known by them, and has other sheep not of this fold whom he must bring. He lays down his life of his own accord and has authority to take it up again. The passage is central to the debate over the extent of the atonement.

John 10:1

1“Most certainly, I tell you, one who doesn’t enter by the door into the sheep fold, but climbs up some other way, is a thief and a robber.

Arminian

Whoever does not enter the sheepfold by the door is a thief and a robber. Arminian readers see a warning against false leaders who exploit God’s people.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the contrast between false shepherds and Christ, the true shepherd.

John 10:2

2But one who enters in by the door is the shepherd of the sheep.

Arminian

The one who enters by the door is the shepherd of the sheep. Arminian readers see Christ’s rightful authority over God’s flock.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as the appointed shepherd of God’s people.

John 10:3

3The gatekeeper opens the gate for him, and the sheep listen to his voice. He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.

Arminian

The sheep hear his voice, and he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out. Arminian readers see a personal call to which sheep respond by listening.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an effectual call to particular sheep, known by name.

John 10:4

4Whenever he brings out his own sheep, he goes before them; and the sheep follow him, for they know his voice.

Arminian

He goes before them, and the sheep follow him, for they know his voice. Arminian readers see following as the ongoing response of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the sheep’s recognition of his voice as the fruit of belonging to him.

John 10:5

5They will by no means follow a stranger, but will flee from him; for they don’t know the voice of strangers.”

Arminian

A stranger they will not follow. Arminian readers see discernment that grows through relationship with Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the sheep kept from following false shepherds.

John 10:6

6Jesus spoke this parable to them, but they didn’t understand what he was telling them.

Arminian

They did not understand what he was saying. Arminian readers see blindness that resists the truth.

Reformed

Reformed readers see spiritual blindness that only God can remove.

John 10:7

7Jesus therefore said to them again, “Most certainly, I tell you, I am the sheep’s door.

Arminian

I am the door of the sheep. Arminian readers see Christ as the one way of access to God, open to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as the exclusive way of salvation.

John 10:8

8All who came before me are thieves and robbers, but the sheep didn’t listen to them.

Arminian

All who came before me are thieves and robbers, but the sheep did not listen to them. Arminian readers see false messiahs and leaders contrasted with Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the sheep kept from deceivers by God’s grace.

John 10:9

9I am the door. If anyone enters in by me, he will be saved, and will go in and go out and will find pasture.

Arminian

If anyone enters by me, he will be saved. Arminian readers stress “anyone”: the door is open to every person who will enter.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a universal invitation that the sheep answer when called.

John 10:10

10The thief only comes to steal, kill, and destroy. I came that they may have life, and may have it abundantly.

Arminian

The thief comes to steal and kill; I came that they may have life abundantly. Arminian readers, especially Wesleyans, see abundant life as present fullness of grace and holiness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see abundant life as the full blessing of salvation in Christ.

John 10:11

11“I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.

Arminian

I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep. Arminian readers note that saying Christ died for the sheep does not mean he died only for them. Elsewhere John says he is the propitiation for the whole world (1 John 2:2).

Reformed

Reformed readers see a definite intent in Christ’s death. He laid down his life for his sheep, which contrasts with those who are “not my sheep” (10:26).

Key contrast Both affirm that the good shepherd dies for the sheep. Arminian readers note that this does not limit his death to the sheep, citing 1 John 2:2. Reformed readers see in it a particular intention to save his sheep, in light of 10:26.

John 10:12

12He who is a hired hand, and not a shepherd, who doesn’t own the sheep, sees the wolf coming, leaves the sheep, and flees. The wolf snatches the sheep and scatters them.

Arminian

The hired hand sees the wolf and flees. Arminian readers see the contrast between Christ’s love and self-interested leaders.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s commitment to his sheep, which never fails.

John 10:13

13The hired hand flees because he is a hired hand and doesn’t care for the sheep.

Arminian

The hired hand flees because he does not care for the sheep. Arminian readers see Christ’s genuine care.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the sheep as Christ’s own possession.

John 10:14

14I am the good shepherd. I know my own, and I’m known by my own;

Arminian

I know my own, and my own know me. Arminian readers see mutual knowledge as a living relationship of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s knowledge of his own as electing love.

John 10:15

15even as the Father knows me, and I know the Father. I lay down my life for the sheep.

Arminian

As the Father knows me and I know the Father; and I lay down my life for the sheep. Arminian readers see the depth of intimacy offered to believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the shepherd’s death tied to his knowledge of particular sheep.

John 10:16

16I have other sheep which are not of this fold. I must bring them also, and they will hear my voice. They will become one flock with one shepherd.

Arminian

I have other sheep not of this fold; I must bring them also, and there will be one flock. Arminian readers see the gospel going to the Gentiles, open to all who will hear.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ already having sheep among the Gentiles before they believe. He must bring them, and they will listen.

Key contrast Both see Gentiles being brought into the one flock. Arminian readers see the gospel extended to all nations. Reformed readers see sheep already belonging to Christ among the nations, whom he will certainly bring in.

John 10:17

17Therefore the Father loves me, because I lay down my life, that I may take it again.

Arminian

The Father loves me because I lay down my life that I may take it up again. Arminian readers see the Father’s love for the Son’s self-giving.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the covenant between Father and Son, fulfilled in Christ’s death and resurrection.

John 10:18

18No one takes it away from me, but I lay it down by myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again. I received this commandment from my Father.”

Arminian

No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord. Arminian readers see Christ’s voluntary sacrifice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s sovereign control over his death, which fulfilled the Father’s charge.

John 10:19

19Therefore a division arose again among the Jews because of these words.

Arminian

There was a division again among the Jews. Arminian readers see people responding differently to the same words.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s words dividing those who belong to him from those who do not.

John 10:20

20Many of them said, “He has a demon and is insane! Why do you listen to him?”

Arminian

“He has a demon and is insane.” Arminian readers see hostile unbelief.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the natural mind’s rejection of spiritual truth.

John 10:21

21Others said, “These are not the sayings of one possessed by a demon. It isn’t possible for a demon to open the eyes of the blind, is it?”

Arminian

“These are not the words of one who is oppressed by a demon.” Arminian readers see some open to the evidence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the beginning of faith in some, worked by God.
