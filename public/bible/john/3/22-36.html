Passage

John 3

Verses 22–36 · WEB · Voices

Jesus baptizes in the Judean countryside while John is still at Aenon. John’s disciples fret that all are going to him. John answers as the friend of the bridegroom: no one receives anything unless it is given from heaven; he is not the Christ; his joy is complete as Jesus increases. The One from above is above all, speaks God’s words, and has the Spirit without measure. Whoever believes in the Son has life, but one who disobeys the Son won’t see life—the wrath of God remains.

John 3:22

22After these things, Jesus came with his disciples into the land of Judea. He stayed there with them and baptized.

Arminian

The night conversation becomes countryside ministry. Jesus stays and baptizes—the kingdom’s door is now a public practice, not only a riddle for Nicodemus.

Remaining with disciples and baptizing is how new-birth news takes up space in Judea. Grace that must beget also meets people in water and company.

Reformed

After teaching Nicodemus, Jesus baptizes in the land. Word and sign travel together.

The Christ who spoke of Spirit-birth now marks a people in Judea. The scene change is still the same Lord gathering those the Father gives.

John 3:23

23John also was baptizing in Enon near Salim, because there was much water there. They came and were baptized;

Arminian

Two baptizing ministries overlap without yet colliding. Plenty of water, people coming—the forerunner has not shut down because the Christ has arrived.

God can use overlapping callings for a season. Coming and being baptized remains a response to preached repentance.

Reformed

Aenon’s plentiful water explains the crowd logistically and theologically: God had appointed John’s work until the prison (v. 24).

People coming to John is still gift, not a rival franchise. Heaven is about to interpret the numbers.

John 3:24

24for John was not yet thrown into prison.

Arminian

A calendar note: this is early, before Herod’s prison. The Baptist’s decrease will include suffering, not only statistics.

Faithfulness can end in a cell. The joy of v. 29 will have to hold when the friend of the Bridegroom is silenced.

Reformed

John’s imprisonment is future and certain in the narrative’s mind. The overlap of ministries is a brief providence.

God times both the forerunner’s voice and its silencing. Nothing here is accidental crowding.

John 3:25

25Therefore a dispute arose on the part of John’s disciples with some Jews about purification.

Arminian

A dispute over purification sets up the jealousy. Ritual questions easily become team questions.

Even baptism-people can turn means of grace into arguments. The coming answer will relocate purity to the Christ from heaven.

Reformed

Purification debate is the fuse. John’s circle is already measuring washings instead of watching the Lamb.

Controversy over rites often masks rivalry. Heaven’s gift (v. 27) will have to interpret the argument.

John 3:26

26They came to John and said to him, “Rabbi, he who was with you beyond the Jordan, to whom you have testified, behold, he baptizes, and everyone is coming to him.”

Arminian

They remember John’s witness and still resent its success. All are going to him is exaggeration born of fear for their rabbi’s share.

Ministry comparison is old. John’s next sentences refuse envy. The right response to people going to Jesus is joy, not market panic.

Reformed

Look: the one you witnessed is gathering the crowd. The disciples treat fruit as theft.

They quote John’s witness against John’s peace. God is answering the witness, and they hear it as a problem to fix.

John 3:27

27John answered, “A man can receive nothing unless it has been given him from heaven.

Arminian

John’s first word is gift: not one thing received unless heaven gave it—neither his crowd nor Jesus’. Wesley applied it to both ministries’ success.

Wesleyan Arminians gladly ascribe every good, including conversions, to grace from heaven. They still hold that people must receive what is given; heaven’s giving does not make envy reasonable or faith automatic.

Reformed

Calvin heard providence: Christ has more disciples because heaven gave them. Receiving is not self-generated inventory.

Ministry numbers are assigned. The Baptist refuses to compete with a God who distributes crowds. What Jesus has, He was given.

Key contrast Both deny that success is self-made. Arminians typically join “given from heaven” to a human receiving that may still refuse the gift; Reformed interpreters usually hear effectual assignment—what each man has, heaven has actually bestowed.

John 3:28

28You yourselves testify that I said, ‘I am not the Christ,’ but, ‘I have been sent before him.’

Arminian

He holds them to their own memory: not the Christ, sent before Him. Identity is gift and limit.

Knowing who you are not is holiness. Grace that sent John also bounded him; staying in that bound is how the friend serves the Bridegroom.

Reformed

Their witness already contained the answer. John is sent before, not instead.

Forerunner is an office God defined. Pretending to be the Christ would be grabbing what heaven did not give (v. 27).

John 3:29

29He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly because of the bridegroom’s voice. Therefore my joy is made full.

Arminian

The bride belongs to the Bridegroom. John’s joy is hearing that voice, not collecting guests for himself. Complete joy is decrease without bitterness.

Love rejoices when people go to Jesus. The friend’s standing and hearing is a picture of ministry that measures success by the Lord’s increase, not by our brand.

Reformed

Bride, Bridegroom, friend: the church is Christ’s, not John’s. Joy is complete when the rightful husband’s voice is heard.

God gives the friend a joy that does not require the starring role. Complete joy is located in Christ’s taking what is His.

John 3:30

30He must increase, but I must decrease.

Arminian

Must: not a mood, a necessity. Jesus’ increase is the point of John’s life.

Decrease is commanded shape, not self-hatred. Grace that made John a voice also teaches the voice to get smaller so the Word is heard.

Reformed

Two musts: Christ’s swelling, John’s shrinking. The grammar is divine necessity.

God has written this ratio. The forerunner’s decrease is as appointed as the Messiah’s increase.

John 3:31

31“He who comes from above is above all. He who is from the earth belongs to the earth and speaks of the earth. He who comes from heaven is above all.

Arminian

The One from heaven is above all, and John says it twice. Earth-speech cannot rival the One from heaven. John’s own ministry is “of the earth” compared with the Descender.

The gap is ontological, not a talent gap. Hearers are summoned to the heavenly Speaker, not to a better earthly rabbi.

Reformed

Origin dictates rank. The heavenly One is above all; earthly voices stay earthly.

This continues 3:13. Revelation’s authority is the Son’s descent, not the Baptist’s volume.

John 3:32

32What he has seen and heard, of that he testifies; and no one receives his witness.

Arminian

Firsthand witness, wholesale non-reception—hyperbole of grief, not a math claim that zero believed (v. 33).

The heavenly testimony can be refused. “No one receives” is the tragedy of resistible light, which is why the next verse’s “witness. He who has received” still matters.

Reformed

Seen and heard, yet unreceived. The pattern matches Nicodemus’s “you do not receive” (v. 11).

Flesh does not take heavenly testimony. Reception, when it happens, will be exception by gift, not the default of the crowd.

John 3:33

33He who has received his witness has set his seal to this, that God is true.

Arminian

Whoever receives—there are some. Receiving the Son’s testimony is signing that God is true.

Classical Arminians place a human seal on a divine truth. Grace enables the receiving; the seal is the believer’s Amen that God does not lie in His Son.

Reformed

Receiving is sealing that God is true. Unbelief calls God a liar; faith notarizes His veracity.

The whoever is as wide as the invitation and as particular as those who actually receive. God’s truth stands; the seal is the creature’s confession of it.

John 3:34

34For he whom God has sent speaks the words of God; for God gives the Spirit without measure.

Arminian

The Sent One speaks God’s words because the Spirit is given to Him without measure—no drip-feed Messiah.

An immeasurable Spirit in Christ means a sufficient gospel for those who hear. The words He utters are God-words, to be received, not sampled.

Reformed

God’s Sent One has the Spirit without rationing. That is why His speech is God’s speech.

The Messiah is not a lesser prophet on a stipend of Spirit. Immeasurable anointing grounds the authority of every word He utters.

John 3:35

35The Father loves the Son, and has given all things into his hand.

Arminian

Love in the Godhead, then a gift: all things in the Son’s hand. Trusting the Son is trusting the Father’s own deposit.

If all things are in His hand, life is there to be asked for. The Father’s love for the Son is the sure place to put a believer’s life.

Reformed

The Father’s love is the fountain of the Son’s universal authority. All things given: including the life of v. 36.

Salvation’s administration is in the beloved Son’s hand, not in our management. What the Father gave, the Son holds.

John 3:36

36One who believes in the Son has eternal life, but one who disobeys the Son won’t see life, but the wrath of God remains on him.”

Arminian

Present tense: believes, has life. The parallel is not-obeying the Son—unbelief as refusal of His rule. Wrath remains; it does not newly appear from a loving God who had been indifferent.

Wesley: the unbeliever remains under the wrath he was under before. Arminians preach urgency without fatalism: believing, one has life; refusing, wrath remains—and the whoever still stands open in the preaching of the Son.

Reformed

Life is had by believing; wrath remains on the disobedient. Calvin insisted both that faith is not in every man’s power and that unbelief is justly condemned, blame with men themselves.

The verse holds both gift and guilt. Eternal life is present possession in the Son; remaining wrath is not canceled by hearing. God gives faith, and God is right to leave wrath on those who will not obey the Son.

Key contrast Both traditions see: believing the Son has life now; not obeying leaves wrath in place. Arminians typically stress a sincere whoever and remaining wrath as the cost of resistible unbelief; Reformed readers emphasize that life-giving faith is heaven’s gift (v. 27) while still locating the blame for unbelief in the sinner, not in a defect of the Son.

Voices from both camps

Gifts from heaven and believing the Son: older Arminian and Reformed writers, each in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“A man can receive nothing—neither he nor I—unless it be given him from heaven. He that believeth on the Son hath everlasting life. He that obeyeth not the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God abideth on him. The unbeliever remains under the wrath he was under before.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 3:27, 36)

Reformed Notable voices

“John ascribes it to the providence of God that Christ has more disciples… A man can receive nothing unless it be given him from heaven. He who believes in the Son has life; he who does not obey the Son shall not see life. Faith is not in every man’s power, and yet unbelief is justly condemned, because the whole blame lies with men themselves.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 3:27, 36)
