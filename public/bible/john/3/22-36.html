Passage

John 3

Verses 22–36 · WEB · Voices

Jesus and his disciples baptize in Judea while John baptizes nearby. When John’s disciples complain that everyone is going to Jesus, John answers that a person can receive only what is given from heaven. He is the friend of the bridegroom and rejoices to hear his voice: he must increase, and I must decrease. The chapter closes by contrasting the one from above with those from the earth. The Father loves the Son and has given all things into his hand. Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life, and whoever does not obey the Son will not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him. Both traditions find verse 36 decisive. They read verse 27 differently.

John 3:22

22After these things, Jesus came with his disciples into the land of Judea. He stayed there with them and baptized.

Arminian

Jesus and his disciples went into Judea and baptized there. Arminian readers note the parallel ministries of John and Jesus.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the transition from John’s preparatory ministry to Jesus’ ministry.

John 3:23

23John also was baptizing in Enon near Salim, because there was much water there. They came and were baptized;

Arminian

John was baptizing at Aenon near Salim, because water was plentiful. Arminian readers see John’s faithful continuance in his calling.

Reformed

Reformed readers see John continuing the work God gave him.

John 3:24

24for John was not yet thrown into prison.

Arminian

John had not yet been put in prison. Arminian readers see John’s eyewitness concern for chronology.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Gospel situating events within the other accounts.

John 3:25

25Therefore a dispute arose on the part of John’s disciples with some Jews about purification.

Arminian

A discussion arose about purification. Arminian readers see questions about rites leading to deeper questions about Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see purification fulfilled in Christ.

John 3:26

26They came to John and said to him, “Rabbi, he who was with you beyond the Jordan, to whom you have testified, behold, he baptizes, and everyone is coming to him.”

Arminian

“Everyone is going to him.” Arminian readers see John’s disciples worried about losing followers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see human jealousy contrasted with John’s humility.

John 3:27

27John answered, “A man can receive nothing unless it has been given him from heaven.

Arminian

A person cannot receive even one thing unless it is given him from heaven. Arminian readers see John accepting his limited role as God’s gift. The verse concerns ministry and calling.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a general principle that applies to salvation as well: everything, including faith, is a gift from heaven.

Key contrast Both see John acknowledging that his role is a gift from God. Arminian readers apply the verse mainly to ministry and calling. Reformed readers see it as stating a broad principle that includes the gift of faith.

John 3:28

28You yourselves testify that I said, ‘I am not the Christ,’ but, ‘I have been sent before him.’

Arminian

I said, I am not the Christ, but I have been sent before him. Arminian readers see John’s faithfulness to his role.

Reformed

Reformed readers see John’s ministry as appointed by God.

John 3:29

29He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly because of the bridegroom’s voice. Therefore my joy is made full.

Arminian

The friend of the bridegroom rejoices greatly at the bridegroom’s voice. Arminian readers see joy in Christ’s success.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as bridegroom of his church, his chosen bride.

John 3:30

30He must increase, but I must decrease.

Arminian

He must increase, but I must decrease. Both traditions treasure this as a model of Christian humility. Arminian readers see a call to self-giving service.

Reformed

Reformed readers see all glory belonging to Christ.

John 3:31

31“He who comes from above is above all. He who is from the earth belongs to the earth and speaks of the earth. He who comes from heaven is above all.

Arminian

He who comes from above is above all. Arminian readers see Christ’s supremacy over every human messenger.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s heavenly origin and authority.

John 3:32

32What he has seen and heard, of that he testifies; and no one receives his witness.

Arminian

He bears witness to what he has seen and heard, yet no one receives his testimony. Arminian readers see general rejection by the world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see natural humanity refusing Christ’s testimony apart from grace.

John 3:33

33He who has received his witness has set his seal to this, that God is true.

Arminian

Whoever receives his testimony sets his seal to this, that God is true. Arminian readers see faith as affirming God’s truthfulness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as confessing God’s truthfulness, a response God enables.

John 3:34

34For he whom God has sent speaks the words of God; for God gives the Spirit without measure.

Arminian

He whom God has sent utters the words of God, for he gives the Spirit without measure. Arminian readers see Christ fully empowered by the Spirit.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son anointed by the Spirit without limit for his mediatorial work.

John 3:35

35The Father loves the Son, and has given all things into his hand.

Arminian

The Father loves the Son and has given all things into his hand. Arminian readers see the Son’s authority over all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Father’s gift of all things to the Son, including his people (6:37).

John 3:36

36One who believes in the Son has eternal life, but one who disobeys the Son won’t see life, but the wrath of God remains on him.”

Arminian

Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him. Arminian readers see belief and unbelief as the decisive human response that determines one’s destiny.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the wrath of God already resting on all outside Christ, with faith as the only way of deliverance.

Key contrast Both affirm that faith in the Son brings eternal life and unbelief leaves people under wrath. Arminian readers stress the human response as decisive. Reformed readers stress that wrath already rests on all by nature, and only faith, God’s gift, delivers from it.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Gifts from heaven and believing the Son: older Arminian and Reformed writers, each in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“A man can receive nothing—neither he nor I—unless it be given him from heaven. He that believeth on the Son hath everlasting life. He that obeyeth not the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God abideth on him. The unbeliever remains under the wrath he was under before.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 3:27, 36)

Reformed Notable voices

“John ascribes it to the providence of God that Christ has more disciples… A man can receive nothing unless it be given him from heaven. He who believes in the Son has life; he who does not obey the Son shall not see life. Faith is not in every man’s power, and yet unbelief is justly condemned, because the whole blame lies with men themselves.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 3:27, 36)
