Passage

John 14

Verses 1–14 · ESV · Voices

Let not your hearts be troubled: believe in God, believe also in me. He goes to prepare a place, and will come again. Thomas asks the way; Jesus is the way, the truth, and the life—no one comes to the Father except through Him. Philip asks to see the Father; to see Jesus is to see the Father. Those who believe will do His works, and whatever they ask in His name He will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.

John 14:1

1“Let not your hearts be troubled. Believe in God; believe also in me.

Arminian

Troubled hearts (13:38 still ringing) are met with a double believe. Faith in the Father and in the Son is the prescribed calm.

Arminian pastoral: the command not to be troubled is not a scold; it is a path. They can believe; grace that has shown them the Son now steadies them in Him.

Reformed

Calvin: faith is the remedy for a troubled heart. Believe in God; believe also in me—one trust with two named objects.

Reformed rest: He does not offer a technique. He offers Himself as the object that stills.

John 14:2

2In my Father’s house are many rooms. If it were not so, would I have told you that I go to prepare a place for you?

Arminian

Many rooms: space in the Father’s house, not a shortage. He goes to prepare.

Arminian welcome: the house is the Father’s and the rooms are many. The going is for them; the question form presses them to trust His speech.

Reformed

Father’s house, many rooms, I go to prepare. The departure is hospitality, not abandonment.

Reformed place: He would not have told them a lie. The preparation is His work in leaving.

John 14:3

3And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and will take you to myself, that where I am you may be also.

Arminian

Go, come again, take you to myself—destination is a person: where I am.

Arminian hope: the coming is as sure as the going. Being with Him is the point of the rooms.

Reformed

I will come again and take you to myself. Reunion is His action.

Reformed future: the prepared place is personal presence. Where I am is the definition of home.

John 14:4

4And you know the way to where I am going.”

Arminian

He says they know the way. They will immediately prove they feel otherwise.

Arminian irony: knowing Jesus is knowing the way, even when they cannot yet diagram it.

Reformed

You know: already true in Him, not yet clear to them.

Reformed givenness: the way is not missing. It is standing in the room.

John 14:5

5Thomas said to him, “Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?”

Arminian

Thomas is honest: no where, so no how. The question is the disciple we need on the page.

Arminian sympathy: asking is not unbelief by itself. Jesus will answer with a person, not a map app.

Reformed

Where and way: Thomas wants coordinates.

Reformed gap: he has heard 13:33–36 and still cannot plot it. The next verse will relocate way from route to Christ.

John 14:6

6Jesus said to him, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.

Arminian

I am—not I show—the way, truth, life. Exclusive: no one comes to the Father except through me. Wesley preached this without embarrassment.

Classical Arminians hold the exclusivity and the wideness together: the only way is sincerely set before the world. Coming is a real act of faith in this Jesus; no rival path, no closed secret for a few who never hear.

Reformed

Calvin: no one comes but by me. Way, truth, life are not three products; they are one Son.

Reformed exclusivity: access to the Father is located in Christ alone. The no one is as wide as humanity and as particular as this Mediator.

Key contrast Shared: Jesus alone is the way to the Father. Arminians typically stress that this unique way is sincerely offered to all who hear, to be taken by faith. Reformed readers typically stress that coming through Him is the only access God has appointed, which He actually grants in the Son.

John 14:7

7If you had known me, you would have known my Father also. From now on you do know him and have seen him.”

Arminian

Knowing Jesus is knowing the Father. From now on: a turn in their knowledge, including seeing.

Arminian knowledge: not a ladder beyond Christ. The from now on is gospel—they may know, because they have seen Him.

Reformed

If you had known me: the Father is not behind Jesus but in Him.

Reformed present: from now on you do know. The seeing of v. 9 is already breaking in.

John 14:8

8Philip said to him, “Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us.”

Arminian

Philip wants a theophany that would be enough. He has been looking at enough and calling it not enough.

Arminian ache: we still ask for a showing beyond the Son. Jesus will not shame the hunger; He will relocate it.

Reformed

Show us the Father: the request sounds pious and misses the Incarnation in the room.

Reformed enough: what Philip calls enough is already given in the face he sees.

John 14:9

9Jesus said to him, “Have I been with you so long, and you still do not know me, Philip? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, ‘Show us the Father’?

Arminian

So long, still not know. Whoever has seen me has seen the Father. Wesley and Calvin both camp here.

Arminian vision: there is no Father-behind-the-curtain who contradicts the Son. Seeing Jesus is the showing Philip asked for.

Reformed

Calvin: the whole fullness dwells in Him. Seen me / seen the Father is not metaphor-only.

Reformed Christology: the request “show the Father” after months with Jesus is the tragedy of unseeing sight.

John 14:10

10Do you not believe that I am in the Father and the Father is in me? The words that I say to you I do not speak on my own authority, but the Father who dwells in me does his works.

Arminian

Mutual indwelling; words not on His own authority; the indwelling Father does the works. Speech and deed are the Father at work in the Son.

Arminian faith-question: do you not believe—belief is the missing piece, and it is called for. The works and words are sufficient ground.

Reformed

I in the Father, Father in me. Words and works are not freelance.

Reformed agency: the Father dwelling does His works. Unbelief here is unbelief in the indwelling God.

John 14:11

11Believe me that I am in the Father and the Father is in me, or else believe on account of the works themselves.

Arminian

Believe me, or else believe the works. Two rungs, same union.

Arminian concession: even works-faith is invited if person-faith is slow. He meets them where they are without making signs the ceiling (cf. 4:48).

Reformed

Believe me / or else the works. He argues for the same truth from two witnesses.

Reformed patience: the works themselves testify. He is not above giving a second handle to a slow faith.

John 14:12

12“Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever believes in me will also do the works that I do; and greater works than these will he do, because I am going to the Father.

Arminian

Whoever believes will do His works, and greater, because He goes. Greater is harvest-wide (Acts), not a boast of better miracles than the incarnation.

Arminian whoever: believing issues in works. The going to the Father (Spirit, 14:16) is why the greater can happen through ordinary believers.

Reformed

Truly, truly: believers as His work-doers, greater because of the going.

Reformed because: the departure is the engine. Pentecostal wideness, not a rivalry with the Son’s unique works.

John 14:13

13Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.

Arminian

Ask in my name, I will do, so that the Father is glorified in the Son. Prayer’s whatever is name-shaped and doxological.

Arminian asking: they are invited to ask. The name binds request to His character; the doing is His, the glory the Father’s.

Reformed

I will do: the Son answers prayer in His name for the Father’s glory.

Reformed aim: that the Father may be glorified in the Son. Prayer is not a vending machine; it is that glory.

John 14:14

14If you ask me anything in my name, I will do it.

Arminian

Ask me—the Son as hearer. Anything in my name, I will do it. The repetition is comfort, not a loophole.

Arminian directness: they may ask Him. Faith prays; He acts. The name remains the fence and the access.

Reformed

If you ask me: Christ the addressee. I will do it: His agency.

Reformed certainty: the promise is as large as the name and as bound to it. Anything is not any whim; it is any request that can live in that name.
