Passage

John 14

Verses 15–31 · ESV · Voices

If they love Him they will keep His commandments. He will ask the Father, who will give another Helper to be with them forever—the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive. He will not leave them as orphans; He and the Father will make their home with the one who loves and keeps. The Helper will teach and remind. Peace He leaves; the ruler of this world is coming and has no claim on Him. He does as the Father commanded so that the world may know He loves the Father: rise, let us go.

John 14:15

15“If you love me, you will keep my commandments.

Arminian

Love shows as keeping. The if is relational, not a wage scale.

Arminian holiness: this is a real linkage. Grace that loves also keeps; claiming love without keeping is a contradiction He will not bless.

Reformed

Calvin: love is the fountain of obedience. Future will keep describes love’s fruit.

Reformed fountain: commandments flow from love He has already been forming, not from a loveless bargain.

Key contrast Shared: love of Jesus keeps His commandments. Arminians typically stress the real condition and enabled keeping. Reformed readers typically stress that keeping is love’s effectual fruit in those who are His.

John 14:16

16And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Helper, to be with you forever,

Arminian

He asks; the Father gives another Helper—forever, not a visit. Another means One like Jesus in advocacy, not a lesser deputy.

Classical Arminians: the Helper is promised to this believing company. Forever-with-you is covenant comfort; the wider world (v. 17) does not receive Him while remaining the world, yet the promise is for those who love and keep, a state people are called into.

Reformed

I will ask / He will give / another Helper / forever. Trinitarian gift to them.

Reformed forever: the Spirit is not a rental. This is Christ’s prayer answered in Pentecost permanence for His own.

Key contrast Shared: the Father gives another Helper, at the Son’s asking, to be with them forever. Arminians typically tie the gift to the loving, commandment-keeping community into which people may enter. Reformed readers typically tie it to Christ’s effectual request for those the Father has given Him.

John 14:17

17even the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees him nor knows him. You know him, for he dwells with you and will be in you.

Arminian

World cannot receive: not seeing, not knowing. You know: with you, will be in you. Two communities, one Spirit of truth.

Arminian tension: cannot receive is the world’s present blindness (3:3–8), not a ban on anyone ever coming. People leave “world” by new birth; the Spirit who will be in the Eleven also convicts the world (16:8).

Reformed

Cannot receive / neither sees nor knows. You know, because He dwells with you and will be in you.

Reformed indwelling: the world’s inability is real. The church’s knowing is already begun (with you) and about to be intensified (in you).

John 14:18

18“I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you.

Arminian

Not orphans; I will come. Coming includes resurrection appearances and the Spirit’s making-Him-present.

Arminian comfort: abandonment is refused. The going of 13:33 is not orphaning.

Reformed

Orphans refused. I will come to you: His own future tense.

Reformed presence: the leaving is not the last structure of the relationship. He comes.

John 14:19

19Yet a little while and the world will see me no longer, but you will see me. Because I live, you also will live.

Arminian

World-not-seeing / you-seeing. Because I live, you will live—His life the cause of theirs.

Arminian life: resurrection life is shared. The because is gospel; they live from His living, and are called to trust that.

Reformed

Because I live, you also will live. Union in life, not imitation only.

Reformed causality: their life is downstream of His. The world’s seeing ends; theirs does not.

John 14:20

20In that day you will know that I am in my Father, and you in me, and I in you.

Arminian

That day: knowledge of a triple indwelling—Son in Father, they in Him, He in them.

Arminian knowing: this is the Spirit-day’s lesson. Union is to be known, not only asserted.

Reformed

You will know: the day teaches coinherence.

Reformed web: Father-Son and Christ-church indwelling are revealed together. That day makes doctrine experiential.

John 14:21

21Whoever has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me. And he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and manifest myself to him.”

Arminian

Whoever: keeping identifies the lover; then Father-love, Jesus-love, manifestation. Wesley would not split love from keeping.

Arminian whoever: this path is described so people may walk it. Manifestation is promised to the loving keeper, a real conditioned experience of the risen Christ.

Reformed

Has and keeps: love’s evidence. Then the Father’s love, the Son’s love, and manifestation.

Reformed chain: manifestation is not a random mystic extra. It is pledged to the one whose love-keeping marks him as His.

Key contrast Shared: keeping commandments marks love, and the lover is loved and given a manifestation of Jesus. Arminians typically hear a living condition. Reformed readers typically hear the described life of those Christ actually loves and indwells.

John 14:22

22Judas (not Iscariot) said to him, “Lord, how is it that you will manifest yourself to us, and not to the world?”

Arminian

The other Judas asks the election-shaped question: why us, not the world? It is a fair question after 3:16 and 4:42.

Arminian hearing: the question keeps the world in view. Jesus’ answer (v. 23) will speak of home-making with the lover, not of a world God never sought.

Reformed

How… to us and not to the world: the manifestation is particular.

Reformed question: the Eleven are about to hear that love-and-keeping is the site of dwelling, which the world as world does not occupy.

John 14:23

23Jesus answered him, “If anyone loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.

Arminian

If anyone: not a closed caste slogan. Love, keep, Father loves, we come, we make home. The Trinity takes up residence.

Classical Arminians: anyone is the wideness inside the condition. The home is truly offered along the path of loving and keeping, which grace enables.

Reformed

If anyone loves: the home is with that person. We will come—Father and Son.

Reformed dwelling: this is the answer to Judas’s how. Manifestation-not-to-the-world is home-making with the lover, a divine we.

John 14:24

24Whoever does not love me does not keep my words. And the word that you hear is not mine but the Father’s who sent me.

Arminian

Not-loving = not-keeping. The word they hear is the Father’s.

Arminian negative: refusal is locatable. One cannot claim the Father while binning the Son’s words.

Reformed

The heard word is the Sender’s. Not keeping is not loving.

Reformed authority: discarding Jesus’ words is discarding the Father. There is no higher appeal.

John 14:25

25“These things I have spoken to you while I am still with you.

Arminian

Spoken while still with them: the earthly syllabus has a clock.

Arminian now: they must hold what He said in the flesh, because the Helper will remind, not replace, these things.

Reformed

While I am still with you: the with-you season of speech.

Reformed limit of mode: the next verse is the other mode of the same teaching.

John 14:26

26But the Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you.

Arminian

Helper = Holy Spirit, Father-sent in the Son’s name, teaches all things, reminds of all Jesus said. Memory and teaching are His work.

Arminian Bible-and-Spirit: He does not erase the spoken words; He reminds. The church still attends to what Jesus said, now tutored from inside.

Reformed

Father will send in my name. Teach and remind: two verbs, one canon of Jesus’ speech.

Reformed guarantee of apostolic memory: the Spirit is why they will not lose what He said. All things is all they need of His teaching, not a rival revelation.

John 14:27

27Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you. Not as the world gives do I give to you. Let not your hearts be troubled, neither let them be afraid.

Arminian

Peace left and given, His, not as the world gives. The chapter’s opening command returns with fear named too.

Arminian gift: peace is given, then hearts are told not to be troubled. Receiving His peace is how the command becomes livable.

Reformed

Calvin: I leave you my peace. World-peace is a different species.

Reformed bequest: this is a last-will verb, peace as His own. Troubled/afraid hearts are addressed by a gift, not a slogan.

John 14:28

28You heard me say to you, ‘I am going away, and I will come to you.’ If you loved me, you would have rejoiced, because I am going to the Father, for the Father is greater than I.

Arminian

If you loved me, you would have rejoiced at His going to the greater Father. Greater here is the economy of mission (the sent Son returning), not a denial of 1:1.

Arminian love: their grief is understandable and still too small. Loving Him includes wanting His glory with the Father.

Reformed

Father is greater than I: the sent one’s return to the Sender. Rejoicing would be love’s fit.

Reformed extra-Calvinisticum not required here: functional greatness of the Father as sender, while 14:9 still stands. They should rejoice at His exaltation.

John 14:29

29And now I have told you before it takes place, so that when it does take place you may believe.

Arminian

Told beforehand so that when it happens they may believe. Prediction is a faith-means.

Arminian so that: He wants them to believe. The coming events are interpreted in advance as grounds for trust.

Reformed

Before it takes place / you may believe. Foretelling serves faith.

Reformed purpose: He scripts their future believing. The hour will not be raw chaos; it will be the thing He said.

John 14:30

30I will no longer talk much with you, for the ruler of this world is coming. He has no claim on me,

Arminian

Little talk left; the ruler is coming; he has no claim on Jesus. The devil’s hour is not a legal right over the Son.

Arminian courage: evil comes and finds no hook in Him. Their peace (v. 27) is that their Lord is not owned by that ruler.

Reformed

No claim: no hold, no debt, no sin for Satan to argue.

Reformed victory already in purity: the coming ruler meets a blameless Christ. The cross will look like Satan’s hour and be Satan’s judgment (16:11).

John 14:31

31but I do as the Father has commanded me, so that the world may know that I love the Father. Rise, let us go from here.

Arminian

He does the Father’s command so the world may know He loves the Father. Then: rise, let us go—toward Gethsemane and the hour.

Arminian public love: the world is meant to know. Obedience unto death is the Son’s love-language, and the going is already that obedience.

Reformed

So that the world may know: even the world is an intended audience of His love for the Father.

Reformed going: rise, let us go. Teaching yields to the commanded path. Love of the Father is about to be visible in blood.
