Passage

John 8

Verses 31–47 · ESV · Voices

Jesus turns to Jews who had believed him: if you abide in my word you are truly disciples; you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free. They protest Abrahamic freedom. He answers that everyone who practices sin is a slave to sin; the slave does not remain in the house forever, the son does; if the Son sets you free, you are free indeed. Offspring of Abraham still seek to kill him because his word finds no place in them. Abraham’s children would do Abraham’s works; instead they do their father’s. They claim God as Father; he says if God were their Father they would love him. They cannot bear to hear his word because they are of their father the devil, a murderer and liar. Whoever is of God hears God’s words; the reason they do not hear is that they are not of God.

John 8:31

31So Jesus said to the Jews who had believed him, “If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples,

Arminian

The if is aimed at people John has just called believers. Abiding in his word is the mark of being truly disciples—not a one-time nod in the treasury. Remaining is how faith proves it is not a mood.

Classical Arminians take the condition at full strength. Beginning well is not finishing. Prevenient and cooperating grace make abiding possible; it can still be refused. True discipleship is continuance in the word, not a label that cannot be lost by walking away from that word.

Reformed

Calvin: it is not enough to have begun well if progress to the end does not correspond. He exhorts to perseverance those who have tasted his doctrine. Many profess discipleship who are not so in reality; hypocrites give way at the start or in the middle, believers persevere to the end.

Reformed reading: the if sorts true disciples from temporary believers. Abiding is the Spirit’s fruit in those actually given to the Son, not a hoop a regenerate person may finally fail. The address is still a real exhortation to this crowd.

Key contrast Both refuse a faith that never continues. Arminians typically hear a genuine condition: abiding is required, grace-enabled, and resistible—discipleship can fail by not remaining. Reformed readers typically hear the same if as the way true disciples are distinguished from hypocrites: those who belong will persevere, and continuance is the evidence, not an uncertain lottery.

John 8:32

32and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free.”

Arminian

Promise chained to abiding: know the truth, and that truth frees. Wesley: the truth written in the heart by the Spirit frees from guilt, sin, misery, Satan. Freedom is not pedigree; it is a gift that arrives in the word remaining in you.

Classical Arminians preach this as a real offer of liberty. The Son means to free these hearers. Remaining in the word is how one comes into that knowledge; refusing the word is how one stays bound while quoting Abraham.

Reformed

Calvin: those who have some knowledge shall know the truth—progress by gift on gift. He impresses the word on hearts by his Spirit and chases ignorance. The fruit is freedom from Satan, sin, and death. By nature we are slaves; regeneration makes us free to obey righteousness; freedom has degrees according to faith’s measure.

Reformed gospel: the truth that frees is not a slogan. It is Christ in the Gospel, restoring liberty none of us hold by bloodline. Papist free-will boasts are exactly what this verse unmasks.

John 8:33

33They answered him, “We are offspring of Abraham and have never been enslaved to anyone. How is it that you say, ‘You will become free’?”

Arminian

Wesley: the other Jews standing by, not those who believed, as the conversation’s tenor shows. “Never enslaved” is a notorious untruth; they were under Rome. Ancestry is their gospel; freedom offered as future gift feels like insult.

Arminian pastoral: pride can answer for a whole crowd. The offer of freedom is still on the table. They would rather defend a false liberty than confess a real yoke. That is how grace is refused in public.

Reformed

Calvin: perhaps a mixed crowd, as Scripture often ascribes to all what belongs to a part. They cannot endure that a holy elect people should be called slaves. They had this excuse—that unjust occupation did not cancel covenant right—yet they missed that Abraham’s freedom stands only in the Mediator, and that degenerate sons are not lawful children without the Spirit’s regeneration.

Reformed edge: false confidence in the flesh drives away the grace of Christ. Almost no one feels he needs that grace while he can still say “Abraham.”

John 8:34

34Jesus answered them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, everyone who practices sin is a slave to sin.

Arminian

Truly, truly: practice of sin is slavery, not a hobby. Wesley: he that commits sin is in fact the slave of sin. The bondage is moral and spiritual before it is political. They asked about becoming free; he names the chain they will not see.

Classical Arminians agree this slavery is real—no Pelagian shrug. They still hold that the slave is addressed with a genuine “if the Son sets you free.” Inability to self-liberate does not cancel the sincere offer of the Son’s freeing work.

Reformed

Calvin: deeper than the philosophers’ “lust makes slaves.” Christ describes the condition of nature, not only bad choices: all not delivered by him are in slavery; all who derive sin’s contagion from corrupted nature are slaves from birth. Yet the slavery is voluntary—they who necessarily sin are not compelled as if dragged against their will.

Reformed anthropology: necessity and willingness together. No one frees himself; the diagnosis is why the Son must do the freeing, not why the command to come is a feint.

Key contrast Both confess real bondage to sin, not mere bad examples. Arminians typically keep the slave genuinely addressable by the Son’s freeing word, grace restoring the ability to yield. Reformed readers typically take practicing-sin slavery as native corruption from birth, voluntary yet needing the Son’s deliverance that actually creates freedom.

John 8:35

35The slave does not remain in the house forever; the son remains forever.

Arminian

Wesley: all sinners shall be cast out of God’s house as the slave was out of Abraham’s; the Son abides forever. Remaining in the house is sonship, not a temporary live-in. The warning is to people who think Abraham’s address is a permanent lease.

Arminian weight: “does not remain” is a real eviction. Privilege without the Son’s freedom expires. The same house still has a Son who would have them stay as freed children, not as squatters on a name.

Reformed

Calvin: from household law—a slave may have power for a time yet is not the heir. No perfect, durable freedom except through the Son. They hold a mask: place in the church like Ishmael rising against the freeborn for a short time.

Reformed reading: empty title is not inheritance. Those who merely occupy Abraham’s house without the Son will not remain. The son’s forever is Christ’s, then theirs by adoption into him.

John 8:36

36So if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed.

Arminian

The if again, now with the Son as Liberator. Wesley: if I make you free, you partake of the same privilege; freed from all guilt and sin, you shall abide in God’s house forever. Indeed-freedom is offered, not a metaphor. He means this crowd could be free.

Classical Arminians preach this as genuine liberty: the Son really sets sinners free, and the condition is receiving that work, abiding in his word. This is not autonomy; it is freedom from sin’s slavery into the house. A freedom that cannot be refused is not the freedom this verse puts in an if.

Reformed

Calvin: the right of freedom belongs to the Son alone; all others, born slaves, cannot be delivered but by his grace. What he has by nature he imparts by adoption when we are ingrafted by faith into his body. The Gospel is the instrument; he regenerates by his Spirit. Truly free stands against their foolish persuasion that they already possess a kingdom while in wretched bondage.

Reformed comfort: free indeed is the Son’s work, not self-emancipation. The if is the way of faith-union, not a maybe at the throne. Those he frees are not almost-free.

Key contrast Shared ground: only the Son gives freedom worthy of the name. Arminians typically hear a sincere, conditional offer of real liberty to these enslaved hearers. Reformed readers typically hear that none but the Son can free, and that those born slaves are actually delivered only when he ingrafts them—true freedom, not a title.

John 8:37

37I know that you are offspring of Abraham; yet you seek to kill me because my word finds no place in you.

Arminian

He grants flesh-descent and denies spiritual kinship. Wesley: Abraham’s offspring after the flesh, not followers of Abraham’s faith; the word has no place in their hearts. Murder-intent is the evidence. Space for the word is the issue, not missing ancestry.

Arminian diagnosis: “no place” is a refusal of room. The word is present enough to kill over. They could have given it place; they seek instead to silence the Speaker. That is responsible unbelief, not a quietly absent decree doing all the work.

Reformed

Calvin: concession that ridicules their title. What avails being called Abraham’s children if they rage against God and his ministers and rush to shed innocent blood? They cannot receive his words because through malice they keep their minds shut.

Reformed note: malice shutting the mind is itself the mark of a nature not yet opened. The word finds no place because the house is already occupied by another father, as the next verses will say.

John 8:38

38I speak of what I have seen with my Father, and you do what you have heard from your father.”

Arminian

Two fathers, two curricula. He speaks from sight with the Father; they act from what they have heard from theirs—not yet named. Deeds already disclose the household.

Classical Arminians: the contrast is meant to wake them. They are doing a father’s will they have chosen to hear. The unnamed father is still a warning, not a tattoo they cannot repent of if they would receive the Son’s word.

Reformed

Calvin: argument from contraries—he brings only what he learned from his Father; their fury at God’s word shows an opposite father. He speaks (office of teacher); they do (labor to extinguish the doctrine).

Reformed hearing: opposition to the Gospel is not surprising in children of the devil. The next lines will strip the remaining masks of Abraham and of God-as-Father.

John 8:39

39They answered him, “Abraham is our father.” Jesus said to them, “If you were Abraham's children, you would be doing the works Abraham did,

Arminian

They repeat the title; he adds an if of resemblance. Abraham’s children do Abraham’s works—faith that receives God’s word, not a plot to kill the speaker of truth. Lineage without likeness is a lie they tell themselves.

Arminian conditionality again: “if you were… you would be doing.” Works do not buy sonship; they show it. The door of becoming true children by faith, as Abraham believed, is the same door Paul will preach—and it is still in front of them.

Reformed

Calvin: he distinguishes bastards from lawful children and will not give the name to those who do not resemble Abraham. Carnal descent without faith is a false pretense. They who reject the promised blessing exclude themselves from Abraham’s family; children of the promise must be born of the Spirit.

Reformed election-note here is not generic: election dwells in Abraham’s seed, yet is free, so that those God sanctifies by the Spirit are the heirs. Empty covering produces hardened opposition to Christ.

John 8:40

40but now you seek to kill me, a man who has told you the truth that I heard from God. This is not what Abraham did.

Arminian

Abraham welcomed messengers from God; they hunt this man for telling truth he heard from God. The contrast is concrete. Murder of a truth-teller is not Abraham’s family custom.

Arminian moral clarity: they are not doomed by DNA. They are contradicting Abraham in real time. The truth is still being told; they may yet do what Abraham did—believe God.

Reformed

Calvin: the effect proves they are not God’s children as they boasted. Nothing in Abraham is more commended than obedience of faith. Empty titles mean nothing with God. Hatred of true doctrine marks the other family.

Reformed evidence: seeking to kill the truth-speaker is the work that names the father. This is not what Abraham did—full stop. Resemblance, not slogan, is the test.

John 8:41

41You are doing the works your father did.” They said to him, “We were not born of sexual immorality. We have one Father—even God.”

Arminian

Wesley: he still does not name the father; when they claim God, then the devil will be named (v. 44). They hear a slur on birth—perhaps even a jab at Jesus’ own origin—and climb to the highest title: God is our Father.

Arminian warning: religious people can answer a moral charge with a higher slogan. “Even God” is not a saving confession when it is used to dodge the works. Fatherhood will be tested by love of the sent Son.

Reformed

Calvin: they imagine God bound to Abraham’s whole seed: adopted family, therefore we are God’s children. They think they have holiness from the womb. Hypocrites separate God from his word, the church from faith, heaven from the Spirit.

Reformed parallel: the same boast lives wherever succession or parentage is treated as a claim-check on God. Christ will not grant the title to those who have corrupted the seed of life.

John 8:42

42Jesus said to them, “If God were your Father, you would love me, for I came from God and I am here. I came not of my own accord, but he sent me.

Arminian

Another if: God’s children love the sent Son. Wesley: I proceeded forth as God, and come as Christ. Love of Jesus is not extra credit; it is the family resemblance. Coming from God and being here is the reason love is obligatory.

Classical Arminians: the if is a real unmasking and a real invitation. They ought to love him; they may still. Hatred of the sent One is how one shows God is not Father, not how one discovers a hidden non-election and shrugs.

Reformed

Calvin: whoever is a child of God will acknowledge his first-born Son; you hate me, therefore you have no reason to boast that you are God’s children. No piety where Christ is rejected. He speaks of office more than essence: nothing in him is contrary to God.

Reformed necessity: love of the Son is not a later hobby of the devout. It is the evidence of having God as Father. Those formed by the Spirit love the One the Father sent.

John 8:43

43Why do you not understand what I say? It is because you cannot bear to hear my word.

Arminian

Cannot is explained, not left as a brute fact. Wesley: ye cannot—such is your stubbornness and pride—hear, that is, receive, obey. Not being desirous to do his will, they cannot understand (7:17). The inability is moral: they will not bear the word.

Classical Arminians take “cannot” seriously as present incapacity of a proud heart, and still as culpable. Grace has been shining in the temple; stubbornness is how they cannot. A cannot caused by pride is not an excuse; it is the charge.

Reformed

Calvin: he reproaches obstinacy so great they cannot endure to hear him, and infers diabolical rage. Why-question takes out of their hands the boast that reason and judgment lead them to oppose him. Stupidity in the first clause; ungovernable hatred in the second.

Reformed inability: cannot bear the word is more than a bad mood. It is the slave-nature and the other father showing. That does not make them victims of a capricious ban; it makes their opposition the sign of whose they are.

Key contrast Both hear a real cannot. Arminians typically trace it to stubbornness and pride that will not receive—an inability that remains responsible and that grace still confronts. Reformed readers typically take the cannot as the deep inability of those not of God, whose hatred of the word discloses a nature that will not hear unless made otherwise.

John 8:44

44You are of your father the devil, and your will is to do your father's desires. He was a murderer from the beginning, and does not stand in the truth, because there is no truth in him. When he lies, he speaks out of his own character, for he is a liar and the father of lies.

Arminian

The unnamed father is named. Will is to do his desires: murder and lie are the family trade. Wesley: murderer in inclination from the beginning of his becoming a devil; he did not abide in the truth; killer of men from the world’s start. When he lies he speaks of his own—origin of lies and of evil in general.

Arminian fairness: this is not a taunt at ethnicity. It is a moral paternity they are enacting by seeking to kill a truth-teller. “Your will is” keeps agency on the table. They are not puppets; they like their father’s work. The Son still tells the truth in their hearing.

Reformed

Calvin: children of the devil not only by imitation but by his instigation—he blinds, moves to unrighteousness, exercises tyranny. Not a Manichean second creator: it is corruption of nature whose cause is revolt, not a substance God made evil. Satan was not wicked by creation; he did not remain in the truth.

Reformed realism: their will matches the devil’s desires. That is why truth is intolerable. The verse describes fallen agency under a tyrant, not an innocent nature trapped by a mean God.

John 8:45

45But because I tell the truth, you do not believe me.

Arminian

Wesley: because I speak the truth—which liars hate. The causal is devastating: truth is the reason for unbelief, not a lack of evidence. They would believe a fellow liar more readily.

Classical Arminians: this is chosen allergy to truth. The same truth is still the instrument of freedom (v. 32). Not-believing because it is true is the height of responsible darkness.

Reformed

Calvin: he confirms the preceding—they have no other reason for opposing but that truth is hateful and intolerable, and so they show they are children of Satan.

Reformed diagnosis: unbelief here is not a waiting period for more light. It is hatred of light because it is light. That is why “of God” and “not of God” will be the next criterion.

John 8:46

46Which one of you convicts me of sin? If I tell the truth, why do you not believe me?

Arminian

Wesley: is not my life as unreprovable as my doctrine? Behavior confirms the teaching. No conviction of sin in him leaves them without a moral alibi for unbelief.

Arminian appeal: the why is a real question they ought to answer. If they cannot convict him, the honest path is to believe. Remaining in unbelief after a sinless speaker of truth is inexcusable.

Reformed

Calvin: the question proceeds from perfect confidence. He does not claim they have not slandered him; he means no crime dwells in him. The defense is of his ministry more than a treatise on the Son’s unique impeccability in the abstract: why, if I speak truth, do you not believe?

Reformed edge: they have no charge, only a father who cannot stand the truth. The unanswered why is itself evidence of the next verse.

John 8:47

47Whoever is of God hears the words of God. The reason why you do not hear them is that you are not of God.”

Arminian

Whoever is of God hears. Wesley: he that is of God—that either loves or fears him—hears with joy and reverence the words God preaches through Christ. Not-hearing is traced to not being of God in the sense of not loving or fearing him—not a shrug that they never had a chance.

Classical Arminians will not erase “you are not of God.” They read it as present spiritual location: not in God’s family by faith and love. The words are still being spoken; hearing remains the duty and the gift of those who will love the Father in the Son. Conditionality of abiding (v. 31) and this verdict belong together: remaining in unbelief is how one shows one is not of God.

Reformed

Calvin: he takes for granted that he is the Father’s faithful ambassador, then kindles: they have nothing in common with God, for they do not hear God’s words. Without talking about himself, he charges them with being at war with God. There is not a more evident sign of a reprobate mind than when one cannot endure Christ’s doctrine; cheerfully embracing it is a visible seal of election.

Reformed close: 8:47 is not generic talk of election imported from elsewhere. Jesus himself says they do not hear because they are not of God. True children hear; those not of God cannot. That is why “if you abide” revealed true disciples, and why “you cannot bear to hear” had a father underneath it.

Key contrast Fair reading, no straw men. Arminians typically take “of God” as the family of those who love and fear him, so not-hearing is culpable unbelief and proud unwillingness—abiding still sincerely offered. Reformed readers typically take Jesus at v. 47 as stating the deep reason: they do not hear because they are not of God—true children versus those whose inability to hear discloses they are not his. Neither camp should be caricatured as denying the verse in front of them.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on abiding, true disciples, slave versus son, and why some cannot hear—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside John 8:31–47.

Arminian Notable voices

“The truth—Written in your hearts by the Spirit of God, shall make you free—From guilt, sin, misery, Satan. He that committeth sin, is, in fact, the slave of sin. And the slave abideth not in the house—All sinners shall be cast out of God’s house, as the slave was out of Abraham’s: but I, the Son, abide therein forever. If I therefore make you free, ye—shall partake of the same privilege: being made free from all guilt and sin, ye shall abide in the house of God forever. Ye cannot—Such is your stubbornness and pride, hear—Receive, obey my word. Not being desirous to do my will, ye cannot understand my doctrine. He that is of God—That either loves or fears him, heareth—With joy and reverence, God’s words.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 8:32–47)

Reformed Notable voices

“If you continue in my word. Here Christ warns them, in the first place, that it is not enough for any one to have begun well, if their progress to the end do not correspond to it; and for this reason he exhorts to perseverance in the faith those who have tasted of his doctrine. … He distinguishes his followers from hypocrites by this mark, that they who falsely boasted of faith give way as soon as they have entered into the course, or at least in the middle of it; but believers persevere constantly to the end. … Every man who committeth sin is the slave of sin. … all who are not delivered by him are in a state of slavery, and … all who derive the contagion of sin from corrupted nature are slaves from their birth. Yet this slavery is voluntary, so that they who necessarily sin are not compelled to sin. If then the Son shall make you free. … the right of freedom belongs to himself alone, and … all others, being born slaves, cannot be delivered but by his grace. … He who is of God hears the words of God. … there is not a more evident sign of a reprobate mind, than when one cannot endure the doctrine of Christ … if we embrace that doctrine cheerfully, we have what may be called a visible seal of our election.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 8:31–47)
