Passage

John 8

Verses 48–59 · ESV · Voices

The dispute turns to insult and then to glory. They call Jesus a Samaritan and say he has a demon. He denies the demon, honors the Father, and does not seek his own glory. If anyone keeps his word, he will never see death. They hear bodily immortality, invoke Abraham and the prophets who died, and ask who he makes himself. He answers that the Father glorifies him; they have not known God; Abraham rejoiced to see his day. They scoff that he is not yet fifty. He answers with the divine present: before Abraham was, I am. They pick up stones; he hides himself and goes out of the temple.

John 8:48

48The Jews answered him, “Are we not right in saying that you are a Samaritan and have a demon?”

Arminian

Wesley: have we not just cause to say, Thou art a Samaritan—an enemy to our church and nation—and hast a devil, possessed by a proud and lying spirit? Insult replaces argument once “you are not of God” has landed.

Arminian pastoral: slander is what a cornered conscience often does with remaining light. They could still repent of the names. Calling the Light a demon is a decision, not an accident of vocabulary.

Reformed

Calvin: more and more stupified by Satan, fully convicted, yet enraged and shameless. Samaritan was a vulgar taunt for apostate and law-corrupter—the worst stigma they could grab at random. They curse him as men infuriated like dogs when they cannot find anything to say.

Reformed note: this is the other father’s mouth. Truth has been met with a smear. The next verses will show Jesus answering the demon charge and leaving the ethnic slur largely to die of its own smallness.

John 8:49

49Jesus answered, “I do not have a demon, but I honor my Father, and you dishonor me.

Arminian

Wesley: I honour my Father—I seek his honour only. The denial is calm. Demon-possession would not produce a life aimed at the Father’s honor. Their dishonor of the Son is already dishonor of that Father.

Classical Arminians: he still reasons with them. Honor and dishonor are on the table as real acts. They may yet stop dishonoring him; the word is not withdrawn because they spat a slur.

Reformed

Calvin: he passes the Samaritan insult and clears the second charge. He who duly honors the Father is guided by God’s Spirit. Dishonor of the Son pours contempt on God himself, whose majesty shines in him.

Reformed union of glories: he does not separate his honor from the Father’s. Enemies of the sent One are not secretly friends of God. The Judge will deal with the insult.

John 8:50

50Yet I do not seek my own glory; there is One who seeks it, and he is the judge.

Arminian

Wesley: as Messiah he consults not his own glory; he need not, for the Father consults it and will pass sentence on them accordingly. Self-glory is their game; he leaves vindication with the Judge.

Arminian comfort and warning: the humble Son is not unprotected. Hearers who dishonor him are already on a docket. That judgment is just, and the same Judge still holds out the next verse’s promise.

Reformed

Calvin hears him tying the Father’s glory to his own so that ambition cannot be alleged. Servants who seek God’s honor may wait for the day of the Lord when men despise them.

Reformed patience: God seeks the Son’s glory. The treasury crowd is not the final court. That is why Jesus can refuse both a demon’s pride and a frantic self-defense.

John 8:51

51Truly, truly, I say to you, if anyone keeps my word, he will never see death.”

Arminian

Truly, truly, and another if: anyone—Wesley notes the wideness—who keeps his word never sees death, meaning death eternal. We keep doctrine by believing, promises by hoping, command by obeying. The slur is answered with a gospel condition.

Classical Arminians: anyone is not a feint. Keeping the word is the same family as abiding in it (v. 31). Eternal life is promised to keepers, not to mere hearers; the promise is sincere, and not keeping remains a real way to miss it.

Reformed

Calvin: he still has curable people in the crowd, so he will not only terrify the desperate. He promises life to disciples who keep the doctrine as treasure, not to those who only prick up ears or approve with the mouth. When faith quickens a man, death’s sting is already drawn.

Reformed pairing: anyone who keeps is the path; keeping is what living faith does. Never see death is spiritual and final, not a claim that the body will not fall asleep.

Key contrast Both hear a real promise to word-keepers. Arminians typically stress the anyone and the if as a genuine, grace-enabled condition. Reformed readers typically stress that keeping is the mark of those quickened by faith, not a work that purchases life from a distant Christ.

John 8:52

52The Jews said to him, “Now we know that you have a demon! Abraham died, as did the prophets, yet you say, ‘If anyone keeps my word, he will never taste death.’

Arminian

They swap see for taste and hear only biology. Abraham’s grave becomes their proof that Jesus is demonic. Flesh cannot imagine a death that is not the last word.

Arminian note: misunderstanding can be willful. They have just been offered never-see-death and spend it on a gotcha. The promise still stands for anyone who would keep the word they are mocking.

Reformed

Calvin: the reprobate persist, unmoved by promises. He does not think they maliciously twist see into taste—both Hebrew idioms for dying—but they apply a spiritual doctrine to the body. Carnal people cannot perceive deliverance unless it appears in flesh.

Reformed caution: judge Christ only by the body’s clock and you will call the Gospel a demon. Believers, born of incorruptible seed, live even when they die.

John 8:53

53Are you greater than our father Abraham, who died? And the prophets died! Who do you make yourself out to be?”

Arminian

The question is the right one asked in the wrong spirit. He is greater than Abraham; they mean it as a reduction. “Who do you make yourself?” assumes self-making rather than being sent.

Classical Arminians: the comparison is invited. If they would let him answer, they would hear I AM. Greatness over Abraham is not ego; it is the only hope that Abraham’s children never see death.

Reformed

Calvin: they try to obscure Christ’s glory with Abraham’s splendor. Stars before the sun. They act unjustly in contrasting servants with the Lord, and even abuse the saints’ names against Christ.

Reformed order: prophets, apostles, and all saints belong far below Christ. Honor them by placing them there. The question “who do you make yourself?” will be answered by the Father’s glorifying, not by Jesus’ self-advertisement.

John 8:54

54Jesus answered, “If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father who glorifies me, of whom you say, ‘He is our God.’

Arminian

Wesley: referring to their “whom makest thou thyself?” Self-glory is nothing; the Father’s glorifying is everything. They claim that Father as our God while dishonoring the Son he glorifies.

Arminian irony: the God of their slogan is the very One vindicating Jesus. To keep the slogan and reject the Son is to empty “our God” of the God who is there.

Reformed

Calvin: he desires no glory but what the Father gives. When God glorifies his Son he will not permit the world to hate him with impunity. He pulls off their mask: they boast they are God’s people, but it is a false title, for they know not God.

Reformed confession: lawful profession proceeds from knowledge, and knowledge from the word. Boasting the name of God without the word is lying. Christ’s conscience is enough even if the whole world rises.

John 8:55

55But you have not known him. I know him. If I were to say that I do not know him, I would be a liar like you, but I do know him and I keep his word.

Arminian

Two knowledges: they have not known; he knows and keeps the Father’s word. He would rather be stoned than lie about that knowledge. “Liar like you” is not cruelty; it is the previous chapter’s father-of-lies logic applied.

Arminian edge: knowing God is possible in the Son and missing in their boast. They could still come to know him by keeping the word this Knower speaks. Remaining in the lie is still a path they are walking, not a stamp they cannot leave.

Reformed

Calvin: if the office constrains him to speak, silence would be a treacherous denial of the truth. God reveals himself that we may confess before men. Hypocrites who deny or glaze the truth to please men are sent back among the devil’s children.

Reformed courage: the Son’s knowledge is unique, and yet his servants learn the same duty—not to lie about God to keep a crowd. Keeping the Father’s word is the Son’s own discipleship, the pattern of v. 51.

John 8:56

56Your father Abraham rejoiced that he would see my day. He saw it and was glad.”

Arminian

Abraham is “your father” in the flesh again, and still a witness against them. Wesley: he saw by faith in types, figures, promises—Melchizedek, Mamre, the seed-blessing of the nations; perhaps a peculiar revelation of first or second coming. Gladness, not murder, was Abraham’s reflex toward Christ’s day.

Classical Arminians: the day is for seeing and rejoicing. What Abraham wanted at a distance is now present. Refusing the present day while claiming Abraham is the opposite of his faith.

Reformed

Calvin grants the name father in words and shows the objection is idle: Abraham’s whole life aimed at seeing Christ’s kingdom flourish. He longed when Christ was absent; they despise him present. The same holds for all saints, with extra weight in Abraham as father of the church.

Reformed joy: day means the time of Christ’s kingdom in the flesh, not a vague eternity. God did not disappoint Abraham’s desire and will not refuse any who truly breathe after Christ. Few desire him; that is why many lack the enjoyment.

John 8:57

57So the Jews said to him, “You are not yet fifty years old, and have you seen Abraham?”

Arminian

They invert the claim: Abraham seeing his day becomes him seeing Abraham, then they age him. Wesley: not yet fifty at the most; gravity, affliction, and labor may have made him look older. They still have him inside a human lifespan.

Arminian note: unbelief does math. If the Speaker is only a not-yet-fifty teacher, I AM is impossible. They are one verse from the answer that will pick up stones.

Reformed

Calvin: they try to refute him by impossibility—he is not fifty, Abraham died centuries ago. They even grant extra years so as not to seem too exact. Speculations that he looked old, or that the years are not solar, labor to no purpose; Papias’s forty-plus years cannot be admitted.

Reformed point: flesh judges by appearance. The next word will refuse that court entirely.

John 8:58

58Jesus said to them, “Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was, I am.”

Arminian

Truly, truly: before Abraham came to be, I am. Wesley: even from everlasting to everlasting—a direct answer, showing how much greater he is than Abraham. The divine name of 8:24 is now unmistakable. This is why keeping his word means never seeing death: the Keeper is the eternal I AM.

Classical Arminians confess full deity here without embarrassment. The same I AM has been offering freedom and life in this chapter. To believe him is to trust the God of Abraham present in flesh; to stone him is to treat Yahweh’s name as blasphemy in his own mouth.

Reformed

Calvin: unbelievers judge from flesh; Christ has something hidden, perceived by faith, by which the fathers could see him before the incarnation. He does not say I was but I am—a condition the same from beginning to end; Abraham was made, and had a beginning. The saying testifies to eternal divinity (Exod. 3:14) and, Calvin adds, to the Mediator’s grace common to all ages—Christ yesterday, today, and forever.

Reformed worship: this is not a man inflating himself. It is the heavenly power that reached through all ages. Abraham rejoiced at that day because that I AM already was.

Key contrast Both camps confess the eternal I AM against a merely fifty-year Jesus. The dispute is not whether v. 58 is high Christology. It is how that Christ has been saving through the chapter: Arminians typically emphasize the I AM sincerely offered to these hearers for faith; Reformed readers typically emphasize the same I AM as the one whose grace ran through the ages and whose identity flesh cannot see unless given eyes.

John 8:59

59So they picked up stones to throw at him, but Jesus hid himself and went out of the temple.

Arminian

Stones for blasphemy (Lev. 24:16) are their answer to I AM. Wesley: they took up stones to stone him as a blasphemer; Jesus concealed himself, probably by becoming invisible, and passed on as if none were there. The temple that heard “light of the world” now sees an attempted lynching.

Arminian close: they could have kept his word; they pick up stones. That is responsible unbelief at its limit. He still does not let them take him before the hour. Departure is judgment and mercy: the I AM will be lifted up another day, and the offer of 8:51 is not cancelled by their rocks.

Reformed

Calvin: inconsiderate zeal has no ears for the cause, but hands ready for murder. Christ rescues himself by secret power under a low appearance, not a full display of deity. Wicked priests keep the outward temple after banishing the One in whom all fullness dwells; they are deceived if they think they have a temple God inhabits.

Reformed close: the chapter that began with a woman not stoned ends with the I AM nearly stoned. His hiding is the Father’s hour, not their victory. Those who will not hear (v. 47) now try to silence. The next sign will be light given to a man born blind.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the Samaritan/demon slander, keeping his word, and “before Abraham was, I am”—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside John 8:48–59.

Arminian Notable voices

“If a man keep my word—We keep his doctrine by believing, his promises by hoping, his command by obeying. He shall never see death—That is, death eternal. He shall live forever. He saw it—By faith in types, figures, and promises; as particularly in Melchisedec; in the appearance of Jehovah to him in the plains of Mamre; and in the promise that in his seed all the nations of the earth shall be blessed. Before Abraham was I AM—Even from everlasting to everlasting. This is a direct answer to the objection of the Jews, and shows how much greater he was than Abraham.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 8:51–58)

Reformed Notable voices

“Christ… promises eternal life to his disciples, but demands disciples who shall not only prick up their ears, like asses, or profess with the mouth that they approve of his doctrine, but who shall keep his doctrine as a precious treasure. He says that they shall never see death; for, when faith quickens the soul of a man, death already has its sting extracted and its venom removed, and so cannot inflict a deadly wound. … Before Abraham was. … he excludes himself from the ordinary rank of men, and claims for himself a power more than human, a power heavenly and divine, the perception of which reached from the beginning of the world through all ages. … he does not say, I was, but I am; by which he denotes a condition uniformly the same from the beginning to the end. … Then they took up stones. … how great is the madness of inconsiderate zeal; for they have no ears to know the cause, but they have hands ready to commit murder.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 8:51–59)
