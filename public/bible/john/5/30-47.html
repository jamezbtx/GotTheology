Passage

John 5

Verses 30–47 · WEB · Voices

Jesus continues his defense. His judgment is just because he seeks the will of the one who sent him. He points to witnesses: John the Baptist, his own works, the Father himself, and the Scriptures, which testify about him. Yet his hearers refuse to come to him for life. They seek glory from one another rather than from God, and they do not truly believe Moses, who wrote about Jesus. Both traditions see the passage as a powerful case for Christ’s identity. They draw different conclusions from verse 40 and verse 44.

John 5:30

30I can of myself do nothing. As I hear, I judge; and my judgment is righteous, because I don’t seek my own will, but the will of my Father who sent me.

Arminian

I can do nothing on my own. My judgment is just, because I seek the will of him who sent me. Arminian readers see the Son’s perfect submission.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s obedience as mediator.

John 5:31

31“If I testify about myself, my witness is not valid.

Arminian

If I alone bear witness about myself, my testimony is not true. Arminian readers see Jesus accepting the legal standard of multiple witnesses.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus pointing beyond himself to other witnesses.

John 5:32

32It is another who testifies about me. I know that the testimony which he testifies about me is true.

Arminian

There is another who bears witness about me. Arminian readers see the Father’s witness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Father testifying to the Son.

John 5:33

33You have sent to John, and he has testified to the truth.

Arminian

You sent to John, and he bore witness to the truth. Arminian readers see John’s witness as evidence available to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God using human witnesses.

John 5:34

34But the testimony which I receive is not from man. However, I say these things that you may be saved.

Arminian

I say these things so that you may be saved. Arminian readers see Jesus’ sincere desire for the salvation of his opponents.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the sincere offer of the gospel, even to those who reject it.

Key contrast Both see Jesus speaking for his hearers’ salvation. Arminian readers see his desire to save every hearer. Reformed readers see a sincere offer made to all, through which God saves his people.

John 5:35

35He was the burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to rejoice for a while in his light.

Arminian

He was a burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to rejoice for a while in his light. Arminian readers see temporary enthusiasm that did not lead to lasting faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see temporary response without true conversion.

John 5:36

36But the testimony which I have is greater than that of John; for the works which the Father gave me to accomplish, the very works that I do, testify about me, that the Father has sent me.

Arminian

The works that the Father has given me bear witness about me. Arminian readers see works as evidence that should lead to faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the works as divine testimony.

John 5:37

37The Father himself, who sent me, has testified about me. You have neither heard his voice at any time, nor seen his form.

Arminian

The Father who sent me has himself borne witness about me. You have never heard his voice. Arminian readers see refusal to hear.

Reformed

Reformed readers see spiritual deafness.

John 5:38

38You don’t have his word living in you, because you don’t believe him whom he sent.

Arminian

You do not have his word abiding in you, for you do not believe the one he has sent. Arminian readers see unbelief as the reason God’s word does not abide in them.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the absence of the word in the heart as both the cause and the result of unbelief.

John 5:39

39“You search the Scriptures, because you think that in them you have eternal life; and these are they which testify about me.

Arminian

You search the Scriptures, because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me. Both traditions see Scripture pointing to Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as the center of all Scripture.

John 5:40

40Yet you will not come to me, that you may have life.

Arminian

Yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life. Arminian readers see “you refuse” as clear evidence that unbelief is a free choice. People do not come because they will not.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that people refuse to come and are responsible for it. They see the unwillingness as rooted in a fallen nature (6:44).

Key contrast Both see refusal to come as real and culpable. Arminian readers see it as a free choice that could have been otherwise. Reformed readers see it as flowing from a fallen nature that will not come unless God draws.

John 5:41

41I don’t receive glory from men.

Arminian

I do not receive glory from people. Arminian readers see Christ’s indifference to human praise.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ seeking only the Father’s glory.

John 5:42

42But I know you, that you don’t have God’s love in yourselves.

Arminian

I know that you do not have the love of God within you. Arminian readers see a lack of love for God as the root of rejection.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the absence of love for God in the unregenerate heart.

John 5:43

43I have come in my Father’s name, and you don’t receive me. If another comes in his own name, you will receive him.

Arminian

I have come in my Father’s name, and you do not receive me. Arminian readers see willful rejection.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the perversity of the human heart.

John 5:44

44How can you believe, who receive glory from one another, and you don’t seek the glory that comes from the only God?

Arminian

How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from God? Arminian readers see pride as a hindrance to faith that people can turn from.

Reformed

Reformed readers see pride making faith impossible apart from grace.

John 5:45

45“Don’t think that I will accuse you to the Father. There is one who accuses you, even Moses, on whom you have set your hope.

Arminian

Do not think that I will accuse you; there is one who accuses you, Moses. Arminian readers see the law exposing unbelief.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Moses’ law as condemning those who reject Christ.

John 5:46

46For if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote about me.

Arminian

If you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of me. Both traditions see Moses pointing to Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the unity of the covenants in Christ.

John 5:47

47But if you don’t believe his writings, how will you believe my words?”

Arminian

If you do not believe his writings, how will you believe my words? Arminian readers see unbelief toward Scripture leading to unbelief toward Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the authority of Scripture as the foundation of faith.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Arminian and Reformed writers of the past on the witnesses to Jesus and the charge “you refuse to come to me that you may have life”, in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“These things—Concerning John, whom ye yourselves reverence, I say, that ye may be saved—So really and seriously did he will their salvation. Yet they were not saved. Most, if not all of them, died in their sins. Yet ye will not come unto me—As they direct you. But I know you—With this ray he pierces the hearts of the hearers. And this doubtless he spake with the tenderest compassion.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 5:34, 40, 42)

Reformed Notable voices

“This is the true way of profiting, when the word of God takes root in us, so that, being impressed on our hearts, it has its fixed abode there. Christ affirms that the heavenly doctrine has no place among the Jews, because they do not receive the Son of God. And you will not come to me. He again reproaches them that it is nothing but their own malice that hinders them from becoming partakers of the life offered in the Scriptures; for when he says that they will not, he imputes the cause of their ignorance and blindness to wickedness and obstinacy.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 5:38, 40)
