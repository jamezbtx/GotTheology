Passage

John 5

Verses 19–29 · WEB · Voices

After healing on the Sabbath and calling God his Father, Jesus explains his relationship to the Father. The Son does only what he sees the Father doing. As the Father raises the dead and gives life, so the Son gives life to whom he will. The Father has given all judgment to the Son, so that all may honor the Son as they honor the Father. Whoever hears Jesus’ word and believes has eternal life and has passed from death to life. An hour is coming when all in the tombs will hear his voice and come out, to the resurrection of life or of judgment. Both traditions find a high Christology here. They read “gives life to whom he will” and the judgment by deeds differently.

John 5:19

19Jesus therefore answered them, “Most certainly, I tell you, the Son can do nothing of himself, but what he sees the Father doing. For whatever things he does, these the Son also does likewise.

Arminian

The Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing. Arminian readers see the perfect harmony of Father and Son.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the unity of divine action, with the Son acting in full accord with the Father.

John 5:20

20For the Father has affection for the Son, and shows him all things that he himself does. He will show him greater works than these, that you may marvel.

Arminian

The Father loves the Son and shows him all that he is doing. Arminian readers see the love within the Godhead.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the eternal love of the Father for the Son.

John 5:21

21For as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, even so the Son also gives life to whom he desires.

Arminian

As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so the Son gives life to whom he will. Arminian readers see the Son willing to give life to those who believe, as verse 24 states.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s sovereign will in giving life, which parallels regeneration.

Key contrast Both affirm that the Son gives life. Arminian readers read “to whom he will” in light of verse 24, as all who hear and believe. Reformed readers see the Son’s sovereign choice in giving spiritual life.

John 5:22

22For the Father judges no one, but he has given all judgment to the Son,

Arminian

The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son. Arminian readers see the Son as the one to whom all will give account.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s divine authority as judge.

John 5:23

23that all may honor the Son, even as they honor the Father. He who doesn’t honor the Son doesn’t honor the Father who sent him.

Arminian

That all may honor the Son as they honor the Father. Both traditions see this as clear evidence of the Son’s deity. Arminian readers stress that rejecting the Son is rejecting the Father.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son as equal in honor with the Father.

John 5:24

24“Most certainly I tell you, he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and doesn’t come into judgment, but has passed out of death into life.

Arminian

Whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life and has passed from death to life. Arminian readers see hearing and believing as the condition of eternal life, which is a present reality.

Reformed

Reformed readers see eternal life as a present possession that guarantees the believer will not come into judgment.

Key contrast Both affirm that the believer has eternal life now. Arminian readers see it as received and held by faith. Reformed readers see it as a settled possession that guarantees the believer will never come into condemnation.

John 5:25

25Most certainly I tell you, the hour comes, and now is, when the dead will hear the Son of God’s voice; and those who hear will live.

Arminian

The hour is coming and is now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live. Arminian readers see the spiritually dead hearing Christ’s call and responding.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an effectual call: the spiritually dead are given life by the voice that raises them.

John 5:26

26For as the Father has life in himself, even so he gave to the Son also to have life in himself.

Arminian

As the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son to have life in himself. Arminian readers see the Son as the source of life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s self-existent life, given within the Trinity’s eternal relationship.

John 5:27

27He also gave him authority to execute judgment, because he is a son of man.

Arminian

He has given him authority to execute judgment, because he is the Son of Man. Arminian readers see Christ’s humanity qualifying him to judge.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Daniel 7’s Son of Man fulfilled in Christ.

John 5:28

28Don’t marvel at this, for the hour comes in which all who are in the tombs will hear his voice

Arminian

All who are in the tombs will hear his voice. Both traditions see a future bodily resurrection of all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the general resurrection at the last day.

John 5:29

29and will come out; those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; and those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment.

Arminian

Those who have done good to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment. Arminian readers see deeds as the evidence of faith or unbelief.

Reformed

Reformed readers see good deeds as the fruit of regeneration, not the ground of salvation.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Older voices from both traditions on John 5:19–29.

Arminian Notable voices

“The Son can do nothing of himself—This is not his imperfection, but his glory, resulting from his eternal, intimate, indissoluble unity with the Father. The power of quickening whom he will follows from the power of judging. That all men may honour the Son, even as they honour the Father—Either willingly, and so escaping condemnation, by faith: or unwillingly, when feeling the wrath of the Judge. This demonstrates the EQUALITY of the Son with the Father. And cometh not into condemnation—Unless he make shipwreck of the faith.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on John 5:19–24)

Reformed Notable voices

“We ought also to observe in what manner Christ bestows life upon us; for he found us all dead, and therefore it was necessary to begin with a resurrection. Again, he does not speak of this life as bestowed indiscriminately on all; for he says that he giveth life to whom he will; by which he means that he specially confers this grace on none but certain men, that is, on the elect.”
John Calvin · Commentary on John (on John 5:21)
