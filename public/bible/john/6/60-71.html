Passage

John 6

Verses 60–71 · WEB

Many of Jesus’ disciples find his teaching hard and grumble. Jesus says the Spirit gives life and the flesh is no help, and that his words are spirit and life. He knew from the beginning who did not believe and who would betray him, and he repeats that no one can come to him unless it is granted by the Father. Many disciples turn back and no longer walk with him. When Jesus asks the Twelve if they will leave too, Peter answers that only Jesus has the words of eternal life. Jesus responds that he chose the Twelve, yet one of them is a devil. Both traditions use this passage in the debate over perseverance and apostasy.

John 6:60

60Therefore many of his disciples, when they heard this, said, “This is a hard saying! Who can listen to it?”

Arminian

Many disciples said, “This is a hard saying; who can listen to it?” Arminian readers see disciples who are struggling with Jesus’ teaching and are at risk.

Reformed

Reformed readers see outward followers whose response reveals the true state of their hearts.

John 6:61

61But Jesus knowing in himself that his disciples murmured at this, said to them, “Does this cause you to stumble?

Arminian

Jesus knew they were grumbling and asked, “Does this offend you?” Arminian readers see Jesus confronting offense to give them a chance to reconsider.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus’ divine knowledge of hearts.

John 6:62

62Then what if you would see the Son of Man ascending to where he was before?

Arminian

What if you were to see the Son of Man ascending where he was before? Arminian readers see further evidence offered to strengthen faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s ascension as confirming his heavenly origin.

John 6:63

63It is the spirit who gives life. The flesh profits nothing. The words that I speak to you are spirit, and are life.

Arminian

It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh is no help. The words I have spoken are spirit and life. Arminian readers see the Spirit working through Jesus’ words to all who hear.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit giving life where the flesh can do nothing. This supports regeneration as a sovereign work.

John 6:64

64But there are some of you who don’t believe.” For Jesus knew from the beginning who they were who didn’t believe, and who it was who would betray him.

Arminian

Some of you do not believe. Jesus knew from the beginning who would not believe and who would betray him. Arminian readers see Jesus’ foreknowledge of unbelief, not a cause of it.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus’ knowledge of his own, rooted in the Father’s purpose.

John 6:65

65He said, “For this cause I have said to you that no one can come to me, unless it is given to him by my Father.”

Arminian

No one can come to me unless it is granted him by the Father. Arminian readers see the Father granting the ability to come to all who do not resist his grace.

Reformed

Reformed readers see coming to Christ as a gift granted to some, not all. They read it as confirming verse 44.

Key contrast Both agree that coming to Christ is a gift of the Father. Arminian readers see that gift offered to all and received by those who do not resist. Reformed readers see it granted specifically to the elect.

John 6:66

66At this, many of his disciples went back and walked no more with him.

Arminian

Many of his disciples turned back and no longer walked with him. Arminian readers see real disciples falling away, which shows that following Jesus can be abandoned.

Reformed

Reformed readers see outward disciples who were never truly given to Christ (v64–65). They read 1 John 2:19 as the explanation.

Key contrast Both see a real departure. Arminian readers see disciples who truly followed and then fell away. Reformed readers see outward followers who showed they had never truly believed.

John 6:67

67Jesus said therefore to the twelve, “You don’t also want to go away, do you?”

Arminian

“Do you want to go away as well?” Arminian readers see Jesus giving the Twelve a real choice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus testing and drawing out the faith of the Twelve.

John 6:68

68Simon Peter answered him, “Lord, to whom would we go? You have the words of eternal life.

Arminian

“Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life.” Arminian readers see Peter’s response as a free choice to stay.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Peter’s confession as the fruit of the Father’s revelation, as in Matthew 16:17.

John 6:69

69We have come to believe and know that you are the Christ, the Son of the living God.”

Arminian

We have believed and have come to know that you are the Holy One of God. Arminian readers see faith growing through experience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see saving faith produced by God.

John 6:70

70Jesus answered them, “Didn’t I choose you, the twelve, and one of you is a devil?”

Arminian

Did I not choose you, the Twelve? Yet one of you is a devil. Arminian readers see Jesus’ choice of Judas to apostleship as showing that being chosen does not guarantee final salvation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the choice of the Twelve as a choice to an office, which differs from election to salvation.

Key contrast Both note that Judas was chosen yet betrayed Jesus. Arminian readers see this as showing that being chosen does not guarantee perseverance. Reformed readers see a choice to apostleship, distinct from election to salvation.

John 6:71

71Now he spoke of Judas, the son of Simon Iscariot, for it was he who would betray him, being one of the twelve.

Arminian

He spoke of Judas, who was going to betray him. Arminian readers see Judas as a sobering example of someone close to Jesus who fell away.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Judas as one who was never truly Christ’s, fulfilling Scripture (17:12).
