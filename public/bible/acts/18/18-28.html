Passage

Acts 18

Verses 18–28 · WEB

Paul stays in Corinth for a while, cuts his hair at Cenchreae because of a vow, and sails with Priscilla and Aquila to Ephesus, where he reasons briefly in the synagogue and promises to return if God wills. He travels to Caesarea, Jerusalem, and Antioch, then strengthens the disciples in Galatia and Phrygia. Meanwhile Apollos, an eloquent Alexandrian who knows only John’s baptism, teaches accurately about Jesus in Ephesus; Priscilla and Aquila explain the way of God more accurately to him, and he goes to Achaia, where he greatly helps believers and refutes opponents from the Scriptures.

Acts 18:18

18Paul, having stayed after this many more days, took his leave of the brothers, and sailed from there for Syria, together with Priscilla and Aquila. He shaved his head in Cenchreae, for he had a vow.

Arminian

Paul cuts his hair at Cenchreae because of a vow. Arminian readers see Paul keeping Jewish practices as personal devotion.

Reformed

Reformed readers see freedom to observe customs that do not compromise the gospel.

Acts 18:19

19He came to Ephesus, and he left them there; but he himself entered into the synagogue and reasoned with the Jews.

Arminian

He reaches Ephesus and reasons in the synagogue. Arminian commentators see a brief first visit to a major city.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see seed sown for later harvest.

Acts 18:20

20When they asked him to stay with them a longer time, he declined;

Arminian

They ask him to stay longer, but he declines. Arminian readers see openness he cannot yet meet.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s timing for the work in Ephesus.

Acts 18:21

21but taking his leave of them, he said, “I must by all means keep this coming feast in Jerusalem, but I will return again to you if God wills.” Then he set sail from Ephesus.

Arminian

He promises to return “if God wills.” Arminian expositors see humility about future plans.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see submission to God’s providence, as James 4:15 counsels.

Acts 18:22

22When he had landed at Caesarea, he went up and greeted the assembly, and went down to Antioch.

Arminian

He lands at Caesarea, greets the church, and goes to Antioch. Arminian readers see connections maintained.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the unity of the churches.

Acts 18:23

23Having spent some time there, he departed and went through the region of Galatia and Phrygia, in order, establishing all the disciples.

Arminian

He travels through Galatia and Phrygia strengthening the disciples. Arminian commentators see care for perseverance.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see ongoing pastoral nurture.

Acts 18:24

24Now a certain Jew named Apollos, an Alexandrian by race, an eloquent man, came to Ephesus. He was mighty in the Scriptures.

Arminian

Apollos, eloquent and mighty in the Scriptures, comes to Ephesus. Arminian readers see gifts ready for God’s use.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God raising up another teacher.

Acts 18:25

25This man had been instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in spirit, he spoke and taught accurately the things concerning Jesus, although he knew only the baptism of John.

Arminian

He was fervent and taught accurately about Jesus, knowing only John’s baptism. Arminian expositors see sincere faith with incomplete knowledge.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the importance of fuller instruction.

Acts 18:26

26He began to speak boldly in the synagogue. But when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside, and explained to him the way of God more accurately.

Arminian

Priscilla and Aquila take him aside and explain the way of God more accurately. Arminian readers see gentle correction in private.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the value of sound doctrine taught by mature believers.

Acts 18:27

27When he had determined to pass over into Achaia, the brothers encouraged him; and wrote to the disciples to receive him. When he had come, he greatly helped those who had believed through grace;

Arminian

He goes to Achaia and greatly helps those who believed through grace. Arminian commentators see Apollos serving those who had come to faith.

Reformed

Reformed commentators note the phrase “believed through grace” and see faith as the fruit of grace.

Acts 18:28

28for he powerfully refuted the Jews, publicly showing by the Scriptures that Jesus was the Christ.

Arminian

He powerfully refutes the Jews, showing from Scripture that Jesus is the Christ. Arminian readers see reasoned argument from the Bible.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Scripture as the decisive authority in debate.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
