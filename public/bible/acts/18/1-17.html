Passage

Acts 18

Verses 1–17 · WEB

In Corinth Paul stays with Aquila and Priscilla, fellow tentmakers expelled from Rome, and reasons in the synagogue each Sabbath. When opposition grows, he turns to the Gentiles and teaches next door in the house of Titius Justus; the synagogue ruler Crispus and many Corinthians believe. The Lord tells Paul in a vision not to be afraid, for no one will harm him and he has many people in this city, so Paul stays eighteen months. When opponents bring him before the proconsul Gallio, Gallio refuses to judge a dispute about Jewish law.

Acts 18:1

1After these things Paul departed from Athens and came to Corinth.

Arminian

Paul leaves Athens for Corinth. Arminian readers see him moving to a busy, morally troubled city.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God sending him where many of his people are.

Acts 18:2

2He found a certain Jew named Aquila, a man of Pontus by race, who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla, because Claudius had commanded all the Jews to depart from Rome. He came to them,

Arminian

He meets Aquila and Priscilla, Jews expelled from Rome under Claudius. Arminian commentators see God connecting believers through ordinary events.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see providence using an imperial edict.

Acts 18:3

3and because he practiced the same trade, he lived with them and worked, for by trade they were tent makers.

Arminian

He works with them as a tentmaker. Arminian readers see Paul supporting himself so the gospel is free.

Reformed

Reformed readers see dignity in ordinary work.

Acts 18:4

4He reasoned in the synagogue every Sabbath and persuaded Jews and Greeks.

Arminian

He reasons in the synagogue every Sabbath, persuading Jews and Greeks. Arminian expositors see persuasion aimed at decision.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see faithful use of means.

Acts 18:5

5When Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul was compelled by the Spirit, testifying to the Jews that Jesus was the Christ.

Arminian

When Silas and Timothy arrive, Paul devotes himself fully to the word. Arminian readers see support freeing him for ministry.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the word as Paul’s central calling.

Acts 18:6

6When they opposed him and blasphemed, he shook out his clothing and said to them, “Your blood be on your own heads! I am clean. From now on, I will go to the Gentiles!”

Arminian

When they oppose him, he shakes out his clothes: “Your blood be on your own heads.” Arminian commentators see their responsibility for refusing the message.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see Paul released from responsibility after faithful witness, as in Ezekiel 33.

Acts 18:7

7He departed there and went into the house of a certain man named Justus, one who worshiped God, whose house was next door to the synagogue.

Arminian

He goes next door to the house of Titius Justus, a worshiper of God. Arminian readers see the mission continuing nearby.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God providing a new base.

Acts 18:8

8Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, believed in the Lord with all his house. Many of the Corinthians, when they heard, believed and were baptized.

Arminian

Crispus, the synagogue ruler, believes with his household, and many Corinthians believe and are baptized. Arminian expositors see hearing, believing, and baptism in sequence.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see whole households brought into the church.

Acts 18:9

9The Lord said to Paul in the night by a vision, “Don’t be afraid, but speak and don’t be silent;

Arminian

The Lord says, “Don’t be afraid, but speak and don’t be silent.” Arminian readers see encouragement given to a weary servant.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ sustaining his messenger.

Acts 18:10

10for I am with you, and no one will attack you to harm you, for I have many people in this city.”

Arminian

“I am with you, and no one will harm you, for I have many people in this city.” Arminian readers see “many people” as those God foreknew would believe in response to Paul’s preaching.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a strong text for election: Christ already calls them “my people” before they have believed, which is why Paul should keep preaching.

Acts 18:11

11He lived there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

Arminian

He stays eighteen months teaching the word. Arminian commentators see sustained ministry building a church.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God’s promise motivating patient labor.

Acts 18:12

12But when Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews with one accord rose up against Paul and brought him before the judgment seat,

Arminian

Under Gallio, opponents bring Paul to the judgment seat. Arminian readers see the promise of protection tested.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God keeping his word through a Roman official.

Acts 18:13

13saying, “This man persuades men to worship God contrary to the law.”

Arminian

They accuse him of persuading people to worship contrary to the law. Arminian expositors see a charge meant to make Christianity illegal.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the gospel’s relation to Judaism at stake.

Acts 18:14

14But when Paul was about to open his mouth, Gallio said to the Jews, “If indeed it were a matter of wrong or of wicked crime, you Jews, it would be reasonable that I should bear with you;

Arminian

Before Paul can speak, Gallio says he would hear a case of actual wrongdoing. Arminian readers see a ruler refusing to be used.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s protection without Paul even needing to speak.

Acts 18:15

15but if they are questions about words and names and your own law, look to it yourselves. For I don’t want to be a judge of these matters.”

Arminian

Questions of words and names and their own law are not his concern. Arminian commentators see Rome treating the faith as a Jewish matter.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see providence granting legal space to the church.

Acts 18:16

16So he drove them from the judgment seat.

Arminian

He drives them from the judgment seat. Arminian readers see the accusers dismissed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lord’s promise of verse 10 fulfilled.

Acts 18:17

17Then all the Greeks seized Sosthenes, the ruler of the synagogue, and beat him before the judgment seat. Gallio didn’t care about any of these things.

Arminian

The crowd beats Sosthenes, the synagogue ruler, while Gallio ignores it. Arminian expositors see disorder unchecked.

Reformed

Reformed expositors note that a Sosthenes later appears as a believer in 1 Corinthians 1:1, perhaps the same man.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
