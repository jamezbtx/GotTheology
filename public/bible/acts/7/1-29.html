Passage

Acts 7

Verses 1–29 · WEB

Asked by the high priest whether the charges are true, Stephen retells Israel’s story. The God of glory appeared to Abraham outside the land, promised it to his offspring, and foretold their slavery and rescue. The patriarchs sold Joseph out of jealousy, yet God was with him and used him to save the family in famine. When Israel multiplied in Egypt and was oppressed, Moses was born, raised in Pharaoh’s house, and later tried to defend his people, but they did not understand that God meant to deliver them through him, and he fled to Midian.

Acts 7:1

1The high priest said, “Are these things so?”

Arminian

The high priest asks, “Are these things so?” Arminian readers see Stephen given a formal chance to answer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the setting for the longest speech in Acts, a defense that becomes an indictment.

Acts 7:2

2He said, “Brothers and fathers, listen. The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia, before he lived in Haran,

Arminian

The God of glory appeared to Abraham in Mesopotamia. Arminian commentators note that God met Abraham far from the temple and the land.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God taking the initiative in calling Abraham, the pattern of all grace.

Acts 7:3

3and said to him, ‘Get out of your land and away from your relatives, and come into a land which I will show you.’

Arminian

God told Abraham to leave his land and relatives. Arminian readers see a call that required a response of trust and obedience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the sovereign call that created the covenant people out of nothing.

Acts 7:4

4Then he came out of the land of the Chaldaeans and lived in Haran. From there, when his father was dead, God moved him into this land where you are now living.

Arminian

Abraham left Chaldea, lived in Haran, and after his father died came to this land. Arminian expositors see faith expressed in steps of obedience.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God moving Abraham by stages according to his plan.

Acts 7:5

5He gave him no inheritance in it, no, not so much as to set his foot on. He promised that he would give it to him for a possession, and to his offspring after him, when he still had no child.

Arminian

God gave him no inheritance, not even a foot of ground, yet promised it to his offspring. Arminian readers see faith that trusts promises not yet seen.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise resting entirely on God, since Abraham had nothing in hand.

Acts 7:6

6God spoke in this way: that his offspring would live as aliens in a strange land, and that they would be enslaved and mistreated for four hundred years.

Arminian

His offspring would be strangers and slaves for four hundred years. Arminian commentators see God foretelling hardship honestly.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see suffering included in God’s announced plan for his people.

Acts 7:7

7‘I will judge the nation to which they will be in bondage,’ said God, ‘and after that they will come out and serve me in this place.’

Arminian

God would judge the oppressing nation and bring his people out to worship him. Arminian readers see worship as the goal of deliverance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness to his word across centuries.

Acts 7:8

8He gave him the covenant of circumcision. So Abraham became the father of Isaac, and circumcised him the eighth day. Isaac became the father of Jacob, and Jacob became the father of the twelve patriarchs.

Arminian

God gave him the covenant of circumcision, and the patriarchs followed. Arminian expositors see a covenant sign given before any temple existed.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the covenant sign passed to the next generation, which paedobaptists connect with baptism.

Acts 7:9

9“The patriarchs, moved with jealousy against Joseph, sold him into Egypt. God was with him

Arminian

The patriarchs sold Joseph out of jealousy, but God was with him. Arminian readers see human sin unable to defeat God’s presence with the faithful.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the pattern of Genesis 50:20, where God meant for good what others meant for evil.

Acts 7:10

10and delivered him out of all his afflictions, and gave him favor and wisdom before Pharaoh, king of Egypt. He made him governor over Egypt and all his house.

Arminian

God rescued Joseph and gave him favor and wisdom before Pharaoh. Arminian commentators see God honoring Joseph’s faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God’s providence raising a rejected brother to save his people, a picture of Christ.

Acts 7:11

11Now a famine came over all the land of Egypt and Canaan, and great affliction. Our fathers found no food.

Arminian

Famine struck Egypt and Canaan, and the fathers found no food. Arminian readers see need that drives the family toward God’s provision.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God arranging circumstances to bring his people where he wants them.

Acts 7:12

12But when Jacob heard that there was grain in Egypt, he sent out our fathers the first time.

Arminian

Jacob sent the fathers to Egypt for grain. Arminian expositors see ordinary decisions within God’s larger story.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see providence working through the most ordinary choices.

Acts 7:13

13On the second time Joseph was made known to his brothers, and Joseph’s family was revealed to Pharaoh.

Arminian

On the second visit Joseph made himself known to his brothers. Arminian readers note that Israel recognized its deliverer only the second time.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same pattern in Israel’s rejection of Jesus, with hope of later recognition.

Acts 7:14

14Joseph sent and summoned Jacob his father and all his relatives, seventy-five souls.

Arminian

Joseph summoned Jacob and his relatives, seventy-five people. Arminian commentators note Stephen follows the Greek Old Testament count.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God preserving the whole covenant family.

Acts 7:15

15Jacob went down into Egypt and he died, himself and our fathers;

Arminian

Jacob went down to Egypt and died there with the fathers. Arminian readers see the patriarchs dying outside the promised land.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith that died still holding the promise, as Hebrews 11:13 says.

Acts 7:16

16and they were brought back to Shechem and laid in the tomb that Abraham bought for a price in silver from the children of Hamor of Shechem.

Arminian

They were buried at Shechem in the tomb Abraham bought. Arminian expositors note Stephen compresses Abraham’s and Jacob’s purchases.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the patriarchs buried in the land as a pledge of future possession.

Acts 7:17

17“But as the time of the promise came close which God had sworn to Abraham, the people grew and multiplied in Egypt,

Arminian

As the time of the promise drew near, the people multiplied in Egypt. Arminian readers see God’s word advancing despite hardship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise fulfilled at the time God set.

Acts 7:18

18until there arose a different king who didn’t know Joseph.

Arminian

A new king arose who did not know Joseph. Arminian commentators see how quickly gratitude can be forgotten.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see a change of rulers serving God’s purpose to bring Israel out.

Acts 7:19

19The same took advantage of our race and mistreated our fathers, and forced them to abandon their babies, so that they wouldn’t stay alive.

Arminian

He exploited the people and forced them to abandon their infants. Arminian readers see cruelty that God will judge.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the oppression that sets the stage for God’s deliverer.

Acts 7:20

20At that time Moses was born, and was exceedingly handsome to God. He was nourished three months in his father’s house.

Arminian

Moses was born, beautiful before God, and nursed for three months at home. Arminian expositors see God’s care for one child amid danger.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God preparing his chosen deliverer from birth.

Acts 7:21

21When he was abandoned, Pharaoh’s daughter took him up and reared him as her own son.

Arminian

Pharaoh’s daughter adopted the abandoned Moses. Arminian readers see God working through an unlikely rescuer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God placing his servant in the enemy’s house.

Acts 7:22

22Moses was instructed in all the wisdom of the Egyptians. He was mighty in his words and works.

Arminian

Moses was educated in Egyptian wisdom and was powerful in words and deeds. Arminian commentators see gifts that God would later use.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see providence equipping Moses for his calling.

Acts 7:23

23But when he was forty years old, it came into his heart to visit his brothers, the children of Israel.

Arminian

At forty, Moses decided to visit his fellow Israelites. Arminian readers see Moses choosing to identify with his people.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the first stirring of the calling God had prepared for him.

Acts 7:24

24Seeing one of them suffer wrong, he defended him and avenged him who was oppressed, striking the Egyptian.

Arminian

He defended an oppressed Israelite and killed the Egyptian. Arminian expositors see zeal acting before God’s timing.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see a deliverer who was not yet sent, acting in his own strength.

Acts 7:25

25He supposed that his brothers understood that God, by his hand, was giving them deliverance; but they didn’t understand.

Arminian

He supposed his brothers would understand that God was delivering them through him, but they did not. Arminian readers see Stephen’s point: Israel failed to recognize its deliverer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the first of several rejections of God’s chosen savior, pointing to Jesus.

Acts 7:26

26“The day following, he appeared to them as they fought, and urged them to be at peace again, saying, ‘Sirs, you are brothers. Why do you wrong one another?’

Arminian

The next day he tried to reconcile two Israelites who were fighting. Arminian commentators see Moses seeking peace among his people.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the deliverer’s concern for justice among the people of God.

Acts 7:27

27But he who did his neighbor wrong pushed him away, saying, ‘Who made you a ruler and a judge over us?

Arminian

The wrongdoer pushed him away: “Who made you a ruler and a judge over us?” Arminian readers see the people rejecting the one God had appointed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the question Israel would also ask of Jesus.

Acts 7:28

28Do you want to kill me as you killed the Egyptian yesterday?’

Arminian

“Do you want to kill me as you killed the Egyptian?” Arminian expositors see Moses’ deed now public.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see rejection driving the deliverer away for a time.

Acts 7:29

29Moses fled at this saying, and became a stranger in the land of Midian, where he became the father of two sons.

Arminian

Moses fled to Midian, where he had two sons. Arminian readers see years of waiting that prepared him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s apparent delays as part of his plan.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
