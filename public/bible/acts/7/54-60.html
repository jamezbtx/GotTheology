Passage

Acts 7

Verses 54–60 · WEB

Enraged, the council grinds their teeth at Stephen. Full of the Holy Spirit, he sees the glory of God and Jesus standing at God’s right hand. They cover their ears, drive him out of the city, and stone him, laying their garments at the feet of a young man named Saul. Stephen commits his spirit to the Lord Jesus and prays that this sin not be held against them, then falls asleep.

Acts 7:54

54Now when they heard these things, they were cut to the heart, and they gnashed at him with their teeth.

Arminian

They are cut to the heart and grind their teeth at him. Arminian readers see conviction turning to rage instead of repentance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the hardness of hearts not renewed by grace.

Acts 7:55

55But he, being full of the Holy Spirit, looked up steadfastly into heaven and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God,

Arminian

Full of the Spirit, Stephen sees God’s glory and Jesus standing at God’s right hand. Arminian expositors see the faithful martyr given assurance at the end.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see Christ standing as witness and advocate for his servant.

Acts 7:56

56and said, “Behold, I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God!”

Arminian

“I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God.” Arminian readers note Stephen uses Jesus’ own title from Daniel 7.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the exalted Christ vindicating his servant before the court that condemned him.

Acts 7:57

57But they cried out with a loud voice and stopped their ears, then rushed at him with one accord.

Arminian

They shout, cover their ears, and rush at him. Arminian commentators see people refusing to hear.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see a willful refusal of truth, which Stephen had just described in verse 51.

Acts 7:58

58They threw him out of the city and stoned him. The witnesses placed their garments at the feet of a young man named Saul.

Arminian

They drag him out and stone him, and the witnesses lay their garments at Saul’s feet. Arminian readers note the first mention of Saul, who will later be transformed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God already present in the scene with the man he will call.

Acts 7:59

59They stoned Stephen as he called out, saying, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit!”

Arminian

Stephen prays, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit!” Arminian expositors see a dying prayer that mirrors Jesus’ own.

Reformed

Reformed expositors note that Stephen addresses his prayer to Jesus, a testimony to Christ’s deity.

Acts 7:60

60He kneeled down and cried with a loud voice, “Lord, don’t hold this sin against them!” When he had said this, he fell asleep.

Arminian

He kneels and prays, “Lord, don’t hold this sin against them,” and falls asleep. Arminian readers see forgiveness offered even to murderers, and many note that Saul was among those for whom Stephen prayed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see death described as sleep for believers, and they see the prayer answered in part in Saul’s later conversion.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
