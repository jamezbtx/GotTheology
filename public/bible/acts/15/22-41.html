Passage

Acts 15

Verses 22–41 · WEB

The apostles, elders, and whole church choose Judas Barsabbas and Silas to go with Paul and Barnabas to Antioch, carrying a letter that disowns those who troubled the Gentiles and states that it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to them to lay on them no burden beyond a few necessary things. The Antioch believers rejoice at the encouragement. Later Paul proposes revisiting the churches, but he and Barnabas disagree sharply over taking John Mark and part ways; Barnabas sails to Cyprus with Mark, while Paul takes Silas through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches.

Acts 15:22

22Then it seemed good to the apostles and the elders, with the whole assembly, to choose men out of their company, and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas: Judas called Barsabbas, and Silas, chief men among the brothers.

Arminian

The apostles, elders, and whole church choose Judas and Silas. Arminian readers see the whole community involved.

Reformed

Reformed readers see leaders and congregation acting together in good order.

Acts 15:23

23They wrote these things by their hand: “The apostles, the elders, and the brothers, to the brothers who are of the Gentiles in Antioch, Syria, and Cilicia: greetings.

Arminian

They write a letter to the Gentile believers in Antioch, Syria, and Cilicia. Arminian commentators see warm greetings across ethnic lines.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see an authoritative decision communicated in writing.

Acts 15:24

24Because we have heard that some who went out from us have troubled you with words, unsettling your souls, saying, ‘You must be circumcised and keep the law,’ to whom we gave no commandment;

Arminian

Some went out from us without authorization and troubled you. Arminian readers see the church distancing itself from false teachers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church guarding the purity of the gospel.

Acts 15:25

25it seemed good to us, having come to one accord, to choose out men and send them to you with our beloved Barnabas and Paul,

Arminian

They agreed with one accord to send chosen men with Barnabas and Paul. Arminian expositors see unity reached through discussion.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the Spirit producing agreement.

Acts 15:26

26men who have risked their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Arminian

These men risked their lives for the name of Jesus. Arminian readers see commendation of faithful servants.

Reformed

Reformed readers see suffering as a mark of genuine ministry.

Acts 15:27

27We have sent therefore Judas and Silas, who themselves will also tell you the same things by word of mouth.

Arminian

Judas and Silas will confirm the message in person. Arminian commentators see the value of personal presence.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see witnesses confirming the written word.

Acts 15:28

28For it seemed good to the Holy Spirit, and to us, to lay no greater burden on you than these necessary things:

Arminian

It seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to lay no greater burden on you. Arminian readers see the Spirit and the church working together.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit guiding the council’s decision.

Acts 15:29

29that you abstain from things sacrificed to idols, from blood, from things strangled, and from sexual immorality, from which if you keep yourselves, it will be well with you. Farewell.”

Arminian

They should abstain from idol sacrifices, blood, strangled things, and sexual immorality. Arminian expositors see guidance for holy living.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see these as expressions of love and purity rather than additions to justification.

Acts 15:30

30So, when they were sent off, they came to Antioch. Having gathered the multitude together, they delivered the letter.

Arminian

They deliver the letter in Antioch. Arminian readers see the matter settled.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church receiving the decision.

Acts 15:31

31When they had read it, they rejoiced over the encouragement.

Arminian

The believers rejoice at the encouragement. Arminian commentators see relief at being freed from the burden.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see joy at the gospel preserved.

Acts 15:32

32Judas and Silas, also being prophets themselves, encouraged the brothers with many words and strengthened them.

Arminian

Judas and Silas, being prophets, encourage and strengthen the brothers. Arminian readers see gifts used to build up the church.

Reformed

Reformed readers see prophecy serving edification.

Acts 15:33

33After they had spent some time there, they were dismissed in peace from the brothers to the apostles.

Arminian

They are sent back in peace. Arminian expositors see a warm farewell.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see fellowship restored.

Acts 15:35

35But Paul and Barnabas stayed in Antioch, teaching and preaching the word of the Lord, with many others also.

Arminian

Paul and Barnabas remain in Antioch, teaching and preaching with many others. Arminian readers see a church rich in teachers. The WEB omits verse 34, a later addition.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the ministry of the word at the center of church life. The WEB omits verse 34.

Acts 15:36

36After some days Paul said to Barnabas, “Let’s return now and visit our brothers in every city in which we proclaimed the word of the Lord, to see how they are doing.”

Arminian

Paul proposes visiting the churches again. Arminian commentators see concern for new believers’ perseverance.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see pastoral care following evangelism.

Acts 15:37

37Barnabas planned to take John, who was called Mark, with them also.

Arminian

Barnabas wants to take John Mark. Arminian readers see Barnabas giving Mark a second chance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the encourager’s instinct toward restoration.

Acts 15:38

38But Paul didn’t think that it was a good idea to take with them someone who had withdrawn from them in Pamphylia, and didn’t go with them to do the work.

Arminian

Paul does not think it wise to take one who had deserted them. Arminian expositors see Paul’s concern for reliability.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see both men acting from principle.

Acts 15:39

39Then the contention grew so sharp that they separated from each other. Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed away to Cyprus,

Arminian

The disagreement becomes sharp, and they separate. Arminian readers see that godly leaders can disagree strongly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God using even conflict to multiply the mission.

Acts 15:40

40but Paul chose Silas and went out, being commended by the brothers to the grace of God.

Arminian

Paul chooses Silas and is commended to God’s grace. Arminian commentators see a new partnership formed.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God’s grace sustaining the mission.

Acts 15:41

41He went through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the assemblies.

Arminian

He goes through Syria and Cilicia strengthening the churches. Arminian readers see follow-up as vital for perseverance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s means of establishing his people.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
