Passage

Acts 2

Verses 37–47 · WEB

Cut to the heart, the crowd asks what they should do. Peter calls them to repent and be baptized in Jesus’ name for the forgiveness of sins, promising the gift of the Spirit to them, their children, and all who are far off, as many as the Lord calls. About three thousand receive his word and are baptized. The new community devotes itself to the apostles’ teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread, and prayer; they share their goods, worship with gladness, and the Lord adds daily those who are being saved.

Acts 2:37

37Now when they heard this, they were cut to the heart, and said to Peter and the rest of the apostles, “Brothers, what shall we do?”

Arminian

The hearers are cut to the heart and ask what to do. Arminian readers see conviction as the Spirit’s work that leads people to a point of decision they must still answer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit’s conviction as the beginning of his effectual work in those God is drawing to himself.

Acts 2:38

38Peter said to them, “Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.

Arminian

“Repent and be baptized, every one of you.” Arminian readers stress that the command goes to everyone present and assumes that each person can respond. Arminians differ on baptism itself, with some practicing believer’s baptism and others infant baptism.

Reformed

Reformed readers see repentance as a duty for all and a gift God gives to his elect (Acts 11:18). Most Reformed churches practice infant baptism and read “for the forgiveness of sins” as sign and seal of forgiveness, while Reformed Baptists hold the same theology of grace and baptize only believers.

Acts 2:39

39For the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are far off, even as many as the Lord our God will call to himself.”

Arminian

The promise is to them, their children, and all who are far off. Arminian expositors see the promise extended as widely as possible, and they read the call of God as going out to all who hear.

Reformed

“As many as the Lord our God will call” stands at the end of the verse. Reformed expositors see here the effectual call that brings God’s people to faith, and paedobaptists add that the mention of children reflects the continuity of the covenant.

Acts 2:40

40With many other words he testified and exhorted them, saying, “Save yourselves from this crooked generation!”

Arminian

“Save yourselves from this crooked generation.” Arminian readers see a direct appeal to the hearers’ responsibility to act.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an urgent command addressed to all, which God uses to bring his people to faith. They note that the call to act and the grace that enables it come together.

Acts 2:41

41Then those who gladly received his word were baptized. There were added that day about three thousand souls.

Arminian

Those who gladly received his word were baptized, about three thousand. Arminian commentators see a free and joyful response to the gospel.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see a harvest that God gathered through his word, and they note that the receiving is described before the baptizing.

Acts 2:42

42They continued steadfastly in the apostles’ teaching and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and prayer.

Arminian

They devote themselves to teaching, fellowship, breaking bread, and prayer. Arminian readers see persevering faith expressed through steady practices.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the ordinary means of grace, word, sacrament, and prayer, sustaining the church from its first days.

Acts 2:43

43Fear came on every soul, and many wonders and signs were done through the apostles.

Arminian

Awe falls on everyone, and the apostles perform many wonders. Arminian expositors see God continuing to confirm the message.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see signs accompanying the apostolic foundation of the church.

Acts 2:44

44All who believed were together, and had all things in common.

Arminian

All who believed were together and held everything in common. Arminian readers see love expressed in practical generosity.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the unity created by the Spirit among those joined to Christ.

Acts 2:45

45They sold their possessions and goods, and distributed them to all, according as anyone had need.

Arminian

They sold possessions and distributed to anyone in need. Arminian commentators note that the sharing was voluntary, as Acts 5:4 confirms.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see generosity flowing from grace rather than imposed by law.

Acts 2:46

46Day by day, continuing steadfastly with one accord in the temple, and breaking bread at home, they took their food with gladness and singleness of heart,

Arminian

They met daily in the temple and broke bread in homes with glad and sincere hearts. Arminian readers see joy as the natural tone of the new life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see both public worship and household fellowship shaping the church’s life.

Acts 2:47

47praising God and having favor with all the people. The Lord added to the assembly day by day those who were being saved.

Arminian

They praised God, had favor with the people, and the Lord added daily those who were being saved. Arminian readers see the church’s witness drawing people to faith, with the Lord receiving those who respond.

Reformed

Reformed readers stress that it was the Lord who added to the church. Salvation is his work from beginning to end, carried out through the witness of his people.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
