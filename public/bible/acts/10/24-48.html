Passage

Acts 10

Verses 24–48 · WEB

Cornelius has gathered relatives and friends to hear Peter. Peter explains that God has shown him not to call anyone unclean, and Cornelius recounts his vision. Peter declares that God shows no partiality and that in every nation whoever fears him is acceptable. He proclaims Jesus, anointed with the Spirit, crucified, raised, and appointed judge, through whose name everyone who believes receives forgiveness. While he is still speaking, the Spirit falls on the Gentile hearers, who speak in tongues and praise God, and Peter orders them baptized.

Acts 10:24

24On the next day they entered into Caesarea. Cornelius was waiting for them, having called together his relatives and his near friends.

Arminian

Cornelius has gathered his relatives and close friends. Arminian readers see a man eager to share what God is doing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a household gathered to receive the word.

Acts 10:25

25When Peter entered, Cornelius met him, fell down at his feet, and worshiped him.

Arminian

Cornelius falls at Peter’s feet in worship. Arminian commentators see sincere but misplaced reverence.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the need to direct worship to God alone.

Acts 10:26

26But Peter raised him up, saying, “Stand up! I myself am also a man.”

Arminian

“Stand up! I myself am also a man.” Arminian readers see humility in Peter.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the boundary between creature and Creator.

Acts 10:27

27As he talked with him, he went in and found many gathered together.

Arminian

Peter finds many people gathered. Arminian expositors see a larger audience than expected.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God assembling hearers.

Acts 10:28

28He said to them, “You yourselves know how it is an unlawful thing for a man who is a Jew to join himself or come to one of another nation, but God has shown me that I shouldn’t call any man unholy or unclean.

Arminian

Peter says God has shown him not to call any person unclean. Arminian readers see the vision interpreted as about people, not only food.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the dividing wall of Ephesians 2:14 coming down.

Acts 10:29

29Therefore I also came without complaint when I was sent for. I ask therefore, why did you send for me?”

Arminian

He came without objection and asks why he was called. Arminian commentators see obedience overcoming reluctance.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see Peter following God’s lead.

Acts 10:30

30Cornelius said, “Four days ago, I was fasting until this hour; and at the ninth hour, I prayed in my house, and behold, a man stood before me in bright clothing

Arminian

Cornelius recounts his vision four days earlier. Arminian readers see his testimony matching Peter’s.

Reformed

Reformed readers see two visions coordinated by one God.

Acts 10:31

31and said, ‘Cornelius, your prayer is heard, and your gifts to the needy are remembered in the sight of God.

Arminian

His prayer was heard and his alms remembered. Arminian expositors see God valuing his seeking heart.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God remembering the work he began in Cornelius.

Acts 10:32

32Send therefore to Joppa and summon Simon, who is also called Peter. He is staying in the house of a tanner named Simon, by the seaside. When he comes, he will speak to you.’

Arminian

He was told to send for Peter. Arminian readers see the message he needs coming through a person.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God binding salvation to the hearing of the gospel.

Acts 10:33

33Therefore I sent to you at once, and it was good of you to come. Now therefore we are all here present in the sight of God to hear all things that have been commanded you by God.”

Arminian

They are all present before God to hear what the Lord commanded. Arminian commentators see a ready audience.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see a congregation gathered before God, not merely before a preacher.

Acts 10:34

34Peter opened his mouth and said, “Truly I perceive that God doesn’t show favoritism;

Arminian

God shows no partiality. Arminian readers see God’s impartial love for all people, without favoritism toward any group.

Reformed

Reformed readers see impartiality regarding nationality and status, which they hold is fully consistent with God’s free election of individuals.

Acts 10:35

35but in every nation he who fears him and works righteousness is acceptable to him.

Arminian

In every nation whoever fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him. Arminian expositors see God receiving any person who responds to the light they have.

Reformed

Reformed expositors read this as acceptable for hearing the gospel, since Cornelius still needed Christ to be saved (11:14).

Acts 10:36

36The word which he sent to the children of Israel, preaching good news of peace by Jesus Christ—he is Lord of all—

Arminian

God sent the word of peace through Jesus Christ, who is Lord of all. Arminian readers see peace offered universally.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s lordship over Jew and Gentile alike.

Acts 10:37

37you yourselves know what happened, which was proclaimed throughout all Judea, beginning from Galilee, after the baptism which John preached;

Arminian

They know what happened throughout Judea after John’s baptism. Arminian commentators see Peter starting from shared knowledge.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the gospel rooted in public history.

Acts 10:38

38how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power, who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with him.

Arminian

God anointed Jesus with the Spirit and power, and he healed those oppressed by the devil. Arminian readers see Jesus’ compassion for the afflicted.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Father anointing the Son by the Spirit for his messianic work.

Acts 10:39

39We are witnesses of everything he did both in the country of the Jews and in Jerusalem; whom they also killed, hanging him on a tree.

Arminian

The apostles witnessed his deeds, and they killed him by hanging him on a tree. Arminian expositors see testimony grounded in what they saw.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the curse of the law borne by Christ.

Acts 10:40

40God raised him up the third day and gave him to be revealed,

Arminian

God raised him on the third day and made him appear. Arminian readers see the resurrection as the heart of the message.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Father vindicating the Son.

Acts 10:41

41not to all the people, but to witnesses who were chosen before by God, to us, who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead.

Arminian

He appeared not to all people but to witnesses chosen beforehand by God. Arminian commentators see God selecting witnesses for a specific role.

Reformed

Reformed commentators note the phrase “chosen before by God” and see God’s sovereign selection at work in revelation.

Acts 10:42

42He commanded us to preach to the people and to testify that this is he who is appointed by God as the Judge of the living and the dead.

Arminian

He commanded them to testify that Jesus is appointed judge of living and dead. Arminian readers see accountability that makes the offer urgent.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ appointed by God to judge, a sign of his authority.

Acts 10:43

43All the prophets testify about him, that through his name everyone who believes in him will receive remission of sins.”

Arminian

Everyone who believes in him receives forgiveness of sins through his name. Arminian expositors see the universal promise: everyone who believes.

Reformed

Reformed expositors fully affirm the promise and see faith as the God-given means of receiving forgiveness.

Acts 10:44

44While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell on all those who heard the word.

Arminian

While Peter is still speaking, the Spirit falls on all who hear. Arminian readers see hearing and believing met immediately by God’s gift.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God acting before anyone could lay on hands or administer baptism, showing salvation is his work.

Acts 10:45

45They of the circumcision who believed were amazed, as many as came with Peter, because the gift of the Holy Spirit was also poured out on the Gentiles.

Arminian

The Jewish believers are amazed that the Gentiles receive the gift. Arminian commentators see prejudice confronted by God’s action.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God’s grace overriding human expectations.

Acts 10:46

46For they heard them speaking in other languages and magnifying God. Then Peter answered,

Arminian

They hear them speaking in tongues and praising God. Arminian readers see the same sign as Pentecost given to Gentiles.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a Gentile Pentecost marking their full inclusion.

Acts 10:47

47“Can anyone forbid these people from being baptized with water? They have received the Holy Spirit just like us.”

Arminian

Who can forbid water for baptism to those who have received the Spirit? Arminian expositors see the inward gift followed by the outward sign.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see baptism as the sign of what God has already done.

Acts 10:48

48He commanded them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. Then they asked him to stay some days.

Arminian

He commands them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. Arminian readers see the new believers welcomed into the church.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a household baptism, which paedobaptists cite alongside 16:15 and 16:33.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
