Passage

Acts 17

Verses 1–15 · WEB

In Thessalonica Paul reasons from the Scriptures for three Sabbaths that the Christ had to suffer and rise, and some Jews and many God-fearing Greeks and leading women are persuaded. Jealous opponents form a mob, attack Jason’s house, and accuse the believers of turning the world upside down and proclaiming another king, Jesus. Sent to Berea, Paul finds Jews more noble-minded, who receive the word eagerly and examine the Scriptures daily, and many believe. When agitators arrive from Thessalonica, Paul is sent on to Athens.

Acts 17:1

1Now when they had passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where there was a Jewish synagogue.

Arminian

They come to Thessalonica, where there is a synagogue. Arminian readers see Paul seeking out those who know the Scriptures.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the pattern of preaching to the Jew first.

Acts 17:2

2Paul, as was his custom, went in to them; and for three Sabbath days reasoned with them from the Scriptures,

Arminian

For three Sabbaths Paul reasons with them from the Scriptures. Arminian commentators see persuasion addressed to the mind.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see Scripture as the ground of all gospel preaching.

Acts 17:3

3explaining and demonstrating that the Christ had to suffer and rise again from the dead, and saying, “This Jesus, whom I proclaim to you, is the Christ.”

Arminian

He explains that the Christ had to suffer and rise and that Jesus is the Christ. Arminian readers see reasoned proof offered for faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the necessity of the cross rooted in God’s plan.

Acts 17:4

4Some of them were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas: of the devout Greeks a great multitude, and not a few of the chief women.

Arminian

Some are persuaded and join Paul and Silas. Arminian expositors see persuasion leading to free decision.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God using persuasion as a means to bring his people to faith.

Acts 17:5

5But the unpersuaded Jews took along some wicked men from the marketplace and gathering a crowd, set the city in an uproar. Assaulting the house of Jason, they sought to bring them out to the people.

Arminian

Unpersuaded Jews gather a mob and attack Jason’s house. Arminian readers see jealousy turning violent.

Reformed

Reformed readers see opposition following the word as before.

Acts 17:6

6When they didn’t find them, they dragged Jason and certain brothers before the rulers of the city, crying, “These who have turned the world upside down have come here also,

Arminian

They drag Jason before the rulers, saying these men have turned the world upside down. Arminian commentators see an unintended compliment.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the gospel’s power to overturn the world’s order.

Acts 17:7

7whom Jason has received. These all act contrary to the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, Jesus!”

Arminian

They claim the believers say there is another king, Jesus. Arminian readers see the political edge of confessing Christ as Lord.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s kingship over every earthly ruler.

Acts 17:8

8The multitude and the rulers of the city were troubled when they heard these things.

Arminian

The crowd and rulers are disturbed. Arminian expositors see the gospel unsettling a city.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God’s kingdom provoking the kingdoms of this world.

Acts 17:9

9When they had taken security from Jason and the rest, they let them go.

Arminian

They take security from Jason and release them. Arminian readers see costly hospitality.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers sharing in the suffering of the messengers.

Acts 17:10

10The brothers immediately sent Paul and Silas away by night to Beroea. When they arrived, they went into the Jewish synagogue.

Arminian

The brothers send Paul and Silas to Berea by night. Arminian commentators see prudence protecting the mission.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see providence opening a new door.

Acts 17:11

11Now these were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, examining the Scriptures daily to see whether these things were so.

Arminian

The Bereans receive the word eagerly and examine the Scriptures daily. Arminian readers see open-minded inquiry as a model response.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Scripture as the test of all teaching, even an apostle’s.

Acts 17:12

12Many of them therefore believed; also of the prominent Greek women, and not a few men.

Arminian

Many of them believe, including prominent Greek women and men. Arminian expositors see careful study leading to faith.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God blessing his word to many hearers.

Acts 17:13

13But when the Jews of Thessalonica had knowledge that the word of God was proclaimed by Paul at Beroea also, they came there likewise, agitating the multitudes.

Arminian

Thessalonian Jews come to stir up the crowds in Berea. Arminian readers see opposition following the mission.

Reformed

Reformed readers see persistent hostility that cannot stop the gospel.

Acts 17:14

14Then the brothers immediately sent out Paul to go as far as to the sea, and Silas and Timothy still stayed there.

Arminian

The brothers send Paul to the coast while Silas and Timothy remain. Arminian commentators see the team adapting.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God preserving both the messenger and the new church.

Acts 17:15

15But those who escorted Paul brought him as far as Athens. Receiving a commandment to Silas and Timothy that they should come to him very quickly, they departed.

Arminian

Paul is escorted to Athens and sends for Silas and Timothy. Arminian readers see Paul alone in a new city.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God leading Paul to the intellectual center of the Greek world.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
