Passage

Acts 11

Verses 19–30 · WEB

Believers scattered after Stephen’s death preach as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, at first only to Jews, but some from Cyprus and Cyrene speak to Greeks, and a great number believe. The Jerusalem church sends Barnabas, who rejoices at God’s grace and encourages them, and many more are added. Barnabas brings Saul from Tarsus, and they teach in Antioch for a year, where the disciples are first called Christians. When Agabus predicts a famine, the disciples send relief to Judea through Barnabas and Saul.

Acts 11:19

19They therefore who were scattered abroad by the oppression that arose about Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, speaking the word to no one except to Jews only.

Arminian

Scattered believers preach as far as Antioch, but only to Jews. Arminian readers see faithful witness within familiar limits.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God spreading the word through persecution.

Acts 11:20

20But there were some of them, men of Cyprus and Cyrene, who, when they had come to Antioch, spoke to the Hellenists, preaching the Lord Jesus.

Arminian

Some from Cyprus and Cyrene speak to Greeks about the Lord Jesus. Arminian commentators see unnamed believers taking a bold step.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God using ordinary believers to open new fields.

Acts 11:21

21The hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number believed and turned to the Lord.

Arminian

The hand of the Lord is with them, and many believe and turn to the Lord. Arminian readers see God blessing faithful preaching while people believe and turn.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lord’s hand as the cause of the many conversions.

Acts 11:22

22The report concerning them came to the ears of the assembly which was in Jerusalem. They sent out Barnabas to go as far as Antioch,

Arminian

The Jerusalem church sends Barnabas. Arminian expositors see care for new believers.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the unity of the church.

Acts 11:23

23who, when he had come, and had seen the grace of God, was glad. He exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they should remain near to the Lord.

Arminian

Barnabas sees the grace of God, rejoices, and urges them to remain with the Lord. Arminian readers see the exhortation to continue as necessary for perseverance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace visible in the believers’ lives and see exhortation as a means God uses to keep them.

Acts 11:24

24For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Spirit and of faith, and many people were added to the Lord.

Arminian

He was a good man, full of the Spirit and faith, and many were added. Arminian commentators see character shaping ministry.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the Spirit at work through a godly servant.

Acts 11:25

25Barnabas went out to Tarsus to look for Saul.

Arminian

Barnabas goes to Tarsus to find Saul. Arminian readers see encouragement extended to Saul again.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God bringing Saul into his mission at the appointed time.

Acts 11:26

26When he had found him, he brought him to Antioch. For a whole year they were gathered together with the assembly, and taught many people. The disciples were first called Christians in Antioch.

Arminian

They teach for a year, and the disciples are first called Christians in Antioch. Arminian expositors see a name linking believers to Christ.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the church identified by its Lord.

Acts 11:27

27Now in these days, prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch.

Arminian

Prophets come from Jerusalem to Antioch. Arminian readers see ongoing ministry of the Spirit.

Reformed

Reformed readers see prophecy serving the church in the apostolic age.

Acts 11:28

28One of them named Agabus stood up and indicated by the Spirit that there should be a great famine all over the world, which also happened in the days of Claudius.

Arminian

Agabus predicts a famine, which happens under Claudius. Arminian commentators see prophecy confirmed by history.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God warning his people in advance.

Acts 11:29

29As any of the disciples had plenty, each determined to send relief to the brothers who lived in Judea;

Arminian

Each disciple decides to send relief according to ability. Arminian readers see voluntary generosity.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace producing generosity across ethnic lines.

Acts 11:30

30which they also did, sending it to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul.

Arminian

They send it to the elders through Barnabas and Saul. Arminian expositors note the first mention of elders in the church.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see elders as an established office, important for church order.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
