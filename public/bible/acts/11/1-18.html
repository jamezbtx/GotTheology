Passage

Acts 11

Verses 1–18 · WEB

News reaches Judea that Gentiles have received God’s word, and circumcised believers criticize Peter for eating with uncircumcised men. Peter explains step by step: the vision, the Spirit’s instruction, the angel’s message to Cornelius, and the Spirit falling on the Gentiles as on the apostles at the beginning. If God gave them the same gift, who was Peter to stand in God’s way? The critics fall silent and glorify God, concluding that God has granted the Gentiles repentance to life.

Acts 11:1

1Now the apostles and the brothers who were in Judea heard that the Gentiles had also received the word of God.

Arminian

The apostles and brothers hear that Gentiles received the word. Arminian readers see news spreading quickly through the church.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a turning point in the history of redemption.

Acts 11:2

2When Peter had come up to Jerusalem, those who were of the circumcision contended with him,

Arminian

The circumcision party criticizes Peter. Arminian commentators see sincere believers struggling with change.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the church needing to submit its traditions to God’s revelation.

Acts 11:3

3saying, “You went in to uncircumcised men and ate with them!”

Arminian

“You went in to uncircumcised men and ate with them!” Arminian readers see table fellowship as the heart of the dispute.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the issue of whether Gentiles must become Jews to belong to God.

Acts 11:4

4But Peter began, and explained to them in order, saying,

Arminian

Peter explains in order. Arminian expositors see patient explanation rather than authority alone.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the church persuaded by God’s acts and word.

Acts 11:5

5“I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision: a certain container descending, like it was a great sheet let down from heaven by four corners. It came as far as me.

Arminian

He recounts the vision of the sheet. Arminian readers see personal testimony as persuasive.

Reformed

Reformed readers see revelation as the basis of the decision.

Acts 11:6

6When I had looked intently at it, I considered, and saw the four-footed animals of the earth, wild animals, creeping things, and birds of the sky.

Arminian

He saw all kinds of animals. Arminian commentators see the vision retold faithfully.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see repetition underscoring importance.

Acts 11:7

7I also heard a voice saying to me, ‘Rise, Peter, kill and eat!’

Arminian

He heard, “Rise, Peter, kill and eat!” Arminian readers see Peter admitting his initial resistance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s command preceding understanding.

Acts 11:8

8But I said, ‘Not so, Lord, for nothing unholy or unclean has ever entered into my mouth.’

Arminian

He refused because nothing unclean had entered his mouth. Arminian expositors see Peter identifying with his critics.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see a sincere conscience corrected by God.

Acts 11:9

9But a voice answered me the second time out of heaven, ‘What God has cleansed, don’t you call unclean.’

Arminian

“What God has cleansed, don’t call unclean.” Arminian readers see the decisive word.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s declaration settling the matter.

Acts 11:10

10This was done three times, and all were drawn up again into heaven.

Arminian

It happened three times. Arminian commentators see certainty in repetition.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God confirming his word.

Acts 11:11

11Behold, immediately three men stood before the house where I was, having been sent from Caesarea to me.

Arminian

Three men arrived at that moment. Arminian readers see timing that points to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see providence coordinating events.

Acts 11:12

12The Spirit told me to go with them without discriminating. These six brothers also accompanied me, and we entered into the man’s house.

Arminian

The Spirit told him to go without discriminating, and six brothers came along. Arminian expositors see witnesses who can confirm the story.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the Spirit removing distinctions.

Acts 11:13

13He told us how he had seen the angel standing in his house and saying to him, ‘Send to Joppa and get Simon, who is called Peter,

Arminian

Cornelius had seen an angel telling him to send for Peter. Arminian readers see God’s work on both sides.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God preparing hearer and preacher.

Acts 11:14

14who will speak to you words by which you will be saved, you and all your house.’

Arminian

Peter would speak words by which he and his household would be saved. Arminian commentators see that even a devout seeker needed the gospel to be saved.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see salvation coming through the word preached, and paedobaptists note the household language.

Acts 11:15

15As I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell on them, even as on us at the beginning.

Arminian

The Spirit fell on them as on the apostles at the beginning. Arminian readers see equal treatment of Gentiles.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same gift showing they belong to the same people.

Acts 11:16

16I remembered the word of the Lord, how he said, ‘John indeed baptized in water, but you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit.’

Arminian

Peter remembered Jesus’ word about baptism in the Spirit. Arminian expositors see Scripture recalled to interpret experience.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the promise of 1:5 extended to Gentiles.

Acts 11:17

17If then God gave to them the same gift as us when we believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I, that I could withstand God?”

Arminian

If God gave them the same gift, who was Peter to hinder God? Arminian readers see God accepting those who believed, and Peter refusing to oppose him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereign action that the church must recognize.

Acts 11:18

18When they heard these things, they held their peace and glorified God, saying, “Then God has also granted to the Gentiles repentance to life!”

Arminian

They glorify God: he has granted the Gentiles repentance unto life. Arminian readers see God opening the way of repentance to the Gentiles as a people, making it possible for them to turn.

Reformed

Reformed readers see repentance described as God’s gift, granted to those he brings to life, a key text for grace preceding human turning.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
