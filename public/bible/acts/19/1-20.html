Passage

Acts 19

Verses 1–20 · WEB

In Ephesus Paul finds about twelve disciples who know only John’s baptism and have not heard of the Holy Spirit. He explains that John pointed to Jesus; they are baptized in Jesus’ name, and when Paul lays hands on them, the Spirit comes and they speak in tongues and prophesy. Paul preaches in the synagogue for three months, then teaches daily in the hall of Tyrannus for two years, so that all Asia hears the word. God does extraordinary miracles through him. When the seven sons of Sceva try to use Jesus’ name as a formula, an evil spirit overpowers them, and many believers confess their practices and burn their magic books. The word of the Lord grows mightily.

Acts 19:1

1While Apollos was at Corinth, Paul, having passed through the upper country, came to Ephesus and found certain disciples.

Arminian

Paul finds disciples in Ephesus. Arminian readers see believers whose knowledge is incomplete.

Reformed

Reformed readers see followers of John who have not yet heard the full gospel.

Acts 19:2

2He said to them, “Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?” They said to him, “No, we haven’t even heard that there is a Holy Spirit.”

Arminian

“Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?” They had not heard of the Spirit. Wesleyan and Pentecostal readers often see a subsequent experience implied; other Arminians see incomplete conversion.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that these men had not yet believed in Jesus as the Christ, so their case is not a pattern of two-stage Christian experience.

Acts 19:3

3He said, “Into what then were you baptized?” They said, “Into John’s baptism.”

Arminian

They were baptized into John’s baptism. Arminian commentators see the gap explained.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see John’s baptism as preparatory.

Acts 19:4

4Paul said, “John indeed baptized with the baptism of repentance, saying to the people that they should believe in the one who would come after him, that is, in Christ Jesus.”

Arminian

John’s baptism was repentance pointing to the one coming after him, Jesus. Arminian readers see repentance leading to faith in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see John pointing beyond himself.

Acts 19:5

5When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.

Arminian

They are baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. Arminian expositors see faith expressed in Christian baptism.

Reformed

Reformed expositors note this is not rebaptism of Christians but the first Christian baptism of John’s followers.

Acts 19:6

6When Paul had laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came on them and they spoke with other languages and prophesied.

Arminian

Paul lays hands on them, the Spirit comes, and they speak in tongues and prophesy. Arminian readers see the Spirit confirming their faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see another moment, like Samaria and Caesarea, marking a new group joined to the church.

Acts 19:7

7They were about twelve men in all.

Arminian

There are about twelve men. Arminian commentators see a small beginning.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see a nucleus for the Ephesian church.

Acts 19:8

8He entered into the synagogue and spoke boldly for a period of three months, reasoning and persuading about the things concerning God’s Kingdom.

Arminian

Paul speaks boldly in the synagogue for three months about the kingdom of God. Arminian readers see persuasion over time.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the kingdom at the heart of his preaching.

Acts 19:9

9But when some were hardened and disobedient, speaking evil of the Way before the multitude, he departed from them and separated the disciples, reasoning daily in the school of Tyrannus.

Arminian

When some become hardened and speak evil of the Way, he moves to the hall of Tyrannus. Arminian expositors see people hardening themselves through persistent refusal.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see hardening as both human refusal and God’s judicial response.

Acts 19:10

10This continued for two years, so that all those who lived in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks.

Arminian

This continues for two years, and all Asia hears the word. Arminian readers see the gospel offered to an entire region.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the word spreading through a central teaching ministry.

Acts 19:11

11God worked special miracles by the hands of Paul,

Arminian

God does extraordinary miracles by Paul’s hands. Arminian commentators see God confirming the message.

Reformed

Reformed commentators note that God is the one working.

Acts 19:12

12so that even handkerchiefs or aprons were carried away from his body to the sick, and the diseases departed from them, and the evil spirits went out.

Arminian

Even cloths from his body heal the sick. Arminian readers see God meeting people at their level of understanding.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an unusual measure of power that does not set a pattern for relics.

Acts 19:13

13But some of the itinerant Jews, exorcists, took on themselves to invoke over those who had the evil spirits the name of the Lord Jesus, saying, “We adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preaches.”

Arminian

Itinerant Jewish exorcists try to use the name of Jesus whom Paul preaches. Arminian expositors see people using Jesus’ name without knowing him.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the name treated as a magic formula.

Acts 19:14

14There were seven sons of one Sceva, a Jewish chief priest, who did this.

Arminian

Seven sons of Sceva, a chief priest, try it. Arminian readers see religious pedigree without faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see outward status without inward grace.

Acts 19:15

15The evil spirit answered, “Jesus I know, and Paul I know, but who are you?”

Arminian

The spirit says, “Jesus I know, and Paul I know, but who are you?” Arminian commentators see that only a personal relationship with Christ carries authority.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the powers of darkness recognizing Christ’s true servants.

Acts 19:16

16The man in whom the evil spirit was leaped on them, overpowered them, and prevailed against them, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded.

Arminian

The man overpowers them, and they flee naked and wounded. Arminian readers see the danger of trifling with spiritual power.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God exposing counterfeits.

Acts 19:17

17This became known to all, both Jews and Greeks, who lived at Ephesus. Fear fell on them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was magnified.

Arminian

Fear falls on all in Ephesus, and Jesus’ name is magnified. Arminian expositors see reverence growing.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God glorifying his Son.

Acts 19:18

18Many also of those who had believed came, confessing and declaring their deeds.

Arminian

Many believers come confessing their practices. Arminian readers see repentance continuing after conversion.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sanctification uncovering hidden sins.

Acts 19:19

19Many of those who practiced magical arts brought their books together and burned them in the sight of all. They counted their price, and found it to be fifty thousand pieces of silver.

Arminian

Practitioners of magic burn books worth fifty thousand pieces of silver. Arminian commentators see costly repentance.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the gospel breaking idolatry’s hold.

Acts 19:20

20So the word of the Lord was growing and becoming mighty.

Arminian

The word of the Lord grows mightily and prevails. Arminian readers see the gospel advancing against opposition.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the word as the agent of the kingdom.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
