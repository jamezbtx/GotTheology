Passage

Acts 9

Verses 20–31 · WEB

Saul immediately proclaims in the synagogues that Jesus is the Son of God, astonishing those who knew why he came. He grows stronger and confounds the Jews of Damascus, who plot to kill him, but the disciples lower him over the wall in a basket. In Jerusalem the disciples fear him until Barnabas vouches for him. He speaks boldly and debates the Hellenists, who also try to kill him, so the believers send him to Tarsus. The church throughout Judea, Galilee, and Samaria has peace and multiplies.

Acts 9:20

20Immediately in the synagogues he proclaimed the Christ, that he is the Son of God.

Arminian

Saul immediately proclaims Jesus as the Son of God. Arminian readers see a complete reversal expressed in bold witness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the new heart producing new speech.

Acts 9:21

21All who heard him were amazed, and said, “Isn’t this he who in Jerusalem made havoc of those who called on this name? And he had come here intending to bring them bound before the chief priests!”

Arminian

All who hear are amazed that the persecutor now preaches. Arminian commentators see a testimony that cannot be explained apart from God.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see grace displayed in the most unlikely convert.

Acts 9:22

22But Saul increased more in strength, and confounded the Jews who lived at Damascus, proving that this is the Christ.

Arminian

Saul grows stronger and confounds the Jews by proving Jesus is the Christ. Arminian readers see Saul using his learning in service of the gospel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God turning Saul’s training toward his purposes.

Acts 9:23

23When many days were fulfilled, the Jews conspired together to kill him,

Arminian

After many days the Jews plot to kill him. Arminian expositors see the persecutor becoming the persecuted.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the suffering foretold in 9:16 beginning.

Acts 9:24

24but their plot became known to Saul. They watched the gates both day and night that they might kill him,

Arminian

The plot becomes known, and they watch the gates day and night. Arminian readers see God giving warning.

Reformed

Reformed readers see providence protecting his servant.

Acts 9:25

25but his disciples took him by night and let him down through the wall, lowering him in a basket.

Arminian

His disciples lower him through the wall in a basket. Arminian commentators note the humble escape, which Paul recalls in 2 Corinthians 11:33.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God preserving Saul through ordinary means.

Acts 9:26

26When Saul had come to Jerusalem, he tried to join himself to the disciples; but they were all afraid of him, not believing that he was a disciple.

Arminian

In Jerusalem the disciples are afraid and doubt his conversion. Arminian readers see understandable caution.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that genuine conversion sometimes needs time and witness to be recognized.

Acts 9:27

27But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles, and declared to them how he had seen the Lord on the way, and that he had spoken to him, and how at Damascus he had preached boldly in the name of Jesus.

Arminian

Barnabas brings him to the apostles and tells his story. Arminian expositors see Barnabas living up to his name as an encourager.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God using one believer to vouch for another.

Acts 9:28

28He was with them entering into Jerusalem,

Arminian

Saul moves freely with them in Jerusalem. Arminian readers see him accepted into fellowship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church receiving him as one of its own.

Acts 9:29

29preaching boldly in the name of the Lord Jesus. He spoke and disputed against the Hellenists, but they were seeking to kill him.

Arminian

He preaches boldly and debates the Hellenists, who seek to kill him. Arminian commentators see Saul engaging the same group that opposed Stephen.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see Saul taking up Stephen’s work.

Acts 9:30

30When the brothers knew it, they brought him down to Caesarea and sent him off to Tarsus.

Arminian

The brothers take him to Caesarea and send him to Tarsus. Arminian readers see the community protecting him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a season of preparation before his wider mission.

Acts 9:31

31So the assemblies throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and were built up. They were multiplied, walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit.

Arminian

The church has peace, is built up, walks in the fear of the Lord and the comfort of the Spirit, and multiplies. Arminian expositors see growth that follows faithful living.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the Spirit’s comfort and the fear of God sustaining and enlarging the church.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
