Passage

Acts 9

Verses 32–43 · WEB

Peter travels among the believers and comes to Lydda, where he heals Aeneas, bedridden for eight years, in the name of Jesus Christ, and the residents of Lydda and Sharon turn to the Lord. In nearby Joppa the disciple Tabitha, known for good works and charity, dies. The believers send for Peter, who prays and tells her to rise, and she comes back to life. Many in Joppa believe, and Peter stays there with Simon the tanner.

Acts 9:32

32As Peter went throughout all those parts, he came down also to the saints who lived at Lydda.

Arminian

Peter travels among the saints and comes to Lydda. Arminian readers see apostolic care for scattered believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that believers are called saints, set apart by God.

Acts 9:33

33There he found a certain man named Aeneas, who had been bedridden for eight years because he was paralyzed.

Arminian

He finds Aeneas, paralyzed and bedridden for eight years. Arminian commentators see long suffering about to be relieved.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see human helplessness awaiting God’s power.

Acts 9:34

34Peter said to him, “Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you. Get up and make your bed!” Immediately he arose.

Arminian

“Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you.” He gets up immediately. Arminian readers see Peter pointing away from himself to Jesus.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ himself as the one who heals.

Acts 9:35

35All who lived at Lydda and in Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord.

Arminian

All in Lydda and Sharon see him and turn to the Lord. Arminian expositors see a sign prompting many to respond.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God using the miracle to bring people to himself.

Acts 9:36

36Now there was at Joppa a certain disciple named Tabitha, which when translated means Dorcas. This woman was full of good works and acts of mercy which she did.

Arminian

Tabitha, or Dorcas, is full of good works and acts of charity. Arminian readers see faith expressed in practical love.

Reformed

Reformed readers see good works as the fruit of grace in a disciple.

Acts 9:37

37In those days, she became sick and died. When they had washed her, they laid her in an upper room.

Arminian

She becomes sick and dies, and they lay her in an upper room. Arminian commentators note the community’s grief.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see death touching even the most faithful.

Acts 9:38

38As Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples, hearing that Peter was there, sent two men to him, imploring him not to delay in coming to them.

Arminian

The disciples send for Peter. Arminian readers see faith that hopes for more than comfort.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers turning to God through his appointed servant.

Acts 9:39

39Peter got up and went with them. When he had come, they brought him into the upper room. All the widows stood by him weeping, and showing the tunics and other garments which Dorcas had made while she was with them.

Arminian

Widows show Peter the garments Dorcas made. Arminian expositors see a life remembered by the people it served.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the visible fruit of a hidden faith.

Acts 9:40

40Peter sent them all out, and knelt down and prayed. Turning to the body, he said, “Tabitha, get up!” She opened her eyes, and when she saw Peter, she sat up.

Arminian

Peter sends them out, prays, and says, “Tabitha, get up!” and she opens her eyes. Arminian readers note the echo of Jesus raising Jairus’s daughter.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that Peter prays first, recognizing the power belongs to God.

Acts 9:41

41He gave her his hand and raised her up. Calling the saints and widows, he presented her alive.

Arminian

He helps her up and presents her alive to the saints and widows. Arminian commentators see joy restored to the community.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see a foretaste of the resurrection.

Acts 9:42

42This became known throughout all Joppa, and many believed in the Lord.

Arminian

This becomes known throughout Joppa, and many believe. Arminian readers see the sign leading people to faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God gathering his people through his works and word.

Acts 9:43

43He stayed many days in Joppa with a tanner named Simon.

Arminian

Peter stays many days with Simon the tanner. Arminian expositors note that tanners were considered unclean, a hint of barriers about to fall.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God positioning Peter for the vision of chapter 10.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
