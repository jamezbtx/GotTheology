Passage

Acts 3

Verses 11–26 · WEB

As a crowd gathers in Solomon’s Porch, Peter denies that the healing came from his own power or piety. The God of Abraham has glorified his servant Jesus, whom the people denied and killed, but whom God raised. Faith in Jesus’ name has made the man whole. Peter grants that they acted in ignorance and that God fulfilled the prophets’ word that the Christ would suffer, then calls them to repent and turn so their sins may be blotted out. Jesus is the prophet Moses promised, and God sent him first to Israel to bless them by turning each one from wickedness.

Acts 3:11

11As the lame man who was healed held on to Peter and John, all the people ran together to them in the porch that is called Solomon’s, greatly wondering.

Arminian

The healed man clings to Peter and John, and the people run to Solomon’s Porch. Arminian readers see gratitude and curiosity drawing a crowd.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the setting prepared for the second great sermon of Acts.

Acts 3:12

12When Peter saw it, he responded to the people, “You men of Israel, why do you marvel at this man? Why do you fasten your eyes on us, as though by our own power or godliness we had made him walk?

Arminian

Peter asks why they stare as if the apostles had done this by their own power or godliness. Arminian commentators see Peter refusing credit that belongs to Christ.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see a firm rejection of any human merit in the work of God.

Acts 3:13

13The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified his Servant Jesus, whom you delivered up and denied in the presence of Pilate, when he had determined to release him.

Arminian

The God of the patriarchs has glorified his Servant Jesus, whom they handed over and denied before Pilate. Arminian readers note that Peter names their choice plainly, even though Pilate wanted to release Jesus.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Servant of Isaiah 52–53 glorified by God after being rejected by his people.

Acts 3:14

14But you denied the Holy and Righteous One and asked for a murderer to be granted to you,

Arminian

They denied the Holy and Righteous One and asked for a murderer. Arminian expositors see the full weight of human responsibility placed on the hearers.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the exchange of Barabbas for Jesus as a picture of substitution, the guilty released while the righteous one dies.

Acts 3:15

15and killed the Prince of life, whom God raised from the dead, to which we are witnesses.

Arminian

They killed the Prince of life, whom God raised. Arminian readers see the irony of killing the one who is the source of life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God overturning the human verdict and confirming the Son through the resurrection.

Acts 3:16

16By faith in his name, his name has made this man strong, whom you see and know. Yes, the faith which is through him has given him this perfect soundness in the presence of you all.

Arminian

Faith in Jesus’ name has made this man strong, and that faith comes through him. Arminian interpreters see faith as the channel through which Christ’s power is received.

Reformed

The phrase “the faith which is through him” leads Reformed interpreters to see faith itself as given by Christ, not produced by the one who believes.

Acts 3:17

17“Now, brothers, I know that you did this in ignorance, as did also your rulers.

Arminian

Peter grants that they acted in ignorance, as did their rulers. Arminian readers see God making room for repentance by not treating them as beyond hope.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that ignorance lessens but does not remove guilt, which is why Peter still calls them to repent.

Acts 3:18

18But the things which God announced by the mouth of all his prophets, that Christ should suffer, he thus fulfilled.

Arminian

God fulfilled what the prophets foretold, that Christ would suffer. Arminian commentators see God’s plan accomplished through the free acts of those who rejected Jesus.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the suffering of Christ as fixed in God’s purpose and announced long in advance.

Acts 3:19

19“Repent therefore, and turn again, that your sins may be blotted out, so that there may come times of refreshing from the presence of the Lord,

Arminian

“Repent therefore, and turn again, that your sins may be blotted out.” Arminian expositors see repentance as the hearers’ response, which God answers with forgiveness and refreshing.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the command addressed to all, while repentance itself is granted by God to those he draws, as verse 26 suggests.

Acts 3:20

20and that he may send Christ Jesus, who was ordained for you before,

Arminian

Repentance leads to times of refreshing and the sending of Christ, appointed for them. Arminian readers see a hope that Israel’s turning would bring blessing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ appointed beforehand as Israel’s Messiah, sent in God’s time.

Acts 3:21

21whom heaven must receive until the times of restoration of all things, which God spoke long ago by the mouth of his holy prophets.

Arminian

Heaven must receive Jesus until the restoration of all things. Arminian commentators see Christ’s present reign in heaven continuing until the renewal of creation.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the word “must” as pointing to God’s plan for the interval between Christ’s ascension and return.

Acts 3:22

22For Moses indeed said to the fathers, ‘The Lord God will raise up a prophet for you from among your brothers, like me. You shall listen to him in all things whatever he says to you.

Arminian

Moses promised that God would raise up a prophet like him. Arminian interpreters see Jesus as that prophet, whose word must be heard.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see Christ fulfilling the prophetic office, one of his three offices as prophet, priest, and king.

Acts 3:23

23It will be that every soul that will not listen to that prophet will be utterly destroyed from among the people.’

Arminian

Everyone who will not listen to that prophet will be cut off. Arminian readers see the seriousness of refusing God’s final messenger.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the covenant warning that those who reject the Messiah exclude themselves from the people of God.

Acts 3:24

24Yes, and all the prophets from Samuel and those who followed after, as many as have spoken, also told of these days.

Arminian

All the prophets from Samuel onward spoke of these days. Arminian expositors see the whole Old Testament leaning toward Christ.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the unity of redemptive history running from the prophets to the apostles.

Acts 3:25

25You are the children of the prophets, and of the covenant which God made with our fathers, saying to Abraham, ‘All the families of the earth will be blessed through your offspring.’

Arminian

They are heirs of the prophets and of the covenant with Abraham: in his seed all families of the earth will be blessed. Arminian commentators see the promise reaching out to every family on earth.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the Abrahamic covenant fulfilled in Christ, the true seed, through whom blessing comes to the nations.

Acts 3:26

26God, having raised up his servant Jesus, sent him to you first to bless you, in turning away every one of you from your wickedness.”

Arminian

God raised his servant and sent him first to them, to bless them by turning each from wickedness. Arminian readers see the gospel offered to Israel first, with a blessing each hearer may receive.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that it is God who blesses by turning people from their sins. Conversion is described as his work through the risen Christ.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
