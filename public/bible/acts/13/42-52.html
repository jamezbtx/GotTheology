Passage

Acts 13

Verses 42–52 · WEB

The people beg Paul and Barnabas to speak again the next Sabbath, and many follow them. When almost the whole city gathers, jealous Jewish leaders contradict and slander Paul. Paul and Barnabas declare that since the word was rejected and the hearers judged themselves unworthy of eternal life, they will turn to the Gentiles, as the Lord commanded them to be a light to the nations. The Gentiles rejoice, and as many as were appointed to eternal life believe. The word spreads, but persecution drives the missionaries out, and they shake the dust off their feet and move on to Iconium, while the disciples are filled with joy and the Spirit.

Acts 13:42

42So when the Jews went out of the synagogue, the Gentiles begged that these words might be preached to them the next Sabbath.

Arminian

The people beg them to speak again next Sabbath. Arminian readers see genuine interest stirred by the message.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God awakening desire to hear.

Acts 13:43

43Now when the synagogue broke up, many of the Jews and of the devout proselytes followed Paul and Barnabas; who, speaking to them, urged them to continue in the grace of God.

Arminian

Many Jews and proselytes follow Paul and Barnabas, who urge them to continue in the grace of God. Arminian commentators see continuing in grace as something believers must do.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see exhortation to continue as one of God’s means of preserving those he has called.

Acts 13:44

44The next Sabbath, almost the whole city was gathered together to hear the word of God.

Arminian

Almost the whole city gathers to hear the word. Arminian readers see the gospel drawing crowds.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God gathering an audience.

Acts 13:45

45But when the Jews saw the multitudes, they were filled with jealousy, and contradicted the things which were spoken by Paul, and blasphemed.

Arminian

The Jews, filled with jealousy, contradict Paul and blaspheme. Arminian expositors see envy closing hearts.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see opposition rising from those who should have welcomed the message.

Acts 13:46

46Paul and Barnabas spoke out boldly, and said, “It was necessary that God’s word should be spoken to you first. Since indeed you thrust it from yourselves, and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles.

Arminian

Since they reject the word and judge themselves unworthy of eternal life, the apostles turn to the Gentiles. Arminian readers stress that the hearers judged themselves unworthy; their own rejection, not a prior decree, sealed their response.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree the hearers are fully responsible for rejecting the word, and they hold that this responsibility stands alongside the divine appointment described two verses later.

Acts 13:47

47For so has the Lord commanded us, saying, ‘I have set you as a light for the Gentiles, that you should bring salvation to the uttermost parts of the earth.’”

Arminian

The Lord commanded them to be a light to the Gentiles and bring salvation to the ends of the earth. Arminian commentators see the worldwide intention of God’s saving purpose.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see Isaiah 49:6 fulfilled as Christ gathers his people from every nation.

Acts 13:48

48As the Gentiles heard this, they were glad and glorified the word of God. As many as were appointed to eternal life believed.

Arminian

The Gentiles rejoice, and “as many as were appointed to eternal life believed.” Arminian readers note that the Greek verb can describe being disposed or ranged toward eternal life, and they connect it with the Gentiles’ eager response in contrast to the Jewish leaders in verse 46. They hold that God’s appointment includes those he foresaw would believe, so the verse does not teach unconditional election.

Reformed

Reformed readers see here one of the plainest statements that divine appointment comes before faith: those whom God had ordained to eternal life are the ones who believed. Belief is the result of the appointment, not its cause, and they see this as consistent with Paul’s teaching in Ephesians 1:4–5.

Acts 13:49

49The Lord’s word was spread abroad throughout all the region.

Arminian

The word of the Lord spreads through the whole region. Arminian expositors see the gospel moving outward freely.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God’s word accomplishing its purpose.

Acts 13:50

50But the Jews stirred up the devout and prominent women and the chief men of the city, and stirred up a persecution against Paul and Barnabas, and threw them out of their borders.

Arminian

The Jews incite prominent women and leading men, stirring persecution that drives them out. Arminian readers see opposition using social influence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God permitting persecution that moves the mission forward.

Acts 13:51

51But they shook off the dust of their feet against them, and came to Iconium.

Arminian

They shake the dust off their feet and go to Iconium. Arminian commentators see Jesus’ instruction followed as a sign of testimony.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see a solemn witness against those who rejected the word.

Acts 13:52

52The disciples were filled with joy and with the Holy Spirit.

Arminian

The disciples are filled with joy and the Holy Spirit. Arminian readers see joy remaining even after the missionaries leave.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit sustaining new believers on his own.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
