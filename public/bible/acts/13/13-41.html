Passage

Acts 13

Verses 13–41 · WEB

Paul’s company sails to Perga, where John Mark leaves them, and goes on to Pisidian Antioch. Invited to speak in the synagogue, Paul recounts God’s choosing of Israel, the exodus, the judges, and the kings up to David, from whose offspring God has brought Israel a Savior, Jesus. Though Jerusalem’s rulers condemned him, fulfilling the prophets, God raised him from the dead, as the psalms foretold. Through him forgiveness of sins is proclaimed, and everyone who believes is justified from everything the law of Moses could not justify. Paul ends by warning his hearers not to be the scoffers the prophets described.

Acts 13:13

13Now Paul and his company set sail from Paphos and came to Perga in Pamphylia. John departed from them and returned to Jerusalem.

Arminian

John Mark leaves them at Perga and returns to Jerusalem. Arminian readers see a failure that later causes conflict but is not final.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God later restoring Mark to usefulness (2 Timothy 4:11).

Acts 13:14

14But they, passing on from Perga, came to Antioch of Pisidia. They went into the synagogue on the Sabbath day and sat down.

Arminian

They go to the synagogue in Pisidian Antioch on the Sabbath. Arminian commentators see Paul using existing gatherings.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the gospel brought first to Israel.

Acts 13:15

15After the reading of the law and the prophets, the rulers of the synagogue sent to them, saying, “Brothers, if you have any word of exhortation for the people, speak.”

Arminian

The synagogue rulers invite them to speak. Arminian readers see an open door.

Reformed

Reformed readers see providence providing an opportunity.

Acts 13:16

16Paul stood up, and gesturing with his hand said, “Men of Israel, and you who fear God, listen.

Arminian

Paul addresses Israelites and God-fearers. Arminian expositors see the message aimed at both groups.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the gospel addressed to Jews and Gentiles.

Acts 13:17

17The God of this people chose our fathers, and exalted the people when they stayed as aliens in the land of Egypt, and with an uplifted arm, he led them out of it.

Arminian

God chose the fathers and exalted the people in Egypt. Arminian readers see corporate election of Israel as a nation for God’s purposes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the election of Israel as an act of sovereign grace that foreshadows the election of the church.

Acts 13:18

18For a period of about forty years he put up with them in the wilderness.

Arminian

God put up with them in the wilderness for forty years. Arminian commentators see God’s patience with a stubborn people.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God’s long-suffering faithfulness.

Acts 13:19

19When he had destroyed seven nations in the land of Canaan, he gave them their land for an inheritance for about four hundred fifty years.

Arminian

He destroyed seven nations and gave them the land. Arminian readers see God keeping his promise to Abraham.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereignty over nations for the sake of his people.

Acts 13:20

20After these things, he gave them judges until Samuel the prophet.

Arminian

He gave them judges until Samuel. Arminian expositors see God providing leaders time and again.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God preserving his people through every period.

Acts 13:21

21Afterward they asked for a king, and God gave to them Saul the son of Kish, a man of the tribe of Benjamin, for forty years.

Arminian

They asked for a king, and God gave them Saul for forty years. Arminian readers see God granting a request that was not ideal.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s purposes advancing even through Israel’s mistaken desires.

Acts 13:22

22When he had removed him, he raised up David to be their king, to whom he also testified, ‘I have found David the son of Jesse, a man after my heart, who will do all my will.’

Arminian

God raised up David, a man after his heart who would do all his will. Arminian commentators see David’s heart as the reason for his selection.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God choosing and raising up David as he pleased.

Acts 13:23

23From this man’s offspring, God has brought salvation to Israel according to his promise,

Arminian

From David’s offspring God has brought a Savior, Jesus. Arminian readers see the promise to David fulfilled.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the covenant line reaching its goal in Christ.

Acts 13:24

24before his coming, when John had first preached the baptism of repentance to Israel.

Arminian

John preached a baptism of repentance to Israel before Jesus came. Arminian expositors see preparation through repentance.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see John as the forerunner appointed by God.

Acts 13:25

25As John was fulfilling his course, he said, ‘What do you suppose that I am? I am not he. But behold, one comes after me, the sandals of whose feet I am not worthy to untie.’

Arminian

John said he was not the one but was preparing the way. Arminian readers see humility in God’s messenger.

Reformed

Reformed readers see John pointing away from himself to Christ.

Acts 13:26

26“Brothers, children of the stock of Abraham, and those among you who fear God, the word of this salvation is sent out to you.

Arminian

To descendants of Abraham and God-fearers, the message of salvation has been sent. Arminian commentators see salvation sent to all present.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the word of salvation sent by God.

Acts 13:27

27For those who dwell in Jerusalem, and their rulers, because they didn’t know him, nor the voices of the prophets which are read every Sabbath, fulfilled them by condemning him.

Arminian

Jerusalem’s rulers did not recognize Jesus and fulfilled the prophets by condemning him. Arminian readers see ignorance that still led to guilt.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God fulfilling Scripture through the very rejection of Christ.

Acts 13:28

28Though they found no cause for death, they still asked Pilate to have him killed.

Arminian

Though they found no cause for death, they asked Pilate to execute him. Arminian expositors see injustice laid bare.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the innocent one condemned for the guilty.

Acts 13:29

29When they had fulfilled all things that were written about him, they took him down from the tree and laid him in a tomb.

Arminian

When they fulfilled everything written about him, they buried him. Arminian readers see every prophecy accounted for.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the plan of God carried out to the last detail.

Acts 13:30

30But God raised him from the dead,

Arminian

But God raised him from the dead. Arminian commentators see God’s answer to human rejection.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the turning point of history in a single sentence.

Acts 13:31

31and he was seen for many days by those who came up with him from Galilee to Jerusalem, who are his witnesses to the people.

Arminian

He appeared for many days to his followers, who are now his witnesses. Arminian readers see reliable testimony.

Reformed

Reformed readers see chosen witnesses carrying the message.

Acts 13:32

32We bring you good news of the promise made to the fathers,

Arminian

We bring you the good news of the promise to the fathers. Arminian expositors see the gospel as fulfilled promise.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the unity of the covenant from Abraham to Christ.

Acts 13:33

33that God has fulfilled this to us, their children, in that he raised up Jesus. As it is also written in the second psalm, ‘You are my Son. Today I have become your father.’

Arminian

God fulfilled it by raising Jesus, as Psalm 2 says: “You are my Son.” Arminian readers see the resurrection declaring Jesus’ identity.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the eternal Son publicly declared at his resurrection, as Romans 1:4 says.

Acts 13:34

34“Concerning that he raised him up from the dead, now no more to return to corruption, he has spoken thus: ‘I will give you the holy and sure blessings of David.’

Arminian

He raised him, no more to return to decay, giving the holy and sure blessings of David. Arminian commentators see covenant promises secured.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the sure mercies of David guaranteed by the risen Christ.

Acts 13:35

35Therefore he says also in another psalm, ‘You will not allow your Holy One to see decay.’

Arminian

“You will not allow your Holy One to see decay.” Arminian readers see Psalm 16 applied again.

Reformed

Reformed readers see apostolic agreement on the meaning of the psalm.

Acts 13:36

36For David, after he had in his own generation served the counsel of God, fell asleep, was laid with his fathers, and saw decay.

Arminian

David served God’s purpose in his generation, died, and decayed. Arminian expositors see a faithful life within its time.

Reformed

Reformed expositors note that David served “the counsel of God,” a phrase they see as describing God’s plan for each generation.

Acts 13:37

37But he whom God raised up saw no decay.

Arminian

But the one God raised saw no decay. Arminian readers see the contrast that proves the point.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ alone fulfilling the promise.

Acts 13:38

38Be it known to you therefore, brothers, that through this man is proclaimed to you remission of sins;

Arminian

Through this man forgiveness of sins is proclaimed. Arminian commentators see forgiveness announced to everyone present.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see forgiveness grounded in Christ’s person and work.

Acts 13:39

39and by him everyone who believes is justified from all things, from which you could not be justified by the law of Moses.

Arminian

Everyone who believes is justified from all things the law of Moses could not justify. Arminian readers see justification by faith offered to anyone who believes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see one of the clearest statements of justification by faith alone in Acts, anticipating Romans and Galatians.

Acts 13:40

40Beware therefore, lest that come on you which is spoken in the prophets:

Arminian

Beware lest what the prophets said come upon you. Arminian expositors see a warning that rejection is possible and serious.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the gospel offer accompanied by solemn warning.

Acts 13:41

41‘Behold, you scoffers! Wonder and perish, for I work a work in your days, a work which you will in no way believe, if one declares it to you.’”

Arminian

“Behold, you scoffers, wonder and perish.” Arminian readers see Habakkuk’s warning applied to those who refuse to believe.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s work proceeding even when people refuse to believe it.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
