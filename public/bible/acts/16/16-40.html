Passage

Acts 16

Verses 16–40 · WEB

In Philippi a slave girl with a spirit of divination follows Paul for days, crying that these men proclaim the way of salvation, until Paul commands the spirit to leave her. Her owners, losing their profit, drag Paul and Silas before the magistrates, who have them beaten and jailed. At midnight, as they pray and sing, an earthquake opens the doors. The jailer is about to kill himself when Paul stops him; trembling, he asks what he must do to be saved, and they answer, “Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household.” He and his household hear the word, are baptized, and rejoice. The next day Paul, a Roman citizen, insists that the magistrates release them personally.

Acts 16:16

16As we were going to prayer, a certain girl having a spirit of divination met us, who brought her masters much gain by fortune telling.

Arminian

A slave girl with a spirit of divination earns money for her owners. Arminian readers see a person exploited by others.

Reformed

Reformed readers see spiritual bondage serving human greed.

Acts 16:17

17Following Paul and us, she cried out, “These men are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to us a way of salvation!”

Arminian

She cries out that these men are servants of the Most High proclaiming the way of salvation. Arminian commentators see truth spoken by an evil source.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see that the gospel does not need demonic endorsement.

Acts 16:18

18She was doing this for many days. But Paul, becoming greatly annoyed, turned and said to the spirit, “I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her!” It came out that very hour.

Arminian

Paul commands the spirit in Jesus’ name to come out, and it does. Arminian readers see Christ’s authority setting her free.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the kingdom overcoming darkness.

Acts 16:19

19But when her masters saw that the hope of their gain was gone, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers.

Arminian

Her owners, seeing their profit gone, seize Paul and Silas. Arminian expositors see greed driving opposition.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the gospel threatening economic idols.

Acts 16:20

20When they had brought them to the magistrates, they said, “These men, being Jews, are agitating our city

Arminian

They accuse them before the magistrates of troubling the city. Arminian readers see false charges based on prejudice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the world misrepresenting God’s servants.

Acts 16:21

21and advocate customs which it is not lawful for us to accept or to observe, being Romans.”

Arminian

They claim the men promote unlawful customs. Arminian commentators see the real motive hidden.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see opposition disguised as civic concern.

Acts 16:22

22The multitude rose up together against them and the magistrates tore their clothes from them, then commanded them to be beaten with rods.

Arminian

The crowd attacks, and the magistrates have them beaten. Arminian readers see injustice without a trial.

Reformed

Reformed readers see suffering permitted for a purpose soon revealed.

Acts 16:23

23When they had laid many stripes on them, they threw them into prison, charging the jailer to keep them safely.

Arminian

They are flogged and thrown into prison. Arminian expositors see a high cost of faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God placing his servants where a jailer needs the gospel.

Acts 16:24

24Having received such a command, he threw them into the inner prison and secured their feet in the stocks.

Arminian

The jailer puts them in the inner prison with feet in stocks. Arminian readers see maximum security.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the scene set for God’s intervention.

Acts 16:25

25But about midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them.

Arminian

At midnight Paul and Silas pray and sing hymns, and the prisoners listen. Arminian commentators see joy in suffering as a witness.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see contentment rooted in trust in God’s sovereignty.

Acts 16:26

26Suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken; and immediately all the doors were opened, and everyone’s bonds were loosened.

Arminian

An earthquake shakes the prison, the doors open, and chains fall off. Arminian readers see God acting powerfully.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s hand opening what humans locked.

Acts 16:27

27The jailer, being roused out of sleep and seeing the prison doors open, drew his sword and was about to kill himself, supposing that the prisoners had escaped.

Arminian

The jailer wakes, sees the doors open, and draws his sword to kill himself. Arminian expositors see despair at a failure that would cost his life.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see a man brought to the end of himself.

Acts 16:28

28But Paul cried with a loud voice, saying, “Don’t harm yourself, for we are all here!”

Arminian

Paul shouts, “Don’t harm yourself, for we are all here!” Arminian readers see compassion for his captor.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace reaching the jailer before he asks.

Acts 16:29

29He called for lights, sprang in, fell down trembling before Paul and Silas,

Arminian

He calls for lights, rushes in, and falls trembling before them. Arminian commentators see fear turning to inquiry.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see conviction produced by God.

Acts 16:30

30brought them out, and said, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”

Arminian

“Sirs, what must I do to be saved?” Arminian readers see a sincere question that expects a response he can make.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a question that God has prompted and will answer through the gospel.

Acts 16:31

31They said, “Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household.”

Arminian

“Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household.” Arminian readers see the simplicity of the gospel condition: believe, and the promise extends to every household member who also believes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as the sole instrument of salvation and the household promise as an expression of covenant blessing. Paedobaptists see family inclusion in covenant signs, while credobaptists note verse 32 that all heard the word.

Acts 16:32

32They spoke the word of the Lord to him, and to all who were in his house.

Arminian

They speak the word of the Lord to him and everyone in his house. Arminian expositors see the message offered to each person.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the word as the means by which God brings the household to faith.

Acts 16:33

33He took them the same hour of the night and washed their stripes, and was immediately baptized, he and all his household.

Arminian

He washes their wounds, and he and his family are baptized immediately. Arminian readers see repentance shown in kindness and baptism following faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see another household baptism, which paedobaptists cite as consistent with covenant practice.

Acts 16:34

34He brought them up into his house and set food before them, and rejoiced greatly with all his household, having believed in God.

Arminian

He brings them into his house, feeds them, and rejoices with his household, having believed in God. Arminian commentators see joy following faith.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the household united in rejoicing because the head of the house believed.

Acts 16:35

35But when it was day, the magistrates sent the sergeants, saying, “Let those men go.”

Arminian

The magistrates send word to release them. Arminian readers see authorities trying to end the matter quietly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s servants vindicated.

Acts 16:36

36The jailer reported these words to Paul, saying, “The magistrates have sent to let you go; now therefore come out and go in peace.”

Arminian

The jailer reports that they may go in peace. Arminian expositors see the jailer now friendly.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see a transformed relationship.

Acts 16:37

37But Paul said to them, “They have beaten us publicly without a trial, men who are Romans, and have cast us into prison! Do they now release us secretly? No, most certainly, but let them come themselves and bring us out!”

Arminian

Paul insists that the magistrates come themselves, since Roman citizens were beaten without trial. Arminian readers see Paul protecting the new church by exposing the injustice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see legitimate use of legal rights for the sake of the gospel.

Acts 16:38

38The sergeants reported these words to the magistrates, and they were afraid when they heard that they were Romans,

Arminian

The magistrates are afraid when they hear they are Roman citizens. Arminian commentators see the tables turned.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God providing protection through civil law.

Acts 16:39

39and they came and begged them. When they had brought them out, they asked them to depart from the city.

Arminian

They come, apologize, and ask them to leave. Arminian readers see public vindication.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church’s reputation protected.

Acts 16:40

40They went out of the prison and entered into Lydia’s house. When they had seen the brothers, they encouraged them, then departed.

Arminian

They visit Lydia’s house, encourage the brothers, and depart. Arminian expositors see care for the new church before leaving.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the church in Philippi established, later receiving Paul’s letter.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
