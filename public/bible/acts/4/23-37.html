Passage

Acts 4

Verses 23–37 · WEB

Released, Peter and John report to the believers, who pray together to the sovereign Lord, Creator of all. Quoting Psalm 2, they acknowledge that Herod, Pilate, the Gentiles, and Israel gathered against Jesus to do what God’s hand and counsel had foreordained. They ask not for safety but for boldness to speak and for God to stretch out his hand to heal. The place shakes, they are filled with the Spirit and speak boldly. The believers are of one heart and soul, sharing everything so that no one is in need, and Barnabas sells a field and brings the money to the apostles.

Acts 4:23

23Being let go, they came to their own company and reported all that the chief priests and the elders had said to them.

Arminian

Peter and John return to their own company and report. Arminian readers see believers drawing strength from fellowship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church as the place where God’s people bring their trials together.

Acts 4:24

24When they heard it, they lifted up their voice to God with one accord and said, “O Lord, you are God, who made the sky, the earth, the sea, and all that is in them;

Arminian

They pray with one voice to the Lord who made heaven, earth, and sea. Arminian commentators see the church grounding its prayer in God’s power as Creator.

Reformed

Reformed commentators note the address “Sovereign Lord” and see the church resting in God’s rule over all things.

Acts 4:25

25who by the mouth of your servant David, said, ‘Why do the nations rage, and the peoples plot a vain thing?

Arminian

They quote David: why do the nations rage? Arminian readers see Scripture giving words to the church in a moment of threat.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Psalm 2 as messianic, describing the world’s opposition to God’s Christ.

Acts 4:26

26The kings of the earth take a stand, and the rulers plot together, against the Lord, and against his Christ.’

Arminian

The kings and rulers gather against the Lord and his Christ. Arminian expositors see a pattern of rebellion repeated in the trial of Jesus.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the psalm’s prophecy fulfilled precisely.

Acts 4:27

27“For truly, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the people of Israel, were gathered together against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed,

Arminian

Herod, Pilate, the Gentiles, and Israel gathered together against Jesus, God’s holy servant. Arminian readers stress that each party acted freely and is held accountable for its part in the crucifixion.

Reformed

Reformed readers see every party in the conspiracy named. Their combined hostility, though fully their own, served the purpose described in the next verse.

Acts 4:28

28to do whatever your hand and your counsel foreordained to happen.

Arminian

They did “whatever your hand and your counsel foreordained to happen.” Arminian interpreters read this as God determining the outcome, the death of his Son for salvation, while foreseeing and permitting the free and guilty acts of those involved. God did not cause their hatred, but he worked through it.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see this as one of the clearest texts for God’s decree, which ordains even the evil acts of sinners without making him the author of sin. The church found comfort here: if God governed the cross, he governs their present threats as well.

Acts 4:29

29Now, Lord, look at their threats, and grant to your servants to speak your word with all boldness,

Arminian

They ask God to consider the threats and grant boldness to speak. Arminian readers note that they pray for courage, not escape.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereignty leading to bolder obedience rather than passivity.

Acts 4:30

30while you stretch out your hand to heal; and that signs and wonders may be done through the name of your holy Servant Jesus.”

Arminian

They ask God to stretch out his hand to heal and to do signs in Jesus’ name. Arminian commentators see the church asking God to confirm the message.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the church depending on God’s hand, the same hand that ordained the events of verse 28.

Acts 4:31

31When they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were gathered together. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they spoke the word of God with boldness.

Arminian

The place is shaken, they are filled with the Spirit, and they speak boldly. Arminian readers see a fresh filling of the Spirit for a new challenge.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God answering the prayer at once, giving the boldness they asked for.

Acts 4:32

32The multitude of those who believed were of one heart and soul. Not one of them claimed that anything of the things which he possessed was his own, but they had all things in common.

Arminian

The believers are of one heart and soul and hold possessions in common. Arminian expositors see love expressed freely in sharing.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the unity the Spirit creates among those united to Christ.

Acts 4:33

33With great power, the apostles gave their testimony of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. Great grace was on them all.

Arminian

The apostles testify with great power, and great grace is upon them all. Arminian readers see grace resting on a community that is faithful in witness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace as the source of both the power of the witness and the generosity of the church.

Acts 4:34

34For neither was there among them any who lacked, for as many as were owners of lands or houses sold them, and brought the proceeds of the things that were sold,

Arminian

No one among them is in need, because owners sell property and bring the proceeds. Arminian commentators recall Deuteronomy 15:4, where God hoped for no poor among his people.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the covenant ideal of Deuteronomy realized by the Spirit in the church.

Acts 4:35

35and laid them at the apostles’ feet; and distribution was made to each, according as anyone had need.

Arminian

They lay the money at the apostles’ feet, and it is distributed according to need. Arminian readers see trust in the church’s leaders.

Reformed

Reformed readers see ordered generosity under recognized oversight.

Acts 4:36

36Joses, who by the apostles was also called Barnabas (which is, being interpreted, Son of Encouragement), a Levite, a man of Cyprus by race,

Arminian

Joseph, called Barnabas, Son of Encouragement, a Levite from Cyprus. Arminian expositors note the nickname given because of his character.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see Barnabas introduced here before his important role later in Acts.

Acts 4:37

37having a field, sold it and brought the money and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

Arminian

Barnabas sells a field and brings the money. Arminian readers see a model of generous response to grace.

Reformed

Reformed readers see his gift as a positive example that contrasts sharply with what follows in chapter 5.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
