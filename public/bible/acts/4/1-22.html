Passage

Acts 4

Verses 1–22 · WEB

The priests, the temple captain, and the Sadducees arrest Peter and John for proclaiming the resurrection in Jesus, yet about five thousand men believe. The next day, before the council, Peter, filled with the Spirit, declares that the lame man was healed in the name of Jesus Christ, whom they crucified and God raised, the rejected stone now made the cornerstone, and that salvation is found in no other name. The rulers are astonished at the boldness of these unschooled men. Unable to deny the miracle, they order them to stop speaking in Jesus’ name, but Peter and John answer that they must obey God and cannot stop telling what they have seen and heard.

Acts 4:1

1As they spoke to the people, the priests and the captain of the temple and the Sadducees came to them,

Arminian

Temple authorities and Sadducees confront Peter and John. Arminian readers see the first organized opposition to the church.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the opposition Jesus foretold beginning as soon as the gospel is preached.

Acts 4:2

2being upset because they taught the people and proclaimed in Jesus the resurrection from the dead.

Arminian

They are disturbed because the apostles proclaim the resurrection in Jesus. Arminian commentators note that the Sadducees denied resurrection altogether.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the resurrection as the point where the gospel most directly offends unbelief.

Acts 4:3

3They laid hands on them, and put them in custody until the next day, for it was now evening.

Arminian

The apostles are jailed overnight. Arminian expositors see faithful witnesses accepting the cost.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God allowing suffering while still governing its outcome.

Acts 4:4

4But many of those who heard the word believed, and the number of the men came to be about five thousand.

Arminian

Many who heard believed, and the number of men reached about five thousand. Arminian readers see hearers responding freely to the message even as its preachers are arrested.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that the word cannot be bound: God gathers his people even while his messengers are in chains.

Acts 4:5

5In the morning, their rulers, elders, and scribes were gathered together in Jerusalem.

Arminian

Rulers, elders, and scribes assemble in Jerusalem. Arminian commentators see the full weight of the religious establishment gathering.

Reformed

Reformed commentators note that the same council condemned Jesus.

Acts 4:6

6Annas the high priest was there, with Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and as many as were relatives of the high priest.

Arminian

Annas, Caiaphas, and the high priest’s family are present. Arminian readers see the men responsible for Jesus’ trial now facing his witnesses.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God bringing the gospel to those who had rejected it before.

Acts 4:7

7When they had stood Peter and John in the middle of them, they inquired, “By what power, or in what name, have you done this?”

Arminian

“By what power, or in what name, have you done this?” Arminian interpreters note that the question opens a door for testimony.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see the rulers unwittingly asking the very question Peter is ready to answer.

Acts 4:8

8Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them, “You rulers of the people and elders of Israel,

Arminian

Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, speaks to the rulers. Arminian readers see Jesus’ promise in Luke 12:12 fulfilled.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit supplying the words, so the witness rests on God’s power, not Peter’s courage.

Acts 4:9

9if we are examined today concerning a good deed done to a crippled man, by what means this man has been healed,

Arminian

They are being examined for a good deed done to a disabled man. Arminian commentators note Peter’s point that kindness is on trial.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the irony of the authorities questioning an act of mercy.

Acts 4:10

10may it be known to you all, and to all the people of Israel, that in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead, this man stands here before you whole in him.

Arminian

The man was healed in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom they crucified and God raised. Arminian expositors see Peter pressing responsibility on the leaders while proclaiming the resurrection.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the contrast between what the rulers did and what God did.

Acts 4:11

11He is ‘the stone which was regarded as worthless by you, the builders, which has become the head of the corner.’

Arminian

Jesus is the stone the builders rejected that has become the cornerstone. Arminian readers see Psalm 118 fulfilled in Israel’s leaders rejecting their Messiah.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God building his people on the very one the leaders discarded.

Acts 4:12

12There is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven that is given among men, by which we must be saved!”

Arminian

There is salvation in no one else, and no other name is given by which we must be saved. Arminian commentators see salvation offered through Christ alone to everyone under heaven.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the exclusive sufficiency of Christ, and they stress that the name is “given” by God as the only way of salvation.

Acts 4:13

13Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and had perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marveled. They recognized that they had been with Jesus.

Arminian

The rulers marvel at the boldness of unschooled men and recognize they had been with Jesus. Arminian readers see ordinary people transformed by time with Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God using those without status so that the glory remains his.

Acts 4:14

14Seeing the man who was healed standing with them, they could say nothing against it.

Arminian

The healed man stands beside them, and they can say nothing. Arminian expositors see undeniable evidence silencing objection.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see that evidence silences opposition without changing hearts.

Acts 4:15

15But when they had commanded them to go aside out of the council, they conferred among themselves,

Arminian

The council sends them out to confer privately. Arminian readers see the leaders deliberating how to respond.

Reformed

Reformed readers see human counsel weighing what to do with God’s work.

Acts 4:16

16saying, “What shall we do to these men? Because indeed a notable miracle has been done through them, as can be plainly seen by all who dwell in Jerusalem, and we can’t deny it.

Arminian

They admit a notable miracle has happened and cannot be denied. Arminian commentators see people who acknowledge the truth but refuse to submit to it.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the hardness of unbelief, which can admit facts yet reject their meaning.

Acts 4:17

17But so that this spreads no further among the people, let’s threaten them, that from now on they don’t speak to anyone in this name.”

Arminian

They decide to threaten the apostles to stop the message spreading. Arminian readers see fear of losing control.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the futility of human efforts to stop what God has set in motion.

Acts 4:18

18They called them, and commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus.

Arminian

They forbid the apostles to speak or teach in Jesus’ name. Arminian expositors note that the name is the very thing at stake.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see a direct conflict between human command and Christ’s commission.

Acts 4:19

19But Peter and John answered them, “Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, judge for yourselves,

Arminian

Peter and John ask whether it is right before God to listen to men rather than God. Arminian readers see conscience bound to God above every human authority.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the principle that obedience to rulers has limits where rulers forbid what God commands.

Acts 4:20

20for we can’t help telling the things which we saw and heard.”

Arminian

“We can’t help telling the things which we saw and heard.” Arminian commentators see witness flowing from personal experience.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the compulsion of those whom God has made his witnesses.

Acts 4:21

21When they had further threatened them, they let them go, finding no way to punish them, because of the people; for everyone glorified God for that which was done.

Arminian

After more threats they release them, because the people are praising God. Arminian readers see public opinion restraining the rulers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God protecting his servants through ordinary circumstances.

Acts 4:22

22For the man on whom this miracle of healing was performed was more than forty years old.

Arminian

The healed man was more than forty years old. Arminian expositors note the detail that makes the healing all the more remarkable.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see a lifelong condition reversed in a moment, displaying God’s power.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
