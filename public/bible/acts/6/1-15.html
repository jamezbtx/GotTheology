Passage

Acts 6

Verses 1–15 · WEB

As the church grows, Greek-speaking Jewish believers complain that their widows are overlooked in the daily distribution. The twelve ask the believers to choose seven men full of the Spirit and wisdom to oversee the work, so the apostles can devote themselves to prayer and the word. The seven, including Stephen and Philip, are set before the apostles, who pray and lay hands on them, and the word spreads, with many priests coming to faith. Stephen, full of grace and power, does great signs, and when opponents cannot withstand his wisdom, they bring false witnesses who accuse him of speaking against the temple and the law. The council sees his face like the face of an angel.

Acts 6:1

1Now in those days, when the number of the disciples was multiplying, a complaint arose from the Hellenists against the Hebrews, because their widows were neglected in the daily service.

Arminian

Greek-speaking believers complain that their widows are neglected. Arminian readers see that growth brings new tensions the church must address honestly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church facing internal problems that require wise order.

Acts 6:2

2The twelve summoned the multitude of the disciples and said, “It is not appropriate for us to forsake the word of God and serve tables.

Arminian

The twelve say they should not neglect the word to serve tables. Arminian commentators see the apostles recognizing their particular calling.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the priority of the ministry of the word for those called to it.

Acts 6:3

3Therefore, select from among you, brothers, seven men of good report, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business.

Arminian

The believers are to choose seven men of good reputation, full of the Spirit and wisdom. Arminian readers see the congregation taking part in choosing its leaders.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the qualifications for church office set out, and many see here the origin of the office of deacon.

Acts 6:4

4But we will continue steadfastly in prayer and in the ministry of the word.”

Arminian

The apostles will devote themselves to prayer and the ministry of the word. Arminian expositors see a balance of responsibilities within the church.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see prayer and preaching as the central tasks of pastoral ministry.

Acts 6:5

5These words pleased the whole multitude. They chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a proselyte of Antioch,

Arminian

The proposal pleases everyone, and they choose Stephen, Philip, and five others. Arminian readers note that all seven have Greek names, which suggests sensitivity to the group that complained.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wisdom and harmony resolving the dispute.

Acts 6:6

6whom they set before the apostles. When they had prayed, they laid their hands on them.

Arminian

The apostles pray and lay hands on them. Arminian commentators see the community’s choice affirmed by the apostles.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see an orderly setting apart to office through prayer and the laying on of hands.

Acts 6:7

7The word of God increased and the number of the disciples greatly multiplied in Jerusalem. A great company of the priests were obedient to the faith.

Arminian

The word of God increases, and many priests become obedient to the faith. Arminian readers see the gospel reaching even the temple establishment.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the phrase “obedient to the faith” and see faith as submission to God’s word.

Acts 6:8

8Stephen, full of faith and power, performed great wonders and signs among the people.

Arminian

Stephen, full of grace and power, does great wonders among the people. Arminian expositors see a servant of tables becoming a powerful witness.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see grace and power coming from the same Spirit.

Acts 6:9

9But some of those who were of the synagogue called “The Libertines”, and of the Cyrenians, of the Alexandrians, and of those of Cilicia and Asia arose, disputing with Stephen.

Arminian

Members of several synagogues dispute with Stephen. Arminian readers see diaspora Jews engaging his message.

Reformed

Reformed readers see opposition rising among those who should have been most open.

Acts 6:10

10They weren’t able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke.

Arminian

They cannot withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he speaks. Arminian commentators recall Jesus’ promise in Luke 21:15.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the Spirit making Stephen’s words irresistible in argument, though not in persuasion of hardened hearts.

Acts 6:11

11Then they secretly induced men to say, “We have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses and God.”

Arminian

They secretly persuade men to accuse him of blasphemy against Moses and God. Arminian readers see deception replacing honest argument.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same tactics used against Jesus now used against his servant.

Acts 6:12

12They stirred up the people, the elders, and the scribes, and came against him and seized him, then brought him in to the council,

Arminian

They stir up the people, elders, and scribes, seize Stephen, and bring him to the council. Arminian expositors see mob pressure turning opinion.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the pattern of Jesus’ passion repeated in his disciple.

Acts 6:13

13and set up false witnesses who said, “This man never stops speaking blasphemous words against this holy place and the law.

Arminian

False witnesses say he constantly speaks against the temple and the law. Arminian readers note the half-truth: Stephen does teach that Jesus fulfills the temple.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the accusation twisting the truth that Christ is greater than the temple.

Acts 6:14

14For we have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place, and will change the customs which Moses delivered to us.”

Arminian

They claim he said Jesus would destroy the temple and change Moses’ customs. Arminian commentators see the fear that the gospel threatens cherished traditions.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the transition from old covenant forms to new covenant reality behind the charge.

Acts 6:15

15All who sat in the council, fastening their eyes on him, saw his face like it was the face of an angel.

Arminian

The council sees his face like the face of an angel. Arminian readers see God’s presence visibly resting on his servant.

Reformed

Reformed readers recall Moses’ shining face and see God vindicating Stephen before he speaks.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
