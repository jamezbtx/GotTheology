Passage

Acts 14

Verses 19–28 · WEB

Opponents from Antioch and Iconium win over the crowd, who stone Paul and drag him out of the city, thinking he is dead. As the disciples gather around him, he gets up and goes back into the city, then leaves for Derbe, where many become disciples. Paul and Barnabas return through Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch, strengthening the disciples, urging them to continue in the faith, and telling them that through many hardships we must enter God’s kingdom. They appoint elders with prayer and fasting, then return to Antioch and report how God had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

Acts 14:19

19But some Jews from Antioch and Iconium came there, and having persuaded the multitudes, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing that he was dead.

Arminian

Jews from Antioch and Iconium persuade the crowd, who stone Paul and leave him for dead. Arminian readers see how quickly praise can turn to violence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul suffering what he once inflicted on Stephen.

Acts 14:20

20But as the disciples stood around him, he rose up, and entered into the city. On the next day he went out with Barnabas to Derbe.

Arminian

The disciples gather around him, he rises, and the next day goes to Derbe. Arminian commentators see remarkable endurance.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see God preserving his servant for further work.

Acts 14:21

21When they had preached the Good News to that city and had made many disciples, they returned to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch,

Arminian

They preach in Derbe and make many disciples, then return to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch. Arminian readers see courage in going back to dangerous places.

Reformed

Reformed readers see pastoral care for those God has gathered.

Acts 14:22

22strengthening the souls of the disciples, exhorting them to continue in the faith, and that through many afflictions we must enter into God’s Kingdom.

Arminian

They strengthen the disciples, exhort them to continue in the faith, and say that through many hardships we must enter the kingdom. Arminian readers see continuing in the faith as a condition believers must meet, which is why it requires exhortation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see exhortation as the means God uses to secure the perseverance he has promised, and they see suffering as appointed on the way to glory.

Acts 14:23

23When they had appointed elders for them in every assembly, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord on whom they had believed.

Arminian

They appoint elders in every church with prayer and fasting and commit them to the Lord. Arminian expositors see churches given leadership and entrusted to God.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see a plurality of elders in each church, important for Reformed church order.

Acts 14:24

24They passed through Pisidia and came to Pamphylia.

Arminian

They pass through Pisidia to Pamphylia. Arminian readers see the journey continuing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the mission advancing on God’s schedule.

Acts 14:25

25When they had spoken the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia.

Arminian

They preach in Perga and go down to Attalia. Arminian commentators note they now preach where they had passed through earlier.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the word sown in another city.

Acts 14:26

26From there they sailed to Antioch, from where they had been committed to the grace of God for the work which they had fulfilled.

Arminian

They sail back to Antioch, where they had been commended to God’s grace. Arminian readers see the mission completed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the work fulfilled by God’s grace.

Acts 14:27

27When they had arrived and had gathered the assembly together, they reported all the things that God had done with them, and that he had opened a door of faith to the nations.

Arminian

They report all God had done and how he opened a door of faith to the Gentiles. Arminian expositors see God opening the opportunity for Gentiles to believe.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see God as the one who opened the door, and many in the tradition read this as God granting faith itself.

Acts 14:28

28They stayed there with the disciples for a long time.

Arminian

They stay a long time with the disciples. Arminian readers see rest and fellowship after hardship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church strengthened through their presence.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
