Passage

Acts 20

Verses 17–38 · WEB

At Miletus Paul summons the Ephesian elders and recalls how he served among them with humility, tears, and trials, declaring everything profitable and testifying to Jews and Greeks about repentance toward God and faith in Jesus. Bound by the Spirit, he goes to Jerusalem expecting imprisonment and does not count his life dear. He is innocent of everyone’s blood because he did not shrink from declaring the whole counsel of God. He charges the elders to guard themselves and the flock, over which the Spirit made them overseers, to shepherd the church of God which he bought with his own blood, and warns that wolves will come. He commends them to God and the word of his grace, reminds them of his labor and Jesus’ words that it is more blessed to give than to receive, prays with them, and departs amid tears.

Acts 20:17

17From Miletus he sent to Ephesus and called to himself the elders of the assembly.

Arminian

Paul calls the Ephesian elders to Miletus. Arminian readers see a farewell to trusted leaders.

Reformed

Reformed readers see elders as the church’s appointed shepherds.

Acts 20:18

18When they had come to him, he said to them, “You yourselves know, from the first day that I set foot in Asia, how I was with you all the time,

Arminian

He reminds them how he lived among them from the first day. Arminian commentators see integrity of life supporting the message.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see an example for ministers.

Acts 20:19

19serving the Lord with all humility, with many tears, and with trials which happened to me by the plots of the Jews;

Arminian

He served the Lord with humility, tears, and trials. Arminian readers see costly faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see suffering as part of gospel ministry.

Acts 20:20

20how I didn’t shrink from declaring to you anything that was profitable, teaching you publicly and from house to house,

Arminian

He did not shrink from declaring anything profitable, publicly and from house to house. Arminian expositors see complete teaching in every setting.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see public preaching and private instruction together.

Acts 20:21

21testifying both to Jews and to Greeks repentance toward God and faith toward our Lord Jesus.

Arminian

He testified to Jews and Greeks of repentance toward God and faith in Jesus. Arminian readers see repentance and faith as the response every hearer is called to make.

Reformed

Reformed readers see repentance and faith as inseparable gifts of grace that the preacher must still call for.

Acts 20:22

22Now, behold, I go bound by the Spirit to Jerusalem, not knowing what will happen to me there;

Arminian

Bound by the Spirit, he goes to Jerusalem without knowing what will happen. Arminian commentators see obedience without full knowledge.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see Paul constrained by God’s will.

Acts 20:23

23except that the Holy Spirit testifies in every city, saying that bonds and afflictions wait for me.

Arminian

The Spirit testifies that chains and afflictions await him. Arminian readers see suffering ahead accepted freely.

Reformed

Reformed readers see suffering appointed by God.

Acts 20:24

24But these things don’t count; nor do I hold my life dear to myself, so that I may finish my race with joy, and the ministry which I received from the Lord Jesus, to fully testify to the Good News of the grace of God.

Arminian

He does not count his life precious, if only he finishes his course and ministry. Arminian expositors see a race to be completed faithfully, implying the need to persevere.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the course given by the Lord Jesus, which Paul is sure to complete by grace.

Acts 20:25

25“Now, behold, I know that you all, among whom I went about preaching God’s Kingdom, will see my face no more.

Arminian

They will not see his face again. Arminian readers see a final farewell.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the ministry entrusted to others.

Acts 20:26

26Therefore I testify to you today that I am clean from the blood of all men,

Arminian

He is innocent of the blood of all. Arminian commentators see faithful warning removing his responsibility.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see Ezekiel’s watchman principle.

Acts 20:27

27for I didn’t shrink from declaring to you the whole counsel of God.

Arminian

He did not shrink from declaring the whole counsel of God. Arminian readers see complete teaching, including hard truths.

Reformed

Reformed readers see “the whole counsel of God” as a call to preach all of Scripture, including God’s sovereign purpose.

Acts 20:28

28Take heed, therefore, to yourselves and to all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the assembly of the Lord and God which he purchased with his own blood.

Arminian

Take heed to yourselves and the flock, over which the Holy Spirit made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God, which he purchased with his own blood. Arminian readers see a strong statement of the cost of redemption and of the Spirit appointing leaders; they also see a duty to guard every member of the flock for whom Christ died.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s blood purchasing the church in particular, a text they cite for definite atonement. They also note that the verse calls it God’s own blood, a strong affirmation of Christ’s deity.

Acts 20:29

29For I know that after my departure, vicious wolves will enter in among you, not sparing the flock.

Arminian

Savage wolves will come in after he leaves. Arminian commentators see real danger to the flock.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the duty of elders to protect against false teaching.

Acts 20:30

30Men will arise from among your own selves, speaking perverse things, to draw away the disciples after them.

Arminian

Men will arise from among them speaking twisted things. Arminian readers see that leaders themselves can fall away.

Reformed

Reformed readers see false teachers emerging from within the church.

Acts 20:31

31Therefore watch, remembering that for a period of three years I didn’t cease to admonish everyone night and day with tears.

Arminian

Watch, remembering his three years of warnings with tears. Arminian expositors see vigilance as necessary.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the pastor’s tender care as a model.

Acts 20:32

32Now, brothers, I entrust you to God and to the word of his grace, which is able to build up and to give you the inheritance among all those who are sanctified.

Arminian

He commends them to God and the word of his grace, which can build them up and give them an inheritance. Arminian readers see the word sustaining those who continue in it.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God and his word as the security of the church, able to bring believers to their inheritance.

Acts 20:33

33I coveted no one’s silver, gold, or clothing.

Arminian

He coveted no one’s silver or gold. Arminian commentators see integrity in ministry.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see freedom from greed.

Acts 20:34

34You yourselves know that these hands served my necessities, and those who were with me.

Arminian

His own hands supplied his needs and his companions’. Arminian readers see self-support for the gospel’s sake.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an example of generous labor.

Acts 20:35

35In all things I gave you an example, that so laboring you ought to help the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, that he himself said, ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’ ”

Arminian

He showed them to help the weak and remember Jesus’ words: it is more blessed to give than to receive. Arminian expositors see a saying of Jesus preserved outside the Gospels.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see generosity as the fruit of grace.

Acts 20:36

36When he had spoken these things, he knelt down and prayed with them all.

Arminian

He kneels and prays with them. Arminian readers see prayer ending the farewell.

Reformed

Reformed readers see dependence on God in parting.

Acts 20:37

37They all wept freely, and fell on Paul’s neck and kissed him,

Arminian

They weep and embrace him. Arminian commentators see deep affection.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see love formed by the gospel.

Acts 20:38

38sorrowing most of all because of the word which he had spoken, that they should see his face no more. Then they accompanied him to the ship.

Arminian

They grieve most because they will not see his face again, and they escort him to the ship. Arminian readers see sorrow in separation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the bond between pastor and people.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
