Passage

Acts 5

Verses 1–16 · WEB

Ananias and Sapphira sell property, keep back part of the price, and present the rest as if it were the whole. Peter tells Ananias he has lied not to men but to the Holy Spirit, and he falls dead; three hours later Sapphira repeats the lie and dies as well, and great fear comes on the church. The apostles perform many signs, the people hold them in high regard, and multitudes of men and women believe. The sick are carried into the streets in the hope that Peter’s shadow might fall on them, and crowds from nearby towns bring the sick and those troubled by unclean spirits, and all are healed.

Acts 5:1

1But a certain man named Ananias, with Sapphira his wife, sold a possession,

Arminian

Ananias and Sapphira sell a property, apparently imitating Barnabas. Arminian readers see people within the fellowship who choose to seek a reputation they have not earned.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the first hypocrisy within the church appearing right after the example of true generosity.

Acts 5:2

2and kept back part of the price, his wife also being aware of it, then brought a certain part and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

Arminian

They keep back part of the money and present the rest as the whole. Arminian commentators note that both spouses agreed, making the deception a shared decision.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see sin working through deliberate deceit, not weakness.

Acts 5:3

3But Peter said, “Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit and to keep back part of the price of the land?

Arminian

Peter asks why Satan has filled Ananias’s heart to lie to the Holy Spirit. Arminian interpreters see Satan tempting while Ananias remains responsible for yielding.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters note that lying to the Spirit is lying to God (verse 4), a clear testimony to the Spirit’s deity.

Acts 5:4

4While you kept it, didn’t it remain your own? After it was sold, wasn’t it in your power? How is it that you have conceived this thing in your heart? You haven’t lied to men, but to God.”

Arminian

The property was his, and the money was in his control; he lied to God, not to men. Arminian readers stress that giving was voluntary, so the sin was the lie, not the keeping.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that the church’s sharing was free and that God judges the heart’s honesty.

Acts 5:5

5Ananias, hearing these words, fell down and died. Great fear came on all who heard these things.

Arminian

Ananias hears these words, falls down, and dies, and great fear seizes all. Arminian expositors see God’s holiness guarding the newborn church.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the judgment as a solemn reminder that God is present among his people.

Acts 5:6

6The young men arose and wrapped him up, and they carried him out and buried him.

Arminian

The young men wrap the body and bury him. Arminian readers note the swiftness and gravity of the scene.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church accepting God’s verdict without protest.

Acts 5:7

7About three hours later, his wife, not knowing what had happened, came in.

Arminian

Sapphira comes in about three hours later, unaware of what has happened. Arminian commentators see that she is given her own opportunity to tell the truth.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see her accountable for her own part, independent of her husband.

Acts 5:8

8Peter answered her, “Tell me whether you sold the land for so much.” She said, “Yes, for so much.”

Arminian

Peter asks whether they sold the land for this amount, and she says yes. Arminian interpreters see a chance to confess that she does not take.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see the lie repeated knowingly.

Acts 5:9

9But Peter asked her, “How is it that you have agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord? Behold, the feet of those who have buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out.”

Arminian

Peter asks why they agreed to test the Spirit of the Lord. Arminian readers see conspiracy against God himself.

Reformed

Reformed readers see testing God as presuming that he will not see or act.

Acts 5:10

10She fell down immediately at his feet and died. The young men came in and found her dead, and they carried her out and buried her by her husband.

Arminian

She falls dead at his feet and is buried beside her husband. Arminian expositors note that shared sin led to shared judgment.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the seriousness of hypocrisy in the presence of a holy God.

Acts 5:11

11Great fear came on the whole assembly, and on all who heard these things.

Arminian

Great fear comes on the whole church. This is the first time Luke uses the word for church in Acts. Arminian readers see reverence as a healthy response.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church learning from the start that God is holy as well as gracious.

Acts 5:12

12By the hands of the apostles many signs and wonders were done among the people. They were all with one accord in Solomon’s porch.

Arminian

The apostles do many signs, and the believers meet together in Solomon’s Porch. Arminian commentators see God continuing to confirm the message.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see signs serving the apostolic witness.

Acts 5:13

13None of the rest dared to join them; however, the people honored them.

Arminian

No one else dares join them, though the people hold them in high regard. Arminian readers see onlookers deterred from casual association.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a holy fear that kept the church from being joined lightly.

Acts 5:14

14More believers were added to the Lord, multitudes of both men and women.

Arminian

Yet more and more believers are added to the Lord, both men and women. Arminian expositors note that fear did not stop genuine faith.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the Lord adding to his people, as in 2:47.

Acts 5:15

15They even carried out the sick into the streets and laid them on cots and mattresses, so that as Peter came by, at least his shadow might overshadow some of them.

Arminian

People lay the sick in the streets so that Peter’s shadow might fall on them. Arminian readers see desperation and faith reaching out for help.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Luke reports the hope without saying the shadow itself healed, keeping the focus on God’s power.

Acts 5:16

16The multitude also came together from the cities around Jerusalem, bringing sick people and those who were tormented by unclean spirits; and they were all healed.

Arminian

Crowds from surrounding towns bring the sick and oppressed, and all are healed. Arminian commentators see God’s mercy extended generously.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the kingdom’s power displayed as Jesus’ ministry continues through his apostles.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
