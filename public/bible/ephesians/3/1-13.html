Passage

Ephesians 3

Verses 1–13 · ESV · Voices

Paul, a prisoner for the Gentiles, unfolds the mystery now revealed: Gentiles are fellow heirs, one body, and partakers of the promise in Christ through the gospel. Grace made him a minister of unsearchable riches so that through the church God’s manifold wisdom would be displayed, according to the eternal purpose realized in Christ, in whom we have bold access by faith.

Ephesians 3:1

1For this reason I, Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus on behalf of you Gentiles—

Arminian

Paul’s chains are “of Christ Jesus” and “on behalf of you Gentiles.” Suffering is not a detour from the gospel’s width; it is the cost of carrying that width to the nations.

Classical Arminians hear a missionary heart: grace that sent Paul to the Gentiles is sincere toward the nations. The prisoner’s “for this reason” looks back to one new humanity and invites all to enter it by faith.

Reformed

The apostle interprets prison as Christ’s assignment for Gentile inclusion. His identity is bound to the Lord’s purpose, not to Rome’s verdict.

Reformed framing: God places even chains in the service of the mystery He is revealing. The Gentile church exists because the Lord willed a messenger and kept him at his post.

Ephesians 3:2

2assuming that you have heard of the stewardship of God’s grace that was given to me for you,

Arminian

Grace here is a stewardship—entrusted, not hoarded. Paul received it “for you,” so the Gentiles would hear and believe.

Arminian pastoral note: office and gift exist for others. The same grace that made Paul a steward still offers the gospel widely; hearers are summoned to receive what God has placed in the church’s care.

Reformed

Stewardship language locates Paul’s ministry in a trust God gave. Grace is God’s, the assignment is God’s, the beneficiaries are the Ephesians.

Reformed accent: the apostle does not freelance. What he administers was given, so the church’s hearing of the gospel is itself a provision of God’s will.

Ephesians 3:3

3how the mystery was made known to me by revelation, as I have written briefly.

Arminian

Mystery is not a riddle for elites; it is a secret God disclosed. Revelation made known what cleverness could not invent.

Arminian emphasis: God sincerely unveils Christ to be preached. Insight is gift, and the gift is meant to be read, understood, and trusted rather than locked in a private circle.

Reformed

Paul traces his gospel to revelation, not to committee. What was hidden, God made known.

Reformed reading: saving knowledge of the mystery originates in God’s disclosing act. The church’s doctrine of Gentile inclusion rests on what God revealed, not on human progress.

Ephesians 3:4

4When you read this, you can perceive my insight into the mystery of Christ,

Arminian

Reading is enough for perception: the mystery of Christ is public in the letter. Insight is meant to be shared, not guarded.

Classical Arminians gladly put Scripture in the people’s hands. Grace that revealed the mystery to Paul also enables hearers to perceive it and respond in faith.

Reformed

Paul expects the church to perceive by reading what God gave him to see. The mystery is Christ-shaped, not a second gospel.

Reformed comfort: illumination has a public text. God grants insight so the body may know the same Christ the apostle knows.

Ephesians 3:5

5which was not made known to the sons of men in other generations as it has now been revealed to his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit.

Arminian

The now of the Spirit is brighter than former generations. Apostles and prophets receive a disclosure meant for the whole church’s era.

Arminian hope: the same Spirit who revealed to apostles still applies the gospel. What was once less clear is now openly preached so that Gentiles may believe.

Reformed

Redemptive history has a “now.” The Spirit’s revelation to holy apostles and prophets marks the gospel age.

Reformed note: God times His disclosure. The mystery’s former hiddenness and present clarity are both His doing, so the church lives by gifted revelation, not by climbing into heaven.

Ephesians 3:6

6This mystery is that the Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel.

Arminian

Three equal shares: fellow heirs, same body, partakers of the promise—in Christ, through the gospel. No second-class Gentile seat.

Classical Arminians preach this as wide mercy: the promise is in Christ for all who believe the gospel. Grace enables a real joining; the invitation to the nations is sincere, not a form of words.

Reformed

The mystery’s content is union: one inheritance, one body, one promise, located in Christ and received through the gospel.

Reformed confession: God has actually made one people in the Son. Gentile inclusion is the outworking of His purpose, accomplished in Christ, announced in the gospel that creates partakers.

Key contrast Both celebrate Gentile inclusion in Christ through the gospel. Arminians typically stress a sincere, grace-enabled offer the nations may receive or refuse; Reformed readers typically stress God’s effectual purpose that actually joins those He calls into the one body.

Ephesians 3:7

7Of this gospel I was made a minister according to the gift of God’s grace, which was given me by the working of his power.

Arminian

Ministry is gift and power, not self-appointment. Paul was made a minister; grace worked in him so the gospel could work through him.

Arminian reading: divine power enables service without erasing the servant’s responsibility. The same grace that made Paul a minister still calls people to believe the message he was given.

Reformed

“Made a minister” and “working of his power” keep Paul’s office on God’s side of the ledger. Grace is not raw potential; it worked.

Reformed accent: the gospel’s advance through Paul is God’s energy. Gifting and power explain how a persecutor became a steward of the mystery.

Ephesians 3:8

8To me, though I am the very least of all the saints, this grace was given, to preach to the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ,

Arminian

Least of saints, yet entrusted with unsearchable riches. Humility and mission live together: the preacher is small; Christ is inexhaustible.

Arminian joy: those riches are to be preached to the Gentiles—announced as truly offered. Grace that saved Paul is the same wealth he now spreads, sufficient for whoever believes.

Reformed

Paul’s “least” magnifies the gift. Unsearchable riches name a Christ too full to be mapped, given to the apostle for Gentile proclamation.

Reformed doxology: God chose a former enemy to display boundless grace. The treasure is Christ’s; the assignment is gift; the nations hear because God gave a preacher.

Ephesians 3:9

9and to bring to light for everyone what is the plan of the mystery hidden for ages in God, who created all things,

Arminian

The plan hidden in the Creator is brought to light “for everyone.” Mystery was never meant to stay locked; creation’s God now publishes His saving design.

Classical Arminians linger on “everyone.” Light is given to be seen. The gospel’s publicity matches a grace that sincerely addresses the world God made.

Reformed

Bringing to light is Paul’s task; the plan was hidden in God the Creator until He said otherwise. Ages of concealment end at God’s initiative.

Reformed framing: the God who created all things also wrote the mystery’s timetable. Illumination of the plan is as sovereign as creation itself.

Ephesians 3:10

10so that through the church the manifold wisdom of God might now be made known to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places.

Arminian

The church is a billboard to the powers: many-colored wisdom, now public. Unity of Jew and Gentile in Christ teaches heaven’s spectators what God is like.

Arminian churchmanship: our life together is testimony. Grace that formed one body still calls the church to display that wisdom rather than hide it in rivalry.

Reformed

God’s audience includes rulers and authorities in the heavenlies. The church is the appointed medium of His manifold wisdom.

Reformed wonder: cosmic powers learn by looking at a people God made one in Christ. The church’s existence is itself a sermon God preaches from His eternal counsel.

Ephesians 3:11

11This was according to the eternal purpose that he has realized in Christ Jesus our Lord,

Arminian

Eternal purpose is realized in a Person, not in speculation. What God planned, He has accomplished in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Arminian care: purpose language exalts God’s initiative without emptying the gospel of a real call. The purpose realized in Christ is preached so people may enter it by faith.

Reformed

“Eternal purpose” and “realized” belong together: God did not improvise Gentile inclusion. He accomplished in the Lord Jesus what He had always intended.

Reformed comfort: history is the stage of a purpose already secured in Christ. The church’s multi-colored witness rests on a plan God has not left unfinished.

Key contrast Both locate the mystery in God’s eternal purpose realized in Christ. Arminians typically describe that purpose as centering on Christ and gathering all who believe; Reformed readers typically describe it as God’s particular, effectual accomplishment of what He eternally willed in the Son.

Ephesians 3:12

12in whom we have boldness and access with confidence through our faith in him.

Arminian

Access is in Christ, enjoyed through faith in Him—boldness, not cringing. The door is a Person; faith is the way we come.

Classical Arminians stress that this faith is a genuine, grace-enabled trust. Confidence is not presumption; it is reliance on the Lord who invites sinners in.

Reformed

Boldness and access are possessions “in whom.” Faith is the instrument, Christ is the ground; confidence is warranted because of Him.

Reformed pastoral weight: we do not invent access. God gives it in the Son, and faith receives what union with Christ already holds.

Ephesians 3:13

13So I ask you not to lose heart over what I am suffering for you, which is your glory.

Arminian

Suffering for them is interpreted as their glory, not their shame. Chains must not talk them out of the gospel that put Paul in prison.

Arminian encouragement: do not lose heart. The cost of Gentile mission is proof of how widely grace is being spent, and hearers are urged to keep trusting the Christ Paul suffers to preach.

Reformed

Paul pastors their emotions from his cell: his pain is for them, and it is glory. Losing heart would misread providence.

Reformed note: God weaves an apostle’s suffering into the church’s good. The mystery is so sure that prison becomes an argument for courage, not despair.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the revealed mystery and grace to the Gentiles—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Ephesians 3:1–13.

Arminian Notable voices

“Unto me, who am less than the least of all saints, is this grace given, to preach among the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ—the unsearchable riches of his grace, mercy, love. How should they who have received the gospel by such means be affected therewith!”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Eph 3:8)

Reformed Notable voices

“By the unsearchable riches of Christ are meant the astonishing and boundless treasures of grace, which God had suddenly and unexpectedly bestowed on the Gentiles. The Ephesians are thus reminded how eagerly the gospel ought to be embraced, and how highly it ought to be esteemed.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Ephesians (on Eph 3:8)
