Passage

Ephesians 4

Verses 17–24 · ESV · Voices

Paul testifies in the Lord that the church must no longer walk in Gentile futility: darkened understanding, alienation from God’s life, ignorance, hardness, callous sensuality. That is not how they learned Christ. As the truth is in Jesus, they are to put off the old self, be renewed in the spirit of their minds, and put on the new self, created after God’s likeness in true righteousness and holiness.

Ephesians 4:17

17Now this I say and testify in the Lord, that you must no longer walk as the Gentiles do, in the futility of their minds.

Arminian

The walk of 4:1 now has a negative: no longer as the Gentiles, in mental futility. Conversion includes a new mind-path, not only new company.

Arminian charge: “must no longer” is a real break. Grace that taught them Christ also enables them to refuse the empty thinking they once called normal.

Reformed

Paul testifies in the Lord, not as a life-coach. Futility of mind is the Gentile walk apart from the life of God.

Reformed diagnosis: the problem is not merely manners but a mind emptied of true purpose. Those in Christ are summoned to a walk that matches the new creation God has begun.

Ephesians 4:18

18They are darkened in their understanding, alienated from the life of God because of the ignorance that is in them, due to their hardness of heart.

Arminian

Darkness, alienation, ignorance, hardness—layered ruin. The heart’s hardness feeds the mind’s night, and distance from God’s life follows.

Classical Arminians take hardness as culpable, not a neutral disability. Grace can soften and enlighten; persistent hardness remains a real refusal of the life God offers.

Reformed

The chain runs heart to ignorance to alienation to dark understanding. Life of God is the thing they are shut out from.

Reformed gravity: hardness is both guilt and bondage. Without God’s regenerating light, the Gentile walk is not a slight delay in education but deathly distance from His life.

Key contrast Both treat hardness as morally serious and spiritually dark. Arminians typically stress grace that can enlighten a resisting heart; Reformed readers typically stress that only God’s effectual work breaks the hardness that keeps people alienated from His life.

Ephesians 4:19

19They have become callous and have given themselves up to sensuality, greedy to practice every kind of impurity.

Arminian

Callousness is chosen: they gave themselves up. Sensuality and greedy impurity are not accidents; they are a handed-over self.

Arminian moral realism: people can yield themselves. The gospel still calls for a different yielding—to Christ—because grace restores the possibility of a new walk.

Reformed

“Given themselves up” names willing bondage. Callousness, sensuality, and impurity are a package, not scattered slips.

Reformed reading: when God is not the life of the heart, appetite becomes a master. The church is being shown what it has been saved from, not a lifestyle to sample.

Ephesians 4:20

20But that is not the way you learned Christ!—

Arminian

Learning Christ has a way, and it is not callous impurity. Discipleship is personal: you learned Him, not merely a system.

Arminian discipleship: Christ taught as He is received. Grace that introduced them to Jesus also defines a path that contradicts their former giving-up of themselves.

Reformed

The sharp “but” splits two schools. Christ is a curriculum whose content is a person, and that lesson contradicts Gentile futility.

Reformed claim: those God has taught in His Son cannot treat the old walk as an option. Learning Christ is conversion’s classroom, not an elective.

Ephesians 4:21

21assuming that you have heard about him and were taught in him, as the truth is in Jesus,

Arminian

Heard about Him, taught in Him, truth in Jesus: instruction is located inside a living Lord. Doctrine has a proper name.

Arminian confidence: the truth in Jesus is publicly taught so people may hear and keep learning. Remaining in that teaching is how the new walk stays new.

Reformed

Hearing and being taught “in him” keep truth from becoming abstraction. Jesus is where truth lives.

Reformed center: union with Christ is the school. What they heard is not a rumor about religion but the truth that exists in the incarnate Lord.

Ephesians 4:22

22to put off your old self, which belongs to your former manner of life and is corrupt through deceitful desires,

Arminian

Put off the old self—the fallen “man” as old as the fall, corrupt through desires that lie. Wesley called those lusts deceitful because they promise what they cannot pay.

Classical Arminians treat putting off as a real, grace-enabled act, not a metaphor to admire. The former manner of life is to be shed, not managed as a pet.

Reformed

The old self is nature from the womb, deeply corrupt, belonging to the former walk. Deceitful desires are its operating system.

Reformed anthropology: what we put off is a whole person-in-Adam, not a few habits. The command assumes God has already begun the new creation that makes such putting off possible.

Key contrast Both insist the old self must be put off, not decorated. Arminians typically describe that putting off as enabled obedience believers must exercise; Reformed readers typically describe it as living out the break with Adam that God has already effected in those united to Christ.

Ephesians 4:23

23and to be renewed in the spirit of your minds,

Arminian

Renewal hits the mind’s spirit—the inward attitude of thought. Futility (v. 17) is answered by renovation, not by willpower alone.

Arminian hope: God renews, and we seek that renewal. A changed walk requires a changed inner mind, continually offered to the Spirit who makes it new.

Reformed

Passive tone matters: be renewed. The spirit of the mind is God’s workshop if darkness is to lift.

Reformed inner life: new ethics start where God recreates thought. The command to walk differently is matched by a promised renovation we cannot perform as a self-upgrade.

Ephesians 4:24

24and to put on the new self, created after the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness.

Arminian

Put on what God created: a new self in His likeness, true righteousness and holiness. Clothes language means a whole identity, not a costume for Sundays.

Arminian holiness: the likeness is real and to be worn. Grace creates the new self; believers put it on in daily trust and obedience, growing into the righteousness that is true, not theatrical.

Reformed

Created after God’s likeness—this is Genesis language reborn. Righteousness and holiness of truth are the image restored, not a human rebrand.

Reformed new creation: we put on what God has made. The new self is His workmanship; wearing it is the Christian’s fitting response to the Creator who remade us in Christ.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the old self, renewed mind, and new creation in God’s likeness—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Ephesians 4:17–24.

Arminian Notable voices

“The old man—coeval with our being, and as old as the fall; our evil nature… which is corrupt according to the deceitful lusts. And to be renewed in the spirit of your mind—which was the first thing lost. And to put on the new man—whose soul was created in the image of God, in righteousness and true holiness.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Eph 4:22–24)

Reformed Notable voices

“The old man is that nature which we bring with us from the womb, and which is so deeply corrupted that it infects the whole man… The new man is that which is formed in us by the Spirit of God. Created according to God—that is, in the likeness of God, which consists in righteousness and holiness of truth.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Ephesians (on Eph 4:22–24)

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
