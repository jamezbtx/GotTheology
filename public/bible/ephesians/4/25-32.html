Passage

Ephesians 4

Verses 25–32 · WEB · Voices

Therefore the new self shows in speech and community: put away falsehood, be angry without sin, give the devil no room, replace stealing with honest work and generosity, and let talk build up and give grace. Do not grieve the Spirit who sealed you for redemption. Put away bitterness and malice; be kind, tenderhearted, and forgiving, as God also in Christ forgave you.

Ephesians 4:25

25Therefore, putting away falsehood, speak truth each one with his neighbor, for we are members of one another.

Arminian

Therefore ties clothes-language to conversation. Falsehood is put away with the old self; members of one body tell the truth to neighbors.

Lying violates membership. Grace that joined us also enables truthful speech; we remain responsible to use words as people who belong to each other.

Reformed

Put-away falsehood is the new self’s grammar. Neighbor-truth follows from being members one of another.

Deceit treats a fellow member as an outsider. The God who created the new self creates a people who owe one another reality, not spin.

Ephesians 4:26

26“Be angry, and don’t sin.” Don’t let the sun go down on your wrath,

Arminian

Anger is not banned; sinning with it is. Sunset is a deadline so heat does not harden into a project.

Grace teaches timely surrender of wrath. We can be righteously moved and still refuse to nurse what would rot the body by morning.

Reformed

Scripture allows anger that does not sin, then fences it with time. Overnight anger is a different creature.

God knows how quickly heat becomes hatred. The new self lives on a short leash with wrath because the Lord is building a people, not a grudge-bank.

Ephesians 4:27

27and don’t give place to the devil.

Arminian

Prolonged anger is a foothold. The devil does not need a full invitation; an opportunity will do.

Spiritual conflict is authentic and our choices matter. Enabled by grace, the church can refuse the footholds it used to hand over in the old walk.

Reformed

The devil looks for room. Unresolved anger is described as giving it to him.

Evil is personal, not only psychological. God commands a closed door because He has already claimed this people by His Spirit.

Ephesians 4:28

28Let him who stole steal no more; but rather let him labor, producing with his hands something that is good, that he may have something to give to him who has need.

Arminian

The old economy was taking; the new is labor plus sharing. Hands once used to steal now serve need.

Change is concrete. Grace that saves a thief also points him into honest work whose goal is generosity, not a new self-centered nest egg.

Reformed

Conversion rewrites labor. Honest work has a purpose clause: share with anyone in need.

The thief is not merely unemployed from crime; he is employed for mercy. God’s re-creation includes the paycheck and the open hand.

Ephesians 4:29

29Let no corrupt speech proceed out of your mouth, but only what is good for building others up as the need may be, that it may give grace to those who hear.

Arminian

Talk can rot or build. Fit-for-the-occasion words are meant to give grace to hearers—speech as a means of kindness.

We are stewards of what comes out. The Spirit who unites the body also enables words that help; corrupting talk is a chosen contradiction of that grace.

Reformed

Mouths are construction tools or demolition tools. The standard is building-up, fittingness, and grace given to hearers.

Grace is not only what we received in chapter 1–3; it is what our sentences should deliver. The new self talks like a member of a body God is building.

Ephesians 4:30

30Don’t grieve the Holy Spirit of God, in whom you were sealed for the day of redemption.

Arminian

The Spirit can be grieved. Wesley pressed “by any way whatsoever,” then clung to the seal unto complete deliverance. Holiness has a personal Someone to hurt or honor.

Arminian interpreters hear both warning and security-in-tension: sealing is true, and grieving is possible. Grace that marked them for redemption also calls them not to wound the Sealer by the old walk.

Reformed

Calvin made grief the Spirit’s response to refused voice and stained holiness, then called the seal a powerful argument for holiness. Redemption’s day is already marked on them.

You were sealed—identity first. The command not to grieve is how sealed people live toward the day God has pledged, not how they earn the seal.

Key contrast Both treat grieving the Spirit as an evil and sealing as God’s act. Arminians typically warn that believers can truly resist and grieve the Spirit who marked them; Reformed readers typically ground the warning in an effectual seal that will hold to the day of redemption while still forbidding all grief to Him.

Ephesians 4:31

31Let all bitterness, wrath, anger, outcry, and slander be put away from you, with all malice.

Arminian

A pile of inner and outer heat: bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, slander, malice—all to be put away. The old self’s emotional kit does not get to stay.

“All” is the word. Grace does not baptize a little slander as personality. The church is told to discard the whole set as people who can, by the Spirit, actually put it away.

Reformed

The catalog is comprehensive so no pet vice survives. Put away matches the old-self clothing of v. 22.

These are not small leaks; they are the former manner of life trying to reenter. God commands a full eviction because He has created a new self.

Ephesians 4:32

32And be kind to one another, tender hearted, forgiving each other, just as God also in Christ forgave you.

Arminian

The replacement wardrobe is kindness, tender hearts, and forgiveness modeled on God in Christ. Wesley heard that forgiveness as free, full, and most tender—so ours should be.

We forgive because we have been forgiven, a pattern to be practiced. Grace that pardoned us enables a tenderness toward one another, not a theoretical courtesy.

Reformed

“As God also in Christ forgave you” is the rule and the fountain. Kindness and tenderheartedness are imitations of a prior divine act.

The standard is not our pain threshold but God’s forgiveness in the Son. Those He has pardoned are made able, and obligated, to pass that kindness along in the body.

Key contrast Christian forgiveness copies God’s forgiveness in Christ. Arminians typically press this as enabled imitation the church must actually extend; Reformed readers press it as the fruit of a pardon God has already fully given in the Son.

What older interpreters in each camp said about truthful speech, the Spirit’s seal, and forgiveness in Christ.

Arminian Notable voices

“Grieve not the Holy Spirit of God—by any way whatsoever. By whom ye are sealed unto the day of redemption—the day of complete deliverance from all sin and sorrow.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Eph 4:30, 32)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Spirit is grieved when we refuse to listen to his voice, or when we corrupt, by wickedness, the holiness he has impressed upon us. We are sealed by him to the day of redemption—a powerful argument to holiness.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Ephesians (on Eph 4:30, 32)
