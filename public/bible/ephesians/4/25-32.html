Passage

Ephesians 4

Verses 25–32 · ESV · Voices

Therefore the new self shows in speech and community: put away falsehood, be angry without sin, give the devil no room, replace stealing with honest work and generosity, and let talk build up and give grace. Do not grieve the Spirit who sealed you for redemption. Put away bitterness and malice; be kind, tenderhearted, and forgiving, as God in Christ forgave you.

Ephesians 4:25

25Therefore, having put away falsehood, let each one of you speak the truth with his neighbor, for we are members one of another.

Arminian

Therefore ties clothes-language to conversation. Falsehood is put away with the old self; members of one body tell the truth to neighbors.

Arminian community ethic: lying violates membership. Grace that joined us also enables truthful speech; we remain responsible to use words as people who belong to each other.

Reformed

Put-away falsehood is the new self’s grammar. Neighbor-truth follows from being members one of another.

Reformed body-life: deceit treats a fellow member as an outsider. The God who created the new self creates a people who owe one another reality, not spin.

Ephesians 4:26

26Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger,

Arminian

Anger is not banned; sinning with it is. Sunset is a deadline so heat does not harden into a project.

Arminian practical holiness: grace teaches timely surrender of wrath. We can be righteously moved and still refuse to nurse what would rot the body by morning.

Reformed

Scripture allows anger that does not sin, then fences it with time. Overnight anger is a different creature.

Reformed pastoral sense: God knows how quickly heat becomes hatred. The new self lives on a short leash with wrath because the Lord is building a people, not a grudge-bank.

Ephesians 4:27

27and give no opportunity to the devil.

Arminian

Prolonged anger is a foothold. The devil does not need a full invitation; an opportunity will do.

Arminian warning: spiritual conflict is real and our choices matter. Enabled by grace, the church can refuse the footholds it used to hand over in the old walk.

Reformed

The devil looks for room. Unresolved anger is described as giving it to him.

Reformed vigilance: evil is personal, not only psychological. God commands a closed door because He has already claimed this people by His Spirit.

Ephesians 4:28

28Let the thief no longer steal, but rather let him labor, doing honest work with his own hands, so that he may have something to share with anyone in need.

Arminian

The old economy was taking; the new is labor plus sharing. Hands once used to steal now serve need.

Arminian repentance: change is concrete. Grace that saves a thief also points him into honest work whose goal is generosity, not a new self-centered nest egg.

Reformed

Conversion rewrites labor. Honest work has a purpose clause: share with anyone in need.

Reformed newness: the thief is not merely unemployed from crime; he is employed for mercy. God’s re-creation includes the paycheck and the open hand.

Ephesians 4:29

29Let no corrupting talk come out of your mouths, but only such as is good for building up, as fits the occasion, that it may give grace to those who hear.

Arminian

Talk can rot or build. Fit-for-the-occasion words are meant to give grace to hearers—speech as a means of kindness.

Arminian tongue-ethic: we are stewards of what comes out. The Spirit who unites the body also enables words that help; corrupting talk is a chosen contradiction of that grace.

Reformed

Mouths are construction tools or demolition tools. The standard is building-up, fittingness, and grace given to hearers.

Reformed speech: grace is not only what we received in chapter 1–3; it is what our sentences should deliver. The new self talks like a member of a body God is building.

Ephesians 4:30

30And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption.

Arminian

The Spirit can be grieved. Wesley pressed “by any way whatsoever,” then clung to the seal unto complete deliverance. Holiness has a personal Someone to hurt or honor.

Classical Arminians hear both warning and security-in-tension: sealing is real, and grieving is possible. Grace that marked them for redemption also calls them not to wound the Sealer by the old walk.

Reformed

Calvin made grief the Spirit’s response to refused voice and stained holiness, then called the seal a powerful argument for holiness. Redemption’s day is already marked on them.

Reformed pastoral hold: you were sealed—identity first. The command not to grieve is how sealed people live toward the day God has pledged, not how they earn the seal.

Key contrast Both treat grieving the Spirit as a real evil and sealing as God’s act. Arminians typically warn that believers can truly resist and grieve the Spirit who marked them; Reformed readers typically ground the warning in an effectual seal that will hold to the day of redemption while still forbidding all grief to Him.

Ephesians 4:31

31Let all bitterness and wrath and anger and clamor and slander be put away from you, along with all malice.

Arminian

A pile of inner and outer heat: bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, slander, malice—all to be put away. The old self’s emotional kit does not get to stay.

Arminian thoroughness: “all” is the word. Grace does not baptize a little slander as personality. The church is told to discard the whole set as people who can, by the Spirit, actually put it away.

Reformed

The catalog is comprehensive so no pet vice survives. Put away matches the old-self clothing of v. 22.

Reformed house-cleaning: these are not small leaks; they are the former manner of life trying to reenter. God commands a full eviction because He has created a new self.

Ephesians 4:32

32Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you.

Arminian

The replacement wardrobe is kindness, tender hearts, and forgiveness modeled on God in Christ. Wesley heard that forgiveness as free, full, and most tender—so ours should be.

Arminian gospel ethic: we forgive because we have been forgiven, a pattern to be practiced. Grace that pardoned us enables a real tenderness toward one another, not a theoretical courtesy.

Reformed

“As God in Christ forgave you” is the rule and the fountain. Kindness and tenderheartedness are imitations of a prior divine act.

Reformed comfort and command: the standard is not our pain threshold but God’s forgiveness in the Son. Those He has pardoned are made able, and obligated, to pass that kindness along in the body.

Key contrast Shared ground: Christian forgiveness copies God’s forgiveness in Christ. Arminians typically press this as enabled imitation the church must actually extend; Reformed readers typically press it as the fruit of a pardon God has already fully given in the Son.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on truthful speech, the Spirit’s seal, and forgiveness in Christ—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Ephesians 4:25–32.

Arminian Notable voices

“Grieve not the Holy Spirit of God—by any way whatsoever. By whom ye are sealed unto the day of redemption—the day of complete deliverance from all sin and sorrow. Be ye kind… forgiving one another; as God in Christ hath forgiven you—freely, fully, and most tenderly.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Eph 4:30, 32)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Spirit is grieved when we refuse to listen to his voice, or when we corrupt, by wickedness, the holiness he has impressed upon us. We are sealed by him to the day of redemption—a powerful argument to holiness. Forgive one another, as God in Christ hath forgiven you: His kindness is the rule of ours.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Ephesians (on Eph 4:30, 32)
