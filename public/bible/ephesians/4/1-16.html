Passage

Ephesians 4

Verses 1–16 · ESV · Voices

The prisoner of the Lord urges a walk worthy of the calling: humility, gentleness, patience, and eagerness to keep the Spirit’s unity in peace. One body, Spirit, hope, Lord, faith, baptism, and Father ground that unity, while Christ’s measured gifts—apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds and teachers—equip the saints until the body grows up into the Head in love.

Ephesians 4:1

1I therefore, a prisoner for the Lord, urge you to walk in a manner worthy of the calling to which you have been called,

Arminian

“Therefore” turns doctrine into a walk. Calling is gift; walking worthy is the fitting answer—urged by a man still in chains for the Lord.

Classical Arminians stress that calling is a holy privilege that must be lived. Grace that called still enables a real, responsible walk; unworthiness is not treated as inevitable fate.

Reformed

Calling here is God’s mercy that admitted them into the church. Paul urges a life that matches what God has already done.

Reformed pastoral weight: we do not walk in order to create the call; we walk because we have been called. The prisoner’s therefore is ethics from election’s prior grace.

Key contrast Both treat calling as grace and walking as necessary. Arminians typically describe the worthy walk as enabled yet truly required of those who may still neglect it; Reformed readers typically describe it as the fruit and evidence of a call God has effectually issued.

Ephesians 4:2

2with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love,

Arminian

Worthy walking has a tone: humility, gentleness, patience, and bearing with one another in love. Unity is not a mood; it is practiced forbearance.

Arminian church life: love is commanded because it can be lived by grace. The Spirit who unites also enables these virtues in people who must still choose to bear with each other.

Reformed

The virtues are relational, not ornamental. Love’s bearing is how a called people occupy the same body without tearing it.

Reformed note: these fruits grow where the Spirit has placed us in one calling. Humility is the ethic of those who know they were admitted by mercy.

Ephesians 4:3

3eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

Arminian

Unity is the Spirit’s; our part is eagerness to maintain it in peace’s bond. Wesley called it labour and care, not drift.

Arminian urgency: what the Spirit works can be damaged by our neglect. Grace makes unity; believers must keep it, resisting the pride that splits the body.

Reformed

Maintain, not manufacture: the Spirit creates the unity, peace binds it, and the church is told to be eager in keeping what God has made.

Reformed accent: eagerness is commanded because the unity is already a divine gift. We guard a harmony we did not invent.

Ephesians 4:4

4There is one body and one Spirit—just as you were called to the one hope that belongs to your call—

Arminian

One body, one Spirit, one hope belonging to the call. The church’s oneness is as Trinitarian as it is practical.

Arminian reading: the hope is shared with all who answer the call in faith. The Spirit who is one also draws many; that drawing is real, and the body is meant to look like it.

Reformed

Oneness is indicative before it is imperative. Body, Spirit, and hope are numbered one because God has made them so in the call.

Reformed confession: our call has one hope because God has one people. Fragmenting the body contradicts the Spirit who indwells it.

Ephesians 4:5

5one Lord, one faith, one baptism,

Arminian

Lord, faith, and baptism are singular. Rival lords and rival gospels have no charter here.

Classical Arminians gladly confess one Lord Jesus and one faith that receives Him. Baptism marks entrance into that one confession, not into a boutique spirituality.

Reformed

The triad shuts the door on multiple Christs or multiple ways. One faith answers one Lord; one baptism seals one people.

Reformed clarity: unity is doctrinal as well as relational. The church does not keep peace by multiplying lords, but by holding the one who bought her.

Ephesians 4:6

6one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in all.

Arminian

The unity list ends in the Father: over, through, and in all. Family oneness has a parental source.

Arminian wideness: “of all” honors God’s fatherly reach. The church’s unity is a local picture of the Father whose presence fills His people.

Reformed

Over all, through all, in all: sovereignty, providence, and indwelling in one confession of the Father.

Reformed rest: the body’s unity is as secure as the God who fathers it. He is not one option among household gods; He is the One over the whole.

Ephesians 4:7

7But grace was given to each one of us according to the measure of Christ’s gift.

Arminian

Oneness does not mean sameness. Each one is given grace measured by Christ, so every member has a real share.

Arminian encouragement: Christ’s measuring is generous, not stingy. Grace given to each is to be received and used, not buried; the Giver enables the serving He assigns.

Reformed

“Each one” and “measure of Christ’s gift” keep diversity under the Lord’s allocation. Grace is distributed, not grabbed.

Reformed accent: Christ decides the measure. Gifts in the body are His royal bounty, so comparison and envy misread the Giver.

Key contrast Both credit Christ with giving grace to each member. Arminians typically emphasize responsible use of gifts sincerely given; Reformed readers typically emphasize Christ’s sovereign measure that both assigns and enables the body’s work.

Ephesians 4:8

8Therefore it says, “When he ascended on high he led a host of captives, and he gave gifts to men.”

Arminian

Psalm language becomes Christ’s victory parade: the ascended Lord leads captives and gives gifts. Ministry gifts are plunder from a won war.

Arminian mission tone: the Victor shares spoils with His people so the nations may be served. Receiving a gift is sharing in His triumph, then putting it to work.

Reformed

Ascension is interpreted as conquest and generosity. Gifts in the church flow from a Lord who has already led captivity captive.

Reformed confidence: we do not staff the church from below. The risen and ascended Christ supplies what His body needs from His triumph.

Ephesians 4:9

9(In saying, “He ascended,” what does it mean but that he had also descended into the lower regions, the earth?

Arminian

Ascent presupposes descent. The gift-giving Lord is the One who first came down to our earth.

Arminian gospel shape: incarnation and humiliation sit under every later gift. The Christ who fills the church is the Christ who came near enough to save.

Reformed

Paul reads “ascended” as proof of a prior descent to earth. The One who gives from on high is the One who first entered the lower regions.

Reformed Christology: humiliation and exaltation are one career. Church gifts are not a second Jesus; they come from the same Lord who came down and went up.

Ephesians 4:10

10He who descended is the one who also ascended far above all the heavens, that he might fill all things.)

Arminian

Same person: descended and ascended, now above the heavens, filling all things. No gap between the Jesus of history and the Lord of the church.

Arminian worship: He fills in order to give. The fullness is not locked in heaven; it is meant to reach the body that still walks on earth.

Reformed

Descent and ascent belong to one Christ, whose goal is to fill all things. Distance of body is not absence of power.

Reformed filling: Calvin stressed that Christ fills by His gifts and Spirit, not by stretching flesh across space. The church lives from a Lord who is truly exalted and truly present.

Ephesians 4:11

11And he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers,

Arminian

People are the gifts: apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds and teachers. Christ stocks His church with servants, not with slogans only.

Arminian use: offices exist to serve, not to lord. Those so given remain responsible to the Head, and the body is responsible to receive their ministry as Christ’s kindness.

Reformed

He gave—Christ is the subject. The fourfold (or fivefold) list is His provision for the church’s health.

Reformed order: ministry is gift before it is career. Shepherds and teachers, like apostles and evangelists, are Christ’s way of caring for a people He already bought.

Ephesians 4:12

12to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ,

Arminian

Leaders equip; saints do ministry; the body is built. The work is not a clerical monopoly.

Arminian congregational life: every saint is summoned into service. Grace equips, and the equipped must actually minister so that Christ’s body grows.

Reformed

The aim of the gifts is equipment, ministry, and upbuilding. Christ gives officers so the whole body works.

Reformed architecture: the saints’ work is real, and it is downstream of Christ’s giving. Building the body is not self-construction; it is the fruit of the Head’s provision.

Ephesians 4:13

13until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to mature manhood, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ,

Arminian

The “until” is corporate maturity: unity of faith and knowledge of the Son, the full stature of Christ. Childhood is not the goal.

Classical Arminians hear a real destination the church must press toward. Grace aims at grown unity; believers cooperate by holding the faith and growing in the Son they know.

Reformed

Maturity is measured by Christ’s fullness, not by market fads. Unity of faith and knowledge of the Son is the appointed end of the gifts.

Reformed patience: “until we all attain” is God’s horizon for the body He is building. The stature is Christ’s; the time is His; the means are the gifts He already gave.

Ephesians 4:14

14so that we may no longer be children, tossed to and fro by the waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine, by human cunning, by craftiness in deceitful schemes.

Arminian

Immaturity looks like drift: waves, winds of doctrine, cunning, schemes. Love without truth leaves the boat unmanned.

Arminian warning: deception is real and resistible under grace. The church must refuse to be children; holding fast to the Son is how we stop being cargo for every new teaching.

Reformed

Children here are unstable, not cute. Human cunning and deceitful schemes explain why gifts for maturity are mercy.

Reformed sobriety: God shepherds His people into doctrine that does not toss. The Head’s gifts are protection against winds He knows will blow.

Ephesians 4:15

15Rather, speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ,

Arminian

The alternative to drift is truth in love, growing up into the Head. Neither harsh truth nor spineless love is the path.

Arminian discipleship: speaking and growing are things the church actually does. Grace enables a mature voice that tells the truth because it loves, aimed at Christ rather than at winning.

Reformed

Truth and love are yoked; the growth is into Christ the Head. Maturity has a person for its measure.

Reformed direction: we grow up into Him, not past Him. The Head supplies both the standard and the life by which the body learns to speak.

Ephesians 4:16

16from whom the whole body, joined and held together by every joint with which it is equipped, when each part is working properly, makes the body grow so that it builds itself up in love.

Arminian

Growth is from the Head, through joints, when each part works. Love is the climate of a body that is actually connected.

Arminian teamwork: unused gifts starve the body. Christ equips joints; members must work properly. Grace makes the growth possible; neglect still harms the whole.

Reformed

“From whom” keeps Christ as source even while “each part” works. Joints are His equipment; love is the building’s atmosphere.

Reformed unity: the body builds itself up only because it is joined to the Head. Proper working is real, and it is the ordained means of a growth Christ originates.

Historic words on the church’s calling, unity, and growth into Christ—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Ephesians 4:1–16.

Arminian Notable voices

“I therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, entreat you to walk worthy of the calling wherewith ye are called… Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit—that unity which the Spirit works and maintains—in the bond of peace. It is a thing that requires labour and care.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Eph 4:1, 3)

Reformed Notable voices

“The calling of which he speaks is that mercy of God by which we are admitted into his church. He reminds them of it, that they may not think it enough to have been once called… The unity of the Spirit is that harmony which the Spirit of God produces among us; peace is the bond by which it is kept.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Ephesians (on Eph 4:1, 3)
