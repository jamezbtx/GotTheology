Passage

1 Peter 1

Verses 22–25 · WEB

Peter ties sincere brotherly love to the new birth. Having purified themselves by obeying the truth, believers are to love one another earnestly, because they were born again through the living and enduring word of God. He seals the point with Isaiah 40: human glory withers, but the Lord’s word endures, and that word is the gospel they heard. Both traditions agree that the preached word is the instrument of new birth; they differ on the order of regeneration and faith.

1 Peter 1:22

22Seeing you have purified your souls in your obedience to the truth through the Spirit in sincere brotherly affection, love one another from the heart fervently,

Arminian

Having purified themselves by obedience to the truth, they must love one another earnestly from the heart. Arminian readers stress obedience to the truth as the believer’s response to the gospel, a real act that brings purification.

Reformed

Reformed readers see obedience to the truth as faith in the gospel, made possible by new birth. The command to love flows from a purification already accomplished. Both agree that sincere love is the necessary fruit of the gospel.

Key contrast Both see purification tied to obeying the truth. Arminians typically stress the believer’s faith-response as the human side of purification; Reformed readers typically stress that this response flows from prior regeneration described in v. 23.

1 Peter 1:23

23having been born again, not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, through the word of God, which lives and remains forever.

Arminian

They were born again through the living and abiding word of God. Arminian readers note that the word is the instrument of new birth, implying that hearing and believing the gospel accompany regeneration.

Reformed

Reformed readers affirm that God regenerates through the word, by his Spirit, so that new birth and faith come together. Many Reformed theologians hold that regeneration logically precedes faith while the word is its instrument. Both agree the seed is imperishable.

1 Peter 1:24

24For, “All flesh is like grass, and all of man’s glory like the flower in the grass. The grass withers, and its flower falls;

Arminian

Peter quotes Isaiah 40: all flesh is like grass, and human glory fades. Arminian and Reformed readers agree that human strength and glory are temporary. The contrast prepares for the enduring word.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Isaiah’s comfort to exiles applied to Christian exiles. Human glory withers, but God’s promise stands.

1 Peter 1:25

25but the Lord’s word endures forever.” This is the word of Good News which was preached to you.

Arminian

The Lord’s word endures forever, and this word is the gospel preached to them. Arminian and Reformed readers agree that the gospel is God’s living word, and that its permanence grounds the new life and love of believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers emphasize the sufficiency and permanence of the preached word as the means of grace. The gospel heard is the very word of the eternal God.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
