Passage

Leviticus 10

Verses 8–11 · WEB

In the only passage in Leviticus where the LORD speaks to Aaron alone, God forbids the priests to drink wine or strong drink when they enter the Tent of Meeting. He then states the priests’ twofold task: to distinguish holy from common and clean from unclean, and to teach Israel all of God’s statutes. Placed directly after the deaths of Nadab and Abihu, the command has led some readers to suspect a link with their sin, but the text does not say so.

Leviticus 10:8

8Then the LORD said to Aaron,

Arminian

This is the only place in Leviticus where the LORD speaks to Aaron alone. Arminian readers note the personal care in God addressing the grieving high priest directly. Both traditions see the timing as significant, giving Aaron guidance immediately after the deaths of his sons.

Reformed

Reformed readers observe that God’s word comes directly to Aaron, confirming his office after the judgment. The instruction that follows shapes the priesthood’s ongoing work. Shared ground: God speaks to Aaron with guidance for faithful ministry, and the setting gives the instruction particular weight.

Leviticus 10:9

9“You and your sons are not to drink wine or strong drink whenever you go into the Tent of Meeting, or you will die. This shall be a statute forever throughout your generations.

Arminian

Priests must not drink wine or strong drink when entering the Tent of Meeting. Its placement here has led some readers to suggest that Nadab and Abihu acted under the influence, but this is a possibility, not something the text states. Arminian readers stress clear-minded service. Both traditions affirm the command’s evident purpose.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the prohibition protects the priests’ ability to discern and teach (10:10–11). Whether it explains the earlier sin is uncertain; the text does not connect them explicitly. Shared ground: those who serve before God need sober judgment, and the statute applies whenever they enter to minister.

Leviticus 10:10

10You are to make a distinction between the holy and the common, and between the unclean and the clean.

Arminian

The priests must distinguish between holy and common, unclean and clean. Arminian readers see this as the core of priestly discernment, enabling Israel to live rightly before God. Both traditions note that these categories organize much of Leviticus, including chapter 11.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the distinction as central to the priesthood’s role: guarding God’s holiness and guiding the people. The categories of holy, common, clean, and unclean are ritual, not simply moral. Shared ground: the priests must discern and maintain these boundaries, which shape Israel’s whole life of worship.

Leviticus 10:11

11You are to teach the children of Israel all the statutes which the LORD has spoken to them by Moses.”

Arminian

The priests are to teach Israel all of God’s statutes. Arminian readers stress that God’s people need instruction to respond rightly, and that teaching is a central priestly duty. Both traditions see here the foundation of a teaching ministry that continues in later prophets and in the church (compare Malachi 2:7).

Reformed

Reformed readers emphasize that God’s word must be taught faithfully, and that the priests serve as guardians of revelation. Malachi 2:7 later says the priest’s lips should keep knowledge. Shared ground: teaching God’s law is essential to the life of his people, and those who lead are accountable for it.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
