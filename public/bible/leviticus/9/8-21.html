Passage

Leviticus 9

Verses 8–21 · WEB

Aaron now officiates for the first time, with his sons assisting. He offers first for himself (a sin offering and a burnt offering), then the people’s sin, burnt, meal and peace offerings. Each step follows the procedures of Leviticus 1–7 and is marked as done according to command.

Leviticus 9:8

8So Aaron came near to the altar, and killed the calf of the sin offering, which was for himself.

Arminian

Aaron draws near to the altar and kills the calf for himself, beginning his ministry with his own need for atonement.

Arminian readers note the humility built into the office.

Reformed

The first act of Aaron’s priesthood is an offering for his own sin.

Reformed readers see the weakness of the Aaronic priests that Hebrews 7:27–28 contrasts with Christ. Arminian readers share this contrast.

Leviticus 9:9

9The sons of Aaron presented the blood to him; and he dipped his finger in the blood, and put it on the horns of the altar, and poured out the blood at the base of the altar;

Arminian

His sons assist by bringing the blood. Blood is placed on the altar’s horns and poured at its base, as Leviticus 4 prescribes.

Arminian readers note that the ministry is shared among the priests.

Reformed

The ritual follows the prescribed pattern exactly.

Reformed readers note the order within the priestly household. Both traditions read the detail descriptively.

Leviticus 9:10

10but the fat, and the kidneys, and the cover from the liver of the sin offering, he burned upon the altar, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The fat and kidneys are burned on the altar “as the LORD commanded Moses.”

Arminian readers see faithful obedience in the details.

Reformed

The refrain of obedience continues from chapter 8.

Reformed readers note that worship continues to follow revealed command. This is shared ground.

Leviticus 9:11

11The meat and the skin he burned with fire outside the camp.

Arminian

The meat and skin are burned outside the camp, as in 8:17.

Arminian readers note again the link drawn in Hebrews 13:11–12.

Reformed

The priest’s sin offering is removed from the camp, consistent with Leviticus 4:11–12.

Reformed readers share the typological reading of Hebrews 13. This is shared ground.

Leviticus 9:12

12He killed the burnt offering; and Aaron’s sons delivered the blood to him, and he sprinkled it around on the altar.

Arminian

The burnt offering is killed, and its blood sprinkled around the altar.

Arminian readers see Aaron moving from atonement to dedication.

Reformed

After the sin offering comes the burnt offering, the same order seen in chapter 8.

Reformed readers note the consistency of the pattern. Both traditions read it alike.

Leviticus 9:13

13They delivered the burnt offering to him, piece by piece, and the head. He burned them upon the altar.

Arminian

The pieces and head are handed to Aaron and burned.

Arminian readers note the cooperation of priests in carrying out the ritual.

Reformed

The whole animal goes up to God, consistent with Leviticus 1.

Reformed readers see total consecration. This is shared ground.

Leviticus 9:14

14He washed the innards and the legs, and burned them on the burnt offering on the altar.

Arminian

The innards and legs are washed and burned on the burnt offering.

Arminian readers note that what is offered to God must be clean.

Reformed

The washing follows Leviticus 1:9.

Reformed readers note the exactness of the procedure. Both traditions read it as obedience to command.

Leviticus 9:15

15He presented the people’s offering, and took the goat of the sin offering which was for the people, and killed it, and offered it for sin, like the first.

Arminian

Now Aaron turns to the people’s offering. The goat is offered for sin “like the first.”

Arminian readers see the priest’s ministry extending from himself to the whole community.

Reformed

Having been atoned for, the priest can now mediate for the people.

Reformed readers note the logical order: the mediator first, then those he represents. Both traditions read the sequence as part of the inauguration’s design.

Leviticus 9:16

16He presented the burnt offering, and offered it according to the ordinance.

Arminian

The people’s burnt offering is offered “according to the ordinance.”

Arminian readers see the community dedicating itself to God.

Reformed

The phrase underlines conformity to God’s prescribed pattern.

Reformed readers stress regulated worship. Arminian readers share the concern for obedience in worship.

Leviticus 9:17

17He presented the meal offering, and filled his hand from there, and burned it upon the altar, in addition to the burnt offering of the morning.

Arminian

The meal offering is added, along with the regular morning burnt offering (Exod 29:38–42).

Arminian readers note that this special day is woven into the ongoing daily worship.

Reformed

The mention of “the burnt offering of the morning” shows that the daily offering is already in place.

Reformed readers note the continuity of regular worship. This is shared ground.

Leviticus 9:18

18He also killed the bull and the ram, the sacrifice of peace offerings, which was for the people. Aaron’s sons delivered to him the blood, which he sprinkled around on the altar;

Arminian

The peace offerings are sacrificed for the people.

Arminian readers see the peace offering as celebrating restored fellowship with God.

Reformed

The peace offering follows the sin and burnt offerings, completing the sequence.

Reformed readers note that peace with God follows atonement. Arminian readers also affirm this order. Both traditions read Romans 5:1 as the fulfillment of this peace in Christ.

Leviticus 9:19

19and the fat of the bull and of the ram, the fat tail, and that which covers the innards, and the kidneys, and the cover of the liver;

Arminian

The fat portions of the bull and ram are gathered: the fat tail, the fat covering the innards, the kidneys, and the cover of the liver. These are the same portions reserved for the LORD in the peace offering (Leviticus 3).

Arminian readers see the best given to God, not leftovers kept back for human use.

Reformed

The details follow Leviticus 3.

Reformed readers note precise obedience. Both traditions read the detail descriptively.

Leviticus 9:20

20and they put the fat upon the breasts, and he burned the fat on the altar.

Arminian

The fat is laid on the breasts and burned on the altar. The breasts will be waved in the next verses as the priests’ portion, while the fat is burned as the LORD’s.

Arminian readers note the careful handling of what belongs to God, kept distinct from what is given to the priests.

Reformed

God’s portion is burned, while the breasts are kept for the wave offering.

Reformed readers note the distinction between God’s portion and the priests’ portion. This is shared ground.

Leviticus 9:21

21Aaron waved the breasts and the right thigh for a wave offering before the LORD, as Moses commanded.

Arminian

The breasts and right thigh are waved before the LORD, then become the priests’ portion (Lev 7:30–34).

Arminian readers see God providing for those who serve him.

Reformed

The wave offering presents these portions to God before they are given to the priests.

Reformed readers note that the priests receive their provision from God’s hand. Both traditions read it the same way.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
