Passage

Leviticus 7

Verses 28–38 · WEB

Leviticus 7:28–38 completes the peace-offering law with the priests’ portions. The worshiper brings the fat and the breast with his own hands, the breast is waved before the LORD, and the right thigh is given to the officiating priest, as Aaron’s family’s portion forever. Verses 37–38 close the whole offering manual of chapters 1–7 and name its source in what the LORD commanded at Sinai. Both traditions read the passage as part of God’s ordered provision for worship, now fulfilled in Christ’s priesthood.

Leviticus 7:28

28The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

A final divine speech covers the priests’ portions from peace offerings. Arminians see God carefully providing for his servants.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and note that God explicitly names the priests’ share, so their support is God’s command, not a custom. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Leviticus 7:29

29“Speak to the children of Israel, saying, ‘He who offers the sacrifice of his peace offerings to the LORD shall bring his offering to the LORD out of the sacrifice of his peace offerings.

Arminian

The worshiper brings his own offering to the LORD from his peace offering. Arminians see the worshiper’s personal participation and responsibility in worship.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the worshiper himself presents the gift. Both traditions read the verse as emphasizing personal involvement in worship and agree on its meaning.

Leviticus 7:30

30With his own hands he shall bring the offerings of the LORD made by fire. He shall bring the fat with the breast, that the breast may be waved for a wave offering before the LORD.

Arminian

‘With his own hands’ he brings the fat with the breast for waving before the LORD. Arminians value this personal, hands-on worship as an expression of willing devotion.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree on the personal act and note that ‘wave offering’ describes a gesture presenting the portion to God before it is given to the priests. Both traditions agree here.

Leviticus 7:31

31The priest shall burn the fat on the altar, but the breast shall be Aaron’s and his sons’.

Arminian

The fat is burned and the breast goes to Aaron and his sons. Arminians see God receiving his portion first, with the priests supported from what is presented to him.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree on the sequence and note the order: God first, then his servants. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Leviticus 7:32

32The right thigh you shall give to the priest for a heave offering out of the sacrifices of your peace offerings.

Arminian

The right thigh is given to the priest as a heave offering. Arminians see the right thigh, often considered a choice portion, as God’s generous provision for his ministers.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and connect the principle with Galatians 6:6. Both traditions read the verse as establishing support for ministry, and they agree on it.

Leviticus 7:33

33He among the sons of Aaron who offers the blood of the peace offerings, and the fat, shall have the right thigh for a portion.

Arminian

The priest who offers the blood and fat receives the right thigh. Arminians note that the one who performs the service receives its benefit.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the officiating priest’s portion is tied to his service. Both traditions agree on the meaning of the verse.

Leviticus 7:34

34For the waved breast and the heaved thigh I have taken from the children of Israel out of the sacrifices of their peace offerings, and have given them to Aaron the priest and to his sons as their portion forever from the children of Israel.’ ”

Arminian

God says he has taken the breast and thigh from Israel and given them to Aaron and his sons as a lasting portion. Arminians note that the priests’ share is God’s gift, not a tax they impose.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that God himself assigns the portion. Both traditions see 1 Corinthians 9:13–14 applying this principle to gospel ministers.

Leviticus 7:35

35This is the consecrated portion of Aaron, and the consecrated portion of his sons, out of the offerings of the LORD made by fire, in the day when he presented them to minister to the LORD in the priest’s office;

Arminian

This is the consecrated portion of Aaron and his sons from the day they were presented to serve. Arminians note that God’s provision accompanies his calling.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that God provides for those he calls into his service. Both traditions agree on the meaning of the verse.

Leviticus 7:36

36which the LORD commanded to be given them of the children of Israel, in the day that he anointed them. It is their portion forever throughout their generations.

Arminian

The LORD commanded this on the day he anointed them, a portion forever. Arminians see the lasting arrangement as God’s faithfulness to the priestly family.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and see the Levitical priesthood’s lasting provision fulfilled and transformed in Christ, the eternal priest (Hebrews 7:24–25). Both traditions agree here.

Leviticus 7:37

37This is the law of the burnt offering, the meal offering, the sin offering, the trespass offering, the consecration, and the sacrifice of peace offerings

Arminian

This summary lists burnt, meal, sin, trespass, consecration, and peace offerings. Arminians see the offerings together as God’s full provision for worship and restored relationship.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and note that ‘consecration’ points ahead to chapter 8’s ordination of the priests. Both traditions see all these offerings fulfilled in Christ (Hebrews 10:1–14).

Leviticus 7:38

38which the LORD commanded Moses in Mount Sinai in the day that he commanded the children of Israel to offer their offerings to the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai.

Arminian

The LORD commanded these at Mount Sinai, in the wilderness of Sinai. Arminians stress that these laws come from God’s covenant revelation, not human invention.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and stress that these laws were given within the covenant God made at Sinai. Both traditions read the closing note as placing Leviticus 1–7 within God’s covenant relationship with Israel.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
