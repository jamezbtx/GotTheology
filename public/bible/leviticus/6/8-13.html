Passage

Leviticus 6

Verses 8–13 · WEB

Leviticus 6:8–13 begins a second section, the ‘laws’ (torot) of the offerings, now addressed to Aaron and his sons. Versification: English 6:8–13 is Hebrew 6:1–6; this page keeps the WEB numbers. Chapters 1–5 told worshipers what to bring, and 6:8–7:38 tells priests how to handle each offering. This paragraph covers the burnt offering’s overnight burning, how the ashes are removed, and the fire that must never go out. Both traditions read the perpetual fire as a sign of continual worship and of God’s unbroken provision for approach, and both see that provision fulfilled in Christ.

Leviticus 6:8

8The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

A new speech formula opens the priests’ instructions. Arminians note that God gives priests detailed responsibilities, so ministry in his presence is a matter of faithful obedience and accountable stewardship.

Reformed

Reformed readers emphasize that worship is ordered by God’s explicit command, not by human preference, a point often tied to the regulative principle. Arminians agree that God, not the worshiper, sets the terms of approach, though the two traditions apply that principle to Christian worship with different degrees of strictness.

Leviticus 6:9

9“Command Aaron and his sons, saying, ‘This is the law of the burnt offering: the burnt offering shall be on the hearth on the altar all night until the morning; and the fire of the altar shall be kept burning on it.

Arminian

The burnt offering stays on the altar all night. Arminians see in the unbroken burning a picture of continual dedication: the worshiper’s whole life offered to God (compare Romans 12:1).

Reformed

Reformed interpreters read the all-night offering as continual consecration and as a reminder that Israel’s access to God rested on an ongoing sacrifice. Both see its fulfillment in Christ’s once-for-all offering, which never needs repeating (Hebrews 10:12–14).

Leviticus 6:10

10The priest shall put on his linen garment, and he shall put on his linen trousers upon his body; and he shall remove the ashes from where the fire has consumed the burnt offering on the altar, and he shall put them beside the altar.

Arminian

Even removing ashes requires holy linen garments. Arminians note that no task in God’s service is too small to be done reverently, and that ordinary duties in his presence carry dignity.

Reformed

Reformed readers also highlight that the priest wears linen while handling holy things at the altar. Both traditions agree that the verse teaches reverence in the ordinary work of worship, without reading more doctrine into the procedure than the text gives.

Leviticus 6:11

11He shall take off his garments, and put on other garments, and carry the ashes outside the camp to a clean place.

Arminian

The priest changes garments before carrying the ashes outside the camp to a clean place. Arminians note the careful line drawn between holy space and ordinary space, which teaches that God’s holiness shapes every part of community life.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see the same distinction between holy and common. Some on both sides connect ‘outside the camp’ with Hebrews 13:11–13, though that passage speaks directly of sin-offering bodies burned outside, so the link to the ashes is illustrative rather than exact.

Leviticus 6:12

12The fire on the altar shall be kept burning on it, it shall not go out; and the priest shall burn wood on it every morning. He shall lay the burnt offering in order upon it, and shall burn on it the fat of the peace offerings.

Arminian

The priest must feed the fire every morning, lay the burnt offering in order, and burn the fat of the peace offerings. Arminians see a call to ongoing, deliberate faithfulness. The fire is kept burning by daily, responsible care.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the fire, lit by God according to 9:24, is maintained by the priests’ obedience. Both traditions hold together God’s gift and human faithfulness in keeping it, and neither treats the priests’ care as earning God’s favor.

Leviticus 6:13

13Fire shall be kept burning on the altar continually; it shall not go out.

Arminian

‘Fire shall be kept burning on the altar continually; it shall not go out.’ Arminians often apply this devotionally to continual prayer and steady devotion, while recognizing that its first sense is the tabernacle altar.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters likewise read the perpetual fire as God’s unbroken provision for approach and continual worship. Both see it fulfilled in Christ’s finished sacrifice and ongoing intercession (Hebrews 7:25), and both caution against allegorizing the detail beyond this.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
