Passage

Leviticus 6

Verses 14–23 · WEB

Leviticus 6:14–23 gives the priests’ law of the meal offering. Versification: English 6:14–23 is Hebrew 6:7–16; this page keeps the WEB numbers. A memorial handful is burned and the rest becomes a most-holy food for the priests, eaten without yeast in the court. Verses 19–23 add the high priest’s own daily grain offering, which is burned entirely because a priest may not eat from an offering made for himself. Arminian and Reformed readers share the main points: holiness, provision for those who serve, and the priest’s own need of offering, which Hebrews contrasts with Christ.

Leviticus 6:14

14“ ‘This is the law of the meal offering: the sons of Aaron shall offer it before the LORD, before the altar.

Arminian

The ‘law of the meal offering’ for the priests continues chapter 2. Arminians note that grain offerings, from the fruit of human labor, show that ordinary work and provision can be offered to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that the grain offering acknowledges God as giver of daily bread and Lord of the harvest. Both traditions read it as an offering of dedication and thanks rather than atonement for sin, and on this they agree.

Leviticus 6:15

15He shall take from there his handful of the fine flour of the meal offering, and of its oil, and all the frankincense which is on the meal offering, and shall burn it on the altar for a pleasant aroma, as its memorial portion, to the LORD.

Arminian

A handful with oil and all the frankincense is burned as a ‘memorial portion’, a pleasant aroma. Arminians see God’s delight in heartfelt worship that returns to him a token of what he has given.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters note that the memorial portion presents the whole offering before God while most of it goes to the priests. Both connect ‘pleasant aroma’ with Ephesians 5:2, where Christ’s self-offering is called a fragrant sacrifice.

Leviticus 6:16

16That which is left of it Aaron and his sons shall eat. It shall be eaten without yeast in a holy place. They shall eat it in the court of the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

The rest is eaten by the priests, unleavened, in a holy place. Arminians see God providing for those who serve him from the gifts of his people, a principle Paul applies in 1 Corinthians 9:13–14.

Reformed

Reformed readers draw the same principle of supporting ministers. They add that the location (‘in the court of the Tent of Meeting’) keeps even the priests’ meal within the bounds of holiness. Both traditions agree here.

Leviticus 6:17

17It shall not be baked with yeast. I have given it as their portion of my offerings made by fire. It is most holy, as are the sin offering and the trespass offering.

Arminian

No yeast, and it is ‘most holy’, like the sin and trespass offerings. Arminians note that God calls it his own gift (‘I have given it’), which makes the priests’ share a matter of grace, not entitlement.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters note that yeast was excluded from offerings burned on the altar (2:11) and that the priests’ portion comes as God’s gift. Many readers in both traditions treat yeast here symbolically, though the text itself gives no stated reason, so caution is appropriate.

Leviticus 6:18

18Every male among the children of Aaron shall eat of it, as their portion forever throughout your generations, from the offerings of the LORD made by fire. Whoever touches them shall be holy.’ ”

Arminian

Only males of Aaron’s line eat it, ‘a portion forever’, and whatever touches it becomes holy. Arminians read the contagious holiness as a warning that holy things are not to be handled casually.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that ‘whoever touches them shall be holy’ may mean that contact brings a person or object under the sanctuary’s claim. Interpreters debate the precise sense, and both traditions agree on the general point that holiness has real effects.

Leviticus 6:19

19The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

A new divine speech introduces the priests’ own offering. Arminians note that the priests who mediate for others must themselves come before God with offerings.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see the same truth: earthly priests are sinners and worshipers like everyone else. Both traditions use this to set up Hebrews’ contrast with Christ, who needed no offering for himself (Hebrews 7:26–27).

Leviticus 6:20

20“This is the offering of Aaron and of his sons, which they shall offer to the LORD in the day when he is anointed: one tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a meal offering perpetually, half of it in the morning, and half of it in the evening.

Arminian

A tenth of an ephah, half morning and half evening, ‘in the day when he is anointed’ and then continually. Arminians see the high priest’s daily offering as a model of steady, ongoing devotion. Interpreters debate whether ‘perpetually’ means from the day of anointing onward or only on that day.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same interpretive question and generally read the offering as a regular one. Both traditions note that the morning and evening pattern echoes the daily burnt offerings (Exodus 29:38–42) and that an ephah is often estimated at about 22 liters.

Leviticus 6:21

21It shall be made with oil in a griddle. When it is soaked, you shall bring it in. You shall offer the meal offering in baked pieces for a pleasant aroma to the LORD.

Arminian

Made with oil on a griddle, soaked, and offered in baked pieces as a pleasant aroma. Arminians note that the careful preparation shows that God values worship offered thoughtfully and well.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that care in preparation honors God. Both traditions note that some rare Hebrew terms in the verse make the exact preparation uncertain, and neither builds doctrine on the details.

Leviticus 6:22

22The anointed priest that will be in his place from among his sons shall offer it. By a statute forever, it shall be wholly burned to the LORD.

Arminian

The successor high priest continues the offering ‘by a statute forever’, and it is wholly burned. Arminians see in the line of successors a reminder that human priests die and must be replaced.

Reformed

Reformed readers draw the same contrast that Hebrews 7:23–24 makes: the Levitical priests were many because death stopped them, while Christ holds his priesthood permanently. Both traditions read the verse this way.

Leviticus 6:23

23Every meal offering of a priest shall be wholly burned. It shall not be eaten.”

Arminian

‘Every meal offering of a priest shall be wholly burned. It shall not be eaten.’ Arminians note that a priest cannot benefit from his own offering, which underlines that it is wholly given to God.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the offering is given up completely. Both note the difference from ordinary grain offerings, where priests ate the remainder, and both read the rule as guarding the priest from profiting from his own offering.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
