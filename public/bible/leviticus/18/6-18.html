Passage

Leviticus 18

Verses 6–18 · WEB

This unit forbids sexual relations with close relatives, using the idiom of uncovering nakedness. The list protects the family from exploitation and confusion of roles, guarding the vulnerable within the household. Arminian and Reformed readers share these moral boundaries and read them as an expression of God’s care for family life, not as a matter of dispute between them.

Leviticus 18:6

6“ ‘None of you shall approach any close relatives, to uncover their nakedness: I am the LORD.

Arminian

No one is to approach close relatives sexually. Arminian readers see God protecting the family from exploitation. The refrain I am the LORD gives the command its authority.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God guarding the family as a basic social order. The heading summarizes the specific cases that follow. Both traditions affirm this moral boundary.

Leviticus 18:7

7“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father, nor the nakedness of your mother: she is your mother. You shall not uncover her nakedness.

Arminian

Relations with one’s father or mother are forbidden. Arminian readers see the most basic family bonds protected. The idiom of uncovering nakedness refers to sexual relations.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see honor for parents protected from violation. Both traditions read the verse as shared moral teaching. The repetition she is your mother underlines the gravity of the offense.

Leviticus 18:8

8“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father’s wife. It is your father’s nakedness.

Arminian

Relations with a father’s wife are forbidden. Arminian readers see this protecting a stepmother and the father’s marriage. Paul confronts this very sin in 1 Corinthians 5:1.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the binding force of this law reaffirmed in the New Testament church. Both traditions share this reading. Marriage creates a bond the son must respect.

Leviticus 18:9

9“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your sister, the daughter of your father, or the daughter of your mother, whether born at home or born abroad.

Arminian

Relations with a sister or half-sister are forbidden, wherever she was raised. Arminian readers see protection extended to every sibling relationship. Family boundaries apply whether or not the sister grew up in the same household.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the comprehensive scope of the prohibition. Both traditions affirm this protection for siblings. Kinship, not upbringing, defines the boundary, which protects vulnerable family members.

Leviticus 18:10

10“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your son’s daughter, or of your daughter’s daughter, even their nakedness; for theirs is your own nakedness.

Arminian

Relations with a granddaughter are forbidden, for her nakedness is one’s own. Arminian readers see the shared flesh of family as the reason. The command protects the young from older relatives.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the kinship reasoning that grounds the law. Both traditions read the verse as shared moral teaching guarding the vulnerable. Older relatives must protect the young.

Leviticus 18:11

11“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father’s wife’s daughter, conceived by your father, since she is your sister.

Arminian

Relations with a father’s wife’s daughter are forbidden, since she is a sister. Arminian readers see family identity extending through marriage. The verse closes a possible loophole.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see precision in protecting family members. Interpreters discuss how this verse relates to verse 9, but the moral point is clear. Both traditions share it.

Leviticus 18:12

12“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father’s sister. She is your father’s near kinswoman.

Arminian

Relations with a father’s sister are forbidden. Arminian readers see protection extended to aunts. The phrase near kinswoman explains the reason. Kinship extends beyond the immediate household.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the circle of protected relationships widening. Both traditions affirm this boundary. The law protects relatives who might otherwise be vulnerable within an extended family household.

Leviticus 18:13

13“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your mother’s sister, for she is your mother’s near kinswoman.

Arminian

Relations with a mother’s sister are forbidden. Arminian readers see the same reasoning applied to the mother’s side. The law treats both sides of the family alike.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see consistency in the law’s protection. Both traditions read the verse as shared ground. Mother’s and father’s relatives receive equal protection under the same reasoning of near kinship.

Leviticus 18:14

14“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father’s brother. You shall not approach his wife. She is your aunt.

Arminian

Relations with an uncle’s wife are forbidden; she is an aunt. Arminian readers see marriage creating real family bonds. Relatives by marriage are also protected.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see affinity treated seriously alongside blood relations. Both traditions share this reading. Marriage makes an uncle’s wife a true relative, and the law treats that bond as real.

Leviticus 18:15

15“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your daughter-in-law. She is your son’s wife. You shall not uncover her nakedness.

Arminian

Relations with a daughter-in-law are forbidden. Arminian readers see protection for a son’s wife within the household. The story of Judah and Tamar in Genesis 38 shows how such boundaries could be confused.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the integrity of a son’s marriage guarded. Both traditions affirm this protection. The father’s authority in the household must never become a means of exploiting his son’s wife.

Leviticus 18:16

16“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of your brother’s wife. It is your brother’s nakedness.

Arminian

Relations with a brother’s wife are forbidden. Arminian readers see the brother’s marriage honored. Deuteronomy 25:5–10 gives a specific exception for levirate marriage after a brother dies childless.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise note the relation between this law and levirate marriage. John the Baptist rebuked Herod on this ground in Mark 6:18. Both traditions share this reading.

Leviticus 18:17

17“ ‘You shall not uncover the nakedness of a woman and her daughter. You shall not take her son’s daughter, or her daughter’s daughter, to uncover her nakedness. They are near kinswomen. It is wickedness.

Arminian

Relations with a woman and her daughter or granddaughter are forbidden as wickedness. Arminian readers see the law preventing exploitation across generations. Such arrangements corrupt the family deeply.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the strong language marking the gravity of the offense. Both traditions affirm this moral boundary. Calling it wickedness marks a deep corruption of family trust across generations.

Leviticus 18:18

18“ ‘You shall not take a wife in addition to her sister, to be a rival, to uncover her nakedness, while her sister is still alive.

Arminian

A man may not take his wife’s sister as a rival wife while his wife lives. Arminian readers see concern for the wife’s dignity and family peace. Jacob’s marriage to Leah and Rachel shows the rivalry such unions caused.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see protection against rivalry and hurt. Interpreters in both traditions discuss whether the verse addresses only sisters or polygamy more broadly. The concern for the wife’s welfare is shared.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
