Passage

Colossians 2

Verses 16–23 · ESV

Because the debt is canceled and the powers disarmed in Christ, Paul frees the church from judgmental pressure over food, drink, festivals, new moons, and Sabbaths—shadows whose substance belongs to Christ. He warns against disqualification through ascetic show, angel worship, and vision-boasting that fails to hold the Head, and against elemental regulations that look wise but cannot restrain the flesh.

Colossians 2:16

16Therefore let no one pass judgment on you in questions of food and drink, or with regard to a festival or a new moon or a Sabbath.

Arminian

“Therefore” looks back to the cross’s cancelation and triumph. No one may reinstate a courtroom over meals and calendar days as if those ruled the conscience.

Arminian pastoral freedom: Christ frees believers from human religious verdicts that add shadows as terms of acceptance. Grace invites a clean conscience before God, not anxiety under food-and-festival judges.

Reformed

Judgment over diet and sacred days is ruled out by what God has already done in Christ. The church must not let shadow-keepers become the bench.

Reformed accent: Christian liberty here is gospel liberty. Belonging is settled at the cross, so ceremonial scorekeeping cannot reopen the case.

Colossians 2:17

17These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ.

Arminian

Shadows had a real pointing role; they were never the destination. The substance—the solid body—belongs to Christ.

Classical Arminians gladly keep Christ at the center: ceremonies may teach, but they must not replace the Savior to whom they pointed. Faith rests in Him, not in the silhouette.

Reformed

Paul’s shadow/substance contrast protects the sufficiency of Christ. Old-covenant patterns anticipated; they do not complete what the Son fulfills.

Reformed clarity: chasing the shadow after the substance has come is a category mistake. Christ is the reality; calendars and menus are not rivals to Him.

Colossians 2:18

18Let no one disqualify you, insisting on asceticism and worship of angels, going on in detail about visions, puffed up without reason by his sensuous mind,

Arminian

Another threat: spiritual showmanship—harsh treatment of the body, angel mediation, vision tours that inflate the ego while claiming humility.

Arminian warning: such religion can genuinely mislead and “disqualify” in the sense of cheating believers of their prize by false standards. Grace calls the church to refuse intimidation dressed as deeper piety.

Reformed

The opponents disqualify by insisting on extras: asceticism, angel worship, visionary detail. Pride hides under voluntary humility.

Reformed diagnosis: a sensuous mind can produce very religious talk. The danger is not too little spirituality but spirituality detached from Christ’s finished work.

Key contrast Both reject vision-and-ascetic elitism. Arminians typically stress the believer’s enabled duty to refuse disqualification; Reformed readers typically stress that God keeps His people by holding them to Christ rather than to inflated spiritual hierarchies.

Colossians 2:19

19and not holding fast to the Head, from whom the whole body, nourished and knit together through its joints and ligaments, grows with a growth that is from God.

Arminian

The root failure is not holding the Head. Real growth is God’s gift through Christ to a body nourished and knit together—not through private ladders around Jesus.

Arminian churchmanship: stay fastened to Christ and to His people. Grace grows the body as members remain in the Head who supplies life.

Reformed

Sever the Head and the body starves, however impressive the angel talk. Growth that is “from God” flows from Christ through the church’s connections.

Reformed comfort: God grows His church by union with the Head. Healthy joints and ligaments matter because they channel life from Christ, not because technique replaces Him.

Colossians 2:20

20If with Christ you died to the elemental spirits of the world, why, as if you were still alive in the world, do you submit to regulations—

Arminian

Death with Christ ends elemental slavery. Returning to regulation-religion acts as if that death never happened.

Classical Arminians press the incongruity: those who have died with Christ are called to live free from world-element bondage. Grace that united them to His death also summons a consistent walk.

Reformed

Union includes death to the elemental powers. Submission to their rulebook denies the new location believers already have in Christ.

Reformed logic: indicative first—you died with Christ—then the question that exposes relapse. Identity in the crucified Lord undercuts re-enslavement.

Colossians 2:21

21“Do not handle, Do not taste, Do not touch”

Arminian

Paul quotes the slogan-stack of restrictive religion. The phrases sound holy and urgent; they function as a new law of avoidance.

Arminian discernment: not every “don’t” is godliness. Grace produces holiness from life in Christ, not from a lengthening list of untouchables that replace the gospel.

Reformed

The triple prohibition satirizes man-made severity. Handle, taste, touch—control by taboo.

Reformed note: such commands often feel safer than faith in Christ. Paul exposes them as the world’s elementary grammar, not the Spirit’s way of holiness.

Colossians 2:22

22(referring to things that all perish as they are used)—according to human precepts and teachings?

Arminian

The objects of these rules perish with use. Building a righteousness system on perishable things, by human precepts, is a tragic misinvestment.

Arminian appeal: human teachings can bind consciences tightly and still miss Christ. Believers are free—and responsible—to measure every precept by the Lord they received.

Reformed

Perishable things cannot carry eternal weight. “Human precepts and teachings” names the authority problem: man speaking where Christ has freed.

Reformed sobriety: doctrines of men may look careful while remaining earthbound. Only Christ’s word and work secure a holiness that outlasts what perishes with use.

Colossians 2:23

23These have indeed an appearance of wisdom in promoting self-made religion and asceticism and severity to the body, but they are of no value in stopping the indulgence of the flesh.

Arminian

The final verdict: impressive packaging, empty power. Self-made religion, asceticism, and bodily severity look wise yet fail to stop fleshly indulgence.

Arminian pastoral realism: grace changes the heart in Christ; harsh religion often feeds pride while leaving appetite untouched. True wisdom stays with the Head, not with self-invented severity.

Reformed

Appearance of wisdom is not wisdom. Will-worship and severity can decorate the flesh rather than crucify it.

Reformed conclusion to the chapter’s warning: only union with the crucified and risen Christ restrains the flesh. Humanly invented religion cannot do what the gospel does.

Key contrast Both deny that ascetic self-made religion defeats the flesh. Arminians typically urge a grace-enabled turn from empty severity to life in Christ; Reformed readers typically insist that only God’s work in union with Christ—not human will-worship—actually breaks fleshly indulgence.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
