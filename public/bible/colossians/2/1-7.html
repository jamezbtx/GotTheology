Passage

Colossians 2

Verses 1–7 · ESV · Voices

Paul opens the heart of his struggle for Colossae and Laodicea: he wants believers he has never met face to face to be encouraged, knit together in love, and brought into the full assurance that God’s mystery is Christ. He warns against plausible delusion and calls them to walk in the Lord they have already received—rooted, built up, established, and thankful.

Colossians 2:1

1For I want you to know how great a struggle I have for you and for those at Laodicea and for all who have not seen me face to face,

Arminian

Paul’s “struggle” is pastoral and intercessory—agonizing care for churches he did not plant in person, including neighboring Laodicea. Absence does not cancel love.

Arminian warmth: the gospel creates real responsibility for people we may never meet. Grace enlarges the heart so that prayer and labor reach beyond our circle of face-to-face ministry.

Reformed

Paul wants them to know the weight of his conflict for them. Ministry is not casual; it is costly concern under Christ for the flock.

Reformed framing: the apostle’s struggle is itself a gift of providence—Christ caring for His churches through a servant who may be distant in body yet burdened in spirit.

Colossians 2:2

2that their hearts may be encouraged, being knit together in love, to reach all the riches of full assurance of understanding and the knowledge of God’s mystery, which is Christ,

Arminian

The goal is not cold information but encouraged hearts, bound together in love, moving toward rich assurance. The mystery is not a secret code—it is Christ Himself.

Classical Arminians gladly stress that full assurance is a treasure God intends believers to reach, as love and understanding grow under grace. Christ is offered as the knowable center of God’s saving purpose.

Reformed

Encouragement, unity in love, and assurance belong together. Paul does not separate doctrine from affection: love knits the body while understanding deepens.

Reformed accent: “God’s mystery, which is Christ” centers everything on the Son. Assurance is rich because the object of knowledge is Christ, not speculative ladders beyond Him.

Colossians 2:3

3in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.

Arminian

All true spiritual treasure is stored in Christ—not partly in Christ and partly in rival systems. Seekers need not hunt a higher wisdom elsewhere.

Arminian invitation: God sincerely sets Christ before the church as sufficient. Grace calls every believer to dig into Him rather than chase impressive alternatives.

Reformed

“Hidden” does not mean unavailable; it means located—wisdom’s vault is the person of Christ. Outside Him, the search for saving wisdom ends empty.

Reformed confidence: God has not left His people to incomplete revelation. In the Son are all the treasures; the church’s safety is to stay where God has placed the wealth.

Colossians 2:4

4I say this in order that no one may delude you with plausible arguments.

Arminian

The danger is not only obvious heresy but persuasive talk. Plausible arguments can sound spiritual while pulling hearts away from Christ.

Classical Arminians hear a real warning: hearers can be genuinely deceived and must stay alert. Grace enlightens and enables discernment; resistance to that light remains a serious possibility.

Reformed

Paul’s Christology is preventive medicine. He teaches who Christ is so that smooth speech cannot kidnap the church.

Reformed sobriety: delusion thrives where Christ is diminished. God preserves His people by the truth of the gospel, not by leaving them defenseless before clever words.

Key contrast Both treat plausible arguments as a real threat. Arminians typically stress enabled responsibility to refuse delusion; Reformed readers typically stress that steadfastness in Christ is secured by God’s preserving truth and grace.

Colossians 2:5

5For though I am absent in body, yet I am with you in spirit, rejoicing to see your good order and the firmness of your faith in Christ.

Arminian

Paul rejoices in their order and firm faith even from afar. True fellowship can be spiritual and affectionate without physical presence.

Arminian pastoral note: firmness of faith is something to celebrate and encourage. Grace has already produced a steadfast stance in Christ; the call is to continue walking in what they have received.

Reformed

Distance does not erase spiritual solidarity. Paul sees—and delights in—ordered life and firm faith as fruits worth rejoicing over.

Reformed reading: “firmness of your faith in Christ” locates stability in Christ Himself. Order and steadfastness are evidences of God’s work among them, not mere organizational polish.

Colossians 2:6

6Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him,

Arminian

Christian living has the same shape as Christian beginning: they received Christ Jesus as Lord; now they must walk in Him. Reception was not a temporary handshake.

Classical Arminians emphasize a living, ongoing walk. Justifying faith receives Christ; persevering grace enables continued trust and obedience—yet the walk is a real, responsible path of discipleship.

Reformed

“Received” and “walk” belong to one Lordship. The Christ they confessed is the Christ they inhabit daily.

Reformed pastoral weight: union with Christ is the root of ethics. We do not graduate from receiving Jesus into a self-powered walk; we continue in the same Lord who first claimed us.

Key contrast Shared ground: the Christian life continues as it began—in Christ the Lord. Arminians typically describe the walk as grace-enabled responsibility that must be exercised; Reformed readers typically describe it as the fruit of ongoing union secured by God’s preserving grace.

Colossians 2:7

7rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, abounding in thanksgiving.

Arminian

Mixed metaphors bless the same reality: rooted like a tree, built like a house, established in the faith they were taught. Gratitude is not an add-on; it overflows.

Arminian encouragement: teaching, rootedness, and thanksgiving mark a healthy walk. Grace plants and builds; believers abound in thanks as they remain in the Christ they learned.

Reformed

Rooted, built, established—stability language piled high. The faith already taught is the soil and the structure; thanksgiving is the atmosphere of a secure people.

Reformed comfort: growth is “in him.” God establishes His people in the apostolic faith so that gratitude, not anxiety or novelty-hunting, becomes their abundance.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Paul opens the heart of his struggle for Colossae and Laodicea: he wants believers he has never met face to face to—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Colossians 2:1–7.

Arminian Notable voices

“How great a conflict - Of care, desire, prayer. As many as have not seen my face - Therefore, in writing to the Colossians, he refrains from those familiar appellations, "Brethren," "Beloved." Unto all riches of the full assurance of understanding, unto the acknowledgment of the mystery of God - That is, unto the fullest and clearest understanding and knowledge of the gospel.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Col 2:1–7)

Reformed Notable voices

“He declares his affection towards them, that he may have more credit and authority; for we readily believe those whom we know to be desirous of our welfare. It is also an evidence of no ordinary affection, that he was concerned about them in the midst of death, that is, when he was in danger of his life; and that he may express the more emphatically the intensity of his affection and concern, he calls it a conflict.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Colossians (on Col 2:1–7)
