Passage

Colossians 4

Verses 1–6 · WEB · Voices

Paul closes the household code and turns to prayer and wise witness. Masters answer to the heavenly Master; the church perseveres in thankful prayer for open doors; speech and conduct toward outsiders are to be gracious and wise.

Colossians 4:1

1Masters, give to your servants that which is just and equal, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven.

Arminian

Power is accountable. Masters must give what is “just and fair,” because they themselves answer to a Master in heaven. Rank does not erase moral responsibility; grace that saves also summons the powerful to restraint and equity.

Classical Arminian pastoral reading: God sincerely holds every person—including those with social advantage—responsible to do right. Enabling grace does not excuse injustice; it exposes it and calls for change under the eye of heaven.

Reformed

The household code ends where sovereignty begins: every earthly master stands under the Lordship of Christ. Justice and fairness are not optional kindnesses but duties before God.

Providence and Lordship cut both ways. God orders stations, yet He also judges those who abuse them. The heavenly Master is no respecter of persons; assurance for the lowly and warning for the high travel together.

Key contrast Both insist masters are accountable to God and must treat servants justly. Arminians emphasize resistible grace that enables obedience (or leaves injustice without excuse); Reformed readers stress Christ’s effectual Lordship that binds the conscience and will judge every abuse of power.

Colossians 4:2

2Continue steadfastly in prayer, watching in it with thanksgiving,

Arminian

Prayer is perseverance, not a mood. “Continue steadfastly,” stay awake, and wrap watchfulness in thanksgiving. Grace invites a living conversation with God that believers must not neglect.

Prevenient and assisting grace keep the door of prayer open; thanksgiving guards the heart from dull duty. The command assumes responsibility—keep praying, keep watching, keep giving thanks.

Reformed

Persistent, watchful, thankful prayer is the church’s appointed means under God’s rule. Steadfastness is not self-generated stamina; it is Spirit-worked loyalty to the means God has given.

God who commands prayer also works prayerfulness in His people. Watchfulness resists spiritual sleep; thanksgiving remembers that every answered and unanswered petition sits under a faithful Father.

Colossians 4:3

3praying together for us also, that God may open to us a door for the word, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I am also in bonds,

Arminian

Paul asks prayer for an open door—mission depends on God’s initiative and the church’s intercession. Chains have not canceled the call to speak the mystery of Christ.

Wesley’s heirs hear both: God opens doors, and the church truly partners by praying. Grace that enables witness remains resistible in hearers, yet the apostle still expects God to create opportunity and clarity for the gospel.

Reformed

Even an apostle in prison seeks God to open the door. Opportunity for the word is God’s gift, not human cleverness. The “mystery of Christ” is the gospel once hidden, now to be declared.

Providence can cage the messenger without silencing the message. God opens doors as He wills; the church’s prayers are means He uses to advance a mission already certain in His purpose.

Key contrast Both treat gospel advance as God-dependent and prayer seriously. Arminians often emphasize sincere partnership and doors that may be refused by hearers; Reformed readers often emphasize God’s effectual opening of opportunity according to His saving purpose.

Colossians 4:4

4that I may reveal it as I ought to speak.

Arminian

Clarity is a moral “ought.” Paul wants to speak the mystery plainly—grace does not baptize fog. Hearers deserve a lucid gospel they can actually receive or refuse.

Assisting grace frees the tongue for faithfulness; the speaker remains responsible to make Christ clear, not impressive.

Reformed

Clarity is obedience. The gospel’s mystery is deep, yet its proclamation should be plain. Paul seeks not novelty but faithfulness in speech.

God who opens the door also grants utterance (cf. Eph 6:19–20). Dependence and duty meet—pray for clarity, then speak as you ought.

Colossians 4:5

5Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time.

Arminian

Mission has a public face: walk wisely toward outsiders and redeem the time. Conduct can adorn or obscure the message grace offers to all.

Opportunities are serious and can be missed. Enabling grace trains wise presence; believers remain responsible not to waste the season God gives for witness and love.

Reformed

Wisdom toward outsiders is discipleship under Christ’s Lordship. Time is a stewardship in a world God rules; making the best use is purposeful living, not frantic activism.

The church’s public walk is part of God’s means for displaying the gospel. Providence supplies the moments; grace forms the wisdom that spends them well.

Colossians 4:6

6Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, that you may know how you ought to answer each one.

Arminian

Paul treats everyday speech as something that matters. Speech should be gracious and seasoned—neither bland nor harsh—so answers fit each person. Grace that saves the tongue also summons it to fit reply.

Classical Arminian pastoral note: God enables fitting speech, yet we must not excuse careless talk. Every outsider is a neighbor who may be helped or hindered by how we answer.

Reformed

Gracious, salty speech comes from hearts claimed by Christ. “Always” sets a high bar; “each one” requires discernment, not a script.

The Spirit who regenerates also seasons the mouth. Answers are owed as duty, and they display the grace that first answered us in the gospel.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What earlier interpreters on each side wrote about Colossians 4:1–6.

Arminian Notable voices

“Equitable - Even beyond the letter of your contract. That God would open to us a door of utterance - That is, give us utterance, that we "may open our mouth boldly," Eph. vi, 19, and give us an opportunity of speaking, so that none may be able to hinder.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Col 4:1–6)

Reformed Notable voices

“He mentions first, what is just, by which term he expresses that kindness, as to which he has given injunction in the Epistle to the Ephesians. (Ephesians 6:8.) But as masters, looking down as it were from aloft, despise the condition of servants, so that they think that they are bound by no law, Paul brings them under control, because both are equally under subjection to the authority of God.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Colossians (on Col 4:1–6)
