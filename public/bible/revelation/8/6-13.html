Passage

Revelation 8

Verses 6–13 · WEB

The first four trumpets bring partial judgments on a third of the earth, sea, rivers, and heavens, echoing the plagues of Egypt. An eagle cries woe on those who dwell on the earth because of the trumpets still to come.

Revelation 8:6

6The seven angels who had the seven trumpets prepared themselves to sound.

Arminian

The angels prepare to sound.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the orderly unfolding of God’s plan.

Revelation 8:7

7The first sounded, and there followed hail and fire, mixed with blood, and they were thrown to the earth. One third of the earth was burned up, and one third of the trees were burned up, and all green grass was burned up.

Arminian

Hail and fire recall the seventh plague in Egypt. The judgments affect a third, so they are partial and meant to call people to repent.

Reformed

Reformed readers see partial judgments as warnings that point to the final judgment.

Revelation 8:8

8The second angel sounded, and something like a great burning mountain was thrown into the sea. One third of the sea became blood,

Arminian

A burning mountain thrown into the sea turns a third of it to blood.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the plagues on Egypt repeated on a wider scale.

Revelation 8:9

9and one third of the living creatures which were in the sea died. One third of the ships were destroyed.

Arminian

A third of sea creatures and ships are destroyed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment on commerce and life alike.

Revelation 8:10

10The third angel sounded, and a great star fell from the sky, burning like a torch, and it fell on one third of the rivers, and on the springs of water.

Arminian

A star falls on rivers and springs.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment touching the sources of life.

Revelation 8:11

11The name of the star is “Wormwood.” One third of the waters became wormwood. Many people died from the waters, because they were made bitter.

Arminian

Wormwood makes the waters bitter, recalling Jeremiah 9:15.

Reformed

Reformed readers see bitterness as the fruit of idolatry.

Revelation 8:12

12The fourth angel sounded, and one third of the sun was struck, and one third of the moon, and one third of the stars, so that one third of them would be darkened; and the day wouldn’t shine for one third of it, and the night in the same way.

Arminian

A third of the lights are darkened, recalling the ninth plague.

Reformed

Reformed readers see darkness as a sign of judgment.

Revelation 8:13

13I saw, and I heard an eagle, flying in mid heaven, saying with a loud voice, “Woe! Woe! Woe to those who dwell on the earth, because of the other blasts of the trumpets of the three angels, who are yet to sound!”

Arminian

The eagle’s threefold woe warns that worse is coming. Interpreters of every school agree that the trumpets escalate.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the woes as a call to repent before the final judgment.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
