Passage

Revelation 18

Verses 1–8 · WEB

An angel announces that Babylon the great has fallen. A voice from heaven calls God’s people to come out of her so that they will not share in her sins or her plagues. Her sins have piled up to heaven, and her judgment will come in a single day.

Revelation 18:1

1After these things, I saw another angel coming down out of the sky, having great authority. The earth was illuminated with his glory.

Arminian

An angel with great authority lights the earth with his glory.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s authority over judgment.

Revelation 18:2

2He cried with a mighty voice, saying, “Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great, and she has become a habitation of demons, a prison of every unclean spirit, and a prison of every unclean and hated bird!

Arminian

“Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great,” echoing Isaiah 21:9.

Reformed

Reformed readers see certain judgment spoken as already done.

Revelation 18:3

3For all the nations have drunk of the wine of the wrath of her sexual immorality, the kings of the earth committed sexual immorality with her, and the merchants of the earth grew rich from the abundance of her luxury.”

Arminian

The nations and kings joined in her immorality and the merchants grew rich.

Reformed

Reformed readers see idolatry and greed joined.

Revelation 18:4

4I heard another voice from heaven, saying, “Come out of her, my people, that you have no participation in her sins, and that you don’t receive of her plagues,

Arminian

“Come out of her, my people.” Arminian readers see a call to separate from sin, which believers must heed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God calling his people out of the world’s idolatry, as he called Israel out of Babylon.

Revelation 18:5

5for her sins have reached to the sky, and God has remembered her iniquities.

Arminian

Her sins are piled up to heaven.

Reformed

Reformed readers see accumulated guilt.

Revelation 18:6

6Return to her just as she returned, and repay her double as she did, and according to her works. In the cup which she mixed, mix to her double.

Arminian

She is repaid double for her deeds.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s just retribution.

Revelation 18:7

7However much she glorified herself and grew wanton, so much give her of torment and mourning. For she says in her heart, ‘I sit a queen, and am no widow, and will in no way see mourning.’

Arminian

Her self-glorification will be matched by torment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see pride brought low.

Revelation 18:8

8Therefore in one day her plagues will come: death, mourning, and famine; and she will be utterly burned with fire, for the Lord God who has judged her is strong.

Arminian

In one day her plagues will come, because the Lord God who judges her is strong.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sudden and certain judgment.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
