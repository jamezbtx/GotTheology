Passage

Revelation 18

Verses 9–24 · WEB

Kings, merchants, and seafarers mourn Babylon’s fall from a distance as her wealth vanishes in an hour. Heaven, saints, apostles, and prophets are called to rejoice, because God has judged her. A mighty angel throws a millstone into the sea as a sign that the city will be found no more, for in her was found the blood of prophets and saints.

Revelation 18:9

9The kings of the earth who committed sexual immorality and lived wantonly with her will weep and wail over her, when they look at the smoke of her burning,

Arminian

The kings who shared her luxury weep.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the grief of those whose hopes were in the world.

Revelation 18:10

10standing far away for the fear of her torment, saying, ‘Woe, woe, the great city, Babylon, the strong city! For your judgment has come in one hour.’

Arminian

They stand far off, afraid of her torment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see self-interest even in mourning.

Revelation 18:11

11The merchants of the earth weep and mourn over her, for no one buys their merchandise any more:

Arminian

The merchants weep because no one buys their goods.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grief over lost profit.

Revelation 18:12

12merchandise of gold, silver, precious stones, pearls, fine linen, purple, silk, scarlet, all expensive wood, every vessel of ivory, every vessel made of most precious wood, and of brass, and iron, and marble;

Arminian

Luxury goods are listed: gold, silver, jewels, fine cloth.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the wealth of the world system.

Revelation 18:13

13and cinnamon, incense, perfume, frankincense, wine, olive oil, fine flour, wheat, cattle, sheep, horses, chariots, and people’s bodies and souls.

Arminian

The list ends with bodies and human souls, a condemnation of the slave trade.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the dehumanizing cost of the world’s commerce.

Revelation 18:14

14The fruits which your soul lusted after have been lost to you. All things that were dainty and sumptuous have perished from you, and you will find them no more at all.

Arminian

The fruit her soul desired is gone.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the emptiness of worldly desire.

Revelation 18:15

15The merchants of these things, who were made rich by her, will stand far away for the fear of her torment, weeping and mourning,

Arminian

The merchants stand far off.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same distancing.

Revelation 18:16

16saying, ‘Woe, woe, the great city, she who was dressed in fine linen, purple, and scarlet, and decked with gold and precious stones and pearls!

Arminian

They lament the city clothed in luxury.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the outward splendor.

Revelation 18:17

17For in an hour such great riches are made desolate.’ Every ship master, and everyone who sails anywhere, and mariners, and as many as gain their living by sea, stood far away,

Arminian

In one hour such wealth is laid waste.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the suddenness of judgment.

Revelation 18:18

18and cried out as they looked at the smoke of her burning, saying, ‘What is like the great city?’

Arminian

Seafarers cry, “What is like the great city?”

Reformed

Reformed readers see the world’s admiration for its own power.

Revelation 18:19

19They cast dust on their heads, and cried, weeping and mourning, saying, ‘Woe, woe, the great city, in which all who had their ships in the sea were made rich by reason of her great wealth!’ For she is made desolate in one hour.

Arminian

They throw dust on their heads in grief.

Reformed

Reformed readers see mourning over lost wealth.

Revelation 18:20

20“Rejoice over her, O heaven, you saints, apostles, and prophets, for God has judged your judgment on her.”

Arminian

Heaven, saints, apostles, and prophets rejoice because God has judged her. Arminian readers see justice for the persecuted rather than personal revenge.

Reformed

Reformed readers see vindication of God’s people and his justice.

Revelation 18:21

21A mighty angel took up a stone like a great millstone and cast it into the sea, saying, “Thus with violence will Babylon, the great city, be thrown down, and will be found no more at all.

Arminian

A millstone thrown into the sea signals permanent destruction, as in Jeremiah 51:63–64.

Reformed

Reformed readers see final judgment.

Revelation 18:22

22The voice of harpists, minstrels, flute players, and trumpeters will be heard no more at all in you. No craftsman of whatever craft will be found any more at all in you. The sound of a mill will be heard no more at all in you.

Arminian

Music and craft will be heard no more.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the end of ordinary life in the city.

Revelation 18:23

23The light of a lamp will shine no more at all in you. The voice of the bridegroom and of the bride will be heard no more at all in you, for your merchants were the princes of the earth; for with your sorcery all the nations were deceived.

Arminian

Her sorceries deceived all nations.

Reformed

Reformed readers see deception at the heart of her power.

Revelation 18:24

24In her was found the blood of prophets and of saints, and of all who have been slain on the earth.”

Arminian

The blood of prophets and saints was found in her.

Reformed

Reformed readers see persecution as the deepest charge.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
