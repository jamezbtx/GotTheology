Passage

Revelation 6

Verses 9–17 · WEB

The fifth seal reveals the souls of martyrs under the altar crying out for justice. They receive white robes and are told to wait until the number of their fellow servants is complete. The sixth seal brings cosmic upheaval, and people of every rank hide from the face of the one on the throne and from the wrath of the Lamb.

Revelation 6:9

9When he opened the fifth seal, I saw underneath the altar the souls of those who had been killed for the Word of God, and for the testimony of the Lamb which they had.

Arminian

The martyrs were killed for the word of God and their testimony. Arminian readers see faithful witnesses who held fast to the end.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the souls under the altar as the faithful dead, alive with God before the resurrection.

Revelation 6:10

10They cried with a loud voice, saying, “How long, Master, the holy and true, until you judge and avenge our blood on those who dwell on the earth?”

Arminian

“How long?” echoes the psalms of lament. The cry is for justice, not personal revenge. Arminian readers see God hearing the prayers of the suffering.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the prayer as an appeal to God’s holy justice, which he will answer in his time.

Revelation 6:11

11A long white robe was given to each of them. They were told that they should rest yet for a while, until their fellow servants and their brothers, who would also be killed even as they were, should complete their course.

Arminian

The martyrs are given white robes and told to rest until the number of their fellow servants is complete. Arminian readers see God’s patience allowing more time for the gospel and for repentance.

Reformed

Reformed readers often see in the “number” God’s fixed purpose, with history moving toward an appointed completion.

Key contrast Arminian readers see the delay as God’s patience and the time it allows for repentance. Reformed readers see a number known to God and a history moving toward its appointed end.

Revelation 6:12

12I saw when he opened the sixth seal, and there was a great earthquake. The sun became black as sackcloth made of hair, and the whole moon became as blood.

Arminian

The sixth seal brings an earthquake and darkened sun and moon. Some read this as literal cosmic collapse at the end, while others read it as prophetic imagery for the fall of powers, as in Isaiah 13 and Joel 2.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same range of interpretations, with many seeing the language of the final day.

Revelation 6:13

13The stars of the sky fell to the earth, like a fig tree dropping its unripe figs when it is shaken by a great wind.

Arminian

Falling stars like unripe figs shaken by wind continue the imagery of upheaval.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the shaking of the created order before the Judge.

Revelation 6:14

14The sky was removed like a scroll when it is rolled up. Every mountain and island was moved out of its place.

Arminian

The sky rolled up like a scroll echoes Isaiah 34:4.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the end of the present order.

Revelation 6:15

15The kings of the earth, the princes, the commanding officers, the rich, the strong, and every slave and free person, hid themselves in the caves and in the rocks of the mountains.

Arminian

People of every rank, from kings to slaves, hide. No status protects anyone in the day of judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see all people equal before God’s judgment.

Revelation 6:16

16They told the mountains and the rocks, “Fall on us, and hide us from the face of him who sits on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb,

Arminian

“The wrath of the Lamb” is a striking phrase. The one who was slain is also the judge. Arminian readers see that rejecting the Lamb’s grace leaves only his judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as both Savior and Judge, and the terror of those who face him without refuge in his grace.

Revelation 6:17

17for the great day of his wrath has come, and who is able to stand?”

Arminian

“Who is able to stand?” Chapter 7 answers the question with the sealed servants and the great multitude.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the answer in chapter 7: those sealed by God and washed in the Lamb’s blood.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
