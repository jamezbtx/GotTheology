Passage

Revelation 4

Verses 1–11 · WEB

John is called up through an open door into heaven and sees a throne with one seated on it, surrounded by a rainbow, twenty-four elders, seven lamps of fire, and four living creatures. The creatures praise God without ceasing as holy, and the elders cast their crowns before the throne, confessing that he is worthy because he created all things.

Revelation 4:1

1After these things I looked and saw a door opened in heaven; and the first voice that I heard, like a trumpet speaking with me, was one saying, “Come up here, and I will show you the things which must happen after this.”

Arminian

The open door and the call to “come up here” begin the main visions. Some pretribulational futurists see John’s ascent as a picture of the church’s removal before the tribulation, while most other interpreters take it simply as John’s prophetic experience.

Reformed

Reformed readers generally take the call as John’s visionary ascent, not a picture of the church’s removal. The point is that history is about to be seen from heaven’s perspective.

Revelation 4:2

2Immediately I was in the Spirit. Behold, there was a throne set in heaven, and one sitting on the throne

Arminian

At the center of heaven is a throne. Arminian readers see God’s rule as the starting point for understanding everything that follows, including the suffering of the churches.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the throne as the central image of the book: God reigns, and everything in history unfolds under his sovereign rule.

Revelation 4:3

3that looked like a jasper stone and a sardius. There was a rainbow around the throne, like an emerald to look at.

Arminian

The one on the throne is described by the brilliance of precious stones rather than by a form, and a rainbow surrounds the throne. Many see the rainbow as a reminder of God’s covenant mercy after the flood.

Reformed

Reformed readers see glory and mercy joined: the rainbow recalls God’s covenant faithfulness even in the midst of judgment.

Revelation 4:4

4Around the throne were twenty-four thrones. On the thrones were twenty-four elders sitting, dressed in white garments, with crowns of gold on their heads.

Arminian

The twenty-four elders may represent the twelve tribes and twelve apostles, the whole people of God, or a class of heavenly beings. Their white robes and crowns suggest victory and reward.

Reformed

Reformed readers commonly see the elders as representing the one people of God from both covenants, seated with God in heaven.

Revelation 4:5

5Out of the throne proceed lightnings, sounds, and thunders. There were seven lamps of fire burning before his throne, which are the seven Spirits of God.

Arminian

Lightning and thunder recall Sinai. The seven lamps are the seven Spirits of God, the Holy Spirit in his fullness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit present before the throne, and the Sinai imagery as a reminder of God’s holy majesty.

Revelation 4:6

6Before the throne was something like a sea of glass, similar to crystal. In the middle of the throne, and around the throne were four living creatures full of eyes before and behind.

Arminian

The sea of glass and the four living creatures draw on Ezekiel 1 and Isaiah 6. The creatures full of eyes suggest God’s watchful knowledge.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the creatures as heavenly beings who represent the whole of creation in worship.

Revelation 4:7

7The first creature was like a lion, the second creature like a calf, the third creature had a face like a man, and the fourth was like a flying eagle.

Arminian

The four faces, lion, calf, man, and eagle, are often taken to represent the noblest, strongest, wisest, and swiftest of creation. Later Christian tradition linked them with the four Gospels.

Reformed

Reformed readers see all creation represented in worship before God.

Revelation 4:8

8The four living creatures, each one of them having six wings, are full of eyes around and within. They have no rest day and night, saying, “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God, the Almighty, who was and who is and who is to come!”

Arminian

“Holy, holy, holy” echoes Isaiah 6:3. God is praised as the one who was, who is, and who is to come.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s holiness as the first note of heavenly worship and his eternity as the ground of his rule.

Revelation 4:9

9When the living creatures give glory, honor, and thanks to him who sits on the throne, to him who lives forever and ever,

Arminian

The living creatures give glory, honor, and thanks to the one who lives forever. Arminian readers see worship as the right response of every creature.

Reformed

Reformed readers see worship as the chief end of creation.

Revelation 4:10

10the twenty-four elders fall down before him who sits on the throne and worship him who lives forever and ever, and throw their crowns before the throne, saying,

Arminian

The elders fall down and cast their crowns before the throne. Arminian readers see that every reward believers receive is laid at God’s feet.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the crowns cast down as a confession that every reward is God’s gift, given by grace.

Revelation 4:11

11“Worthy are you, our Lord and God, the Holy One, to receive the glory, the honor, and the power, for you created all things, and because of your desire they existed and were created!”

Arminian

God is worthy because he created all things, and by his will they existed and were created. Arminian readers see God as the source of all being and the rightful Lord of his creation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see creation as the ground of God’s sovereignty: all things exist by his will and for his glory.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
