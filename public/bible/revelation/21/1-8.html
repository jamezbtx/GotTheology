Passage

Revelation 21

Verses 1–8 · WEB

John sees a new heaven and a new earth, and the holy city, New Jerusalem, coming down from God like a bride adorned for her husband. A loud voice declares that God’s dwelling is with people; he will wipe away every tear, and death, mourning, crying, and pain will be no more. The one on the throne says he makes all things new, offers the water of life freely to the thirsty, promises that the one who overcomes will inherit all things, and warns that the faithless and the wicked have their part in the lake of fire.

Revelation 21:1

1I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth have passed away, and the sea is no more.

Arminian

The first heaven and earth pass away and the sea is no more. Arminian commentators read this as renewal rather than abandonment of creation, and note that the sea, the source of the beast in 13:1, is gone.

Reformed

Reformed commentators often stress continuity, as in Romans 8:21, where creation is set free from its bondage. God redeems what he made rather than discarding it.

Revelation 21:2

2I saw the holy city, New Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared like a bride adorned for her husband.

Arminian

The holy city comes down out of heaven, prepared like a bride. Arminian readers see the church perfected, ready because she has been prepared through faithful response to grace.

Reformed

The city comes down from God; it is not built up from the earth. Reformed readers see salvation from first to last as God’s gift to his people.

Revelation 21:3

3I heard a loud voice out of heaven saying, “Behold, God’s dwelling is with people; and he will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God.

Arminian

God’s tabernacle is with people, and he will dwell with them as their God. Arminian interpreters note that the covenant promise of Leviticus 26:12 now reaches its fullest form.

Reformed

Many manuscripts read “peoples,” pointing to the nations gathered in. Reformed interpreters see the covenant of grace reach its fulfillment, with God dwelling among those he has redeemed.

Revelation 21:4

4He will wipe away every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more; neither will there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain any more. The first things have passed away.”

Arminian

God wipes away every tear, and death, mourning, crying, and pain are gone. Arminian writers find here the healing of every hurt and loss suffered in a fallen world.

Reformed

The first things have passed away. Reformed writers see sin’s curse fully reversed, and they treat this promise as certain because God himself has made it.

Revelation 21:5

5He who sits on the throne said, “Behold, I am making all things new.” He said, “Write, for these words of God are faithful and true.”

Arminian

“Behold, I am making all things new.” Arminian expositors see the God who creates and redeems bringing his good purpose for creation to completion.

Reformed

John is told to write because these words are faithful and true. Reformed expositors stress that the new creation rests on God’s word, which cannot fail.

Revelation 21:6

6He said to me, “I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End. I will give freely to him who is thirsty from the spring of the water of life.

Arminian

“It is done.” The one who is Alpha and Omega gives the water of life freely to anyone who is thirsty. Arminian readers find the free offer of the gospel at the very end of history, open to all who come.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree the gift is free, and they add that thirst for God is itself a sign of his work in the heart. The Alpha and Omega who began all things also finishes them.

Revelation 21:7

7He who overcomes, I will give him these things. I will be his God, and he will be my son.

Arminian

The one who overcomes will inherit all things and will be God’s son. Arminian commentators read the condition seriously: perseverance in faith is required, and overcoming is something believers must actually do.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see those who overcome as the elect, kept by God and given an inheritance as his children. Adoption is the language of grace, not of wages.

Revelation 21:8

8But for the cowardly, unbelieving, sinners, abominable, murderers, sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars, their part is in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur, which is the second death.”

Arminian

The cowardly, unbelieving, and wicked have their part in the lake of fire. Arminian readers note that the list begins with the cowardly, those who fell away under pressure, and take it as a warning against apostasy.

Reformed

Reformed readers see in the list the fruit of unbelief, describing those who never truly belonged to Christ. The verse functions both as a warning and as a description of the final separation.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
