Passage

Revelation 21

Verses 9–27 · WEB

One of the seven angels shows John the bride, the wife of the Lamb, as the holy city Jerusalem descending from heaven with God’s glory. The city has twelve gates named for the tribes of Israel and twelve foundations named for the apostles. It is measured as a perfect cube, built of gold and precious stones, with gates of pearl. There is no temple, because God and the Lamb are its temple, and no sun or moon, because God’s glory lights it. The nations walk in its light, and only those written in the Lamb’s book of life enter it.

Revelation 21:9

9One of the seven angels who had the seven bowls which were loaded with the seven last plagues came, and he spoke with me, saying, “Come here. I will show you the bride, the Lamb’s wife.”

Arminian

The same kind of angel who showed John the prostitute in 17:1 now shows him the bride. Arminian interpreters see the contrast between two cities and two loyalties, and every reader is called to choose.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see the two women standing for the world and the church. The bride is the community redeemed and joined to the Lamb.

Revelation 21:10

10He carried me away in the Spirit to a great and high mountain, and showed me the holy city, Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God,

Arminian

John is carried to a high mountain to see the holy city coming down. Arminian readers note the echo of Ezekiel 40, where the prophet saw the restored temple from a mountain.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the city as the fulfillment of Old Testament hopes for Zion, now realized in Christ and his people.

Revelation 21:11

11having the glory of God. Her light was like a most precious stone, like a jasper stone, clear as crystal;

Arminian

The city has God’s glory, shining like jasper and clear as crystal. Arminian writers see the presence of God making the city radiant.

Reformed

Reformed writers note that the city’s light is not its own. The church shines only with the glory God gives.

Revelation 21:12

12having a great and high wall with twelve gates, and at the gates twelve angels, and names written on them, which are the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel.

Arminian

The wall has twelve gates with twelve angels, and the names of the twelve tribes are on them. Arminian commentators see Israel’s story included in the final city.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the unity of God’s people across both testaments, with the tribes forming the gates.

Revelation 21:13

13On the east were three gates, and on the north three gates, and on the south three gates, and on the west three gates.

Arminian

Three gates face each direction. Arminian readers find in the open gates on every side a picture of the gospel reaching every quarter of the world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the city drawing its people from all directions, as the elect are gathered from the four winds.

Revelation 21:14

14The wall of the city had twelve foundations, and on them twelve names of the twelve Apostles of the Lamb.

Arminian

The wall has twelve foundations bearing the names of the twelve apostles. Arminian expositors see the apostolic witness as the base of the church.

Reformed

Reformed expositors connect this with Ephesians 2:20, where the church is built on the apostles and prophets with Christ as cornerstone.

Revelation 21:15

15He who spoke with me had for a measure a golden reed to measure the city, its gates, and its walls.

Arminian

The angel has a golden measuring rod. Arminian readers see that the city is measured, known, and protected.

Reformed

Reformed readers recall 11:1, where the temple was measured. Measurement signifies God’s ownership and care.

Revelation 21:16

16The city is square. Its length is as great as its width. He measured the city with the reed: twelve thousand twelve stadia. Its length, width, and height are equal.

Arminian

The city is a cube, twelve thousand stadia in each direction. Arminian interpreters note that the Most Holy Place in Solomon’s temple was also a cube, so the whole city has become God’s holy dwelling.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see completeness in the twelve multiplied by a thousand. The size reaches beyond any earthly city, signaling a home for the full number of God’s people.

Revelation 21:17

17Its wall is one hundred forty-four cubits, by the measure of a man, that is, of an angel.

Arminian

The wall is one hundred forty-four cubits by human measure, which is also the angel’s. Arminian readers note the number’s link to the 144,000 of chapter 7.

Reformed

Reformed readers take the number as a symbol of the whole people of God, twelve times twelve, kept secure.

Revelation 21:18

18The construction of its wall was jasper. The city was pure gold, like pure glass.

Arminian

The wall is jasper and the city pure gold like clear glass. Arminian commentators see beauty beyond anything on earth.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the glory of God reflected in a people made pure.

Revelation 21:19

19The foundations of the city’s wall were adorned with all kinds of precious stones. The first foundation was jasper, the second sapphire, the third chalcedony, the fourth emerald,

Arminian

The foundations are adorned with precious stones, beginning with jasper and sapphire. Arminian readers note that many of the stones recall the high priest’s breastpiece in Exodus 28.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a priestly people, as 1:6 declared, now built into the very foundations of the city.

Revelation 21:20

20the fifth sardonyx, the sixth sardius, the seventh chrysolite, the eighth beryl, the ninth topaz, the tenth chrysoprase, the eleventh jacinth, and the twelfth amethyst.

Arminian

The list of stones continues through chrysoprase and amethyst. Arminian writers see the variety of the stones as a sign of the many nations brought together.

Reformed

Reformed writers see the richness of God’s workmanship displayed in his people.

Revelation 21:21

21The twelve gates were twelve pearls. Each one of the gates was made of one pearl. The street of the city was pure gold, like transparent glass.

Arminian

Each of the twelve gates is a single pearl, and the street is pure gold, transparent as glass. Arminian expositors note the welcome implied by gates of such beauty.

Reformed

Reformed expositors recall Jesus’ parable of the pearl of great price and see the infinite worth of Christ’s kingdom.

Revelation 21:22

22I saw no temple in it, for the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb are its temple.

Arminian

There is no temple in the city, because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple. Arminian readers see direct fellowship with God that no longer needs a building or a mediator’s veil.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the goal of every tabernacle and temple reached: God dwelling fully with his people through the Lamb.

Revelation 21:23

23The city has no need for the sun or moon to shine, for the very glory of God illuminated it and its lamp is the Lamb.

Arminian

The city needs neither sun nor moon, because God’s glory lights it and the Lamb is its lamp. Arminian commentators recall Isaiah 60:19–20.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the Son as the one through whom God’s glory is seen, just as he was in his earthly ministry (John 1:14).

Revelation 21:24

24The nations will walk in its light. The kings of the earth bring the glory and honor of the nations into it.

Arminian

The nations walk by its light, and the kings of the earth bring their glory into it. Arminian readers find hope here for the nations who once followed the beast, since some from among them turn and come.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the redeemed from every nation, with the best of human culture purified and offered to God.

Revelation 21:25

25Its gates will in no way be shut by day (for there will be no night there),

Arminian

The gates are never shut by day, and there is no night there. Arminian interpreters see the open gates as the lasting welcome of God’s presence.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see a city with no threat left, so the gates no longer need to close.

Revelation 21:26

26and they shall bring the glory and the honor of the nations into it so that they may enter.

Arminian

They bring the glory and honor of the nations into it. Arminian expositors see human history’s good gifts gathered into God’s kingdom.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the cultural mandate of Genesis 1 reaching its fulfillment in the new creation.

Revelation 21:27

27There will in no way enter into it anything profane, or one who causes an abomination or a lie, but only those who are written in the Lamb’s book of life.

Arminian

Nothing unclean will enter, nor anyone who practices abomination and lies, but only those written in the Lamb’s book of life. Arminian readers stress that the warning applies to all who would turn from holiness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lamb’s book of life as the register of those chosen and redeemed by Christ. Entry rests on his grace, and the holiness of the city is secured.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
