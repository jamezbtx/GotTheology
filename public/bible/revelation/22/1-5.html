Passage

Revelation 22

Verses 1–5 · WEB

John’s vision of the new Jerusalem ends with Eden restored and surpassed. The river of the water of life flows from the throne of God and of the Lamb, and the tree of life bears fruit every month, its leaves for the healing of the nations. The curse is gone. God’s servants will see his face, bear his name, live in his light, and reign forever.

Revelation 22:1

1He showed me a river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb,

Arminian

The river flows from the throne of God and of the Lamb, so life comes from God and from the Lamb together. The image draws on Ezekiel 47 and Genesis 2. Arminian readers see the fulfillment of God’s purpose to restore life to his creation through Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the throne is shared by God and the Lamb, a strong testimony to Christ’s deity. Life flows from God’s sovereign rule, and the source of every blessing in the city is the throne itself.

Revelation 22:2

2in the middle of its street. On this side of the river and on that was the tree of life, bearing twelve kinds of fruits, yielding its fruit every month. The leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations.

Arminian

The tree of life, closed to humanity after the fall (Genesis 3:22–24), is open again. Its leaves are for the healing of the nations, a picture of the gospel’s reach to every people. Interpreters differ on whether to read the vision as a literal description of the new creation or as symbolic language for its reality, but most agree that it pictures full restoration.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the tree of life as the restoration of what was lost in Adam and secured in Christ, the last Adam. The healing of the nations shows the international scope of redemption, with people from every nation gathered in the city. Interpreters across views agree that the image conveys a complete restoration, whatever its level of literal detail.

Revelation 22:3

3There will be no curse any more. The throne of God and of the Lamb will be in it, and his servants will serve him.

Arminian

“There will be no curse any more” answers Genesis 3. God’s servants will serve him in his presence. Arminian readers see the end of sin’s effects and the fulfillment of humanity’s calling to worship and serve God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the curse removed because Christ bore it (Galatians 3:13). The throne of God and of the Lamb is at the center, and service to God is the joy of the redeemed.

Revelation 22:4

4They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads.

Arminian

To see God’s face was denied even to Moses (Exodus 33:20). Here it is the privilege of all God’s servants. The name on their foreheads marks them as belonging to God, in contrast to the mark of the beast in chapter 13.

Reformed

Reformed readers often call this the beatific vision, the highest blessing of the redeemed. The name on the forehead signifies God’s ownership and the permanence of their belonging to him.

Revelation 22:5

5There will be no night, and they need no lamp light or sun light; for the Lord God will illuminate them. They will reign forever and ever.

Arminian

The Lord God is their light, so no lamp or sun is needed. They will reign forever, sharing in Christ’s rule as he promised in 3:21. Arminian readers see the final reward of those who have persevered in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers read the reign of the saints as their share in Christ’s kingdom, given by grace. Many Reformed interpreters emphasize that this eternal reign is the goal toward which the whole story of redemption moves.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
