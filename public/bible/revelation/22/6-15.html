Passage

Revelation 22

Verses 6–15 · WEB

An angel affirms that these words are faithful and true, and Jesus promises to come soon. John falls to worship the angel and is told to worship God alone. The book is not to be sealed, because the time is near. Jesus speaks as the Alpha and the Omega, who brings his reward and repays each one by his work, and he blesses those who will enter the city by its gates.

Revelation 22:6

6He said to me, “These words are faithful and true. The Lord God of the spirits of the prophets sent his angel to show to his bondservants the things which must happen soon.”

Arminian

The words are faithful and true because they come from the Lord God of the spirits of the prophets. “The things which must happen soon” gives the book urgency. Interpreters read “soon” differently: preterists see events in the first century, futurists see the end, and many take it as the nearness of the last days throughout the church age.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise note the range of views on “soon.” Many Reformed interpreters read it as describing the whole period between Christ’s first and second coming, during which the end is always near. The certainty of God’s word is the main point.

Revelation 22:7

7“Behold, I am coming soon! Blessed is he who keeps the words of the prophecy of this book.”

Arminian

“Behold, I am coming soon!” is the first of three such promises in this chapter. The blessing on the one who keeps the words of the prophecy echoes 1:3. Arminian readers stress that keeping the words involves obedient perseverance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise of Christ’s coming as the church’s hope. Keeping the words of the prophecy is the fruit of faith, and the blessing is given to those who hold fast to his word.

Revelation 22:8

8Now I, John, am the one who heard and saw these things. When I heard and saw, I fell down to worship before the feet of the angel who had shown me these things.

Arminian

John testifies that he himself heard and saw these things, grounding the book in eyewitness testimony. His impulse to worship the angel shows how overwhelming the revelation was.

Reformed

Reformed readers note John’s personal testimony as the authority behind the book. Even an apostle could be overwhelmed and fall into error, which leads to the correction that follows.

Revelation 22:9

9He said to me, “You must not do that! I am a fellow bondservant with you and with your brothers, the prophets, and with those who keep the words of this book. Worship God.”

Arminian

The angel refuses worship and calls himself a fellow servant. Worship belongs to God alone. Arminian readers see a warning against honoring any creature in place of God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the refusal as a clear line between Creator and creature. Since the Lamb receives worship in chapter 5, the contrast supports the deity of Christ. Worship belongs to God alone.

Revelation 22:10

10He said to me, “Don’t seal up the words of the prophecy of this book, for the time is at hand.

Arminian

Daniel was told to seal up his prophecy until the time of the end (Daniel 12:4). John is told not to seal his, because the time is at hand. Arminian readers see the message as open to every reader and urgent for every generation.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the contrast with Daniel: what was sealed is now opened, because the last days have begun with Christ. The prophecy is meant to be read and obeyed now.

Revelation 22:11

11He who acts unjustly, let him act unjustly still. He who is filthy, let him be filthy still. He who is righteous, let him do righteousness still. He who is holy, let him be holy still.”

Arminian

This verse can sound fatalistic, but Arminian readers usually take it as a solemn warning that the time for change is short. As the end draws near, people will be confirmed in the course they have chosen. The verse urges repentance now, while there is still time.

Reformed

Reformed readers read the verse as a declaration that character becomes fixed at the end. It reflects Daniel 12:10, where the wicked continue in wickedness and the wise understand. Reformed interpreters see God’s judgment confirming each person in what he is, while the call to repent elsewhere in the book remains sincere.

Key contrast Both traditions read the verse as a solemn statement about the end. Arminian readers stress that people are confirmed in a course they have chosen and that the verse urges repentance while there is time. Reformed readers see God’s final judgment fixing each person’s character, without denying that the call to repent is sincere.

Revelation 22:12

12“Behold, I am coming soon! My reward is with me, to repay to each man according to his work.

Arminian

Christ’s reward is with him, to repay each one according to his work. Arminian readers see the judgment as taking account of how people have lived, with works as the evidence of faith or unbelief.

Reformed

Reformed readers affirm judgment according to works, understood as the evidence of a person’s standing, not the ground of justification. Those who belong to Christ are justified by faith alone, and their works are the fruit of that grace.

Revelation 22:13

13I am the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End.

Arminian

Christ takes titles used of God in 1:8 and Isaiah 44:6. He is the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end of all history. Both traditions see a clear confession of his deity.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same confession of Christ’s deity. He stands at the beginning and end of history, so its course is in his hands.

Revelation 22:14

14Blessed are those who do his commandments, that they may have the right to the tree of life, and may enter in by the gates into the city.

Arminian

The WEB, following the Majority Text, reads “those who do his commandments,” while many other manuscripts read “those who wash their robes.” Arminian readers note that either way the verse joins faith and obedience, since the obedient are those who belong to the Lamb. The right to the tree of life is given, not earned.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same textual difference and often favor the reading about washed robes, which echoes 7:14. With either reading, access to the tree of life comes by grace through Christ, and obedience is the sign of those who belong to him.

Revelation 22:15

15Outside are the dogs, the sorcerers, the sexually immoral, the murderers, the idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood.

Arminian

Outside the city are those who persist in sin without repentance. The list echoes 21:8. Arminian readers stress that the list describes a way of life people have chosen, and the call to repent is open until the end.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the final separation between those who belong to God and those who do not. Many who once lived this way are inside the city because they were washed (1 Corinthians 6:9–11). Those outside are those who love falsehood and refused the truth.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
