Passage

Revelation 19

Verses 11–21 · WEB

Heaven opens, and a rider on a white horse, called Faithful and True, comes to judge and make war in righteousness. His robe is dipped in blood, his name is the Word of God, and the armies of heaven follow him. A sharp sword comes from his mouth, and on his robe is written King of kings and Lord of lords. An angel summons the birds to a great supper of God, the beast and the false prophet are captured and thrown alive into the lake of fire, and the rest are killed by the sword from the rider’s mouth.

Revelation 19:11

11I saw heaven opened, and behold, a white horse, and he who sat on it is called Faithful and True. In righteousness he judges and makes war.

Arminian

The rider is called Faithful and True, the same titles Jesus used of himself in 3:14. Arminian readers take his righteous war as the answer to the martyrs’ cry, carried out by one who has shown patience for a long time.

Reformed

The rider judges and makes war in righteousness, so his victory is never arbitrary. Reformed readers see Christ the King carrying out the just purpose of God that was settled long before this day.

Revelation 19:12

12His eyes are a flame of fire, and on his head are many crowns. He has names written and a name written which no one knows but he himself.

Arminian

His eyes are a flame of fire, and he wears many crowns. Arminian writers contrast these crowns with the dragon’s seven and the beast’s ten, since only Christ’s authority is rightful.

Reformed

He bears a name that no one knows but himself. Reformed writers take this as a reminder that even in revelation Christ remains beyond full human comprehension.

Revelation 19:13

13He is clothed in a garment sprinkled with blood. His name is called “The Word of God.”

Arminian

His robe is dipped in blood before the battle begins. Many Arminian interpreters see the blood of the cross, the Lamb’s own sacrifice, rather than the blood of enemies; others hear Isaiah 63 and the winepress of judgment.

Reformed

His name is called the Word of God, tying this vision to John 1. Reformed interpreters see the same eternal Son who created and redeemed now coming as judge, so creation, redemption, and judgment belong to one Lord.

Revelation 19:14

14The armies which are in heaven, clothed in white, pure, fine linen, followed him on white horses.

Arminian

The armies of heaven follow him on white horses, clothed in white linen. Arminian expositors note that they carry no weapons, which suggests that the victory belongs to Christ alone while his people share it.

Reformed

The linen of the armies matches the bride’s clothing in 19:8. Reformed expositors identify these armies with the redeemed, who follow the Lamb wherever he goes.

Revelation 19:15

15Out of his mouth proceeds a sharp, double-edged sword that with it he should strike the nations. He will rule them with an iron rod. He treads the wine press of the fierceness of the wrath of God, the Almighty.

Arminian

The sword comes out of his mouth, so he conquers by his word. Arminian readers see judgment pronounced by the same word that once called the nations to repent.

Reformed

He rules the nations with a rod of iron and treads the winepress of God’s wrath, fulfilling Psalm 2. Reformed readers see the Son executing the Father’s decree over the nations.

Revelation 19:16

16He has on his garment and on his thigh a name written, “KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS.”

Arminian

“King of kings, and Lord of lords” is written on his robe and thigh. Arminian commentators note that the Lamb was already given this title in 17:14 when he overcame the beast’s allies, and now it is displayed openly.

Reformed

The title is displayed where all can read it. Reformed commentators see Christ’s universal kingship made public, so every earthly ruler stands under his authority.

Revelation 19:17

17I saw an angel standing in the sun. He cried with a loud voice, saying to all the birds that fly in the sky, “Come! Be gathered together to the great supper of God,

Arminian

An angel standing in the sun summons the birds to the great supper of God. Arminian readers feel the grim contrast with the marriage supper of the Lamb: everyone ends at one table or the other.

Reformed

The two suppers mark the two outcomes of history. Reformed readers see God’s justice as thorough, so that the enemies of the Lamb are wholly overcome.

Revelation 19:18

18that you may eat the flesh of kings, the flesh of captains, the flesh of mighty men, and the flesh of horses and of those who sit on them, and the flesh of all men, both free and slave, small and great.”

Arminian

The list runs from kings to slaves, free and small and great. Arminian interpreters observe that rank offers no shelter in judgment, just as it offers no advantage in the invitation to the Lamb’s feast.

Reformed

The imagery comes from Ezekiel 39. Reformed interpreters see the prophetic pattern of God defeating the gathered nations brought to its final fulfillment.

Revelation 19:19

19I saw the beast, the kings of the earth, and their armies, gathered together to make war against him who sat on the horse and against his army.

Arminian

The beast, the kings of the earth, and their armies gather to make war against the rider. Arminian readers see human rebellion choosing its side to the very end.

Reformed

The gathering of the nations recalls 16:14 and Armageddon. Reformed readers note that the battle is announced but never described, because the outcome was never in doubt.

Revelation 19:20

20The beast was taken, and with him the false prophet who worked the signs in his sight, with which he deceived those who had received the mark of the beast and those who worshiped his image. These two were thrown alive into the lake of fire that burns with sulfur.

Arminian

The beast and the false prophet, who deceived those who took the mark, are thrown alive into the lake of fire. Arminian expositors stress that the deceived were still responsible for receiving the mark and worshiping the image.

Reformed

The deceivers meet the fate reserved for them. Reformed expositors see the end of the counterfeit trinity’s earthly agents, cast out by the true King.

Revelation 19:21

21The rest were killed with the sword of him who sat on the horse, the sword which came out of his mouth. So all the birds were filled with their flesh.

Arminian

The rest are killed by the sword from the rider’s mouth, and the birds are filled. Arminian readers find the scene sobering, since it closes the long season in which the gospel was offered to these same nations.

Reformed

Victory comes by the word of Christ, not by any effort of his followers. Reformed readers see the scene as proof that the Lamb’s triumph rests entirely on his own power.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
