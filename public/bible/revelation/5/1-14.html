Passage

Revelation 5

Verses 1–14 · WEB

The one on the throne holds a scroll sealed with seven seals, and no one is found worthy to open it. John weeps until he is told that the Lion of the tribe of Judah has overcome. He sees a Lamb standing as though slain, who takes the scroll. Heaven bursts into a new song: the Lamb is worthy because he was slain and purchased people for God from every tribe, language, people, and nation. All creation joins the praise.

Revelation 5:1

1I saw, in the right hand of him who sat on the throne, a book written inside and outside, sealed shut with seven seals.

Arminian

The scroll sealed with seven seals likely contains God’s purposes for history and judgment. Interpreters differ on its exact content, but all agree it is held by God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the scroll as God’s decree for history, sealed until Christ opens it.

Revelation 5:2

2I saw a mighty angel proclaiming with a loud voice, “Who is worthy to open the book, and to break its seals?”

Arminian

The angel’s question exposes that no creature is worthy to open the scroll. Arminian readers see humanity’s need for a redeemer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the inability of every creature to carry out God’s purposes apart from Christ.

Revelation 5:3

3No one in heaven above, or on the earth, or under the earth, was able to open the book or to look in it.

Arminian

No one in heaven, on earth, or under the earth was able to open it. The search is complete and fails.

Reformed

Reformed readers see creation’s helplessness to accomplish redemption.

Revelation 5:4

4Then I wept much, because no one was found worthy to open the book or to look in it.

Arminian

John weeps because no one is worthy. His grief shows how much hangs on the scroll’s opening.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the weeping as the right response to the hopelessness of the world without Christ.

Revelation 5:5

5One of the elders said to me, “Don’t weep. Behold, the Lion who is of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has overcome: he who opens the book and its seven seals.”

Arminian

The Lion of Judah and the Root of David are messianic titles (Genesis 49:9; Isaiah 11:1). He has overcome, so he can open the scroll.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s victory as the basis of his right to open the scroll.

Revelation 5:6

6I saw in the middle of the throne and of the four living creatures, and in the middle of the elders, a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God, sent out into all the earth.

Arminian

John hears of a lion and sees a lamb standing as though slain. Arminian readers see Christ’s victory won through sacrifice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the slain Lamb as the center of God’s purposes. The seven horns and eyes speak of his full power and knowledge.

Revelation 5:7

7Then he came, and he took it out of the right hand of him who sat on the throne.

Arminian

The Lamb takes the scroll from the one on the throne. Arminian readers see Christ entrusted with the unfolding of history.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son receiving authority from the Father to carry out his plan.

Revelation 5:8

8Now when he had taken the book, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each one having a harp, and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints.

Arminian

The elders fall before the Lamb with harps and bowls of incense, which are the prayers of the saints. Arminian readers see that the prayers of believers matter in God’s purposes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lamb receiving worship given to God, and prayer as part of the means God uses to carry out his plan.

Revelation 5:9

9They sang a new song, saying, “You are worthy to take the book and to open its seals, for you were killed, and bought us for God with your blood out of every tribe, language, people, and nation,

Arminian

“You were killed, and bought us for God with your blood out of every tribe, language, people, and nation.” Arminian readers see Christ’s death as the price paid for all, with the redeemed drawn from every people as the gospel goes out. The verse affirms the worldwide scope of redemption.

Reformed

Reformed readers see in the verse a particular redemption: Christ purchased people for God out of every nation. Many cite it in support of definite atonement, since the ransom is described as effective for those it purchased.

Key contrast This verse is read on both sides of the debate over the extent of the atonement. Arminian readers stress the worldwide scope of the redeemed and see Christ’s death as provision for all. Reformed readers stress the phrase “out of every tribe” and see a ransom that actually secures a people for God.

Revelation 5:10

10and made us kings and priests to our God; and we will reign on the earth.”

Arminian

The redeemed are made a kingdom and priests who reign on the earth. Premillennial readers often see a future reign, while others see the present reign of believers with Christ or the reign of the new creation.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same range of views. Many amillennial interpreters see the reign as present and also as fulfilled in the new earth.

Revelation 5:11

11I looked, and I heard something like a voice of many angels around the throne, the living creatures, and the elders. The number of them was ten thousands of ten thousands, and thousands of thousands,

Arminian

Countless angels join the praise. Arminian readers see heaven’s unanimous worship of the Lamb.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the angels honoring the Lamb as the agent of redemption.

Revelation 5:12

12saying with a loud voice, “Worthy is the Lamb who has been killed to receive the power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and blessing!”

Arminian

The Lamb who was slain is worthy to receive power, riches, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and blessing. Sevenfold praise suggests completeness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lamb receiving the same praise as God, a witness to his deity.

Revelation 5:13

13I heard every created thing which is in heaven, on the earth, under the earth, on the sea, and everything in them, saying, “To him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be the blessing, the honor, the glory, and the dominion, forever and ever! Amen!”

Arminian

Every creature joins the praise to the one on the throne and the Lamb together. Arminian readers see the goal of all creation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Father and the Son worshiped together, and the final submission of all creation.

Revelation 5:14

14The four living creatures said, “Amen!” Then the elders fell down and worshiped.

Arminian

The living creatures say “Amen,” and the elders fall down and worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see heaven’s worship as the model for the church’s worship.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
