Passage

Revelation 7

Verses 9–17 · WEB

John sees a great multitude no one could count, from every nation, tribe, people, and language, standing before the throne in white robes. They cry that salvation belongs to God and the Lamb. An elder explains that these have come out of the great suffering and washed their robes in the blood of the Lamb. God shelters them, and the Lamb shepherds them to springs of living water.

Revelation 7:9

9After these things I looked, and behold, a great multitude which no man could count, out of every nation and of all tribes, peoples, and languages, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, dressed in white robes, with palm branches in their hands.

Arminian

The multitude comes from every nation. Arminian readers see the fruit of a gospel offered to all peoples.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the elect gathered from every nation, the fulfillment of God’s promise to Abraham.

Revelation 7:10

10They cried with a loud voice, saying, “Salvation be to our God, who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb!”

Arminian

“Salvation be to our God … and to the Lamb.” Arminian readers see that the redeemed credit God for their salvation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see salvation ascribed wholly to God and the Lamb, with no share claimed by the redeemed.

Revelation 7:11

11All the angels were standing around the throne, the elders, and the four living creatures; and they fell on their faces before his throne, and worshiped God,

Arminian

Angels, elders, and creatures fall down in worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see all heaven joining the praise of redemption.

Revelation 7:12

12saying, “Amen! Blessing, glory, wisdom, thanksgiving, honor, power, and might, be to our God forever and ever! Amen.”

Arminian

A sevenfold doxology praises God forever.

Reformed

Reformed readers see completeness of praise.

Revelation 7:13

13One of the elders answered, saying to me, “These who are arrayed in the white robes, who are they, and where did they come from?”

Arminian

The elder’s question prepares for the explanation.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the teaching pattern.

Revelation 7:14

14I told him, “My lord, you know.” He said to me, “These are those who came out of the great suffering. They washed their robes and made them white in the Lamb’s blood.

Arminian

They came out of the great suffering and washed their robes in the Lamb’s blood. Futurists usually see a specific future tribulation; others see the suffering of believers throughout the age. Arminian readers note that washing their robes describes their response of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see cleansing by Christ’s blood as the only ground of their standing. Many take the great suffering as the trials of the whole church age.

Revelation 7:15

15Therefore they are before the throne of God, and they serve him day and night in his temple. He who sits on the throne will spread his tabernacle over them.

Arminian

They serve God day and night, and he shelters them with his presence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the fulfillment of God dwelling with his people.

Revelation 7:16

16They will never be hungry or thirsty any more. The sun won’t beat on them, nor any heat;

Arminian

No more hunger, thirst, or scorching heat, echoing Isaiah 49:10.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the end of all suffering.

Revelation 7:17

17for the Lamb who is in the middle of the throne shepherds them and leads them to springs of life-giving waters. And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.”

Arminian

The Lamb is their shepherd, and God wipes away every tear. Arminian readers see the tender care that awaits all who persevere.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lamb as shepherd, as in John 10, who keeps and leads his flock.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
