Passage

Revelation 9

Verses 13–21 · WEB

The sixth trumpet releases four angels bound at the Euphrates and a vast cavalry that kills a third of humanity with fire, smoke, and sulfur. Yet the survivors do not repent of their idolatry, murders, sorceries, immorality, or thefts.

Revelation 9:13

13The sixth angel sounded. I heard a voice from the horns of the golden altar which is before God,

Arminian

A voice comes from the golden altar, where the prayers of the saints were offered.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the link between prayer and judgment.

Revelation 9:14

14saying to the sixth angel who had the trumpet, “Free the four angels who are bound at the great river Euphrates!”

Arminian

The Euphrates was the boundary of the Roman world and the direction of feared invaders.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an image of invasion from beyond.

Revelation 9:15

15The four angels were freed who had been prepared for that hour and day and month and year, so that they might kill one third of mankind.

Arminian

The angels were prepared for the exact hour, day, month, and year. Arminian readers see God’s timing in judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s precise sovereign control over history.

Revelation 9:16

16The number of the armies of the horsemen was two hundred million. I heard the number of them.

Arminian

Two hundred million horsemen. Most take the number as symbolic of overwhelming force; some futurists see a literal army.

Reformed

Reformed readers usually read the number as symbolic.

Revelation 9:17

17Thus I saw the horses in the vision and those who sat on them, having breastplates of fiery red, hyacinth blue, and sulfur yellow; and the horses’ heads resembled lions’ heads. Out of their mouths proceed fire, smoke, and sulfur.

Arminian

Fiery, blue, and yellow breastplates; horses with lion heads breathing fire, smoke, and sulfur.

Reformed

Reformed readers see demonic terror.

Revelation 9:18

18By these three plagues, one third of mankind was killed: by the fire, the smoke, and the sulfur, which proceeded out of their mouths.

Arminian

A third of humanity is killed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a severe but still partial judgment.

Revelation 9:19

19For the power of the horses is in their mouths and in their tails. For their tails are like serpents, and have heads; and with them they harm.

Arminian

Their power is in mouths and serpent-like tails.

Reformed

Reformed readers see deception and destruction joined.

Revelation 9:20

20The rest of mankind, who were not killed with these plagues, didn’t repent of the works of their hands, that they wouldn’t worship demons, and the idols of gold, and of silver, and of brass, and of stone, and of wood, which can’t see, hear, or walk.

Arminian

The survivors did not repent of idolatry. Arminian readers see the judgments as calls to repentance that people refuse, so the responsibility rests with them.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the hardness of the human heart, which judgment alone does not change. Only grace brings repentance.

Key contrast Both agree the survivors are responsible for refusing to repent. Arminian readers see the judgments as calls that people could have heeded. Reformed readers see that judgment alone does not change the heart, and that repentance comes only by God’s grace.

Revelation 9:21

21They didn’t repent of their murders, their sorceries, their sexual immorality, or their thefts.

Arminian

They did not repent of murders, sorceries, immorality, or thefts.

Reformed

Reformed readers see idolatry producing moral corruption, as in Romans 1.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
