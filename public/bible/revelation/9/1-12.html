Passage

Revelation 9

Verses 1–12 · WEB

The fifth trumpet opens the abyss, and smoke releases locust-like creatures that torment for five months those who lack God’s seal. Their king is the angel of the abyss, Abaddon or Apollyon. This is the first woe.

Revelation 9:1

1The fifth angel sounded, and I saw a star from the sky which had fallen to the earth. The key to the pit of the abyss was given to him.

Arminian

A fallen star receives the key to the abyss. Many take the star as an angel, possibly Satan; the key is given, so his power is permitted.

Reformed

Reformed readers stress that the key was given: even the powers of darkness act only under God’s permission.

Revelation 9:2

2He opened the pit of the abyss, and smoke went up out of the pit, like the smoke from a burning furnace. The sun and the air were darkened because of the smoke from the pit.

Arminian

Smoke darkens the sun and air.

Reformed

Reformed readers see spiritual darkness released as judgment.

Revelation 9:3

3Then out of the smoke came locusts on the earth, and power was given to them, as the scorpions of the earth have power.

Arminian

Locusts recall Joel and the eighth plague. Interpreters read them as demonic forces, invading armies, or symbols of spiritual torment.

Reformed

Reformed readers commonly see demonic powers permitted to afflict unbelievers.

Revelation 9:4

4They were told that they should not hurt the grass of the earth, neither any green thing, neither any tree, but only those people who don’t have God’s seal on their foreheads.

Arminian

The locusts may harm only those without God’s seal. Arminian readers see God protecting his own.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the seal of chapter 7 at work: God’s people are protected from this spiritual torment.

Revelation 9:5

5They were given power, not to kill them, but to torment them for five months. Their torment was like the torment of a scorpion when it strikes a person.

Arminian

They may torment but not kill, for five months, a limited time.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment restrained and limited.

Revelation 9:6

6In those days people will seek death, and will in no way find it. They will desire to die, and death will flee from them.

Arminian

People seek death and cannot find it.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the misery of life under judgment without God.

Revelation 9:7

7The shapes of the locusts were like horses prepared for war. On their heads were something like golden crowns, and their faces were like people’s faces.

Arminian

The locusts look like war horses with crowns and human faces.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a picture of terrifying power that imitates authority.

Revelation 9:8

8They had hair like women’s hair, and their teeth were like those of lions.

Arminian

Hair like women’s and teeth like lions.

Reformed

Reformed readers see deceptive attraction joined with ferocity.

Revelation 9:9

9They had breastplates like breastplates of iron. The sound of their wings was like the sound of many chariots and horses rushing to war.

Arminian

Iron breastplates and the roar of chariots.

Reformed

Reformed readers see invincible-seeming forces.

Revelation 9:10

10They have tails like those of scorpions, with stingers. In their tails they have power to harm men for five months.

Arminian

Stinging tails, with power limited to five months.

Reformed

Reformed readers note again the limit.

Revelation 9:11

11They have over them as king the angel of the abyss. His name in Hebrew is “Abaddon”, but in Greek, he has the name “Apollyon”.

Arminian

Their king is Abaddon or Apollyon, meaning Destruction or Destroyer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Satan as the destroyer, in contrast to Christ the Savior.

Revelation 9:12

12The first woe is past. Behold, there are still two woes coming after this.

Arminian

The first woe is past.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the progression toward the end.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
