Passage

Revelation 12

Verses 1–6 · WEB

A woman clothed with the sun gives birth to a son who will rule the nations, while a great red dragon waits to devour the child. The child is caught up to God and his throne, and the woman flees into the wilderness, where God nourishes her for 1,260 days.

Revelation 12:1

1A great sign was seen in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars.

Arminian

The woman clothed with the sun, with twelve stars, recalls Joseph’s dream (Genesis 37:9). Most interpreters see her as the people of God, Israel and the church, from whom the Messiah comes. Roman Catholic tradition also sees Mary.

Reformed

Reformed readers usually identify the woman as the one people of God across both covenants, from whom Christ was born and who continues in the church.

Revelation 12:2

2She was with child. She cried out in pain, laboring to give birth.

Arminian

Her labor pains recall Israel’s longing for the Messiah (Isaiah 66:7–8).

Reformed

Reformed readers see the travail of God’s people awaiting the promised seed.

Revelation 12:3

3Another sign was seen in heaven. Behold, a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns, and on his heads seven crowns.

Arminian

The dragon has seven heads, ten horns, and seven crowns, a picture of great and hostile power.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Satan in his claim to worldly authority.

Revelation 12:4

4His tail drew one third of the stars of the sky, and threw them to the earth. The dragon stood before the woman who was about to give birth, so that when she gave birth he might devour her child.

Arminian

The dragon waits to devour the child, recalling Herod’s attempt to kill Jesus.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Satan’s opposition to Christ from his birth.

Revelation 12:5

5She gave birth to a son, a male child, who is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron. Her child was caught up to God and to his throne.

Arminian

The son will rule the nations with a rod of iron (Psalm 2) and is caught up to God. The vision moves from Christ’s birth to his ascension. Arminian readers see Christ’s victory as complete.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the whole of Christ’s earthly work summarized, with his ascension securing his rule.

Revelation 12:6

6The woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared by God, that there they may nourish her one thousand two hundred sixty days.

Arminian

The woman flees to the wilderness, where God nourishes her for 1,260 days. Futurists often see protection for Israel in a future tribulation, while many others see the church kept by God throughout the present age.

Reformed

Reformed readers commonly see the church preserved in the world, as Israel was in the wilderness, during the whole period between Christ’s ascension and return.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
