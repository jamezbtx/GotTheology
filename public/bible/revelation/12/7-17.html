Passage

Revelation 12

Verses 7–17 · WEB

War breaks out in heaven, and Michael and his angels throw down the dragon, the ancient serpent called the devil and Satan. Heaven celebrates that the accuser is cast down, and the saints overcome him by the blood of the Lamb and their testimony. Enraged, the dragon pursues the woman, who is protected, and then makes war on the rest of her offspring, who keep God’s commandments and hold the testimony of Jesus.

Revelation 12:7

7There was war in the sky. Michael and his angels made war on the dragon. The dragon and his angels made war.

Arminian

Michael and his angels fight the dragon. Some date the war to a primeval fall of Satan, others to Christ’s victory at the cross, and some to a future event.

Reformed

Reformed readers commonly connect the war with Christ’s victory at the cross and ascension, as verses 10–11 suggest.

Revelation 12:8

8They didn’t prevail. No place was found for them any more in heaven.

Arminian

The dragon loses his place in heaven.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Satan’s defeat.

Revelation 12:9

9The great dragon was thrown down, the old serpent, he who is called the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world. He was thrown down to the earth, and his angels were thrown down with him.

Arminian

The dragon is named: the old serpent, the devil, Satan, deceiver of the whole world. Arminian readers see deception as his chief weapon.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the serpent of Genesis 3 defeated, in fulfillment of Genesis 3:15.

Revelation 12:10

10I heard a loud voice in heaven, saying, “Now the salvation, the power, and the Kingdom of our God, and the authority of his Christ has come; for the accuser of our brothers has been thrown down, who accuses them before our God day and night.

Arminian

The accuser of the brothers is thrown down. Arminian readers see that Christ’s victory silences Satan’s charges against believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the accuser silenced because Christ’s death has answered every charge (Romans 8:33–34).

Revelation 12:11

11They overcame him because of the Lamb’s blood, and because of the word of their testimony. They didn’t love their life, even to death.

Arminian

They overcame by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony and did not love their lives to the death. Arminian readers see Christ’s victory received and lived out by faithful believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lamb’s blood as the ground of victory and faithful testimony as its fruit.

Key contrast Both agree the victory rests on the Lamb’s blood. Arminian readers stress the believers’ faithful testimony as their participation in it. Reformed readers stress that the testimony itself is the fruit of Christ’s finished work in them.

Revelation 12:12

12Therefore rejoice, heavens, and you who dwell in them. Woe to the earth and to the sea, because the devil has gone down to you, having great wrath, knowing that he has but a short time.”

Arminian

Heaven rejoices, but woe to the earth, because the devil knows his time is short.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Satan’s fury as the rage of a defeated enemy.

Revelation 12:13

13When the dragon saw that he was thrown down to the earth, he persecuted the woman who gave birth to the male child.

Arminian

The dragon persecutes the woman.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church as the target of Satan’s hostility.

Revelation 12:14

14Two wings of the great eagle were given to the woman, that she might fly into the wilderness to her place, so that she might be nourished for a time, times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent.

Arminian

She is given eagle’s wings, recalling the exodus (Exodus 19:4), and is nourished for a time, times, and half a time.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s care for his church in the world.

Revelation 12:15

15The serpent spewed water out of his mouth after the woman like a river, that he might cause her to be carried away by the stream.

Arminian

The serpent spews a flood after her.

Reformed

Reformed readers see attempts to overwhelm the church by persecution or deception.

Revelation 12:16

16The earth helped the woman, and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed up the river which the dragon spewed out of his mouth.

Arminian

The earth helps her and swallows the flood.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God using his creation to protect his people.

Revelation 12:17

17The dragon grew angry with the woman, and went away to make war with the rest of her offspring, who keep God’s commandments and hold Jesus’ testimony.

Arminian

The dragon makes war on the rest of her offspring, who keep God’s commandments and the testimony of Jesus. Arminian readers see faithful believers who obey and witness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers in every generation as the target of Satan’s war, marked by obedience and testimony.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
