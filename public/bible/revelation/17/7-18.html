Passage

Revelation 17

Verses 7–18 · WEB

The angel explains the beast that was, and is not, and is about to come, the seven heads as seven mountains and seven kings, and the ten horns as kings who will war against the Lamb. The Lamb will overcome them, and those with him are called, chosen, and faithful. In the end the beast and the horns turn on the prostitute and destroy her, because God put it into their hearts to fulfill his purpose.

Revelation 17:7

7The angel said to me, “Why do you wonder? I will tell you the mystery of the woman and of the beast that carries her, which has the seven heads and the ten horns.

Arminian

The angel promises to explain the mystery.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God giving understanding.

Revelation 17:8

8The beast that you saw was, and is not; and is about to come up out of the abyss and to go into destruction. Those who dwell on the earth and whose names have not been written in the book of life from the foundation of the world will marvel when they see that the beast was, and is not, and shall be present.

Arminian

Those whose names were not written in the book of life from the foundation of the world will marvel at the beast. Arminian readers read the book of life in light of foreknowledge and continued faith, as in 3:5.

Reformed

Reformed readers see here the clearest statement that names are written before creation, supporting unconditional election.

Key contrast This verse bears on election. Reformed readers see names written before creation, so the elect are kept from the beast’s deception. Arminian readers see God’s foreknowledge of those who would believe, with the book of life also linked to perseverance in 3:5.

Revelation 17:9

9Here is the mind that has wisdom. The seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman sits.

Arminian

The seven heads are seven mountains, often linked with Rome’s seven hills.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the widely accepted link with Rome.

Revelation 17:10

10They are seven kings. Five have fallen, the one is, and the other has not yet come. When he comes, he must continue a little while.

Arminian

Seven kings, five fallen, one is, one yet to come. Many attempt to match Roman emperors; others read seven as a symbolic number of completeness.

Reformed

Reformed readers caution against precise schemes.

Revelation 17:11

11The beast that was, and is not, is himself also an eighth, and is of the seven; and he goes to destruction.

Arminian

The beast is an eighth and belongs to the seven.

Reformed

Reformed readers see recurring opposition to God that goes to destruction.

Revelation 17:12

12The ten horns that you saw are ten kings who have received no kingdom as yet, but they receive authority as kings with the beast for one hour.

Arminian

Ten horns are ten kings who receive authority for one hour.

Reformed

Reformed readers see short-lived powers.

Revelation 17:13

13These have one mind, and they give their power and authority to the beast.

Arminian

They give their power to the beast.

Reformed

Reformed readers see united opposition to God.

Revelation 17:14

14These will war against the Lamb, and the Lamb will overcome them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings; and those who are with him are called, chosen, and faithful.”

Arminian

The Lamb will overcome them, and those with him are called, chosen, and faithful. Arminian readers stress that the chosen are also faithful, joining divine call and human faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the order of called, chosen, and faithful as reflecting God’s gracious choice that produces faithfulness.

Key contrast Both affirm all three words. Reformed readers see election producing faithfulness. Arminian readers see calling, choosing, and faithful response held together without a sequence that makes faith the result of an unconditional decree.

Revelation 17:15

15He said to me, “The waters which you saw, where the prostitute sits, are peoples, multitudes, nations, and languages.

Arminian

The waters are peoples, multitudes, nations, and languages.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the world-wide reach of Babylon’s influence.

Revelation 17:16

16The ten horns which you saw, they and the beast will hate the prostitute, will make her desolate, will strip her naked, will eat her flesh, and will burn her utterly with fire.

Arminian

The beast and the horns turn on the prostitute and destroy her. Evil destroys itself.

Reformed

Reformed readers see evil turning on evil under God’s judgment.

Revelation 17:17

17For God has put in their hearts to do what he has in mind, to be of one mind, and to give their kingdom to the beast, until the words of God should be accomplished.

Arminian

God put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose. Arminian readers see God working through the free choices of evil powers without causing their sin.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereignty over even the plans of his enemies, as in Proverbs 21:1.

Key contrast Both see God’s rule over history. Reformed readers see God directing even wicked hearts to fulfill his decree. Arminian readers see God using and permitting evil choices for his ends without being their author.

Revelation 17:18

18The woman whom you saw is the great city which reigns over the kings of the earth.”

Arminian

The woman is the great city that rules the kings of the earth.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Rome in John’s day and the world city in every age.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
