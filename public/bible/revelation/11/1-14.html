Passage

Revelation 11

Verses 1–14 · WEB

John measures God’s temple and its worshipers but leaves out the outer court, which is trampled for forty-two months. Two witnesses prophesy for 1,260 days with power like Moses and Elijah. When their testimony is finished, the beast kills them, and their bodies lie in the street of the great city. After three and a half days God raises them and takes them to heaven, and an earthquake strikes. This is the second woe.

Revelation 11:1

1A reed like a rod was given to me. Someone said, “Rise and measure God’s temple, and the altar, and those who worship in it.

Arminian

Measuring the temple marks protection. Futurists often see a rebuilt temple in Jerusalem; preterists see the temple before AD 70; many others see the church as God’s temple, protected spiritually even as it suffers.

Reformed

Reformed readers usually take the temple as the church, as in 1 Corinthians 3:16, measured and kept by God.

Revelation 11:2

2Leave out the court which is outside of the temple, and don’t measure it, for it has been given to the nations. They will tread the holy city under foot for forty-two months.

Arminian

The outer court is trampled for forty-two months. The same period appears as 1,260 days and “a time, times, and half a time,” drawn from Daniel. Futurists read a literal three and a half years; many others read a symbolic period of persecution, often the whole church age.

Reformed

Reformed readers commonly read the period symbolically, describing the time between Christ’s ascension and return during which the church is both protected and persecuted.

Revelation 11:3

3I will give power to my two witnesses, and they will prophesy one thousand two hundred sixty days, clothed in sackcloth.”

Arminian

The two witnesses prophesy in sackcloth. They are read as two individuals, sometimes Moses and Elijah or Enoch and Elijah, or as symbols of the witnessing church.

Reformed

Reformed readers often see the witnesses as the church in its prophetic witness, with two being the number for valid testimony.

Revelation 11:4

4These are the two olive trees and the two lamp stands, standing before the Lord of the earth.

Arminian

The olive trees and lampstands recall Zechariah 4.

Reformed

Reformed readers see witnesses empowered by the Spirit.

Revelation 11:5

5If anyone desires to harm them, fire proceeds out of their mouth and devours their enemies. If anyone desires to harm them, he must be killed in this way.

Arminian

Fire from their mouths devours enemies, recalling Elijah. Many read the fire as the power of their word.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the power of God’s word in their testimony.

Revelation 11:6

6These have the power to shut up the sky, that it may not rain during the days of their prophecy. They have power over the waters, to turn them into blood, and to strike the earth with every plague, as often as they desire.

Arminian

They can shut the sky and turn water to blood, like Elijah and Moses.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the witnesses standing in the line of the prophets.

Revelation 11:7

7When they have finished their testimony, the beast that comes up out of the abyss will make war with them, and overcome them, and kill them.

Arminian

When their testimony is finished, the beast kills them. Arminian readers see that God protects his witnesses until their work is done.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that the witnesses cannot be stopped until God’s appointed time.

Revelation 11:8

8Their dead bodies will be in the street of the great city, which spiritually is called Sodom and Egypt, where also their Lord was crucified.

Arminian

The great city is called Sodom and Egypt and is where their Lord was crucified. Some identify it with Jerusalem; others see it as the world city opposed to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers note both readings, with many seeing a symbol of the world in rebellion.

Revelation 11:9

9From among the peoples, tribes, languages, and nations, people will look at their dead bodies for three and a half days, and will not allow their dead bodies to be laid in a tomb.

Arminian

People from all nations look at their bodies and refuse them burial.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the world’s contempt for God’s witnesses.

Revelation 11:10

10Those who dwell on the earth will rejoice over them, and they will be glad. They will give gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who dwell on the earth.

Arminian

The earth rejoices over their death because they tormented them with their message.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the world’s hostility to God’s word.

Revelation 11:11

11After the three and a half days, the breath of life from God entered into them, and they stood on their feet. Great fear fell on those who saw them.

Arminian

The breath of life from God enters them, recalling Ezekiel 37. Arminian readers see God vindicating his faithful servants.

Reformed

Reformed readers see resurrection and vindication as God’s answer to the church’s suffering.

Revelation 11:12

12I heard a loud voice from heaven saying to them, “Come up here!” They went up into heaven in a cloud, and their enemies saw them.

Arminian

They ascend in a cloud while their enemies watch.

Reformed

Reformed readers see vindication before the world.

Revelation 11:13

13In that day there was a great earthquake, and a tenth of the city fell. Seven thousand people were killed in the earthquake, and the rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven.

Arminian

An earthquake kills seven thousand, and the rest are terrified and give glory to God. Interpreters differ on whether this describes repentance or fearful acknowledgment. Arminian readers often see a hopeful response to God’s judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same question. Some see a turning of many to God, which some connect with a future conversion of Israel, while others see only fearful acknowledgment.

Revelation 11:14

14The second woe is past. Behold, the third woe comes quickly.

Arminian

The second woe is past, and the third comes quickly.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the movement toward the climax.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
