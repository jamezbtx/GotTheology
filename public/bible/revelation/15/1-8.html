Passage

Revelation 15

Verses 1–8 · WEB

Seven angels appear with the seven last plagues, which complete God’s wrath. Beside a sea of glass mixed with fire, those who overcame the beast sing the song of Moses and of the Lamb, praising God’s just and true ways. The heavenly temple opens, the angels receive seven golden bowls of wrath, and the temple fills with smoke from God’s glory.

Revelation 15:1

1I saw another great and marvelous sign in the sky: seven angels having the seven last plagues, for in them God’s wrath is finished.

Arminian

The seven last plagues complete God’s wrath. Arminian readers see judgment that comes after repeated calls to repent.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the completion of God’s just purposes.

Revelation 15:2

2I saw something like a sea of glass mixed with fire, and those who overcame the beast, his image, and the number of his name, standing on the sea of glass, having harps of God.

Arminian

The victors stand beside a sea of glass mixed with fire, holding harps.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the redeemed safe beyond judgment, as Israel stood beyond the Red Sea.

Revelation 15:3

3They sang the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, “Great and marvelous are your works, Lord God, the Almighty! Righteous and true are your ways, you King of the nations.

Arminian

They sing the song of Moses and of the Lamb, joining the exodus and the cross. God’s works are great and marvelous, his ways just and true.

Reformed

Reformed readers see one story of redemption from the exodus to Christ, with God praised for his justice and faithfulness.

Revelation 15:4

4Who wouldn’t fear you, Lord, and glorify your name? For you only are holy. For all the nations will come and worship before you. For your righteous acts have been revealed.”

Arminian

“All the nations will come and worship before you.” Arminian readers see God’s desire for all peoples to worship him, fulfilled as nations turn to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise that God will gather worshipers from all nations, as Psalm 86:9 foretold.

Revelation 15:5

5After these things I looked, and the temple of the tabernacle of the testimony in heaven was opened.

Arminian

The heavenly temple of the tabernacle of testimony opens.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment proceeding from God’s holy presence.

Revelation 15:6

6The seven angels who had the seven plagues came out, clothed with pure, bright linen, and wearing golden sashes around their chests.

Arminian

The angels wear pure linen and golden sashes, like priests.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment carried out in holiness.

Revelation 15:7

7One of the four living creatures gave to the seven angels seven golden bowls full of the wrath of God, who lives forever and ever.

Arminian

A living creature gives them bowls full of the wrath of God, who lives forever.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wrath as God’s holy response to sin.

Revelation 15:8

8The temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from his power. No one was able to enter into the temple until the seven plagues of the seven angels would be finished.

Arminian

No one can enter the temple until the plagues are finished, recalling the glory that filled the tabernacle and temple.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the majesty of God in judgment.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
