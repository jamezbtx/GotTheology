Passage

Revelation 2

Verses 12–17 · WEB

Pergamum lives where Satan’s throne is, yet it held to Christ’s name even when Antipas was killed. But some in the church follow the teaching of Balaam and the Nicolaitans. Christ calls them to repent and promises the overcomer hidden manna and a white stone with a new name.

Revelation 2:12

12“To the angel of the assembly in Pergamum write: “He who has the sharp two-edged sword says these things:

Arminian

The sharp two-edged sword is Christ’s word of judgment, which will be turned against false teaching if the church does not repent.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s word as the instrument by which he rules and judges his church.

Revelation 2:13

13“I know your works and where you dwell, where Satan’s throne is. You hold firmly to my name, and didn’t deny my faith in the days of Antipas my witness, my faithful one, who was killed among you, where Satan dwells.

Arminian

Satan’s throne may refer to the city’s emperor worship, its great altar, or its role as a seat of Roman authority. The church held fast even when Antipas died. Arminian readers see faithfulness Christ honors.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith preserved in a hostile setting. Antipas is called “my faithful one,” a title Christ shares with him (1:5).

Revelation 2:14

14But I have a few things against you, because you have there some who hold the teaching of Balaam, who taught Balak to throw a stumbling block before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed to idols, and to commit sexual immorality.

Arminian

The teaching of Balaam refers to Numbers 25 and 31:16, where Israel was led into idolatry and immorality. Some in Pergamum were tolerating similar compromise.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the danger of tolerating teaching that blends faith with idolatry. Church discipline is part of faithfulness.

Revelation 2:15

15So also you likewise have some who hold to the teaching of the Nicolaitans.

Arminian

The Nicolaitans seem to have taught a similar compromise. Arminian readers see a warning against teaching that excuses sin.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church’s duty to guard both doctrine and life.

Revelation 2:16

16Repent therefore, or else I am coming to you quickly and I will make war against them with the sword of my mouth.

Arminian

The call to repent is addressed to the whole church, not only to the false teachers. Arminian readers see the conditional threat as a real summons to respond.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s threat as a means of purifying his church. He will fight the false teachers with his word.

Revelation 2:17

17He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the assemblies. To him who overcomes, to him I will give of the hidden manna, and I will give him a white stone, and on the stone a new name written which no one knows but he who receives it.

Arminian

The hidden manna recalls God’s provision in the wilderness, and the white stone may refer to admission tokens or acquittal verdicts. The new name speaks of personal belonging to Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the new name as a sign of Christ’s personal knowledge of each of his people. The overcomer receives intimacy with him that no one else can share.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
