Passage

Revelation 2

Verses 18–29 · WEB

To Thyatira, Christ speaks as the Son of God with eyes like fire. He praises its growing love, faith, and service, but rebukes it for tolerating a false prophetess called Jezebel. She was given time to repent and refused. The rest are told to hold fast until he comes, and the overcomer is promised authority over the nations and the morning star.

Revelation 2:18

18“To the angel of the assembly in Thyatira write: “The Son of God, who has his eyes like a flame of fire, and his feet are like burnished brass, says these things:

Arminian

Christ is the Son of God, the only time the title appears in Revelation, with eyes that search and feet of bronze. Arminian readers see a Lord who sees everything.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the echo of Psalm 2, quoted in verse 27, where the Son receives the nations.

Revelation 2:19

19“I know your works, your love, faith, service, patient endurance, and that your last works are more than the first.

Arminian

Thyatira’s love, faith, service, and endurance are growing. Arminian readers see progress in grace that Christ notices.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the fruits of grace in a church where sanctification is advancing.

Revelation 2:20

20But I have this against you, that you tolerate your woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess. She teaches and seduces my servants to commit sexual immorality and to eat things sacrificed to idols.

Arminian

“Jezebel” is a symbolic name for a woman teaching compromise with idolatry and immorality, recalling Ahab’s queen. Arminian readers see a church at fault for tolerating her.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church’s failure to exercise discipline. Tolerating false teaching is itself a sin.

Revelation 2:21

21I gave her time to repent, but she refuses to repent of her sexual immorality.

Arminian

“I gave her time to repent, but she refuses” is often cited by Arminian readers. God’s patience offered a real opportunity, and her refusal was her own.

Reformed

Reformed readers also see God’s patience and her responsibility. God’s forbearance leaves the impenitent without excuse.

Key contrast Arminian readers see in this verse grace offered and freely refused. Reformed readers agree that her refusal was her own and that she is responsible, while holding that repentance, when it occurs, is God’s gift.

Revelation 2:22

22Behold, I will throw her and those who commit adultery with her into a bed of great oppression, unless they repent of her works.

Arminian

Judgment will fall on her and on those who join her unless they repent. The offer of repentance remains open even here.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the warning as a call to those led astray to turn back before judgment comes.

Revelation 2:23

23I will kill her children with Death, and all the assemblies will know that I am he who searches the minds and hearts. I will give to each one of you according to your deeds.

Arminian

Christ searches minds and hearts and gives to each according to deeds. Arminian readers see judgment that takes account of each person’s life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ claiming God’s prerogative of searching hearts (Jeremiah 17:10). Judgment according to deeds reveals the reality of each person’s standing.

Revelation 2:24

24But to you I say, to the rest who are in Thyatira—as many as don’t have this teaching, who don’t know what some call ‘the deep things of Satan’—to you I say, I am not putting any other burden on you.

Arminian

Those who have not followed Jezebel are given no further burden. “The deep things of Satan” may be a mocking twist on claims to deep knowledge.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s tenderness toward the faithful remnant.

Revelation 2:25

25Nevertheless, hold that which you have firmly until I come.

Arminian

“Hold that which you have firmly until I come” calls for perseverance. Arminian readers see a command that assumes believers can let go.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the command as the means by which Christ keeps his own until his return.

Revelation 2:26

26He who overcomes, and he who keeps my works to the end, to him I will give authority over the nations.

Arminian

The overcomer is the one who keeps Christ’s works to the end. Arminian readers stress “to the end” as part of the condition.

Reformed

Reformed readers see perseverance to the end as the mark of the true believer, and the authority given as a share in Christ’s reign.

Revelation 2:27

27He will rule them with a rod of iron, shattering them like clay pots, as I also have received of my Father;

Arminian

The promise draws on Psalm 2:9. Believers share in Christ’s rule, received from the Father. Premillennial readers often connect this with a future earthly reign, while amillennial and postmillennial readers often see it fulfilled in the present and final victory of Christ’s kingdom.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same range of millennial views. All agree that Christ shares his rule with his people.

Revelation 2:28

28and I will give him the morning star.

Arminian

The morning star is Christ himself (22:16). The overcomer is given Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the greatest gift as Christ himself.

Revelation 2:29

29He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the assemblies.

Arminian

The refrain calls every church to hear.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit’s word reaching all the churches.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
