Passage

Revelation 16

Verses 12–21 · WEB

The sixth bowl dries up the Euphrates for the kings from the east, and demonic spirits gather the kings of the world for battle at Armageddon. Christ warns that he comes like a thief. The seventh bowl brings a voice declaring “It is done,” a great earthquake, the fall of Babylon, and huge hailstones, yet people still curse God.

Revelation 16:12

12The sixth poured out his bowl on the great river, the Euphrates. Its water was dried up, that the way might be prepared for the kings that come from the sunrise.

Arminian

The Euphrates dries up for the kings from the east. Interpreters link this to Parthian invaders, to future armies, or to a symbol of the world’s powers gathering against God.

Reformed

Reformed readers usually read the imagery symbolically.

Revelation 16:13

13I saw coming out of the mouth of the dragon, and out of the mouth of the beast, and out of the mouth of the false prophet, three unclean spirits, something like frogs;

Arminian

Frog-like unclean spirits come from the dragon, beast, and false prophet.

Reformed

Reformed readers see deception at the root of the world’s opposition.

Revelation 16:14

14for they are spirits of demons, performing signs, which go out to the kings of the whole inhabited earth, to gather them together for the war of that great day of God the Almighty.

Arminian

Demonic spirits gather the kings for the war of the great day of God the Almighty.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God permitting even the gathering of his enemies for their defeat.

Revelation 16:15

15“Behold, I come like a thief. Blessed is he who watches, and keeps his clothes, so that he doesn’t walk naked, and they see his shame.”

Arminian

“Behold, I come like a thief. Blessed is he who watches.” Arminian readers see a call to watchfulness that applies to all believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see watchfulness as the mark of those who belong to Christ.

Revelation 16:16

16He gathered them together into the place which is called in Hebrew, “Harmagedon”.

Arminian

Harmagedon is often linked with Megiddo, the site of famous battles. Futurists often expect a literal battle there, while others see a symbol of the final conflict.

Reformed

Reformed readers usually see the final defeat of God’s enemies rather than a geographic forecast.

Revelation 16:17

17The seventh poured out his bowl into the air. A loud voice came out of the temple of heaven, from the throne, saying, “It is done!”

Arminian

The seventh bowl brings a voice from the throne: “It is done!”

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s purpose accomplished.

Revelation 16:18

18There were lightnings, sounds, and thunders; and there was a great earthquake such as has not happened since there were men on the earth—so great an earthquake and so mighty.

Arminian

The greatest earthquake in history follows.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the shaking of all things before the end.

Revelation 16:19

19The great city was divided into three parts, and the cities of the nations fell. Babylon the great was remembered in the sight of God, to give to her the cup of the wine of the fierceness of his wrath.

Arminian

Babylon is split, the cities of the nations fall, and God remembers Babylon.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the judgment of the world system.

Revelation 16:20

20Every island fled away, and the mountains were not found.

Arminian

Islands flee and mountains disappear.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the end of the present order.

Revelation 16:21

21Great hailstones, about the weight of a talent, came down out of the sky on people. People blasphemed God because of the plague of the hail, for this plague was exceedingly severe.

Arminian

Huge hailstones fall, and people curse God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see rebellion persisting to the end.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
