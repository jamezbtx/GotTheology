Passage

Revelation 16

Verses 1–11 · WEB

The first five bowls are poured out: sores, a sea turned to blood, rivers turned to blood, scorching sun, and darkness on the beast’s throne. An angel and the altar declare God’s judgments true and righteous. People curse God and do not repent.

Revelation 16:1

1I heard a loud voice out of the temple, saying to the seven angels, “Go and pour out the seven bowls of the wrath of God on the earth!”

Arminian

A voice from the temple sends the angels to pour out the bowls.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment under God’s command.

Revelation 16:2

2The first went, and poured out his bowl into the earth, and it became a harmful and painful sore on the people who had the mark of the beast, and who worshiped his image.

Arminian

Sores fall on those with the beast’s mark, recalling the sixth plague of Egypt.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment aimed at those who worshiped the beast.

Revelation 16:3

3The second angel poured out his bowl into the sea, and it became blood as of a dead man. Every living thing in the sea died.

Arminian

The sea becomes like a dead man’s blood.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment now complete rather than partial, as with the trumpets.

Revelation 16:4

4The third poured out his bowl into the rivers and springs of water, and they became blood.

Arminian

Rivers and springs become blood.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the plagues of Egypt intensified.

Revelation 16:5

5I heard the angel of the waters saying, “You are righteous, who are and who were, O Holy One, because you have judged these things.

Arminian

The angel of the waters declares God righteous in his judgments. Arminian readers see that God’s judgment is just.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God vindicated in his justice.

Revelation 16:6

6For they poured out the blood of saints and prophets, and you have given them blood to drink. They deserve this.”

Arminian

Those who shed the blood of saints receive blood to drink. The punishment fits the crime.

Reformed

Reformed readers see just retribution.

Revelation 16:7

7I heard the altar saying, “Yes, Lord God, the Almighty, true and righteous are your judgments.”

Arminian

The altar answers that God’s judgments are true and righteous.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the martyrs’ prayers answered.

Revelation 16:8

8The fourth poured out his bowl on the sun, and it was given to him to scorch men with fire.

Arminian

The sun scorches people with fire.

Reformed

Reformed readers see creation turned against rebels.

Revelation 16:9

9People were scorched with great heat, and people blasphemed the name of God who has the power over these plagues. They didn’t repent and give him glory.

Arminian

People blaspheme God and do not repent. Arminian readers see hardness as their own response to judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that judgment alone does not soften hearts.

Key contrast Both affirm human responsibility for refusing to repent. Arminian readers see a refusal that could have gone otherwise. Reformed readers see the heart’s hardness, which only grace overcomes.

Revelation 16:10

10The fifth poured out his bowl on the throne of the beast, and his kingdom was darkened. They gnawed their tongues because of the pain,

Arminian

The beast’s kingdom is darkened, and people gnaw their tongues in pain.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the beast’s power unable to protect his followers.

Revelation 16:11

11and they blasphemed the God of heaven because of their pains and their sores. They still didn’t repent of their works.

Arminian

They curse God and still do not repent.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the persistence of rebellion.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
