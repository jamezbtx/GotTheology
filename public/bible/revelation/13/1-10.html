Passage

Revelation 13

Verses 1–10 · WEB

A beast rises from the sea with ten horns and seven heads, given power by the dragon. One head seems fatally wounded but is healed, and the world follows the beast in wonder. He blasphemes God and makes war on the saints for forty-two months. Everyone whose name is not written in the Lamb’s book of life worships him. The saints are called to endurance and faith.

Revelation 13:1

1Then I stood on the sand of the sea. I saw a beast coming up out of the sea, having ten horns and seven heads. On his horns were ten crowns, and on his heads, blasphemous names.

Arminian

The beast from the sea draws on Daniel 7. Preterists identify it with Rome and Nero, historicists with the papacy or other powers, futurists with a coming Antichrist, and idealists with the recurring pattern of state power that demands worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers have held many of these views. Older Protestant writers often identified the beast with the papacy, while many modern Reformed interpreters see political power opposed to God throughout history, culminating at the end.

Revelation 13:2

2The beast which I saw was like a leopard, and his feet were like those of a bear, and his mouth like the mouth of a lion. The dragon gave him his power, his throne, and great authority.

Arminian

Leopard, bear, and lion recall Daniel’s four beasts combined into one.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the beast as the sum of earthly empires opposed to God.

Revelation 13:3

3One of his heads looked like it had been wounded fatally. His fatal wound was healed, and the whole earth marveled at the beast.

Arminian

The healed head is often linked with the Nero legend or with a parody of Christ’s resurrection.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a counterfeit of Christ’s death and resurrection.

Revelation 13:4

4They worshiped the dragon because he gave his authority to the beast; and they worshiped the beast, saying, “Who is like the beast? Who is able to make war with him?”

Arminian

People worship the dragon and the beast. Arminian readers see deception that people embrace.

Reformed

Reformed readers see false worship as the core of the conflict.

Revelation 13:5

5A mouth speaking great things and blasphemy was given to him. Authority to make war for forty-two months was given to him.

Arminian

The beast’s authority is “given” for forty-two months.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the limits God places on evil.

Revelation 13:6

6He opened his mouth for blasphemy against God, to blaspheme his name, his dwelling, and those who dwell in heaven.

Arminian

He blasphemes God’s name and dwelling.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the arrogance of power that claims divine honors.

Revelation 13:7

7It was given to him to make war with the saints and to overcome them. Authority over every tribe, people, language, and nation was given to him.

Arminian

He is allowed to make war on the saints and overcome them. Arminian readers see that faithful believers may suffer and die, while remaining victorious in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see suffering permitted by God, which does not overturn the church’s final victory.

Revelation 13:8

8All who dwell on the earth will worship him, everyone whose name has not been written from the foundation of the world in the book of life of the Lamb who has been killed.

Arminian

Those whose names are not written in the Lamb’s book of life from the foundation of the world worship the beast. Arminian readers often note that the phrase “from the foundation of the world” may attach to the Lamb who was killed, as in some translations, and they read the book of life alongside 3:5, which speaks of names not being blotted out. They also hold that God’s foreknowledge of believers does not cause their faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the verse as strong support for unconditional election: names written from the foundation of the world are kept from the beast’s deception. In the WEB, the phrase “from the foundation of the world” is joined to the writing of names, as in 17:8.

Key contrast This verse bears directly on election. Reformed readers see names written before creation, so that the elect are kept from worshiping the beast. Arminian readers note that the phrase may describe the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world, and they read the book of life in terms of foreknowledge and continued faith.

Revelation 13:9

9If anyone has an ear, let him hear.

Arminian

“If anyone has an ear, let him hear” calls every reader to attention.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the refrain of the letters applied to the whole church.

Revelation 13:10

10If anyone is to go into captivity, he will go into captivity. If anyone is to be killed with the sword, he must be killed. Here is the endurance and the faith of the saints.

Arminian

Captivity and the sword come to those destined for them; this calls for the endurance and faith of the saints. Arminian readers see a call to faithful endurance rather than resistance by force.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s providence over the sufferings of his people and the endurance that marks them.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
