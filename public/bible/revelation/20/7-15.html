Passage

Revelation 20

Verses 7–15 · WEB

When the thousand years end, Satan is released and gathers the nations, Gog and Magog, against the camp of the saints and the beloved city, but fire from heaven consumes them and the devil is thrown into the lake of fire. John then sees a great white throne. The dead, great and small, are judged according to their works from the books, and anyone not found written in the book of life is thrown into the lake of fire, which is the second death.

Revelation 20:7

7And after the thousand years, Satan will be released from his prison

Arminian

Satan is released from his prison. Arminian premillennialists see a final test at the end of Christ’s earthly reign, showing that even ideal conditions do not change hearts that refuse God.

Reformed

Amillennial and postmillennial Reformed readers usually take the release as a brief time of intense deception just before Christ returns. Both stress that the release, like the binding, happens only by God’s permission.

Revelation 20:8

8and he will come out to deceive the nations which are in the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them together to the war, whose number is as the sand of the sea.

Arminian

He goes out to deceive the nations at the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, as numerous as the sand of the sea. Arminian commentators note that the nations are deceived, yet they still choose to follow and are held responsible.

Reformed

The names come from Ezekiel 38–39. Reformed commentators often see this battle as the same final conflict described in 16:14 and 19:19, told again from another angle.

Revelation 20:9

9They went up over the width of the earth and surrounded the camp of the saints and the beloved city. Fire came down out of heaven from God and devoured them.

Arminian

The nations surround the camp of the saints and the beloved city, and fire comes down from heaven to devour them. Arminian readers see that the church does not win by its own strength; God defends his people.

Reformed

There is no battle, only the fire of God. Reformed readers find the scene a clear picture of God’s sovereign protection of his church in its most vulnerable hour.

Revelation 20:10

10The devil who deceived them was thrown into the lake of fire and sulfur, where the beast and the false prophet are also. They will be tormented day and night forever and ever.

Arminian

The devil joins the beast and the false prophet in the lake of fire, tormented day and night forever. Arminian writers see the final defeat of the deceiver who had opposed God’s purposes since Eden.

Reformed

The enemy who accused the brothers in 12:10 is now judged forever. Reformed writers see the decisive end of Satan’s work, which was never outside God’s control.

Revelation 20:11

11I saw a great white throne and him who sat on it, from whose face the earth and the sky fled away. There was found no place for them.

Arminian

A great white throne appears, and earth and sky flee from the face of the one seated on it. Arminian interpreters see the holiness of God before which no created thing can stand on its own.

Reformed

The one on the throne is not named, but John 5:22 says the Father has given all judgment to the Son. Reformed interpreters see the triune God judging through the Son.

Revelation 20:12

12I saw the dead, the great and the small, standing before the throne, and they opened books. Another book was opened, which is the book of life. The dead were judged out of the things which were written in the books, according to their works.

Arminian

The dead, great and small, stand before the throne. Books recording their works are opened, and so is the book of life. Arminian theologians emphasize that people are judged according to their works, which shows that human choices carry eternal weight; many also hold that a name can be blotted from the book of life (3:5).

Reformed

Reformed theologians note that the book of life is distinct from the books of works and point to 13:8 and 17:8, which speak of names written from the foundation of the world. Works are the evidence that is weighed, while life itself rests on God’s prior grace.

Revelation 20:13

13The sea gave up the dead who were in it. Death and Hades gave up the dead who were in them. They were judged, each one according to his works.

Arminian

The sea, death, and Hades give up their dead, and each one is judged by his works. Arminian readers see a universal judgment that leaves no one out and treats each person individually.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the judgment is just because it is according to works. For believers, those works are covered by Christ’s righteousness and stand as the fruit of a faith that was given.

Revelation 20:14

14Death and Hades were thrown into the lake of fire. This is the second death, the lake of fire.

Arminian

Death and Hades are thrown into the lake of fire, which is the second death. Arminian expositors see the final defeat of death itself, as Paul says in 1 Corinthians 15:26.

Reformed

Reformed expositors see the enemies that entered through sin finally destroyed. The second death is the eternal separation that the first resurrection, in verse 6, escapes.

Revelation 20:15

15If anyone was not found written in the book of life, he was cast into the lake of fire.

Arminian

Anyone not found in the book of life is thrown into the lake of fire. Arminian readers stress that this verse describes the result of persistent refusal, not a sentence that God fixed before people could respond.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the book of life as the record of God’s gracious election. Those not written there are judged for their own sins, and those written there are saved by mercy, not merit.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
