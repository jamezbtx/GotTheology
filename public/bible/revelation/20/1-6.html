Passage

Revelation 20

Verses 1–6 · WEB

An angel binds the dragon, who is the devil and Satan, for a thousand years and seals him in the abyss so that he can no longer deceive the nations. John sees thrones and the souls of those beheaded for the testimony of Jesus, who come to life and reign with Christ for a thousand years. This is the first resurrection, and the second death has no power over those who share in it. The passage is the source of the premillennial, amillennial, and postmillennial positions, and each is held by both Arminian and Reformed believers.

Revelation 20:1

1I saw an angel coming down out of heaven, having the key of the abyss and a great chain in his hand.

Arminian

An angel comes down holding the key of the abyss and a great chain. Arminian readers note the chapter’s placement after the rider’s victory in chapter 19, which premillennialists take as a sequence in time. Many Methodists, Wesleyans, and Pentecostals hold some form of premillennialism, though others in the same traditions are amillennial.

Reformed

The key and chain show that Satan is under God’s authority. Reformed readers are divided on how chapter 20 relates to chapter 19. Many confessional Reformed churches read the chapter as amillennial, recapitulating the church age, while some Reformed and Puritan writers held postmillennial or premillennial views.

Revelation 20:2

2He seized the dragon, the old serpent, who is the devil and Satan, who deceives the whole inhabited earth, and bound him for a thousand years,

Arminian

Satan is seized and bound for a thousand years. Premillennialists, including many Arminians, expect this binding after Christ’s return, when the devil’s influence on earth will be removed for a long period.

Reformed

Amillennial Reformed interpreters connect the binding with Jesus’ words about binding the strong man in Matthew 12:29, so that it began with Christ’s first coming. Postmillennial Reformed writers also place it in the present age and expect its effects to grow as the gospel spreads.

Revelation 20:3

3and cast him into the abyss, and shut it and sealed it over him, that he should deceive the nations no more until the thousand years were finished. After this, he must be freed for a short time.

Arminian

The purpose of the binding is stated: he will deceive the nations no more until the thousand years end. Arminian premillennialists take this to mean a time with no satanic deception at all, which they find hard to fit with the present world.

Reformed

Amillennialists answer that the binding is limited to one purpose, so that Satan cannot stop the gospel reaching the nations. Postmillennialists add that this freedom of the gospel will produce a long period of widespread faith before Christ returns.

Revelation 20:4

4I saw thrones, and they sat on them, and judgment was given to them. I saw the souls of those who had been beheaded for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God, and such as didn’t worship the beast nor his image, and didn’t receive the mark on their forehead and on their hand. They lived and reigned with Christ for a thousand years.

Arminian

Thrones are set, and the souls of the martyrs and of those who refused the beast’s mark live and reign with Christ. Arminian premillennialists usually read “they lived” as bodily resurrection at Christ’s return, followed by a reign on earth.

Reformed

Amillennial Reformed readers usually see the souls of believers who have died reigning with Christ in heaven now, during the interval before the final resurrection. Postmillennialists vary, but many see the reign as the triumph of the martyrs’ cause through the church on earth.

Revelation 20:5

5The rest of the dead didn’t live until the thousand years were finished. This is the first resurrection.

Arminian

The rest of the dead do not live until the thousand years end, and the first resurrection is named. Premillennialists, Arminian and Reformed alike, argue that the same verb for “live” should mean bodily resurrection in both halves of the verse, giving two resurrections separated by the thousand years.

Reformed

Many Reformed amillennialists answer that the first resurrection is spiritual, either new birth or the believer’s entrance into Christ’s presence at death, while the general resurrection of all comes at the end. They point to John 5:25–29, where spiritual and bodily resurrection appear side by side.

Revelation 20:6

6Blessed and holy is he who has part in the first resurrection. Over these, the second death has no power, but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and will reign with him one thousand years.

Arminian

Those who share in the first resurrection are called blessed and holy; the second death has no power over them, and they serve as priests of God and Christ. Arminian readers find comfort here regardless of their millennial view, since faithfulness under pressure is rewarded.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the promise rests on Christ, with whom believers reign. Whatever the timing of the thousand years, the security of those who belong to him is the point all three views share.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
