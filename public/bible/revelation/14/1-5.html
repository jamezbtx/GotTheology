Passage

Revelation 14

Verses 1–5 · WEB

The Lamb stands on Mount Zion with the 144,000 who bear his name and his Father’s name. They sing a new song only they can learn, follow the Lamb wherever he goes, and are blameless, redeemed as firstfruits to God and the Lamb.

Revelation 14:1

1I saw, and behold, the Lamb standing on Mount Zion, and with him a number, one hundred forty-four thousand, having his name and the name of his Father written on their foreheads.

Arminian

The Lamb on Mount Zion with the 144,000 answers the beast and his mark. They bear the names of the Lamb and his Father.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the security of God’s people, sealed with his name.

Revelation 14:2

2I heard a sound from heaven like the sound of many waters and like the sound of a great thunder. The sound which I heard was like that of harpists playing on their harps.

Arminian

A sound like many waters and harps fills heaven.

Reformed

Reformed readers see heavenly worship.

Revelation 14:3

3They sing a new song before the throne and before the four living creatures and the elders. No one could learn the song except the one hundred forty-four thousand, those who had been redeemed out of the earth.

Arminian

Only those redeemed from the earth can learn the new song. Arminian readers see the song of those who have experienced redemption.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a song known only to the redeemed, purchased from the earth.

Revelation 14:4

4These are those who were not defiled with women, for they are virgins. These are those who follow the Lamb wherever he goes. These were redeemed by Jesus from among men, the first fruits to God and to the Lamb.

Arminian

They are virgins who follow the Lamb wherever he goes, redeemed as firstfruits. Most read virginity as spiritual purity from idolatry, since idolatry is described as immorality in the book.

Reformed

Reformed readers see spiritual faithfulness and wholehearted devotion to the Lamb. Firstfruits suggests a people belonging wholly to God.

Revelation 14:5

5In their mouth was found no lie, for they are blameless.

Arminian

No lie was found in their mouths. Arminian readers see holiness of life in those redeemed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see blamelessness as the fruit of redemption, standing before God in Christ.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
