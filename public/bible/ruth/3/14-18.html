Passage

Ruth 3

Verses 14–18 · WEB

Ruth leaves before dawn, and Boaz guards her reputation. He sends her home with six measures of barley so she will not return empty to Naomi. Naomi counsels patience: Boaz will not rest until the matter is settled that day.

Ruth 3:14

14She lay at his feet until the morning, then she rose up before one could discern another. For he said, “Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor.”

Arminian

Ruth stays at his feet, as the text specifies, and rises before dawn. Boaz’s concern that her visit not be known protects her reputation from misunderstanding; the night has been honorable, and he wants the outcome decided by the proper public process rather than by gossip.

Reformed

The narrator again places Ruth “at his feet,” keeping the scene clear. Boaz’s discretion guards Ruth’s good name and the integrity of the legal matter still to be settled. Both traditions see wisdom here: avoiding even the appearance of wrong while doing right.

Ruth 3:15

15He said, “Bring the mantle that is on you, and hold it.” She held it; and he measured six measures of barley, and laid it on her; then he went into the city.

Arminian

Boaz fills Ruth’s mantle with six measures of barley, a generous gift whose exact quantity the text does not specify. It sends her home with a tangible pledge of his intent and care. Then he goes into the city to act.

Reformed

The six measures of barley are a visible token of Boaz’s goodwill and promise; the Hebrew leaves the unit unstated, so the precise amount is uncertain. His immediate departure for the city shows his promise is not delayed. Both traditions see generosity and resolve in the redeemer’s actions.

Ruth 3:16

16When she came to her mother-in-law, she said, “How did it go, my daughter?” She told her all that the man had done for her.

Arminian

Naomi’s question, “How did it go, my daughter?” shows her eager concern. Ruth reports all that Boaz did. Open communication between the two women continues the trust built through the book.

Reformed

Ruth reports faithfully to Naomi, who had sent her. Reformed readers note how the narrative keeps every step transparent within the family. The whole account is brought into the light.

Ruth 3:17

17She said, “He gave me these six measures of barley; for he said, ‘Don’t go empty to your mother-in-law.’ ”

Arminian

Ruth adds Boaz’s words, “Don’t go empty to your mother-in-law.” Naomi had said she returned “empty” (1:21); now the LORD, through Boaz, begins to fill her. The gift is aimed at Naomi as much as Ruth.

Reformed

The word “empty” deliberately recalls Naomi’s lament in 1:21. The barley is a sign that the Lord is reversing her emptiness through Boaz’s care. Both traditions read this as God’s kindness reaching a grieving widow through ordinary means.

Ruth 3:18

18Then she said, “Wait, my daughter, until you know what will happen; for the man will not rest until he has settled this today.”

Arminian

Naomi counsels patience: Ruth must wait while Boaz acts. Naomi, who sought “rest” for Ruth (3:1), now says the man “will not rest” until the matter is settled. Arminian readers see trust in God paired with confidence in a righteous man’s freely given word.

Reformed

Naomi’s counsel to wait expresses trust that the matter is now in good hands. The wordplay on rest closes the chapter: Boaz will not rest so that Ruth may have rest. Reformed readers, like Arminians, see providence working through human faithfulness, here leading toward redemption and the Davidic line.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
