Passage

Matthew 17

Verses 9–13 · WEB · Voices

Coming down from the mountain, Jesus forbids reporting the vision until the resurrection. The disciples ask about Elijah’s expected coming; Jesus teaches that Elijah has already come in John the Baptist’s rejected ministry, and that the Son of Man will likewise suffer.

Matthew 17:9

9As they were coming down from the mountain, Jesus commanded them, saying, “Don’t tell anyone what you saw, until the Son of Man has risen from the dead.”

Arminian

Glory without the cross would mislead. Silence is temporary stewardship until resurrection interprets the vision.

Timing matters in bearing witness. Grace teaches when to speak; the risen Christ will authorize open proclamation.

Reformed

Christ controls the publicity of His own glory. Resurrection is the appointed unlock for this testimony.

Providence governs revelation’s schedule. The Son of Man’s rising will make the mountain’s meaning clear.

Matthew 17:10

10His disciples asked him, saying, “Then why do the scribes say that Elijah must come first?”

Arminian

The disciples voice their confusion honestly. If Jesus is Messiah and glory has appeared, where is the promised Elijah?

Sincere questions are welcomed. Grace leads learners from tradition’s slogans to Jesus’ explanation.

Reformed

They bring scribal expectation to the Teacher. Scripture’s promise is assumed; its fulfillment needs Christ’s light.

Christ is the interpreter of prophecy. Disciples grow as He corrects and completes their categories.

Matthew 17:11

11Jesus answered them, “Elijah indeed comes first, and will restore all things;

Arminian

Jesus affirms the promise: Elijah’s restoring work is made in earnest. Hope in God’s Word is not dismissed.

Classical Arminians hear confirmation that God keeps covenant speech. The question becomes how the promise lands in history—and how hearers respond.

Reformed

Christ grants the premise before He redefines the timeline. Prophecy stands; fulfillment is His to unfold.

God’s Word does not fail. The Son explains how the Father’s plan has already moved.

Matthew 17:12

12but I tell you that Elijah has come already, and they didn’t recognize him, but did to him whatever they wanted to. Even so the Son of Man will also suffer by them.”

Arminian

John was the Elijah who was to come—and was unrecognized, mistreated, killed. The same pattern awaits the Son of Man.

Light can be resisted. Grace brought John’s call to repent; rejection was culpable. The cross likewise meets a blameworthy “Anyone.”

Reformed

Recognition failed; violence followed. Jesus binds John’s fate to His own certain suffering.

Hostility to God’s messengers is not surprise but part of the redemptive path the Father has ordained for the Son.

Key contrast Both take rejection of John and of Jesus as serious guilt. Arminians stress resistible light and responsible refusal; Reformed readers emphasize that even this hostility serves the Father’s certain purpose for the suffering Son.

Matthew 17:13

13Then the disciples understood that he spoke to them of John the Baptizer.

Arminian

Understanding finally dawns. Prophecy and recent memory click together under Jesus’ word.

Teaching that clarifies Scripture enables a truer response. Disciples keep learning as grace opens meaning.

Reformed

Christ’s explanation grants insight they did not produce alone. John is identified; the path of suffering is clarified.

Spiritual understanding is given. The Lord who veiled the vision now unveils John’s place in the plan.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Coming down the mountain, Jesus forbids reporting the vision until the resurrection: older Arminian and Reformed writers, each in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“Tell the vision to no man - Not to the rest of the disciples, lest they should be grieved and discouraged because they were not admitted to the sight: nor to any other persons, lest it should enrage some the more, and his approaching sufferings shall make others disbelieve it; till the Son of man be risen again - Till the resurrection should make it credible, and confirm their testimony about it. Why then say the scribes, that Elijah must come first - Before the Messiah?”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 17:9–13)

Reformed Notable voices

“And as they were going down from the mountain. We have said that the time for making known the vision was not yet fully come; and, indeed, the disciples would not have believed it, if Christ had not given a more striking proof of his glory in his resurrection.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 17:9–13)
