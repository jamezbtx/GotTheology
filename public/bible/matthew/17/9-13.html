Passage

Matthew 17

Verses 9–13 · ESV · Voices

Coming down the mountain, Jesus forbids reporting the vision until the resurrection. The disciples ask about Elijah’s expected coming; Jesus teaches that Elijah has already come in John the Baptist’s rejected ministry, and that the Son of Man will likewise suffer.

Matthew 17:9

9And as they were coming down the mountain, Jesus commanded them, “Tell no one the vision, until the Son of Man is raised from the dead.”

Arminian

Glory without the cross would mislead. Silence is temporary stewardship until resurrection interprets the vision.

Arminian reading: timing in witness matters. Grace teaches when to speak; the risen Christ will authorize open proclamation.

Reformed

Christ controls the publicity of His own glory. Resurrection is the appointed unlock for this testimony.

Reformed note: providence governs revelation’s schedule. The Son of Man’s rising will make the mountain’s meaning clear.

Matthew 17:10

10And the disciples asked him, “Then why do the scribes say that first Elijah must come?”

Arminian

Honest confusion surfaces. If Jesus is Messiah and glory has appeared, where is the promised Elijah?

Arminian pastoral tone: sincere questions are welcomed. Grace leads learners from tradition’s slogans to Jesus’ explanation.

Reformed

They bring scribal expectation to the Teacher. Scripture’s promise is assumed; its fulfillment needs Christ’s light.

Reformed framing: Christ is the interpreter of prophecy. Disciples grow as He corrects and completes their categories.

Matthew 17:11

11He answered, “Elijah does come, and he will restore all things.

Arminian

Jesus affirms the promise: Elijah’s restoring work is real. Hope in God’s Word is not dismissed.

Classical Arminians hear confirmation that God keeps covenant speech. The question becomes how the promise lands in history—and how hearers respond.

Reformed

Christ grants the premise before He redefines the timeline. Prophecy stands; fulfillment is His to unfold.

Reformed confidence: God’s Word does not fail. The Son explains how the Father’s plan has already moved.

Matthew 17:12

12But I tell you that Elijah has already come, and they did not recognize him, but did to him whatever they pleased. So also the Son of Man will certainly suffer at their hands.”

Arminian

John was the Elijah who was to come—and was unrecognized, mistreated, killed. The same pattern awaits the Son of Man.

Arminian warning: light can be resisted. Grace brought John’s call to repent; rejection was culpable. The cross likewise meets a real, blameworthy “No.”

Reformed

Recognition failed; violence followed. Jesus binds John’s fate to His own certain suffering.

Reformed gravity: hostility to God’s messengers is not surprise but part of the redemptive path the Father has ordained for the Son.

Key contrast Both take rejection of John and of Jesus as serious guilt. Arminians typically stress resistible light and responsible refusal; Reformed readers typically stress that even this hostility serves the Father’s certain purpose for the suffering Son.

Matthew 17:13

13Then the disciples understood that he was speaking to them of John the Baptist.

Arminian

Understanding dawns. Prophecy and recent memory click together under Jesus’ word.

Arminian joy: teaching that clarifies Scripture enables a truer response. Disciples keep learning as grace opens meaning.

Reformed

Christ’s explanation grants insight they did not produce alone. John is identified; the path of suffering is clarified.

Reformed note: spiritual understanding is given. The same Lord who veiled the vision now unveils John’s place in the plan.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on coming down the mountain, Jesus forbids reporting the vision until the resurrection—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 17:9–13.

Arminian Notable voices

“Tell the vision to no man - Not to the rest of the disciples, lest they should be grieved and discouraged because they were not admitted to the sight: nor to any other persons, lest it should enrage some the more, and his approaching sufferings shall make others disbelieve it; till the Son of man be risen again - Till the resurrection should make it credible, and confirm their testimony about it. Why then say the scribes, that Elijah must come first - Before the Messiah?”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 17:9–13)

Reformed Notable voices

“And as they were going down from the mountain. We have said that the time for making known the vision was not yet fully come; and, indeed, the disciples would not have believed it, if Christ had not given a more striking proof of his glory in his resurrection.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 17:9–13)
