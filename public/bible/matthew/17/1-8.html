Sample passage

Matthew 17

Verses 1–8 · ESV

Six days after Peter’s confession and the first passion prediction, Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up a high mountain. He is transfigured; Moses and Elijah appear; the Father’s voice from the bright cloud commands them to listen to the beloved Son. The disciples fall in fear; Jesus touches them, and they see Jesus only.

Matthew 17:1

1And after six days Jesus took with him Peter and James, and John his brother, and led them up a high mountain by themselves.

Arminian

After the hard word of the cross (16:21–28), Jesus grants a selected three a mountain vision. Grace often prepares faith with both warning and wonder.

Arminian pastoral reading: God sincerely strengthens disciples for the road ahead. The invitation upward is gift; they must still climb in obedient trust.

Reformed

Timing (“after six days”) ties glory to the passion path already announced. Christ chooses witnesses according to His purpose.

Reformed accent: revelation is scheduled by the Lord, not seized by the crowd. What they will see is preparation for Gethsemane and the cross.

Matthew 17:2

2And he was transfigured before them, and his face shone like the sun, and his clothes became white as light.

Arminian

The veiled glory of the Son briefly shines. Disciples glimpse who has been walking with them in ordinary flesh.

Classical Arminians rejoice that God makes Christ known so that faith may rest on a real Person. The light is given to be received, not earned by spiritual achievement.

Reformed

Metamorphosis before them: the Son’s majesty breaks through. This is not borrowed radiance but His own.

Reformed comfort: the same Lord who will suffer is already glorious. Faith is steadied by God’s disclosure of who Jesus truly is.

Matthew 17:3

3And behold, there appeared to them Moses and Elijah, talking with him.

Arminian

Law and Prophets stand with Jesus—not as rivals, but as witnesses. Conversation centers on Him.

Arminian hope: all prior revelation leads to Christ and still calls for a living response. Scripture’s storyline presses hearers toward the Son.

Reformed

Moses and Elijah appear as servants flanking the Master. Redemptive history converges on this mountain.

Reformed note: the covenant Word finds its goal in Christ. God Himself arranges the scene so that Jesus is not one prophet among equals.

Matthew 17:4

4And Peter said to Jesus, “Lord, it is good that we are here. If you wish, I will make three tents here, one for you and one for Moses and one for Elijah.”

Arminian

Peter’s zeal is sincere and confused. Equal tents would flatten the hierarchy the Father is about to declare.

Arminian caution: warm religious impulse still needs correcting by grace. Loving Jesus does not exempt us from learning His unique place.

Reformed

“If you wish” sounds humble, yet the plan levels Christ with Moses and Elijah. Affection can still mis-order glory.

Reformed realism: even favored disciples need the Father’s voice to reorder their theology. Christ will not share equal booths with the servants.

Matthew 17:5

5He was still speaking when, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them, and a voice from the cloud said, “This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased; listen to him.”

Arminian

The Father interrupts Peter’s building plan. Identity (“beloved Son”) and command (“listen to him”) are the heart of the vision.

Classical Arminians hear a genuine summons to all: grace reveals the Son and enables obedience; hearers remain responsible to listen rather than silence Him.

Reformed

Shekinah cloud and heavenly voice seal Jesus’ identity. Pleasure and belovedness echo the baptism; the added “listen” crowns the revelation.

Reformed weight: saving knowledge of the Son is the Father’s gift and charge. The church lives under this royal command.

Key contrast Both receive the Father’s word as decisive. Arminians typically stress grace that enables a real, obedient hearing of the Son; Reformed readers typically stress the Father’s effectual revelation that secures true listening in His people.

Matthew 17:6

6When the disciples heard this, they fell on their faces and were terrified.

Arminian

Holy fear is the right first response to the Father’s voice. Proximity to glory undoes casual religion.

Arminian pastoral note: conviction and awe prepare the heart for the comfort Jesus will give. Grace both humbles and restores.

Reformed

Hearing produces prostration. Sinners cannot stand unshaken before unveiled majesty.

Reformed sobriety: true revelation includes holy dread. God is not managed; disciples are undone before they are lifted.

Matthew 17:7

7But Jesus came and touched them, saying, “Rise, and have no fear.”

Arminian

The same Jesus who shines in glory comes near with a human touch. Comfort follows command.

Arminian gospel tone: the Son who must be heard is also the Savior who raises the fearful. Grace invites rising trust, not paralysis.

Reformed

Incarnate kindness meets holy terror. Touch and word together restore the witnesses.

Reformed comfort: the Mediator stands between glory and frailty. He who is the beloved Son also shepherds trembling disciples.

Matthew 17:8

8And when they lifted up their eyes, they saw no one but Jesus only.

Arminian

Moses and Elijah vanish; the vision ends with Jesus alone. The point was never equal tents but exclusive attention to the Son.

Arminian application: Christian life keeps lifting the eyes to Christ. Grace continually recenters faith on Him alone for salvation and obedience.

Reformed

“Jesus only” is the theological summary of the mountain. Servants withdraw; the Son remains.

Reformed confession: all lesser lights yield to Christ. The church’s hope, hearing, and holiness are fixed on Him alone.

About this sample. Commentary here is written for product demonstration. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words on revelation, the beloved Son, and listening to Christ—so each camp speaks beside the Transfiguration.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… according to the scriptures, many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“Hear ye him—As if he had said, Hear not Moses, or Elijah: they were only servants; hear Him, who is my beloved Son.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 17:5)

Reformed Notable voices

“Listen to him. These words are very emphatic; for they mean that… we must listen to him alone… The Father commands us to listen to him alone.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 17:5)
“Why is he called God’s only begotten Son, when we also are children of God? Because Christ alone is the eternal, natural Son of God. We, however, are children of God by adoption, through grace, for Christ’s sake.”
Heidelberg Catechism · Q&A 33 (1563)

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
