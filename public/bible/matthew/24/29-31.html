Passage

Matthew 24

Verses 29–31 · ESV

After the tribulation of those days, Jesus describes cosmic shaking, the sign of the Son of Man, worldwide mourning, and His cloud-coming in power and glory. Angels with trumpet gather His elect from the four winds—public, royal, and final for the hope of His people.

Matthew 24:29

29"Immediately after the tribulation of those days the sun will be darkened, and the moon will not give its light, and the stars will fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens will be shaken.

Arminian

Immediately after that tribulation, lights fail and heavens shake. Creation itself signals the next act.

Classical Arminians hear a call to sober hope: the darkening is not meaningless terror. Grace invites readiness for the Son who comes after the shaking, not despair under the sky’s collapse.

Reformed

Cosmic disorder follows tribulation on Jesus’ timetable—“immediately after.” Sun, moon, stars, and powers answer His word.

Reformed awe: the Creator who hung the lights also darkens them. Providence rules the heavens’ shaking as stage-setting for the Son of Man.

Matthew 24:30

30Then will appear in heaven the sign of the Son of Man, and then all the tribes of the earth will mourn, and they will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory.

Arminian

The sign appears; tribes mourn; all see Him coming on the clouds with power and glory. No secret tip-off needed.

Arminian urgency: mourning can still turn to faith for those who hear now. Grace offers Christ before the sky splits; the public coming vindicates the gospel already preached.

Reformed

Heaven’s sign, earth’s mourning, and cloud-coming with power and glory fulfill Daniel’s Son of Man vision under Jesus’ claim.

Reformed confession: every tribe will see what God has appointed. The glory is His; mourning meets majesty on a day no rumor can manufacture.

Key contrast Both confess a public, glorious coming. Arminians typically emphasize present gospel urgency before that day; Reformed readers typically emphasize God’s appointed unveiling that every tribe will witness.

Matthew 24:31

31And he will send out his angels with a loud trumpet call, and they will gather his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other."

Arminian

Angels, trumpet, and gathering of His elect from everywhere. The separation is personal and complete.

Classical Arminians rejoice that the gathered are Christ’s. Grace that called them also keeps them to this assembly; the trumpet is comfort for those who endure in Him.

Reformed

He sends angels; the trumpet sounds; the elect are gathered from the four winds.

Reformed comfort: election and gathering meet. The same Lord who chose them sends for them—none of His are lost in the cosmic shake.

Key contrast Both rejoice in the elect’s gathering. Arminians typically stress grace that sustains unto that assembly; Reformed readers typically stress God’s electing purpose executed by angelic gathering at Christ’s command.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
