Passage

Matthew 27

Verses 57–61 · ESV · Voices

Evening falls. Joseph of Arimathea, a rich disciple, asks Pilate for Jesus’ body, wraps it in clean linen, and lays it in his own new rock tomb; Mary Magdalene and the other Mary sit opposite, watching.

Matthew 27:57

57When it was evening, there came a rich man from Arimathea, named Joseph, who also was a disciple of Jesus.

Arminian

Evening brings Joseph—rich, named, a disciple. Courage surfaces when the body needs burying.

Arminian encouragement: grace can embolden hidden disciples at the needed hour. Wealth is no barrier when put to Christ’s honor.

Reformed

Joseph of Arimathea steps forward. Providence supplies a tomb through a rich disciple.

Reformed comfort: God had prepared burial means. Isaiah’s “with a rich man in his death” hangs over the scene.

Matthew 27:58

58He went to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus. Then Pilate ordered it to be given to him.

Arminian

He asks Pilate for the body—public identification with the Crucified. Pilate grants it.

Arminian pastoral: costly association is part of discipleship. Grace enables going public when fear would stay home.

Reformed

Body requested and given. Rome releases what it killed; God steers the corpse toward a known tomb.

Reformed note: no lost body, no anonymous pit. Providence guards the burial for Easter’s sake.

Matthew 27:59

59And Joseph took the body and wrapped it in a clean linen shroud

Arminian

Clean linen for a holy body. Care replaces the morning’s scorn.

Arminian tone: love handles Jesus with honor. Grace teaches reverence for the Lord who died for us.

Reformed

Clean shroud: dignity in burial. The Passion’s humiliation does not end in refuse.

Reformed sobriety: even burial details serve the story God is telling toward resurrection.

Matthew 27:60

60and laid it in his own new tomb, which he had cut in the rock. And he rolled a great stone to the entrance of the tomb and went away.

Arminian

His own new tomb, rock-cut, stone rolled. Jesus rests in a borrowed, unused grave.

Arminian gospel: He occupies death’s space for us. Grace invites trust that the stone is not the last word.

Reformed

New tomb, great stone: secure by human measure. The stage is set for a sealed, guarded, then empty grave.

Reformed assurance: God appoints a known tomb so resurrection will be public, not private spiritualizing.

Matthew 27:61

61Mary Magdalene and the other Mary were there, sitting opposite the tomb.

Arminian

Two Marys sit opposite the tomb—watching, grieving, remembering the place.

Arminian pastoral: faithful presence matters. Grace keeps witnesses near so hope can find them at dawn.

Reformed

Women opposite the tomb: continuity of witness from cross to burial.

Reformed preparation: they know the location. Easter morning will not be a hunt for the wrong grave.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on evening falls—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 27:57–61.

Arminian Notable voices

“When the evening was come - That is, after three o'clock; the time from three to six they termed the evening.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 27:57–61)

Reformed Notable voices

“Let it be understood that Joseph did not come in the dusk of the evening, but before sunset, that he might perform this office of kindness to his Master, without violating the Sabbath; for the Sabbath commenced in the evening, and therefore it was necessary that Christ should be laid in the grave before night came on. Now from the time that Christ died until the Sabbath began to be observed, there were three free days.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 27:57–61)
