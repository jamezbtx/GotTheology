Passage

Matthew 27

Verses 62–66 · ESV

After Preparation Day, chief priests and Pharisees ask Pilate to secure the tomb against a stolen-body story. Pilate grants a guard; they seal the stone—unwittingly strengthening the later witness of resurrection.

Matthew 27:62

62The next day, that is, after the day of Preparation, the chief priests and the Pharisees gathered before Pilate

Arminian

Leaders gather again—this time about a tomb. Anxiety about Jesus outlives His death.

Arminian note: unbelief stays busy. Grace still offers a better gathering—around the living Christ—yet they organize against hope.

Reformed

Post-Preparation counsel before Pilate. Providence records their fear of the third day.

Reformed irony: enemies remember Jesus’ words better than some friends. Their gathering will serve Easter apologetics.

Matthew 27:63

63and said, “Sir, we remember how that impostor said, while he was still alive, ‘After three days I will rise.’

Arminian

They call Him impostor yet quote His rising word. Memory without faith becomes a security strategy.

Arminian warning: recalling Jesus’ promises while refusing Him is possible. Grace invites remembering unto trust, not unto sabotage.

Reformed

“After three days I will rise”—quoted by foes. Scripture lets enemies become transmitters of the promise.

Reformed comfort: God can use hostile memory to set the stage. The word of rising stands, even in a sneer.

Key contrast Both notice foes remember the rising claim. Arminians typically stress responsible misuse of remembered light; Reformed readers typically stress providence turning their quote into Easter scaffolding.

Matthew 27:64

64Therefore order the tomb to be made secure until the third day, lest his disciples go and steal him away and tell the people, ‘He has risen from the dead,’ and the last fraud will be worse than the first.”

Arminian

Secure until the third day—they fear a stolen-body sermon. Fraud language reveals hearts that will not allow resurrection as option.

Arminian application: controlling the narrative is not the same as seeking truth. Grace calls for open honesty before God’s power.

Reformed

Security requested against “fraud.” Their plan aims to kill hope; God will overturn the seal.

Reformed confidence: the worse-fraud fear becomes the stronger witness when the tomb empties under guard.

Matthew 27:65

65Pilate said to them, “You have a guard of soldiers. Go, make it as secure as you can.”

Arminian

Make it as secure as you can—human maximum security. Pilate shrugs responsibility again while enabling their plan.

Arminian note: “as you can” has limits. Grace points beyond human seals to the God who raises the dead.

Reformed

A guard granted. Rome’s power pads the stone.

Reformed irony: “as secure as you can” will prove how little “you can” against God’s yes to the Son.

Matthew 27:66

66So they went and made the tomb secure by sealing the stone and setting a guard.

Arminian

Seal and guard: official finality. Chapter 27 ends with a locked grave and anxious religion.

Arminian close: the story is not finished. Grace invites waiting in hope for the God who breaks seals. Freeze the heart, not the gospel—Christ will rise.

Reformed

Sealed stone, set guard: enemies finish their precautions. Matthew ends the chapter on their terms so Easter can shatter them.

Reformed doxology: providence lets them seal what God will open. The Passion chapter closes; resurrection dawn is next.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
