Passage

Matthew 27

Verses 62–66 · WEB · Voices

After Preparation Day, chief priests and Pharisees ask Pilate to secure the tomb against a stolen-body story. Pilate grants a guard; they seal the stone—unwittingly strengthening the later witness of resurrection.

Matthew 27:62

62Now on the next day, which was the day after the Preparation Day, the chief priests and the Pharisees were gathered together to Pilate,

Arminian

Leaders gather again—this time about a tomb. Anxiety about Jesus outlives His death.

Unbelief stays busy. Grace still offers a better gathering—around the living Christ—yet they organize against hope.

Reformed

The day after the Preparation, the leaders take counsel before Pilate. Providence records their fear of the third day.

Enemies remember Jesus’ words better than some friends. Their gathering will serve Easter apologetics.

Matthew 27:63

63saying, “Sir, we remember what that deceiver said while he was still alive: ‘After three days I will rise again.’

Arminian

They call Him impostor yet quote His rising word. Memory without faith becomes a security strategy.

Recalling Jesus’ promises while refusing Him is possible. Grace invites remembering unto trust, not unto sabotage.

Reformed

“After three days I will rise”—quoted by foes. Scripture lets enemies become transmitters of the promise.

God can use hostile memory to set the stage. The word of rising stands, even in a sneer.

Key contrast Both notice foes remember the rising claim. Arminians emphasize responsible misuse of remembered light; Reformed commentators stress providence turning their quote into Easter scaffolding.

Matthew 27:64

64Command therefore that the tomb be made secure until the third day, lest perhaps his disciples come at night and steal him away, and tell the people, ‘He is risen from the dead;’ and the last deception will be worse than the first.”

Arminian

Secure until the third day—they fear a stolen-body sermon. Fraud language reveals hearts that will not allow resurrection as option.

Controlling the narrative is not the same as seeking truth. Grace calls for open honesty before God’s power.

Reformed

Security requested against fraud. Their plan aims to kill hope; God will overturn the seal.

The worse-fraud fear becomes the stronger witness when the tomb empties under guard.

Matthew 27:65

65Pilate said to them, “You have a guard. Go, make it as secure as you can.”

Arminian

Make it as secure as you can—human maximum security. Pilate shrugs responsibility again while enabling their plan.

“as you can” has limits. Grace points beyond human seals to the God who raises the dead.

Reformed

Pilate grants them a guard. Rome’s power pads the stone.

“As secure as you can” will prove how little “you can” against God’s yes to the Son.

Matthew 27:66

66So they went with the guard and made the tomb secure, sealing the stone.

Arminian

Seal and guard: official finality. Chapter 27 ends with a locked grave and anxious religion.

The story is not finished. Grace invites waiting in hope for the God who breaks seals. Freeze the heart, not the gospel—Christ will rise.

Reformed

Sealed stone, set guard: enemies finish their precautions. Matthew ends the chapter on their terms so Easter can shatter them.

Providence lets them seal what God will open. The Passion chapter closes; resurrection dawn is next.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Arminian and Reformed writers of the past on chief priests and Pharisees ask Pilate to secure the tomb against a stolen-body story, in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“On the morrow, the day that followed the day of the preparation - The day of preparation was the day before the Sabbath, whereon they were to prepare for the celebration of it. The next day then was the Sabbath according to the Jews. But the evangelist seems to express it by this circumlocution, to show the Jewish Sabbath was then abolished.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 27:62–66)

Reformed Notable voices

“In this narrative Matthew did not so much intend to show with what determined rage the scribes and priests pursued Christ, as to exhibit to us, as in a mirror, the amazing providence of God in proving the resurrection of his Son. Cunning men, practiced at least in fraud and treachery, plot among themselves, and contrive a method by which they may extinguish the memory of a dead man; for they see that they have gained nothing, if they do not destroy the certainty of the resurrection.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 27:62–66)
