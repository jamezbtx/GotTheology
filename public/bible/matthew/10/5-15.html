Passage

Matthew 10

Verses 5–15 · ESV

Jesus sends the Twelve on a time-bound mission to Israel’s lost sheep: proclaim the kingdom, heal freely, travel light, seek worthy houses, and treat rejection with solemn witness. Priority to Israel here does not cancel later Gentile mission; it honors covenant order as the King advances.

Matthew 10:5

5These twelve Jesus sent out, instructing them, “Go nowhere among the Gentiles and enter no town of the Samaritans,

Arminian

Sending comes with limits: not Gentiles, not Samaritan towns—yet. Mission is obedience to Jesus’ timing, not freelance zeal.

Classical Arminians hear a temporary focus, not a closed heart. The same Lord will later command all nations (28:19). Grace that orders steps also widens the field in due time.

Reformed

Instruction precedes travel. The King’s strategy is specific: Israel first in this charge.

Reformed accent: redemptive history has sequence. Covenant priority is not ethnic pride; it is the Father’s wise order culminating in a gospel for the nations through Israel’s Messiah.

Matthew 10:6

6but go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.

Arminian

“Lost sheep” names need without denying belonging. Israel is addressed as the covenant people who have wandered; mercy seeks them first in this sending.

Arminian pastoral reading: the Shepherd’s heart pursues the lost. Hearers remain responsible to return; the seekers are sent because grace initiates the chase.

Reformed

Lost, yet “of the house of Israel.” Election’s story runs through a people God has claimed, now summoned by messengers of the King.

Reformed comfort: Christ defines the field. The Twelve do not invent the flock; they go where He points, under the same mercy that will later gather other folds.

Matthew 10:7

7And proclaim as you go, saying, ‘The kingdom of heaven is at hand.’

Arminian

Message matches Jesus’ own (4:17): the kingdom is near. Preaching is not optional add-on; it is the center of the journey.

Arminians stress offer and urgency. “At hand” presses decision—repent and believe while the King draws near. Grace enables response; delay is perilous.

Reformed

The heralds announce what God is bringing, not a human program. Kingdom nearness is Christ’s presence and reign breaking in.

Reformed reading: proclamation is effectual in God’s hand. The same word that announces also creates hearers who receive—and hardens those who refuse (cf. vv. 14–15).

Matthew 10:8

8Heal the sick, raise the dead, cleanse lepers, cast out demons. You received without paying; give without pay.

Arminian

Works of mercy authenticate the message. Freely received, freely given: kingdom power is not for sale.

Arminian application: ministry that monetizes grace contradicts the charge. What Christ gave without price, messengers must not peddle. Serve generously because you have been served.

Reformed

Authority from v. 1 becomes concrete commands. Healing, raising, cleansing, exorcism display the King’s reign against curse and chaos.

Reformed accent: “without paying… without pay” guards the freeness of grace. Gifts are stewardship, not inventory. The Sender remains the source.

Key contrast Both reject commercializing gospel power. Arminians often emphasize generous response to free grace; Reformed readers often emphasize that every gift remains God’s, exercised under Christ’s lordship.

Matthew 10:9

9Acquire no gold or silver or copper for your belts,

Arminian

Travel light: no stockpiled coins. Dependence is discipleship in motion.

Arminian pastoral note: trust is practiced, not merely affirmed. The command invites real reliance on the Father’s care through His people.

Reformed

Provision strategy is theological. Belts without metal declare that the mission rests on God’s faithfulness, not apostolic reserves.

Reformed comfort: providence funds the work. The King who sends also supplies—often through the worthy houses of vv. 11–13.

Matthew 10:10

10no bag for your journey, or two tunics or sandals or a staff, for the laborer deserves his food.

Arminian

Minimal gear, maximum trust. Yet “the laborer deserves his food”—dependence is not denial of fair support from those who receive the word.

Arminians hold both: go without grasping, and receive with gratitude. Hospitality is partnership in the gospel, not a tip for services.

Reformed

Limits on packing protect focus. The laborers’ due food is God’s ordinance through the flock, not a wage negotiated against the message.

Reformed reading: Christ authorizes support without authorizing greed. Worthiness of food is His verdict on the work, not a market rate.

Matthew 10:11

11And whatever town or village you enter, find out who is worthy in it and stay there until you depart.

Arminian

Seek the “worthy”—those open to the message—and abide. Stability beats restless shopping for better lodging.

Arminian note: worthiness here is receptive faith and hospitality, not moral perfection. People may welcome or refuse; the messengers must discern and remain faithful.

Reformed

Inquiry and endurance: find receptivity, then stay. Mission is not tourism.

Reformed accent: God prepares households. “Worthy” marks those in whom grace has made room for the King—discerned in practice by welcome of His word and His servants.

Matthew 10:12

12As you enter the house, greet it.

Arminian

Greeting is more than manners; it offers peace (v. 13). Enter with blessing ready.

Arminian application: approach homes and hearts with goodwill. The gospel’s first word is not scorn but peace extended in Christ’s name.

Reformed

Entrance begins with spoken peace. The messengers carry Christ’s benediction into ordinary rooms.

Reformed reading: even greeting is ministry. What they bring is not their private charm but the King’s peace under His authority.

Matthew 10:13

13And if the house is worthy, let your peace come upon it, but if it is not worthy, let your peace return to you.

Arminian

Peace lands where welcome is real; it returns where it is refused. The offer is sincere either way.

Classical Arminians hear genuine conditionality in human response. Grace truly extends peace; households are responsible for how they receive the messengers of the King.

Reformed

Peace is not magic; it follows worthiness as Jesus defines it. Rejection does not empty the blessing—it returns to the messengers.

Reformed accent: God’s peace is never wasted. Refusal exposes the house; the apostles are kept. Providence sorts reception without making the gospel less free in its offer.

Key contrast Both affirm a real offer of peace and real refusal. Arminians typically stress responsible welcome or rejection; Reformed readers typically stress that receptivity itself is grace-wrought, while refusal remains culpable.

Matthew 10:14

14And if anyone will not receive you or listen to your words, shake off the dust from your feet when you leave that house or town.

Arminian

Rejection of messengers is rejection of the message. Dust-shaking is solemn testimony, not petty revenge—leave the refusal on record and move on.

Arminian pastoral balance: keep offering elsewhere; do not pretend indifference is neutral. Listeners are accountable for shutting the door on Christ’s words.

Reformed

Symbolic act from Jewish travel practice becomes prophetic protest. Unbelief is not a lifestyle option; it is judgment already signaled.

Reformed reading: the apostles’ departure is itself a word. Christ authorizes both persistence and this stark sign when hearing is refused.

Matthew 10:15

15Truly, I say to you, it will be more bearable on the day of judgment for the land of Sodom and Gomorrah than for that town.

Arminian

Amen-word of Jesus: greater light means greater accountability. Sodom lacked this kingdom announcement; refusing apostles is graver.

Arminian urgency: the day of judgment is real, and present opportunity is mercy. Do not harden while the King sends His peace to your door.

Reformed

Degrees of judgment match degrees of revelation refused. “Truly” seals divine certainty.

Reformed sobriety: the same mission that saves also leaves towns without excuse. Christ’s “I say to you” is the court already speaking in advance of the last day.

Key contrast Both take judgment with full seriousness. Arminians typically press the urgent, responsible choice while light is given; Reformed readers typically stress God’s just grading of privilege refused under His sovereign moral government.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
