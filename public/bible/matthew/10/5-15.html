Passage

Matthew 10

Verses 5–15 · WEB · Voices

Jesus sends the Twelve on a time-bound mission to Israel’s lost sheep: proclaim the kingdom, heal freely, travel light, seek worthy houses, and treat rejection with solemn witness. Priority to Israel here does not annul later Gentile mission; it honors covenant order as the King advances.

Matthew 10:5

5Jesus sent these twelve out and commanded them, saying, “Don’t go among the Gentiles, and don’t enter into any city of the Samaritans.

Arminian

Sending comes with limits: not Gentiles, not Samaritan towns—yet. Mission is obedience to Jesus’ timing, not freelance zeal.

Wesleyan Arminians find a temporary focus, not a closed heart. The same Lord will later command all nations (28:19). Grace that orders steps also widens the field in due time.

Reformed

Jesus gives instructions before they travel. The King’s strategy is specific: Israel first in this charge.

Redemptive history has sequence. Covenant priority is not ethnic pride; it is the Father’s wise order culminating in a gospel for the nations through Israel’s Messiah.

Matthew 10:6

6Rather, go to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.

Arminian

“Lost sheep” names need without denying belonging. Israel is addressed as the covenant people who have wandered; mercy seeks them first in this sending.

The Shepherd’s heart pursues the lost. Hearers remain responsible to return; the seekers are sent because grace initiates the chase.

Reformed

Lost, yet “of the house of Israel.” Election’s story runs through a people God has claimed, now summoned by messengers of the King.

Christ defines the field. The Twelve do not invent the flock; they go where He points, under the same mercy that will later gather other folds.

Matthew 10:7

7As you go, preach, saying, ‘The Kingdom of Heaven is at hand!’

Arminian

Message matches Jesus’ own (4:17): the kingdom is near. Preaching is not optional add-on; it is the center of the journey.

Arminians stress offer and urgency. “At hand” presses decision—repent and believe while the King draws near. Grace enables response; delay is perilous.

Reformed

The heralds announce what God is bringing, not a human program. Kingdom nearness is Christ’s presence and reign breaking in.

Proclamation is effectual in God’s hand. The same word that announces also creates hearers who receive—and hardens those who refuse (cf. vv. 14–15).

Matthew 10:8

8Heal the sick, cleanse the lepers, and cast out demons. Freely you received, so freely give.

Arminian

Works of mercy authenticate the message. Freely received, freely given: kingdom power is not for sale.

Ministry that monetizes grace contradicts the charge. What Christ gave without price, messengers must not peddle. Serve generously because you have been served.

Reformed

Authority from v. 1 becomes concrete commands. Healing, raising, cleansing, exorcism display the King’s reign against curse and chaos.

“without paying… without pay” guards the freeness of grace. Gifts are stewardship, not inventory. The Sender remains the source.

Key contrast Both reject commercializing gospel power. Arminians often emphasize generous response to free grace; Reformed readers often emphasize that every gift remains God’s, exercised under Christ’s lordship.

Matthew 10:9

9Don’t take any gold, silver, or brass in your money belts.

Arminian

Travel light: no stockpiled coins. Dependence is discipleship in motion.

Trust is practiced, not merely affirmed. The command invites reliance on the Father’s care through His people.

Reformed

Provision strategy is theological. Belts without metal declare that the mission rests on God’s faithfulness, not apostolic reserves.

Providence funds the work. The King who sends also supplies—often through the worthy houses of vv. 11–13.

Matthew 10:10

10Take no bag for your journey, neither two coats, nor sandals, nor staff: for the laborer is worthy of his food.

Arminian

The disciples take minimal gear and exercise maximum trust. Yet “the laborer is worthy of his food”—dependence is not denial of fair support from those who receive the word.

Arminians hold both: go without grasping, and receive with gratitude. Hospitality is partnership in the gospel, not a tip for services.

Reformed

Limits on packing protect focus. The laborers’ due food is God’s ordinance through the flock, not a wage negotiated against the message.

Christ authorizes support without authorizing greed. Worthiness of food is His verdict on the work, not a market rate.

Matthew 10:11

11Into whatever city or village you enter, find out who in it is worthy, and stay there until you go on.

Arminian

Seek the “worthy”—those open to the message—and abide. Stability beats restless shopping for better lodging.

Worthiness here is receptive faith and hospitality, not moral perfection. People may welcome or refuse; the messengers must discern and remain faithful.

Reformed

Inquiry and endurance: find receptivity, then stay. Mission is not tourism.

God prepares households. “Worthy” marks those in whom grace has made room for the King—discerned in practice by welcome of His word and His servants.

Matthew 10:12

12As you enter into the household, greet it.

Arminian

Greeting is more than manners; it offers peace (v. 13). Enter with blessing ready.

Approach homes and hearts with goodwill. The gospel’s first word is not scorn but peace extended in Christ’s name.

Reformed

Entrance begins with spoken peace. The messengers carry Christ’s benediction into ordinary rooms.

Even greeting is ministry. What they bring is not their private charm but the King’s peace under His authority.

Matthew 10:13

13If the household is worthy, let your peace come on it, but if it isn’t worthy, let your peace return to you.

Arminian

Peace lands where welcome is not in doubt; it returns where it is refused. The offer is sincere either way.

The passage carries conditionality in human response. Grace truly extends peace; households are responsible for how they receive the messengers of the King.

Reformed

Peace is not magic; it follows worthiness as Jesus defines it. Rejection does not empty the blessing—it returns to the messengers.

God’s peace is never wasted. Refusal exposes the house; the apostles are kept. Providence sorts reception without making the gospel less free in its offer.

Key contrast Both affirm a serious offer of peace and refusal. Arminians stress responsible welcome or rejection; Reformed commentators press that receptivity itself is grace-wrought, while refusal remains culpable.

Matthew 10:14

14Whoever doesn’t receive you or hear your words, as you go out of that house or that city, shake the dust off your feet.

Arminian

Rejection of messengers is rejection of the message. Dust-shaking is solemn testimony, not petty revenge—leave the refusal on record and move on.

Keep offering elsewhere; do not pretend indifference is neutral. Listeners are accountable for shutting the door on Christ’s words.

Reformed

Symbolic act from Jewish travel practice becomes prophetic protest. Unbelief is not a lifestyle option; it is judgment already signaled.

The apostles’ departure is itself a word. Christ authorizes both persistence and this stark sign when hearing is refused.

Matthew 10:15

15Most certainly I tell you, it will be more tolerable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrah in the day of judgment than for that city.

Arminian

Amen-word of Jesus: greater light means greater accountability. Sodom lacked this kingdom announcement; refusing apostles is graver.

The day of judgment is in earnest, and present opportunity is mercy. Do not harden while the King sends His peace to your door.

Reformed

Degrees of judgment match degrees of revelation refused. Truly seals divine certainty.

The same mission that saves also leaves towns without excuse. Christ’s “I tell you” is the court already speaking in advance of the last day.

Key contrast Both take judgment with full seriousness. Arminians typically press the urgent, responsible choice while light is given; Reformed readers emphasize God’s just grading of privilege refused under His sovereign moral government.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Older voices from both traditions on Matthew 10:5–15.

Arminian Notable voices

“These twelve Jesus sent forth - Herein exercising his supreme authority, as God over all. None but God can give men authority to preach his word. Go not - Their commission was thus confined now, because the calling of the Gentiles was deferred till after the more plentiful effusion of the Holy Ghost on the day of pentecost.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 10:5–15)

Reformed Notable voices

“As the embassy was of such a nature, that Christ wished the disciples to traverse the whole of Judea within a few days, and immediately to return to him, he forbids to carry luggage with them, by which this speed may be retarded. Some have ignorantly supposed that the rule here laid down for the ministers of the word, or for the apostles, is perpetual.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 10:5–15)
