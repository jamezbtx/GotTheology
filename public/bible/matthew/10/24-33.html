Sample passage

Matthew 10

Verses 24–33 · ESV

Jesus reframes fear: disciples share the Master’s mistreatment, yet must proclaim openly. Fear God rather than body-killers; trust the Father who numbers hairs and values sparrows; acknowledge Christ before people, for He will acknowledge or deny before the Father.

Matthew 10:24

24“A disciple is not above his teacher, nor a servant above his master.

Arminian

Status check: followers do not outrank Jesus. Expect to walk His path, not a safer one.

Arminian application: humility protects from entitlement. If the Teacher suffered, disciples should not demand exemption.

Reformed

Hierarchy of the kingdom: Teacher and Master first. Discipleship is conformation, not promotion above Christ.

Reformed accent: union with Christ includes sharing His pattern of rejection under the Father’s will.

Matthew 10:25

25It is enough for the disciple to be like his teacher, and the servant like his master. If they have called the master of the house Beelzebul, how much more will they malign those of his household.

Arminian

“Enough” is contentment with likeness to Jesus—even when that likeness means being labeled demonic. Household members share the Master’s slander.

Arminian pastoral care: do not be shocked by misrepresentation. Stay faithful; likeness to Christ is the goal, not public applause.

Reformed

Beelzebul already flung at Jesus (cf. 9:34; 12:24) forecasts worse for the household. Maligning is “how much more,” not less.

Reformed comfort: insult for Christ’s sake confirms belonging. The household is real; so is the hostility aimed at its Head.

Matthew 10:26

26“So have no fear of them, for nothing is covered that will not be revealed, or hidden that will not be known.

Arminian

Therefore: no fear of slanderers. Hidden truth will come out; God vindicates in His time.

Arminians hear an invitation to courage. Choose fearlessness because revelation is certain—walk in the light you have.

Reformed

Fear is answered by eschatological disclosure. Cover-ups fail; God’s verdict stands.

Reformed reading: providence and judgment guarantee exposure. Courage rests on God’s future, not on present reputation management.

Matthew 10:27

27What I tell you in the dark, say in the light, and what you hear whispered, proclaim on the housetops.

Arminian

Private instruction becomes public proclamation. Whisper to housetop: the gospel is not a secret society.

Arminian zeal: speak openly. Grace that opened your ear now opens your mouth for others to hear and respond.

Reformed

Transmission mandate: dark to light, whisper to housetops. Revelation received must be revelation published.

Reformed accent: Christ authors both the quiet teaching and the loud mission. Bold speech is obedience to the King’s communications plan.

Matthew 10:28

28And do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Rather fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell.

Arminian

Right fear displaces wrong fear. Body-killers have a limit; God alone holds soul and body under judgment.

Classical Arminians press holy fear that leads to repentance and fidelity. The warning is real: hell is not metaphor, and people must take God seriously.

Reformed

Theology of fear: creaturely violence is finite; divine judgment is total. Hell language is Jesus’ own.

Reformed sobriety and comfort: fearing God frees from tyranny of men. The Judge who can destroy is also the Father of vv. 29–31 for His own.

Key contrast Both reject fearing men above God and affirm hell’s reality. Arminians typically emphasize the warning as a real call to repentant fear; Reformed readers typically emphasize God’s absolute judicial rights and the security of those who fear Him aright.

Matthew 10:29

29Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? And not one of them will fall to the ground apart from your Father.

Arminian

Cheap sparrows still fall only under the Father’s care. Lowly lives are not ignored.

Arminian comfort: the Father’s attentiveness invites trust. You may cast anxiety on Him because nothing is random to Him.

Reformed

Providence reaches the cheapest bird. “Apart from your Father” denies chance.

Reformed accent: meticulous care. If sparrows are not outside His rule, disciples under persecution are never outside His will.

Matthew 10:30

30But even the hairs of your head are all numbered.

Arminian

Hyper-particular care: numbered hairs. The Father’s knowledge is intimate, not abstract.

Arminian pastoral note: you are known. Approach Him boldly; He already counts what you cannot.

Reformed

Omniscient tenderness. Numbered hairs are covenant reassurance under threat of death.

Reformed comfort: the God who elects also inventories. No detail of His people escapes His decree or His love.

Matthew 10:31

31Fear not, therefore; you are of more value than many sparrows.

Arminian

Therefore fear not: worth exceeds sparrows. Value language motivates trust and courage.

Arminians hear the Father’s heart inviting confidence. Receive the comfort; refuse panic.

Reformed

Comparative worth seals the argument. Fear dissolves under valuation by the Father.

Reformed reading: worth is assigned by God, not earned by sparrows-or-better performance. The Father’s appraisal grounds fearlessness.

Matthew 10:32

32So everyone who acknowledges me before men, I also will acknowledge before my Father who is in heaven,

Arminian

Public acknowledgment of Jesus meets His acknowledgment before the Father. Confession is not optional piety; it is the path of salvation’s open loyalty.

Arminian emphasis: a real condition with a real promise. Grace enables confession; people must confess. Christ truly owns those who own Him.

Reformed

Reciprocal acknowledgment: before men / before the Father. The Son’s advocacy is tied to open allegiance.

Reformed accent: those effectually drawn confess; Christ confesses them in heaven. The verse describes the saved life, secured by the Son before the Father.

Key contrast Both require public confession of Christ. Arminians typically stress a grace-enabled condition people may meet or refuse; Reformed readers typically stress that true confessors are those Christ savingly owns, whose confession He Himself secures.

Matthew 10:33

33but whoever denies me before men, I also will deny before my Father who is in heaven.

Arminian

Denial before people meets denial before the Father. Soft discipleship that hides Christ courts eternal loss.

Arminian warning with tears: do not disown Him under pressure. The threat is sincere; repentance remains available while it is today.

Reformed

Symmetric severity: deny / be denied. Final denial marks those never truly His in saving faith (cf. 7:23), while temporary failure (Peter) is restored by grace.

Reformed pastoral care: the warning is real and drives perseverance; it does not make salvation a wage. Christ keeps His own even as He threatens empty profession.

About this sample. Commentary here is written for product demonstration. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
