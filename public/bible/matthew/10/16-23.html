Passage

Matthew 10

Verses 16–23 · ESV · Voices

Jesus widens the mission charge into realism: sheep among wolves, courts and kings, family betrayal, hatred for His name, endurance unto salvation, and flight from town to town until the Son of Man comes. Courage here is Spirit-supplied witness, not naïveté.

Matthew 10:16

16“Behold, I am sending you out as sheep in the midst of wolves, so be wise as serpents and innocent as doves.

Arminian

Behold: the Sender knows the danger. Sheep among wolves need both shrewdness and purity—neither cynicism nor gullibility.

Classical Arminians hear a real charge to cultivate wisdom and innocence. Grace enables; disciples remain responsible to walk carefully without becoming wolves themselves.

Reformed

Christ sends into hostility under His care. Serpent-wisdom and dove-innocence are gifts of character for a hostile age.

Reformed accent: the “I am sending” governs the scene. Danger is not accident; mission proceeds under the King’s providence and moral formation.

Matthew 10:17

17Beware of men, for they will deliver you over to courts and flog you in their synagogues,

Arminian

Beware is pastoral, not paranoid. Religious courts will flog messengers of the kingdom. Expect opposition inside familiar structures.

Arminian application: clear eyes protect soft hearts. Knowing the cost helps disciples choose faithfulness when pressure comes.

Reformed

Men will hand over; synagogues will flog. Conflict is foretold so faith is not shocked into collapse.

Reformed comfort: Jesus scripts the trouble before it arrives. Suffering for the gospel is not outside His plan.

Matthew 10:18

18and you will be dragged before governors and kings for my sake, to bear witness before them and the Gentiles.

Arminian

Civil power becomes a pulpit. “For my sake” and “to bear witness” redefine humiliation as opportunity toward rulers and Gentiles.

Arminians stress the purpose: testimony offered so hearers may believe. Even coercion can open doors for a free gospel invitation.

Reformed

Dragged, yet purposeful. Witness before governors and Gentiles is the Father’s stage for the Son’s name.

Reformed reading: persecution advances mission. What enemies intend for silence, God turns into proclamation under His decree.

Matthew 10:19

19When they deliver you over, do not be anxious how you are to speak or what you are to say, for what you are to say will be given to you in that hour.

Arminian

Anxiety about words is forbidden because help is promised in the hour. Prepare a life of faith; trust timely speech.

Arminian pastoral note: the promise invites dependence, not laziness. God supplies what obedience needs when the crisis comes.

Reformed

Deliverance to courts meets deliverance of words. “Will be given” is divine provision in the crisis moment.

Reformed accent: the Spirit’s timing is perfect. Anxiety yields because God Himself undertakes the testimony.

Matthew 10:20

20For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father speaking through you.

Arminian

The Father’s Spirit speaks through willing messengers. Human agency remains; the power is divine.

Classical Arminians treasure this cooperation: disciples open their mouths; the Spirit fills them. Shared ground: witness is Spirit-empowered, never mere rhetoric.

Reformed

Ultimate speaker: the Spirit of your Father. Apostolic courage is Trinitarian help.

Reformed comfort: God does not abandon His witnesses. Through them He Himself addresses courts and kings.

Key contrast Both confess Spirit-empowered speech. Arminians typically emphasize grace-enabled human speaking; Reformed readers typically emphasize that God Himself is the decisive speaker through His servants.

Matthew 10:21

21Brother will deliver brother over to death, and the father his child, and children will rise against parents and have them put to death,

Arminian

Family ties fracture under allegiance to Jesus. Love of kin does not excuse betrayal of the truth—or guarantee loyalty to it.

Arminian warning: choose Christ even when homes divide. The gospel’s claim is personal and costly.

Reformed

Domestic hatred is prophesied, not celebrated. The sword of division (vv. 34–36) already casts its shadow.

Reformed sobriety: sin’s reach into households shows how deep the conflict with the King runs. Endurance will need more than natural affection.

Matthew 10:22

22and you will be hated by all for my name’s sake. But the one who endures to the end will be saved.

Arminian

Hatred “for my name’s sake” marks true discipleship. Salvation is promised to those who endure—perseverance is necessary and grace-enabled.

Arminian pastoral reading: endure; do not quit. God’s grace sustains, and believers must continue. Final salvation and present faithfulness belong together.

Reformed

Universal hostility for the Name, then the promise: endurance to the end marks the saved.

Reformed accent: those God saves He keeps, and He keeps them in the path of endurance. Perseverance is fruit of grace, not a human ladder to earn life.

Key contrast Both require endurance and reject cheap discipleship. Arminians typically stress necessary, grace-enabled continuance that can be forsaken; Reformed readers typically stress that the elect will endure because God preserves them to the end.

Matthew 10:23

23When they persecute you in one town, flee to the next, for truly, I say to you, you will not have gone through all the towns of Israel before the Son of Man comes.

Arminian

Flight is permitted wisdom, not cowardice. Keep moving with the message. The Son of Man’s coming presses urgency on Israel’s towns.

Arminian note: use prudence; keep proclaiming. Timing language fuels mission zeal without licensing despair.

Reformed

Fleeing town to town is commanded strategy under persecution. The “truly” promise ties Israel’s unfinished circuit to the Son of Man’s coming (debated as AD 70, resurrection, or consummation—Matthew’s point is certainty and urgency).

Reformed reading: Christ’s coming governs the calendar of mission. Messengers are not to seek martyrdom for its own sake; they are to finish the assigned circuit under His lordship.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Jesus widens the mission charge into realism: sheep among wolves, courts and kings, family betrayal—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 10:16–23.

Arminian Notable voices

“But think not that all your innocence and all your wisdom will screen you from persecution. They will scourge you in their synagogues - In these the Jews held their courts of judicature, about both civil and ecclesiastical affairs. Take no thought - Neither at this time, on any sudden call, need we be careful how or what to answer.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 10:16–23)

Reformed Notable voices

“Behold, I send you out The exhortation which immediately follows plainly shows the design of this admonition; and therefore the order of the passage must be explained in this manner: "You have need of wisdom and of harmlessness, because you will be like sheep in the midst of wolves " The reason is drawn from the necessity of the case: for if they did not wisely exercise caution, they might be immediately devoured by the wolves; and, on the other hand, if they trembled at the rage of the wolves, or were incautious, they would presently waver, and would at length fail to perform their duty.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 10:16–23)
