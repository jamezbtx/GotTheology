Passage

Matthew 10

Verses 40–42 · ESV · Voices

The discourse closes with hospitality theology: receiving messengers is receiving Christ and the Father; prophetic and righteous welcome carry matching reward; even a cup of cold water to a little disciple is remembered. Mission hardship is matched by kingdom honor for those who welcome the sent.

Matthew 10:40

40“Whoever receives you receives me, and whoever receives me receives him who sent me.

Arminian

Chain of reception: you → Me → the Father. How people treat messengers is how they treat God.

Arminian pastoral weight: welcome the gospel’s servants sincerely. The offer of Christ arrives in human faces; receiving them is a real response to grace.

Reformed

Identification theology: apostles represent Christ; Christ represents the Sender. Hospitality becomes liturgy of recognition.

Reformed accent: union and mission meet. God has so bound Himself to His sent ones that reception of them is reception of Him.

Matthew 10:41

41The one who receives a prophet because he is a prophet will receive a prophet’s reward, and the one who receives a righteous person because he is a righteous person will receive a righteous person’s reward.

Arminian

Motive matters: receive “because” of what they are in God’s work. Shared reward language honors partnership with the righteous and the prophetic word.

Arminians encourage intentional welcome. You may share in the fruit of ministries you support. Grace invites generous recognition of God’s servants.

Reformed

Because-clauses protect against celebrity hospitality. Reward tracks recognition of God’s offices and righteousness.

Reformed reading: God repays according to truth. Union with prophet or righteous person in welcome is ordered by the King who assigns rewards.

Matthew 10:42

42And whoever gives one of these little ones even a cup of cold water because he is a disciple, truly, I say to you, he will by no means lose his reward.”

Arminian

Little ones and cold water: small kindness to a disciple is seen. “By no means lose” is emphatic assurance.

Arminian encouragement: no act of mercy toward Christ’s people is wasted. You are invited into simple, sincere care—and God remembers.

Reformed

Amen-certainty: the smallest cup given for discipleship’s sake is secured reward. “Little ones” are precious to the King.

Reformed comfort: God notices what the world discounts. Reward is His promise, not human accounting—and it will not be lost.

Key contrast Both honor tiny mercies to disciples. Arminians typically stress the sincere, responsible kindness God invites and rewards; Reformed readers typically stress that such fruit and its reward rest on God’s faithful promise to those united to Christ.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on The discourse closes with hospitality theology: receiving messengers is receiving Christ and the Father; prophetic—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 10:40–42.

Arminian Notable voices

“He that entertaineth a prophet - That is, a preacher of the Gospel: In the name of a prophet - That is, because he is such, shall share in his reward. One of these little ones - The very least Christian.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 10:40–42)

Reformed Notable voices

“He who loveth father or mother As it is exceedingly harsh, and is contrary to natural feelings, to make enemies of those who ought to have been in closest alliance with us, so Christ now says that we cannot be his disciples on any other condition. He does not indeed enjoin us to lay aside human affections, or forbid us to discharge the duties of relationship, but only desires that all the mutual love which exists among men should be so regulated as to assign the highest rank to piety.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 10:40–42)
