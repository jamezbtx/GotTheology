Passage

Matthew 28

Verses 11–15 · ESV · Voices

While the women go with Easter news, some of the guard report to the chief priests. The leaders bribe the soldiers to spread a theft story, promise protection from the governor, and a rival account takes root that Matthew says persists to his day.

Matthew 28:11

11While they were going, behold, some of the guard went into the city and told the chief priests all that had taken place.

Arminian

Parallel paths: women run with truth; guards report “all that had taken place” to priests. Facts reach the council even as gospel runs to disciples.

Arminian warning: knowledge of events is not repentance. Hearing what happened at the tomb can still harden into damage control instead of faith.

Reformed

Providence lets soldiers carry news to the same leaders who sealed the tomb. “All that had taken place” enters hostile ears.

Reformed sobriety: God’s act is not hidden from opponents. The question becomes what sinners do with undeniable disturbance.

Matthew 28:12

12And when they had assembled with the elders and taken counsel, they gave a sufficient sum of money to the soldiers

Arminian

Assembly, counsel, cash—religion manages crisis by bribery. A “sufficient sum” buys a story. Silver again stains holy office.

Arminian application: hearts can prefer paid narrative over living Christ. Grace still calls leaders to truth; counsel without conversion invents cover.

Reformed

Elders join priests; money follows counsel. Institutional power tries to purchase silence about resurrection.

Reformed clarity: human plotting is real and culpable. God’s risen Son is not undone by a budget line for false witness.

Matthew 28:13

13and said, “Tell people, ‘His disciples came by night and stole him away while we were asleep.’

Arminian

The official line is absurd by design: sleeping guards who somehow know thieves were disciples. Lie upon lie to blunt Easter.

Arminian pastoral: unbelief often prefers a story it can control. Grace invites honest facing of the empty tomb; this counsel invites people to swallow contradiction rather than trust Christ.

Reformed

Theft-while-asleep is the council’s catechism for the public. False witness is commissioned as policy.

Reformed diagnosis: suppressing resurrection truth is active sin. The alternative narrative exposes how far hard hearts will go.

Key contrast Both reject the theft tale as dishonest. Arminians typically press how resistible light is smothered by chosen falsehood; Reformed readers typically press the culpability of leaders under providence that still lets the true word advance.

Matthew 28:14

14And if this comes to the governor’s ears, we will satisfy him and keep you out of trouble.”

Arminian

Political cover is promised: we will satisfy the governor. Fear of Rome is managed so fear of God need not be faced.

Arminian edge: security without truth is a cruel kindness. Grace offers a better protection—the risen Lord—than elite patronage over a lie.

Reformed

Soldiers are shielded from Pilate if the story leaks. Earthly power underwrites the fiction.

Reformed note: the world’s peacekeeping can become partnership in deceit. God’s court will weigh both the bribe and the promise to “satisfy” men.

Matthew 28:15

15So they took the money and did as they were directed. And this story has been spread among the Jews to this day.

Arminian

Money taken; directions followed; rumor spreads “to this day.” Matthew refuses to pretend everyone believed. Competing stories endure.

Arminian urgency: persistence of a false account does not make it true. Grace still sets the empty tomb and the living Christ before every generation that hears both tales.

Reformed

Compliance is bought; the rumor has staying power. Scripture records the counter-gospel without fear.

Reformed confidence: God’s Word names the lie and outlasts it. The church confesses resurrection not because propaganda vanished, but because Christ lives.

Key contrast Both treat the lasting rumor as real history and false explanation. Arminians typically urge a fresh, responsible turn from propaganda to the risen Lord; Reformed readers typically stress God’s preservation of true witness amid enduring opposition He foreknew.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on while the women go with Easter news, some of the guard report to the chief priests—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 28:11–15.

Arminian Notable voices

“Say, his disciples came by night, and stole him while we slept - Is it possible, that any man of sense should digest this poor, shallow inconsistency? If ye were awake, why did you let the disciples steal him?”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 28:11–15)

Reformed Notable voices

“It is not only credible, but the fact is manifest, that the soldiers, to whom had been entrusted the charge of the sepulcher, were corrupted by a bribe, so that they were prepared to tell a lie at the bidding of the priests. They knew well that there was nothing which the priests dreaded more than that a report should gain credit that Christ rose on the third day after his death; and they knew that they had been sent there, that, by guarding the body, they might suppress that report.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 28:11–15)
