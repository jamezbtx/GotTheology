Passage

Matthew 13

Verses 44–46 · ESV · Voices

Jesus pairs two brief kingdom images: treasure hidden in a field and a merchant finding one pearl of great value—both ending in joyful, total exchange for what is found.

Matthew 13:44

44“The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field, which a man found and covered up. Then in his joy he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field.

Arminian

Hidden treasure, found and covered, then sold-all purchase in joy. The kingdom’s worth reorders every lesser claim.

Classical Arminians hear a sincere summons to count the cost and still choose Christ. Grace enables the joyful exchange; the response of selling all remains a real, glad act of trust.

Reformed

The kingdom is treasure worth everything. Joy fuels the sale of all lesser goods for the field that holds it.

Reformed accent: God awakens desire for the surpassing worth of His kingdom. The man’s joy and total exchange display a heart God has claimed.

Key contrast Both treat the kingdom as worth total surrender. Arminians typically emphasize grace-enabled, responsible joyful exchange; Reformed readers typically emphasize God’s effectual awakening of delight that makes selling all inevitable for those He draws.

Matthew 13:45

45“Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a merchant in search of fine pearls,

Arminian

A merchant searching for fine pearls pictures earnest seeking. The kingdom meets those who look for excellence and find more than they expected.

Arminian encouragement: seeking is not unbelief; grace often works through honest search until the true pearl appears.

Reformed

Search precedes finding. The merchant’s vocation becomes a kingdom parable under Christ’s word.

Reformed note: God may use human seeking as the path along which He discloses the one pearl that ends the hunt.

Matthew 13:46

46who, on finding one pearl of great value, went and sold all that he had and bought it.

Arminian

One pearl of great value ends the search: sell all, buy it. Singularity matters—many fine pearls, one supreme.

Arminian call: do not settle for lesser pearls. Assisting grace enables the decisive purchase of Christ and His kingdom above every rival.

Reformed

Finding leads to total divestment and purchase. The pearl’s value creates the economy of the exchange.

Reformed doxology: when God shows the pearl, lesser treasures lose their hold. Saving discovery is His gift that reorders the whole life.

Key contrast Both see exclusive worth in the one pearl. Arminians typically stress a grace-enabled decisive choice; Reformed readers typically stress God’s revelatory gift that effectually reorders what the heart will sell.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Jesus pairs two brief kingdom images: treasure hidden in a field and a merchant finding one pearl of great value—both—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 13:44–46.

Arminian Notable voices

“The three following parables are proposed, not to the multitude, but peculiarly to the apostles: the two former of them relate to those who receive the Gospel; the third, both to those who receive, and those who preach it. The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hid in a field - The kingdom of God within us is a treasure indeed, but a treasure hid from the world, and from the most wise and prudent in it.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 13:44–46)

Reformed Notable voices

“The first two of these parables are intended to instruct believers to prefer the Kingdom of heaven to the whole world, and therefore to deny themselves and all the desires of the flesh, that nothing may prevent them from obtaining so valuable a possession. We are greatly in need of such a warning; for we are so captivated by the allurements of the world, that eternal life fades from our view; and in consequence of our carnality, the spiritual graces of God are far from being held by us in the estimation which they deserve.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 13:44–46)
