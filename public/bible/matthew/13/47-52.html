Passage

Matthew 13

Verses 47–52 · ESV · Voices

Jesus adds the dragnet parable of final separation, then asks whether the disciples understand and compares the trained kingdom scribe to a householder bringing out new and old treasure.

Matthew 13:47

47“Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a net that was thrown into the sea and gathered fish of every kind.

Arminian

A net gathers fish of every kind. The kingdom’s catch is mixed in this age—broad gathering before sorting.

Arminian mission tone: cast the net widely. Grace invites many kinds into the hearing of the gospel while warning that gathering is not yet final approval.

Reformed

The net’s catch is indiscriminate in kind. Kingdom history includes a mixed haul until the shore.

Reformed framing: God’s kingdom purpose includes present gathering that awaits eschatological sorting.

Matthew 13:48

48When it was full, men drew it ashore and sat down and sorted the good into containers but threw away the bad.

Arminian

Fullness brings the shore, sitting, sorting—good kept, bad thrown away. Separation is careful and final for the catch.

Arminian gravity: do not confuse being in the net with being kept. Grace calls for living faith that proves “good” in the Master’s sort.

Reformed

Timing (“when it was full”) and sorting belong to the fishermen’s work at shore.

Reformed note: fullness and separation are appointed. God will complete the catch’s evaluation.

Matthew 13:49

49So it will be at the end of the age. The angels will come out and separate the evil from the righteous

Arminian

Jesus applies the net: end of the age, angels separate evil from righteous. Final judgment is personal and moral, not merely institutional.

Arminian warning and hope: repent and trust Christ now. Grace still distinguishes paths while the net is in the water.

Reformed

Angelic separation at the end of the age is certain. Evil and righteous will not remain mingled forever.

Reformed assurance: God’s agents execute His verdict. History bends toward that shoreline.

Key contrast Both affirm angelic final separation. Arminians typically press present grace-enabled repentance before the sort; Reformed readers typically stress the certainty of God’s decreed end-of-age separation.

Matthew 13:50

50and throw them into the fiery furnace. In that place there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

Arminian

Again the furnace, weeping, and gnashing. Jesus repeats hard eschatology so hearers cannot shrug.

Classical Arminians keep the invitation open and the warning sharp: flee to Christ while grace calls.

Reformed

The repeated furnace line seals the net parable to the weeds explanation. Judgment language is Jesus’ own.

Reformed confession: God’s justice will match the Son’s word—severe against unrepentant evil.

Matthew 13:51

51“Have you understood all these things?” They said to him, “Yes.”

Arminian

Jesus checks understanding; they answer “Yes.” Discipleship includes accountable comprehension, not endless fog.

Arminian pastoral care: claim understanding only if you will live it. Grace enables true yes; empty assent is rocky-soil danger.

Reformed

Christ examines His own: Have you understood? Their yes becomes the hinge for the next image.

Reformed weight: understanding is gift and stewardship. The Teacher who gave parables also quizzes the heart.

Matthew 13:52

52And he said to them, “Therefore every scribe who has been trained for the kingdom of heaven is like a master of a house, who brings out of his treasure what is new and what is old.”

Arminian

A kingdom-trained scribe is like a householder bringing new and old from treasure. Torah and Jesus’ kingdom word belong together in wise stewardship.

Arminian calling: be trained, then share generously. Grace forms teachers who refuse both novelty-idolatry and dead traditionalism.

Reformed

Training for the kingdom produces a householder of treasure—new and old brought out under Christ.

Reformed vision: Scripture’s fullness and the Messiah’s fulfillment are both wealth. God equips scribes for the church’s storehouse.

Key contrast Both honor trained kingdom teaching that holds new and old. Arminians typically emphasize grace-enabled responsible stewardship of the treasure; Reformed readers typically emphasize God’s training and provision of teachers for His house.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Jesus adds the dragnet parable of final separation—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 13:47–52.

Arminian Notable voices

“Every scribe instructed unto the kingdom of heaven - That is, every duly prepared preacher of the Gospel has a treasure of Divine knowledge, out of which he is able to bring forth all sorts of instructions. The word treasure signifies any collection of things whatsoever, and the places where such collections are kept.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 13:47–52)

Reformed Notable voices

“The first two of these parables are intended to instruct believers to prefer the Kingdom of heaven to the whole world, and therefore to deny themselves and all the desires of the flesh, that nothing may prevent them from obtaining so valuable a possession. We are greatly in need of such a warning; for we are so captivated by the allurements of the world, that eternal life fades from our view; and in consequence of our carnality, the spiritual graces of God are far from being held by us in the estimation which they deserve.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 13:47–52)
