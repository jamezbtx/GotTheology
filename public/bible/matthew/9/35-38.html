Sample passage

Matthew 9

Verses 35–38 · ESV

Jesus’ Galilean circuit—teaching, kingdom gospel, and healing—frames His compassion for harassed, shepherdless crowds. He turns disciples toward prayer: plentiful harvest, few laborers, ask the Lord of the harvest to send workers.

Matthew 9:35

35And Jesus went throughout all the cities and villages, teaching in their synagogues and proclaiming the gospel of the kingdom and healing every disease and every affliction.

Arminian

Comprehensive mission: teach, proclaim, heal—cities and villages alike. The gospel of the kingdom is good news announced, not mere advice.

Arminian pastoral reading: Jesus’ pattern is the church’s model under grace—word and mercy together. The offer goes widely; hearers are summoned to respond.

Reformed

The King’s itinerary is exhaustive in scope and authority. Synagogue, gospel, healing—one Messiah doing the Father’s works.

Reformed accent: “every” disease underlines sovereignty of power, not a promise that every illness in every age ends on demand, but that none could resist Him then.

Matthew 9:36

36When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd.

Arminian

Compassion is Jesus’ gut-level mercy for people chewed up and lying down—shepherdless sheep. Seeing precedes sending.

Arminian application: let His compassion shape yours. Lostness is real; the Shepherd still cares. You may come as harassed and find Him moved with mercy.

Reformed

Ezekiel’s shepherdless flock meets the true Shepherd’s pity. Compassion here is divine character in the flesh.

Reformed comfort: election’s Shepherd is not cold. His pity grounds mission; chapter 10’s sending flows from this sight.

Matthew 9:37

37Then he said to his disciples, “The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few;

Arminian

Harvest imagery: field ready, workers scarce. Urgency without despair—opportunity outruns staffing.

Arminian challenge: do not shrug at scarcity. The plentiful harvest implies a real call to labor; disciples are responsible to care and to pray.

Reformed

Kingdom agriculture under the Lord’s ownership. Plentiful harvest is God’s doing; few laborers is the church’s felt need.

Reformed reading: scarcity of workers does not threaten God’s election; it drives dependent prayer and obedient going.

Matthew 9:38

38therefore pray earnestly to the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers into his harvest.”

Arminian

First response is earnest prayer to the Lord of the harvest—He owns the field and sends workers. Prayer precedes programs.

Classical Arminians stress sincere petition that God would raise and send laborers, and that we remain willing to be among those sent. Shared ground: mission begins on our knees before the Lord who harvests.

Reformed

“Lord of the harvest” names divine ownership and authority to thrust out workers. Prayer is the appointed means.

Reformed accent: God sends; we ask. Chapter 10 will answer this prayer in the Twelve—sovereign sending that matches compassionate seeing.

Key contrast Both urge earnest prayer for laborers. Arminians typically emphasize willing participation in a sincerely offered mission; Reformed readers typically emphasize that the Lord of the harvest effectually sends those He appoints, in answer to the prayers He ordains.

About this sample. Commentary here is written for product demonstration. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
