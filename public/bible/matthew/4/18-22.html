Passage

Matthew 4

Verses 18–22 · ESV · Voices

Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus calls Simon Peter and Andrew from their nets, then James and John from their boat with Zebedee; all four leave immediately and follow Him.

Matthew 4:18

18While walking by the Sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon (who is called Peter) and Andrew his brother, casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen.

Arminian

Jesus walks, sees, and finds brothers at ordinary work—casting nets. Discipleship begins in a workplace, not a seminary lobby.

Arminian warmth: the call meets people where they are. Grace notices fishermen; response will soon be asked in freedom under that grace.

Reformed

The King is walking and seeing. Simon and Andrew are located by providence at the nets when the word comes.

Reformed framing: vocation becomes the stage for calling. “He saw” is not casual glance; it is the Shepherd identifying those He will summon.

Matthew 4:19

19And he said to them, “Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men.”

Arminian

“Follow me” is personal command; “I will make you” is promise. Jesus reshapes their trade into mission—people, not only fish.

Classical Arminians hear a real invitation with real enabling: follow, and He will form you. Shared ground: discipleship is Jesus’ making, not self-reinvention.

Reformed

Imperative and future meet: follow, and I will make. The Maker of fishers speaks first; transformation is His work.

Reformed accent: effectual calling is audible here. The same voice that commands also creates the new craft—missionaries formed by the King’s word.

Key contrast Both affirm Jesus’ initiative and the promise to remake them. Arminians typically stress a sincere, grace-enabled call they freely obey; Reformed readers typically stress an effectual call that infallibly produces followers and fishers.

Matthew 4:20

20Immediately they left their nets and followed him.

Arminian

Immediately—nets left, path taken. Delay would have been another sermon; obedience is the commentary.

Arminian pastoral note: responsive faith acts. Grace that calls also enables leaving; responsibility remains to walk after Him without postponing forever.

Reformed

Immediacy marks the power of the call. Nets that defined them are released in a moment.

Reformed comfort: when the Shepherd calls, sheep follow. Leaving is evidence of a word that works—not mere advice filed for later.

Matthew 4:21

21And going on from there he saw two other brothers, James the son of Zebedee and John his brother, in the boat with Zebedee their father, mending their nets, and he called them.

Arminian

Another pair, same pattern: He sees, He calls. James and John are mending nets with their father—family business interrupted by the King.

Arminian reading: the call is personal and costly. Grace does not erase fathers and boats; it reorders loves so Christ comes first.

Reformed

Going on, seeing, calling—Jesus’ initiative repeats. Zebedee is present; the summons still lands on the sons.

Reformed accent: the same sovereign voice that took Peter and Andrew now claims James and John. The church’s core is gathered by calling, not by committee.

Matthew 4:22

22Immediately they left the boat and their father and followed him.

Arminian

Boat and father left immediately. Loyalty to Jesus outranks livelihood and even family claims when they compete.

Classical Arminians preach costly, willing discipleship under grace. The leave-taking is real choice enabled by the call—shared with Reformed readers that following Jesus may rearrange every tie.

Reformed

Immediate departure from boat and father displays the call’s force. Priority shifts because the King has spoken.

Reformed pastoral note: discipleship can mean leaving good gifts for a greater Lord. The Father who calls also holds families; yet when His Son summons, obedience does not negotiate forever.

Key contrast Both honor costly, immediate following. Arminians often emphasize grace-enabled free response that leaves nets and father; Reformed readers often emphasize effectual calling that produces such leaving as its fruit.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus calls Simon Peter and Andrew from their nets—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 4:18–22.

Arminian Notable voices

“Simon called Peter, and Andrew his brother - Why did not Jesus Christ call some of the eminent Scribes or Pharisees to publish his Gospel, and not poor unlearned fishermen, without credit or authority? Because it was the kingdom of heaven they were to preach, and their teaching must come from above: besides, the conversion of sinners, though it be effected instrumentally by the preaching of the Gospel, yet the grand agent in it is the Spirit of God.”
Adam Clarke · Commentary on the New Testament (on Matt 4:18–22)

Reformed Notable voices

“As this history is placed by Luke after the two miracles, which we shall afterwards see, an opinion has commonly prevailed, that the miracle, which is here related by him, was performed some time after that they had been called by Christ. But the reason, which they allege, carries little weight: for no fixed and distinct order of dates was observed by the Evangelists in composing their narratives.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 4:18–22)
