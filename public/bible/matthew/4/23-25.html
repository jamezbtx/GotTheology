Passage

Matthew 4

Verses 23–25 · ESV · Voices

Jesus travels throughout Galilee teaching in synagogues, proclaiming the gospel of the kingdom, and healing every disease and affliction; fame spreads to Syria, the sick are brought, and great crowds follow from Galilee, the Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea, and beyond the Jordan.

Matthew 4:23

23And he went throughout all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues and proclaiming the gospel of the kingdom and healing every disease and every affliction among the people.

Arminian

A threefold ministry: teaching, proclaiming kingdom gospel, healing. Word and mercy travel together through all Galilee.

Arminian hope: the good news is announced widely in synagogues and towns. Grace that heals bodies also invites hearts—sincere proclamation paired with compassion.

Reformed

The Messiah’s program is comprehensive under providence: synagogue teaching, kingdom proclamation, and healing authority.

Reformed accent: “every disease and every affliction” signals royal power, not limited technique. The King who calls disciples now displays the kingdom’s arrival in word and deed.

Matthew 4:24

24So his fame spread throughout all Syria, and they brought him all the sick, those afflicted with various diseases and pains, those oppressed by demons, those having seizures, and paralytics, and he healed them.

Arminian

Fame crosses into Syria; crowds bring every kind of misery—disease, pain, demons, seizures, paralysis. Jesus heals them.

Classical Arminians see open-handed mercy: sufferers are brought, and He heals. The narrative invites trust—come with what oppresses you; the Physician is willing.

Reformed

Report runs ahead of Him; need streams toward Him. Categories of suffering pile up, and He heals them all in Matthew’s summary.

Reformed comfort: authority over body and demon is the Son’s. Fame is providence’s megaphone so that the afflicted may be carried to the only sufficient Healer.

Key contrast Both glory in Christ’s healing mercy. Arminians typically stress a wide, sincere compassion that welcomes all who are brought; Reformed readers typically stress sovereign power that actually heals as the kingdom advances on God’s schedule.

Matthew 4:25

25And great crowds followed him from Galilee and the Decapolis, and from Jerusalem and Judea, and from beyond the Jordan.

Arminian

Great crowds from north, east, south, and across the Jordan—Jew and Gentile regions alike. The light in Galilee draws a wide following.

Arminian mission tone: the invitation’s reach matches Isaiah’s Gentile Galilee. Many follow; each still must move from crowd to disciple under grace.

Reformed

Geography of followers maps the promise: Galilee, Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea, beyond the Jordan. The King’s draw is already regional.

Reformed reading: crowds are not the final measure, yet they signal that God is gathering. The same Lord who called four from nets now pulls multitudes—preparing the Sermon on the Mount and the sharper calls still to come.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Jesus travels throughout Galilee teaching in synagogues, proclaiming the gospel of the kingdom—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 4:23–25.

Arminian Notable voices

“The Gospel of the kingdom - The Gospel, that is, the joyous message, is the proper name of our religion: as will be amply verified in all who earnestly and perseveringly embrace it. Through all Syria - The whole province, of which the Jewish country was only a small part.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 4:23–25)

Reformed Notable voices

“As this history is placed by Luke after the two miracles, which we shall afterwards see, an opinion has commonly prevailed, that the miracle, which is here related by him, was performed some time after that they had been called by Christ. But the reason, which they allege, carries little weight: for no fixed and distinct order of dates was observed by the Evangelists in composing their narratives.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 4:23–25)
