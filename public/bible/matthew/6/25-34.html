Passage

Matthew 6

Verses 25–34 · WEB · Voices

Having unmasked mammon, Jesus addresses anxiety about food, drink, and clothing: the Father feeds birds and clothes lilies; disciples of little faith are worth more; seek first the kingdom and His righteousness, and leave tomorrow’s trouble to tomorrow.

Matthew 6:25

25Therefore I tell you, don’t be anxious for your life: what you will eat, or what you will drink; nor yet for your body, what you will wear. Isn’t life more than food, and the body more than clothing?

Arminian

“Therefore” links anxiety to the prior warning about treasure and masters. Worry about necessities is forbidden not because needs are unreal, but because life is larger than provisions and God is Father. Shared comfort: anxiety is not the disciple’s assigned posture.

Arminian readers find a sincere call to trust—grace-enabled release of grasping fear. Responsibility remains: refuse to let need become a rival god.

Reformed

Jesus commands against anxiety about life’s basics and reframes value: life and body outrank food and clothes. The “therefore” follows from exclusive service of God.

The Father’s providence underwrites the command. Worry shrinks when the heart remembers who rules and provides.

Matthew 6:26

26See the birds of the sky, that they don’t sow, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns. Your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren’t you of much more value than they?

Arminian

Birds neither farm nor warehouse, yet the heavenly Father feeds them. The argument is from lesser to greater: you are worth more. Shared gospel ground—creation care reveals fatherly kindness.

The Father’s sincere care invites trust. Grace enables disciples to look and learn, resting in a God who values them and calls them to stop anxious self-sufficiency.

Reformed

Providence is concrete: the Father feeds birds. Disciple-value exceeds theirs—an a fortiori of care for His own.

Reformed assurance leans hard here: God’s fatherly provision is certain for those He claims. Anxiety disputes the character of the One who feeds the air’s birds.

Matthew 6:27

27“Which of you by being anxious, can add one moment to his lifespan?

Arminian

Anxiety is powerless—it cannot lengthen life by an hour. Worry wastes the present without securing the future. Shared realism: fear is not control.

Grace frees us to admit limits and entrust lifespan to God—trust instead of the illusion that fretting manages outcomes.

Reformed

Jesus mocks anxiety’s pretension. No hour is added by worry. Lifespan rests in God’s hand, not in our nervous calculations.

Sovereignty here is comfort. The God who numbers our days is trusted, not negotiated with by anxiety.

Matthew 6:28

28Why are you anxious about clothing? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow. They don’t toil, neither do they spin,

Arminian

Lilies neither do they spin, yet they grow under God’s care. Jesus invites contemplative trust—look at the field and learn. Shared invitation: creation preaches against panic over clothes.

Wesleyan Arminians stress responsive faith: consider, believe, and cease anxious striving that treats God as unreliable.

Reformed

“Consider” is a command to study providence in the ordinary beauty of wildflowers. Growth without toil or spinning displays God’s dressing of the field.

The same Lord who clothes grass is the disciples’ Father—His care is not abstract.

Matthew 6:29

29yet I tell you that even Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these.

Arminian

Solomon’s glory loses to a lily. Royal wardrobe cannot match God’s artistry in a flower. Shared wonder: God’s generosity outshines human splendor.

Such beauty is meant to draw trust—grace opening eyes so that anxiety about covering the body yields to confidence in the Creator’s kindness.

Reformed

Jesus ranks wild lilies above Solomon’s court dress. Providence is not bare minimum; it is lavish in the small.

The King of glory arrays grass better than Israel’s greatest king arrayed himself—how much more will He care for His people.

Matthew 6:30

30But if God so clothes the grass of the field, which today exists and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, won’t he much more clothe you, you of little faith?

Arminian

Grass is brief fuel for the oven—yet God clothes it. “Much more” clothes you. “Little faith” is a gentle rebuke, not rejection. Shared pastoral tone: anxiety is faith too small for the Father you have.

The rebuke invites growth—grace-enabled trust that can enlarge. Disciples are called to answer with fuller reliance, not despair.

Reformed

A fortiori again: transient grass clothed implies greater care for disciples. “Little faith” diagnoses the root of worry.

The promise is “much more” for His own. Faith rests on the Father’s comparative certainty, not on the disciple’s strong nerves.

Matthew 6:31

31“Therefore don’t be anxious, saying, ‘What will we eat?’, ‘What will we drink?’ or, ‘With what will we be clothed?’

Arminian

The refrain returns: do not be anxious, rehearsing tomorrow’s menu and wardrobe as if orphaned. Shared command: stop the anxious liturgy of need.

Arminians hear responsibility joined to promise—grace enables a different speech: petition and trust instead of panic’s questions.

Reformed

Jesus forbids the anxious script about eat, drink, and wear. The disciple’s mouth is to be retrained by the Father’s care.

Providence silences pagan-style fretting. We ask because He knows; we do not spiral as those without a Father.

Matthew 6:32

32For the Gentiles seek after all these things; for your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things.

Arminian

Gentile seeking after necessities marks life without the Father’s known care. By contrast, “your heavenly Father knows that you need all.” Shared comfort: omniscient fatherliness meets need.

Arminian reading stresses sincere fatherly knowledge that invites confident asking and rest—grace-taught children need not chase as orphans do.

Reformed

Two theologies of provision: pagan scramble versus Father-knowledge. God already knows every need of His people.

The Father’s certain knowledge of His own underwrites freedom from anxiety. Need is not illusory; orphanhood is not.

Matthew 6:33

33But seek first God’s Kingdom and his righteousness; and all these things will be given to you as well.

Arminian

Priority is reset: seek first God’s kingdom and righteousness; necessities are added. The call is sincere and active—pursue the King’s reign and right living. Shared gospel: God and His ways before the shopping list.

Classical Arminian accent: grace enables this seeking as obedient trust. Believers are responsible to reorder aims; the promise of added things encourages persevering faith, not laziness.

Reformed

Kingdom-first seeking and His righteousness redefine the disciple’s pursuit. Provision is promised as addition, not as the main hunt.

Such seeking is Spirit-wrought reorientation—new priority flowing from a claimed heart. The Father who commands also adds what His people need.

Key contrast Both urge seeking first the kingdom and righteousness, with necessities added by God. Arminians commonly stress a sincere, grace-enabled call and free responsibility to prioritize God; Reformed readers commonly stress Spirit-wrought reorientation and the Father’s sure provision for His own.

Matthew 6:34

34Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.

Arminian

Final “therefore”: refuse tomorrow’s anxiety today. Tomorrow carries its own worry; today has trouble enough. Shared realism and mercy—Jesus names trouble without baptizing panic.

Grace enables present-tense trust and obedience. Meet today’s duty and need with the Father; do not borrow tomorrow’s fear as if He will be absent then.

Reformed

Jesus limits the horizon of concern. Tomorrow’s anxiety is not today’s assignment; each day has sufficient trouble under God’s care.

Providence is daily. The Father who rules tomorrow frees His people to live faithfully in today’s allotted trouble without anxious projection.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Older voices from both traditions on Matthew 6:25–34.

Arminian Notable voices

“And if you serve God, you need be careful for nothing. Therefore take no thought - That is, be not anxiously careful. Beware of worldly cares; for these are as inconsistent with the true service of God as worldly desires. - And if God give the greater gift, will he deny the smaller?”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 6:25–34)

Reformed Notable voices

“He argues from the greater to the less. He had forbidden them to be excessively anxious about the way in which life might be supported; and he now assigns the reason. The Lord, who has given life itself, will not suffer us to want what is necessary for its support.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 6:25–34)
