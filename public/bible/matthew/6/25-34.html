Passage

Matthew 6

Verses 25–34 · ESV · Voices

Having unmasked mammon, Jesus addresses anxiety about food, drink, and clothing: the Father feeds birds and clothes lilies; disciples of little faith are worth more; seek first the kingdom and His righteousness, and leave tomorrow’s trouble to tomorrow.

Matthew 6:25

25“Therefore I tell you, do not be anxious about your life, what you will eat or what you will drink, nor about your body, what you will put on. Is not life more than food, and the body more than clothing?

Arminian

“Therefore” links anxiety to the prior warning about treasure and masters. Worry about necessities is forbidden not because needs are unreal, but because life is larger than provisions and God is Father. Shared comfort: anxiety is not the disciple’s assigned posture.

Classical Arminians hear a sincere call to trust—grace-enabled release of grasping fear. Responsibility remains: refuse to let need become a rival god.

Reformed

Jesus commands against anxiety about life’s basics and reframes value: life and body outrank food and clothes. The “therefore” follows from exclusive service of God.

Reformed pastoral note: the Father’s providence underwrites the command. Worry shrinks when the heart remembers who rules and provides.

Matthew 6:26

26Look at the birds of the air: they neither sow nor reap nor gather into barns, and yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not of more value than they?

Arminian

Birds neither farm nor warehouse, yet the heavenly Father feeds them. The argument is from lesser to greater: you are worth more. Shared gospel ground—creation care reveals fatherly kindness.

Arminian emphasis: the Father’s sincere care invites trust. Grace enables disciples to look and learn, resting in a God who values them and calls them to stop anxious self-sufficiency.

Reformed

Providence is concrete: the Father feeds birds. Disciple-value exceeds theirs—an a fortiori of care for His own.

Reformed assurance leans hard here: God’s fatherly provision is certain for those He claims. Anxiety disputes the character of the One who feeds the air’s birds.

Matthew 6:27

27And which of you by being anxious can add a single hour to his span of life?

Arminian

Anxiety is powerless—it cannot lengthen life by an hour. Worry wastes the present without securing the future. Shared realism: fear is not control.

Arminian pastoral word: grace frees us to admit limits and entrust lifespan to God—real trust instead of the illusion that fretting manages outcomes.

Reformed

Jesus mocks anxiety’s pretension. No hour is added by worry. Lifespan rests in God’s hand, not in our nervous calculations.

Reformed accent: sovereignty here is comfort. The God who numbers our days is trusted, not negotiated with by anxiety.

Matthew 6:28

28And why are you anxious about clothing? Consider the lilies of the field, how they grow: they neither toil nor spin,

Arminian

Lilies neither toil nor spin, yet they grow under God’s care. Jesus invites contemplative trust—look at the field and learn. Shared invitation: creation preaches against panic over clothes.

Classical Arminians stress responsive faith: consider, believe, and cease anxious striving that treats God as unreliable.

Reformed

“Consider” is a command to study providence in the ordinary beauty of wildflowers. Growth without toil or spinning displays God’s dressing of the field.

Reformed comfort: the same Lord who clothes grass is the disciples’ Father—His care is not abstract.

Matthew 6:29

29yet I tell you, even Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these.

Arminian

Solomon’s glory loses to a lily. Royal wardrobe cannot match God’s artistry in a flower. Shared wonder: God’s generosity outshines human splendor.

Arminian note: such beauty is meant to draw trust—grace opening eyes so that anxiety about covering the body yields to confidence in the Creator’s kindness.

Reformed

Jesus ranks wild lilies above Solomon’s court dress. Providence is not bare minimum; it is lavish in the small.

Reformed praise: the King of glory arrays grass better than Israel’s greatest king arrayed himself—how much more will He care for His people.

Matthew 6:30

30But if God so clothes the grass of the field, which today is alive and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, will he not much more clothe you, O you of little faith?

Arminian

Grass is brief fuel for the oven—yet God clothes it. “Much more” clothes you. “Little faith” is a gentle rebuke, not rejection. Shared pastoral tone: anxiety is faith too small for the Father you have.

Arminian accent: the rebuke invites growth—grace-enabled trust that can enlarge. Disciples are called to answer with fuller reliance, not despair.

Reformed

A fortiori again: transient grass clothed implies greater care for disciples. “Little faith” diagnoses the root of worry.

Reformed assurance: the promise is “much more” for His own. Faith rests on the Father’s comparative certainty, not on the disciple’s strong nerves.

Matthew 6:31

31Therefore do not be anxious, saying, ‘What shall we eat?’ or ‘What shall we drink?’ or ‘What shall we wear?’

Arminian

The refrain returns: do not be anxious, rehearsing tomorrow’s menu and wardrobe as if orphaned. Shared command: stop the anxious liturgy of need.

Classical Arminians hear responsibility joined to promise—grace enables a different speech: petition and trust instead of panic’s questions.

Reformed

Jesus forbids the anxious script about eat, drink, and wear. The disciple’s mouth is to be retrained by the Father’s care.

Reformed note: providence silences pagan-style fretting. We ask because He knows; we do not spiral as those without a Father.

Matthew 6:32

32For the Gentiles seek after all these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them all.

Arminian

Gentile seeking after necessities marks life without the Father’s known care. By contrast, “your heavenly Father knows that you need them all.” Shared comfort: omniscient fatherliness meets real need.

Arminian reading stresses sincere fatherly knowledge that invites confident asking and rest—grace-taught children need not chase as orphans do.

Reformed

Two theologies of provision: pagan scramble versus Father-knowledge. God already knows every need of His people.

Reformed assurance: the Father’s certain knowledge of His own underwrites freedom from anxiety. Need is real; orphanhood is not.

Matthew 6:33

33But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.

Arminian

Priority is reset: seek first God’s kingdom and righteousness; necessities are “added.” The call is sincere and active—pursue the King’s reign and right living. Shared gospel: God and His ways before the shopping list.

Classical Arminian accent: grace enables this seeking as real obedient trust. Believers are responsible to reorder aims; the promise of added things encourages persevering faith, not laziness.

Reformed

Kingdom-first seeking and His righteousness redefine the disciple’s pursuit. Provision is promised as addition, not as the main hunt.

Reformed accent: such seeking is Spirit-wrought reorientation—new priority flowing from a claimed heart. The Father who commands also adds what His people need.

Key contrast Both urge seeking first the kingdom and righteousness, with necessities added by God. Arminians commonly stress a sincere, grace-enabled call and real responsibility to prioritize God; Reformed readers commonly stress Spirit-wrought reorientation and the Father’s sure provision for His own.

Matthew 6:34

34“Therefore do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble.

Arminian

Final “therefore”: refuse tomorrow’s anxiety today. Tomorrow carries its own worry; today has trouble enough. Shared realism and mercy—Jesus names trouble without baptizing panic.

Arminian pastoral close: grace enables present-tense trust and obedience. Meet today’s duty and need with the Father; do not borrow tomorrow’s fear as if He will be absent then.

Reformed

Jesus limits the horizon of concern. Tomorrow’s anxiety is not today’s assignment; each day has sufficient trouble under God’s care.

Reformed comfort: providence is daily. The Father who rules tomorrow frees His people to live faithfully in today’s allotted trouble without anxious projection.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Jesus addresses anxiety about food, drink—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 6:25–34.

Arminian Notable voices

“And if you serve God, you need be careful for nothing. Therefore take no thought - That is, be not anxiously careful. Beware of worldly cares; for these are as inconsistent with the true service of God as worldly desires. - And if God give the greater gift, will he deny the smaller?”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 6:25–34)

Reformed Notable voices

“He argues from the greater to the less. He had forbidden them to be excessively anxious about the way in which life might be supported; and he now assigns the reason. The Lord, who has given life itself, will not suffer us to want what is necessary for its support.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 6:25–34)
