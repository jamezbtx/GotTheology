Passage

Matthew 2

Verses 13–15 · ESV · Voices

After the Magi leave, an angel warns Joseph in a dream to flee to Egypt with the child and Mary; Joseph obeys by night, and Matthew hears in their sojourn the fulfillment of “Out of Egypt I called my son.”

Matthew 2:13

13Now when they had departed, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream and said, “Rise, take the child and his mother, and flee to Egypt, and remain there until I tell you, for Herod is about to search for the child, to destroy him.”

Arminian

God does not abandon the holy family to Herod’s rage. Angelic warning names the threat and gives a clear path: rise, take, flee, remain until further word.

Classical Arminians see assisting grace in narrative form—God enlightens and enables Joseph’s obedience without erasing the cost or the choice to trust and move.

Reformed

The same Lord who announced the birth now preserves the child. Revelation is specific, timely, and sufficient for obedience.

Reformed comfort: Christ’s mission will not die in a crib. God’s decree includes the means—warning, flight, and a servant who rises when spoken to.

Matthew 2:14

14And he rose and took the child and his mother by night and departed to Egypt

Arminian

Joseph’s obedience is prompt and costly—night travel, exile, upheaval for the sake of the child. Faith acts on God’s word without delay.

Arminian preaching treasures this as real human faithfulness under grace: Joseph could hesitate; instead he rises. Families under threat still find a pattern here—trust and go.

Reformed

Night departure shows urgency and the fruit of God’s intervening word. Joseph’s rising is the obedience God worked through the dream.

Reformed pastoral use: God not only plans the Son’s safety; He works willingness in the guardian He appointed.

Matthew 2:15

15and remained there until the death of Herod. This was to fulfill what the Lord had spoken by the prophet, “Out of Egypt I called my son.”

Arminian

They stay until God says otherwise; then Matthew reads Hosea’s line over Jesus’ story. Israel’s exodus pattern finds its climax in the true Son.

Arminians and Reformed alike confess fulfillment here. The pastoral invitation is trust: the God who called Israel and sheltered Jesus still keeps His people through dark chapters.

Reformed

Hosea 11:1, spoken of Israel, is fulfilled typologically in Christ—the true Son recapitulating the people’s story in purity and purpose.

Reformed emphasis: Scripture’s deep unity is God-authored. Exile in Egypt is not detour alone; it is ordained fulfillment on the way to redemption.

Key contrast Both receive Matthew’s Hosea fulfillment as Christ-centered. Arminians often highlight God’s faithful care inviting trust and obedient waiting; Reformed theology often highlights typological providence—the Son’s path appointed to fulfill Scripture with certainty.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on an angel warns Joseph in a dream to flee to Egypt with the child and Mary; Joseph obeys by night—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 2:13–15.

Arminian Notable voices

“That it might be fulfilled - That is, whereby was fulfilled. The original word frequently signifies, not the design of an action, but barely the consequence or event of it. Which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet - on another occasion: Out of Egypt have I called my Son - which was now fulfilled as it were anew; Christ being in a far higher sense the Son of God than Israel, of whom the words were originally spoken.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 2:13–15)

Reformed Notable voices

“And when they had departed How many days elapsed from the departure of the Magi, till Joseph was ordered to flee into Egypt, is not known, nor is it of much importance to inquire: only it is probable that the Lord spared Mary, till she was so far recovered from childbirth as to be able to perform the journey. It was a wonderful purpose of God, that he chose to preserve his Son by flight.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 2:13–15)
