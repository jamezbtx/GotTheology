Passage

Matthew 2

Verses 19–23 · WEB · Voices

After Herod’s death, an angel tells Joseph to return to Israel; fearing Archelaus, Joseph is warned again and settles in Nazareth, fulfilling the word that the Messiah will be called a Nazarene.

Matthew 2:19

19But when Herod was dead, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt, saying,

Arminian

Tyrants die; God’s purpose continues. The angel returns to Joseph in Egypt with the next step of guidance.

Seasons of waiting end at God’s timing. Grace that sent them out now calls them onward—keep listening.

Reformed

Herod’s death is a hinge God had named in advance (“until the death of Herod”). The dream arrives on schedule with the Lord’s chronology.

Providence is patient and precise: the God who hid the Son now opens the way home.

Matthew 2:20

20“Arise and take the young child and his mother, and go into the land of Israel, for those who sought the young child’s life are dead.”

Arminian

The command mirrors the flight order—rise, take, go—now toward home. Danger’s agents are dead; obedience still required.

Faith responds to fresh mercies with fresh movement. God does not only rescue; He restores path and place.

Reformed

Echoes of Exodus language (“those who sought the young child’s life are dead”) cast Jesus as the true Son brought toward the land.

God’s verdict on the hunters stands. The child’s life was never finally in Herod’s hand.

Matthew 2:21

21He arose and took the young child and his mother, and came into the land of Israel.

Arminian

Again Joseph obeys promptly. The pattern of chapter 1–2 is consistent: revelation, then rising.

Pastoral takeaway across traditions: delayed obedience would leave the family under the wrong sky. When God says go, go.

Reformed

Joseph’s rising is the appointed means of return. God works through a willing servant to place His Son where prophecy and providence require.

Obedience here is fruit of prior grace, not a ladder to earn care.

Matthew 2:22

22But when he heard that Archelaus was reigning over Judea in the place of his father, Herod, he was afraid to go there. Being warned in a dream, he withdrew into the region of Galilee,

Arminian

Joseph’s fear is intelligible—Archelaus was brutal—and God meets it with another warning. Prudence and revelation agree in steering the family north.

Arminians often note human deliberation under grace: fear is deeply felt, guidance is true, and the path adjusts without shame.

Reformed

God accounts for political danger and redirects Joseph by dream. Even fear becomes an occasion for further leading.

The Messiah’s geography is guided at every fork. Galilee is not Plan B in God’s mind; it is the next appointed step.

Key contrast Both affirm fear and divine guidance. Arminians typically describe warning grace cooperating with Joseph’s responsible caution; Calvin’s heirs generally describe God’s effectual redirection securing the Son’s path into Galilee.

Matthew 2:23

23and came and lived in a city called Nazareth; that it might be fulfilled which was spoken through the prophets that he will be called a Nazarene.

Arminian

Nazareth—obscure, scorned—becomes home. Matthew hears prophetic fulfillment in the Messiah’s lowly label. Glory hides in an unlikely town.

Classical Arminian warmth: God draws near in humility and still invites the world to this Jesus of Nazareth. No pedigree of place is required to come and worship Him.

Reformed

Spoken by the prophets (plural) points to a thematic fulfillment—the Branch/Netzer hope and the pattern of a despised servant—gathered into the name “Nazarene.”

Every move from Bethlehem to Egypt to Nazareth fulfills Scripture. The King God sent is precisely the Nazarene He intended.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What older interpreters in each camp said about an angel tells Joseph to return to Israel; fearing Archelaus, Joseph is warned again and settles in Nazareth.

Arminian Notable voices

“He was afraid to go thither - into Judea; and so turned aside into the region of Galilee - a part of the land of Israel not under the jurisdiction of Archelaus. He came and dwelt in Nazareth - (where he had dwelt before he went to Bethlehem) a place contemptible to a proverb.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 2:19–23)

Reformed Notable voices

“But when Herod was dead These words show the perseverance of Joseph's faith. He kept his feet firm in Egypt, till he was recalled to his native country by a command of God. We see, at the same time, that the Lord never disappoints his own people, but renders them seasonable aid.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 2:19–23)
