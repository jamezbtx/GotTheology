Passage

Matthew 7

Verses 21–23 · ESV

Jesus presses past religious speech and even impressive ministry claims to the heart of kingdom entry: doing the Father’s will—and the dread verdict for workers of lawlessness He never knew.

Matthew 7:21

21“Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of my Father who is in heaven.

Arminian

Orthodox address—“Lord, Lord”—is not enough. Entry belongs to those who do the Father’s will. Jesus claims to decide kingdom entrance and ties it to obedient discipleship, not empty confession.

Classical Arminians hear a sincere call to living faith that obeys. Grace enables doing the Father’s will; lip-service without obedience is a real, damning refusal. Shared ground: Jesus is Lord, and lordship means obedience.

Reformed

Saying “Lord” without doing the Father’s will is empty religion. Jesus Himself is the gatekeeper of the kingdom; confession must be joined to obedience that grace produces.

Reformed accent: true faith works. Doing the Father’s will is not a second ladder beside Christ; it is the fruit of knowing Him—yet Jesus will not flatter fruitless orthodoxy.

Matthew 7:22

22On that day many will say to me, ‘Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and do many mighty works in your name?’

Arminian

“That day” is judgment day. Many will plead ministry credentials—prophecy, exorcism, miracles—all “in your name.” Impressive activity can coexist with a heart Jesus does not own.

Arminian warning: do not trust gifts or results as proof of saving relationship. Grace calls for obedient trust, not résumé religion. Shared confession: ministry success is not the same as being known by Christ.

Reformed

Eschatological surprise: many claim Jesus’ name over powerful works. Charisma is not regeneration. The plea assumes that spectacular service settles the case; Jesus will show it does not.

Reformed sobriety: gifts can be present where saving grace is not. Providence may allow mighty works; only new birth and persevering faith mark those Christ knows.

Matthew 7:23

23And then will I declare to them, ‘I never knew you; depart from me, you workers of lawlessness.’

Arminian

The verdict is relational and moral: “I never knew you,” and “workers of lawlessness.” Jesus speaks as Judge. Lawless practice under religious cover is exposed.

Arminian pastoral gravity: knowing Christ in living faith matters more than naming Him in ministry. The warning invites self-examination and renewed obedience while mercy still calls. Shared ground: departure from Christ is the ultimate loss.

Reformed

“I never knew you” is devastating covenant language—no saving relationship, despite the name on the résumé. Lawlessness names the true allegiance of their works.

Reformed assurance for the humble: those Christ knows, He keeps. The terror of this verse is for false profession; the comfort is that being known by Jesus, not our mighty works, is the ground of hope.

Key contrast Both insist that empty “Lord, Lord” and even mighty works cannot replace doing the Father’s will in a living relationship with Jesus. Arminians commonly stress grace-enabled obedient faith against résumé religion; Reformed readers commonly stress that being known by Christ—and the fruit that follows—is the decisive reality His judgment reveals.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
