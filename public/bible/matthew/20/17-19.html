Passage

Matthew 20

Verses 17–19 · ESV · Voices

On the road up to Jerusalem, Jesus takes the Twelve aside for a third, sharper passion prediction: delivery to Jewish leaders, Gentile mockery and crucifixion, and resurrection on the third day—setting the cross before the disciples’ ambitions that follow.

Matthew 20:17

17And as Jesus was going up to Jerusalem, he took the twelve disciples aside, and on the way he said to them,

Arminian

The ascent to Jerusalem is deliberate. Jesus pulls the Twelve aside—private instruction before public crisis—so they may hear the path He chooses.

Arminian pastoral reading: grace still teaches even when hearts are slow. He does not leave disciples guessing; He speaks plainly and invites them to trust what they cannot yet prefer.

Reformed

Matthew marks another stage on the appointed road. Taking the Twelve aside shows the Lord’s initiative: He defines the journey before ambition speaks.

Reformed framing: providence is tightening toward the cross. The Teacher who leads them up also rules the “must” of suffering that He is about to name.

Matthew 20:18

18“See, we are going up to Jerusalem. And the Son of Man will be delivered over to the chief priests and scribes, and they will condemn him to death

Arminian

“See” calls for attention: Jerusalem is not a sightseeing stop but the place of delivery and condemnation. The Son of Man will be handed to priestly and scribal authority that will sentence Him.

Classical Arminians hear a sincere disclosure meant to prepare faith. Grace unveils the cost of redemption; disciples remain responsible to receive the hard word rather than edit it.

Reformed

Jesus speaks with certainty: delivery and condemnation are ahead, not hypotheticals. The Son of Man title meets judicial murder in the holy city.

Reformed confession: this is the path God has written. Human courts will act wickedly, yet their verdict sits inside the Father’s saving purpose for the crucified Messiah.

Matthew 20:19

19and deliver him over to the Gentiles to be mocked and flogged and crucified, and he will be raised on the third day.”

Arminian

The prediction widens: Gentile mockery, flogging, crucifixion—then the third-day rising. Shame and victory are told together so hope is not stripped from the warning.

Arminian gospel: the cross is sufficient and sincerely proclaimed; the resurrection invites trust. Grace that shows the cup also shows the empty tomb—calling hearers to follow this Lord.

Reformed

Detail piles on: mocked, flogged, crucified—then raised. Death to the Gentiles and life on the third day are equally sure in Jesus’ mouth.

Reformed comfort: atonement and vindication are decree-shaped. The Son will drink the cup fully, and the Father will raise Him; the church’s hope rests on both halves of the word.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on on the road up to Jerusalem, Jesus takes the Twelve aside for a third—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 20:17–19.

Arminian Notable voices

“And Jesus going up - From Jericho to Jerusalem, See Matthew 19:15. The Son of man shall be betrayed - Or, will be delivered up. This is the third time that our Lord informed his disciples of his approaching sufferings and death. This was a subject of the utmost importance, and it was necessary they should be well prepared for such an awful event.”
Adam Clarke · Commentary on the New Testament (on Matt 20:17–19)

Reformed Notable voices

“Took the twelve disciples apart in the way It may appear surprising that he makes the twelve alone acquainted with his secret, since all have need of consolation, for all had been alike seized with fear. I consider the reason why he did not publish his death to have been, that the report might not spread too widely before the time.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 20:17–19)
