Passage

Matthew 26

Verses 57–68 · ESV

Jesus is led to Caiaphas; the scribes and elders gather. Peter follows at a distance into the courtyard. The council seeks false testimony, finally charges Jesus under the temple saying and His confession as the Christ, the Son of God. The high priest declares blasphemy; they condemn Him as deserving death and begin to mock and strike Him.

Matthew 26:57

57Then those who had seized Jesus led him to Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and the elders had gathered.

Arminian

Seized and led to Caiaphas: formal religion hosts the miscarriage of justice. Scribes and elders are already gathered.

Arminian warning: office without fear of God can still assemble against the truth. Light resisted becomes tribunal.

Reformed

To Caiaphas’s house the story moves. Leadership that should know Scripture gathers for verdict.

Reformed frame: human court in session under a higher court. Their gathering serves ends they do not mean.

Matthew 26:58

58And Peter was following him at a distance, as far as the courtyard of the high priest, and going inside he sat with the guards to see the end.

Arminian

Peter follows—at a distance—then sits with guards to see the end. Loyalty mixed with self-preservation.

Arminian realism: responsible disciples can edge near danger without full confession. Distance foreshadows denial.

Reformed

Distance discipleship enters the courtyard. Peter positions himself to watch, not yet to witness.

Reformed pastoral: remaining near enough to fall is still under the Lord’s eye who foretold the denials.

Matthew 26:59

59Now the chief priests and the whole council were seeking false testimony against Jesus that they might put him to death,

Arminian

They seek false testimony to kill. Verdict first, evidence second. Injustice is intentional.

Arminian moral clarity: free agents invent lies against the Truth. Grace does not make God the liar; they choose falsehood.

Reformed

The council’s aim is death; false witness is the tool. Matthew strips their piety bare.

Reformed holding: wicked seeking and divine purpose can coincide without God authoring the lie.

Matthew 26:60

60but they found none, though many false witnesses came forward. At last two came forward

Arminian

Many liars, no usable case—until two come forward. Even corrupted process struggles to pin the Holy One.

Arminian note: truth has a stubborn edge. Falsehood multiplies and still needs a final pair to force the charge.

Reformed

Failure of false witness, then a last pair. Providence frustrates easy lies before the decisive accusation.

Reformed patience of narrative: the story will turn on Jesus’ own confession more than on their fabrications.

Matthew 26:61

61and said, “This man said, ‘I am able to destroy the temple of God, and to rebuild it in three days.’”

Arminian

Twisted temple saying becomes the charge. They distort Jesus’ words about the temple of His body.

Arminian caution: mishandling Scripture and Jesus’ words is accountable. Half-quotes can serve murderous ends.

Reformed

Misrepresented speech about the temple. Irony thickens: the true temple stands on trial.

Reformed irony: what they mock as threat is the sign of death and resurrection they cannot see.

Matthew 26:62

62And the high priest stood up and said, “Have you no answer to make? What is it that these men testify against you?”

Arminian

Caiaphas presses for an answer. Silence will soon speak louder than defense lawyers.

Arminian observation: Jesus is not obligated to validate a rigged court; His later confession will be voluntary truth-telling.

Reformed

The high priest demands reply. The court performs legality over predetermined death.

Reformed note: questioning continues until the Christological climax they will call blasphemy.

Matthew 26:63

63But Jesus remained silent. And the high priest said to him, “I adjure you by the living God, tell us if you are the Christ, the Son of God.”

Arminian

Silence, then adjuration by the living God: Are you the Christ, the Son of God? The question is the right one asked in the wrong spirit.

Classical Arminians hear a moment where truth is demanded. Jesus will answer sincerely; hearers remain responsible for what they do with the confession.

Reformed

Silent Lamb, then oath-bound question naming Christ and Son of God. Heaven’s identity is put under earthly curse-formula.

Reformed weight: the confession they extract is the truth that condemns their unbelief and saves those who believe.

Matthew 26:64

64Jesus said to him, “You have said so. But I tell you, from now on you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven.”

Arminian

“You have said so,” plus Daniel/Psalm glory: Son of Man at Power’s right hand, coming on clouds. Jesus answers under oath with messianic claim and future vindication.

Arminian gospel: the confession is clear enough for faith or rejection. Grace enables receiving this Christ; the court will choose rage instead.

Reformed

Assent plus exaltation. Jesus claims the throne and the coming. The judged One announces the Judge.

Reformed confession: this is the Christological center of the trial. His word is true whether they believe it or not; many will yet see as He said.

Key contrast Both receive Jesus’ claim as true and decisive. Arminians typically stress a clear confession that sincerely summons faith or leaves rejecters without excuse; Reformed readers typically stress the Son of Man’s sure exaltation and coming as God’s appointed vindication.

Matthew 26:65

65Then the high priest tore his robes and said, “He has uttered blasphemy. What further witnesses do we need? You have now heard his blasphemy.

Arminian

Robes torn, blasphemy declared, witnesses dismissed. They treat truth as blasphemy because they will not worship Him.

Arminian tragedy: light called darkness. Responsible leaders close the case against the Son of God.

Reformed

Theatrical horror at the truth. Blasphemy charge seals their verdict path.

Reformed irony: the blasphemy is theirs—condemning the Son—while they claim to defend God’s name.

Matthew 26:66

66What is your judgment?” They answered, “He deserves death.”

Arminian

Unanimous human judgment: death. The Innocent is sentenced by those who needed Him most.

Arminian moral: corporate injustice does not dilute personal guilt. Each voice for death answers for itself.

Reformed

Death sentence from the council. The Lamb is condemned in history’s most unjust verdict.

Reformed gospel paradox: their “deserves death” becomes the means of the death that saves the undeserving.

Matthew 26:67

67Then they spit in his face and struck him. And some slapped him,

Arminian

Spit, strikes, slaps: contempt after verdict. Theology turned to violence against the face of God-with-us.

Arminian lament: free cruelty. The same hands that handled Scripture now abuse the Word made flesh.

Reformed

Shame heaped on the Messiah. Isaiah’s servant pattern thickens.

Reformed worship: He endures scorn without calling the legions. This humiliation belongs to the cup He accepted.

Matthew 26:68

68saying, “Prophesy to us, you Christ! Who is it that struck you?”

Arminian

Mock prophecy game: tell us who hit you, Christ. They use His title as a taunt.

Arminian warning: sneering at revelation hardens the heart. Grace had spoken; mockery is chosen response.

Reformed

Christ-title as joke while they strike Him. Blindness performs comedy over judgment day.

Reformed certainty: the real Prophet will be vindicated. Their game does not erase His word about clouds and Power.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
