Passage

Matthew 26

Verses 57–68 · WEB · Voices

Jesus is led to Caiaphas; the scribes and elders gather. Peter follows at a distance into the courtyard. The council seeks false testimony, finally charges Jesus under the temple saying and His confession as the Christ, the Son of God. The high priest declares blasphemy; they condemn Him as deserving death and begin to mock and strike Him.

Matthew 26:57

57Those who had taken Jesus led him away to Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and the elders were gathered together.

Arminian

Seized and led to Caiaphas: formal religion hosts the miscarriage of justice. Scribes and elders are already gathered.

Office without fear of God can still assemble against the truth. Light resisted becomes tribunal.

Reformed

To Caiaphas’s house the story moves. Leadership that should know Scripture gathers for verdict.

Human court in session under a higher court. Their gathering serves ends they do not mean.

Matthew 26:58

58But Peter followed him from a distance to the court of the high priest, and entered in and sat with the officers, to see the end.

Arminian

Peter follows—at a distance—then sits with officers, to see the end. Peter’s loyalty is mixed with self-preservation.

Responsible disciples can edge near danger without full confession. The distance foreshadows the denial.

Reformed

Distance discipleship enters the courtyard. Peter positions himself to watch, not yet to witness.

Remaining near enough to fall is still under the Lord’s eye who foretold the denials.

Matthew 26:59

59Now the chief priests, the elders, and the whole council sought false testimony against Jesus, that they might put him to death,

Arminian

They seek false testimony to kill. Verdict first, evidence second. Injustice is intentional.

Free agents invent lies against the Truth. Grace does not make God the liar; they choose falsehood.

Reformed

The council’s aim is death; false witness is the tool. Matthew strips their piety bare.

Wicked seeking and divine purpose can coincide without God authoring the lie.

Matthew 26:60

60and they found none. Even though many false witnesses came forward, they found none. But at last two false witnesses came forward

Arminian

Many liars, no usable case—until two come forward. Even corrupted process struggles to pin the Holy One.

Truth has a stubborn edge. Falsehood multiplies and still needs a final pair to force the charge.

Reformed

Failure of false witness, then a last pair. Providence frustrates easy lies before the decisive accusation.

The story will turn on Jesus’ own confession more than on their fabrications.

Matthew 26:61

61and said, “This man said, ‘I am able to destroy the temple of God, and to build it in three days.’”

Arminian

Twisted temple saying becomes the charge. They distort Jesus’ words about the temple of His body.

Mishandling Scripture and Jesus’ words is accountable. Half-quotes can serve murderous ends.

Reformed

Misrepresented speech about the temple. Irony thickens: the true temple stands on trial.

What they mock as threat is the sign of death and resurrection they cannot see.

Matthew 26:62

62The high priest stood up and said to him, “Have you no answer? What is this that these testify against you?”

Arminian

Caiaphas presses for an answer. Silence will soon speak louder than defense lawyers.

Jesus is not obligated to validate a rigged court; His later confession will be voluntary truth-telling.

Reformed

The high priest demands reply. The court performs legality over predetermined death.

Questioning continues until the Christological climax they will call blasphemy.

Matthew 26:63

63But Jesus stayed silent. The high priest answered him, “I adjure you by the living God that you tell us whether you are the Christ, the Son of God.”

Arminian

Silence, then adjuration by the living God: Are you the Christ, the Son of God? The question is the right one asked in the wrong spirit.

Arminian interpreters hear a moment where truth is demanded. Jesus will answer sincerely; hearers remain responsible for what they do with the confession.

Reformed

Silent Lamb, then oath-bound question naming Christ and Son of God. Heaven’s identity is put under earthly curse-formula.

The confession they extract is the truth that condemns their unbelief and saves those who believe.

Matthew 26:64

64Jesus said to him, “You have said so. Nevertheless, I tell you, after this you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of Power, and coming on the clouds of the sky.”

Arminian

“You have said so,” plus Daniel/Psalm glory: Son of Man at Power’s right hand, coming on clouds. Jesus answers under oath with messianic claim and future vindication.

The confession is clear enough for faith or rejection. Grace enables receiving this Christ; the court will choose rage instead.

Reformed

Jesus gives his assent and adds a claim to exaltation. Jesus claims the throne and the coming. The judged One announces the Judge.

This is the Christological center of the trial. His word is true whether they believe it or not; many will yet see as He said.

Key contrast Both receive Jesus’ claim as true and decisive. Arminians typically stress a clear confession that sincerely summons faith or leaves rejecters without excuse; Reformed commentators emphasize the Son of Man’s sure exaltation and coming as God’s appointed vindication.

Matthew 26:65

65Then the high priest tore his clothing, saying, “He has spoken blasphemy! Why do we need any more witnesses? Behold, now you have heard his blasphemy.

Arminian

Robes torn, blasphemy declared, witnesses dismissed. They treat truth as blasphemy because they will not worship Him.

Light called darkness. Responsible leaders close the case against the Son of God.

Reformed

Theatrical horror at the truth. Blasphemy charge seals their verdict path.

The blasphemy is theirs—condemning the Son—while they claim to defend God’s name.

Matthew 26:66

66What do you think?” They answered, “He is worthy of death!”

Arminian

The human court is unanimous: death. The Innocent is sentenced by those who needed Him most.

Corporate injustice does not dilute personal guilt. Each voice for death answers for itself.

Reformed

Death sentence from the council. The Lamb is condemned in history’s most unjust verdict.

Their “is worthy of death” becomes the means of the death that saves the undeserving.

Matthew 26:67

67Then they spat in his face and beat him with their fists, and some slapped him,

Arminian

Spit, strikes, slaps: contempt after verdict. Theology turned to violence against the face of God-with-us.

The cruelty is given freely. The same hands that handled Scripture now abuse the Word made flesh.

Reformed

Shame heaped on the Messiah. Isaiah’s servant pattern thickens.

He endures scorn without calling the legions. This humiliation belongs to the cup He accepted.

Matthew 26:68

68saying, “Prophesy to us, you Christ! Who hit you?”

Arminian

Mock prophecy game: tell us who hit you, Christ. They use His title as a taunt.

Sneering at revelation hardens the heart. Grace had spoken; mockery is chosen response.

Reformed

Christ-title as joke while they strike Him. Blindness performs comedy over judgment day.

The Prophet will be vindicated. Their game does not erase His word about clouds and Power.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Classic comment on Jesus is led to Caiaphas; the scribes and elders gather, from both sides of the debate.

Arminian Notable voices

“They led him away to Caiaphas - From the house of Annas, the father-in-law of Caiaphas, to whom they had carried him first. But Peter followed him afar off - Variously agitated by conflicting passions; love constrained him to follow his Master; fear made him follow afar off.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 26:57–68)

Reformed Notable voices

“It is certain that Christ was silent when false witnesses pressed hard upon him, not only because they did not deserve a reply, but because he did not seek to be now acquitted, knowing that his hour was come. But Caiaphas triumphs over him on account of his silence, as if he was struck dumb by being vanquished; which is usually the case with men who are conscious of having done wrong.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 26:57–68)
