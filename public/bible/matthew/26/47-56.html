Passage

Matthew 26

Verses 47–56 · WEB · Voices

Judas arrives with a crowd from the chief priests and elders, betrays Jesus with a kiss, and the arrest follows. A disciple strikes with the sword; Jesus rebukes violence, affirms He could call legions of angels, and submits so the Scriptures are fulfilled. The disciples flee.

Matthew 26:47

47While he was still speaking, behold, Judas, one of the twelve, came, and with him a great multitude with swords and clubs, from the chief priests and elders of the people.

Arminian

Still speaking—then Judas, one of the Twelve, with a crowd and weapons. Betrayal arrives mid-word, armed by religious authority.

Chosen nearness abused. Grace had given Judas light; he brings force instead of repentance.

Reformed

Timing matches Jesus’ announcement. “One of the twelve” and official force fill the garden.

Providence is punctual. The hour Jesus named is populated by Judas and the crowd God will overrule.

Matthew 26:48

48Now he who betrayed him had given them a sign, saying, “Whoever I kiss, he is the one. Seize him.”

Arminian

A kiss as signal: intimacy weaponized. Judas plans the mark of friendship for seizure.

Religious gesture can cloak rebellion. Responsible evil often borrows holy forms.

Reformed

Prearranged sign exposes calculated treachery. The kiss is strategy, not impulse alone.

Scripture records the scheme without softening it. God is not surprised; Judas is not excused.

Matthew 26:49

49Immediately he came to Jesus, and said, “Greetings, Rabbi!” and kissed him.

Arminian

Immediate approach, rabbi greeting, kiss. The act is personal and false.

Arminian interpreters see culpable hypocrisy under light. Jesus remains greetable; Judas chooses the traitor’s intimacy.

Reformed

The kiss lands on the Son of Man. The greeting and the betrayal come in the same moment.

The Holy One receives a traitor’s kiss on the way to the cross written for Him.

Matthew 26:50

50Jesus said to him, “Friend, why are you here?” Then they came and laid hands on Jesus, and took him.

Arminian

Friend, do what you came to do. Jesus neither panics nor flatters; He permits the deed Judas came for. Hands seize Him.

Patience toward the betrayer is not approval. Grace had been true; judgment now lets the chosen path run its course.

Reformed

Jesus authorizes the moment’s advance: do what you came to do. Seizure follows His word as much as their grip.

He is not finally overpowered; He hands Himself into the hour. Jesus calls Judas “friend” while remaining sovereign over the hour.

Matthew 26:51

51Behold, one of those who were with Jesus stretched out his hand and drew his sword, and struck the servant of the high priest, and cut off his ear.

Arminian

Sword for the Messiah’s defense—zeal without understanding. A disciple meets spiritual crisis with steel.

Sincere loyalty can still choose the wrong means. Grace calls for Jesus’ way, not our reflex violence.

Reformed

Fleshly defense erupts. The servant’s ear becomes a sign of misguided zeal.

The kingdom Jesus brings will not be secured by that sword. His rebuke is coming.

Matthew 26:52

52Then Jesus said to him, “Put your sword back into its place, for all those who take the sword will die by the sword.

Arminian

Put it back. Jesus rejects sword-logic for this mission. Taking the sword as the kingdom’s method invites perishing by it.

Responsible followers must obey this restraint. The cross path is chosen, not a holy war of panic.

Reformed

Command and proverb: sheath the sword. Jesus governs His arrest without revolutionary steel.

The Passion will proceed by Scripture and submission, not by disciple blades.

Matthew 26:53

53Or do you think that I couldn’t ask my Father, and he would even now send me more than twelve legions of angels?

Arminian

Jesus could appeal and receive legions at once. Restraint is voluntary power, not helplessness.

Arminian interpreters stress free, loving refusal of rescue that would abort redemption. He stays for us.

Reformed

Angel armies are available; the Son does not call them. The Father’s will holds the legions back because the cup must be drunk.

Omnipotence deferred is not impotence. Providence keeps the cross on course.

Key contrast Both deny a trapped, powerless Jesus. Arminians emphasize His free choice to forgo rescue; Reformed interpreters emphasize the Father’s will and purpose that withhold the legions for the appointed atonement.

Matthew 26:54

54How then would the Scriptures be fulfilled that it must be so?”

Arminian

Scripture must be fulfilled—that it must be so. Jesus interprets His path as obedience to written promise, not fate without meaning.

“must” is moral-theological necessity of God’s saving plan embraced, not bare coercion that erases love.

Reformed

Fulfillment language is decisive. The arrest serves what God promised.

Scripture’s “must” stands under God’s decree. The story cannot be rewritten by a sword.

Matthew 26:55

55In that hour Jesus said to the multitudes, “Have you come out as against a robber with swords and clubs to seize me? I sat daily in the temple teaching, and you didn’t arrest me.

Arminian

He exposes their theater: treated like a robber though He taught openly. Night force confesses fear of light.

Rejecting public truth often prefers shadows. Responsibility grows with how much teaching they already heard.

Reformed

Temple teaching versus night clubs: their method indicts them. Jesus’ public ministry left them without excuse.

The hour hides what daylight would have complicated—yet fulfills the written path.

Matthew 26:56

56But all this has happened that the Scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled.” Then all the disciples left him and fled.

Arminian

Prophetic fulfillment, then total flight. Jesus frames the night; disciples abandon Him as He said they would.

Their fleeing is failure under pressure—later restorable. Grace had warned; they still must own the scatter.

Reformed

Fulfillment summary, then empty garden of friends. The sheep scatter as written.

Their flight fulfills the word and does not void Galilee. The Shepherd remains, though alone among sinners’ hands.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Older voices from both traditions on Matthew 26:47–56.

Arminian Notable voices

“The heroic behaviour of the blessed Jesus, in the whole period of his sufferings, will be observed by every attentive eye, and felt by every pious heart: although the sacred historians, according to their usual but wonderful simplicity, make no encomiums upon it. With what composure does he go forth to meet the traitor!”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Matt 26:47–56)

Reformed Notable voices

“And, lo, one of those who were with Jesus. Luke says, that all the disciples made an agreement together to fight for their Master. Hence it is again evident, that we are much more courageous and ready for fighting than for bearing the cross; and, therefore, we ought always to deliberate wisely what the Lord commands, and what he requires from every one of us, lest the fervor of our zeal exceed the bounds of reason and moderation.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 26:47–56)
