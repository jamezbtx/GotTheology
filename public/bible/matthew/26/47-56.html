Passage

Matthew 26

Verses 47–56 · ESV

Judas arrives with a crowd from the chief priests and elders, betrays Jesus with a kiss, and the arrest follows. A disciple strikes with the sword; Jesus rebukes violence, affirms He could call legions of angels, and submits so the Scriptures are fulfilled. The disciples flee.

Matthew 26:47

47While he was still speaking, Judas came, one of the twelve, and with him a great crowd with swords and clubs, from the chief priests and the elders of the people.

Arminian

Still speaking—then Judas, one of the Twelve, with a crowd and weapons. Betrayal arrives mid-word, armed by religious authority.

Arminian gravity: chosen nearness abused. Grace had given Judas light; he brings force instead of repentance.

Reformed

Timing matches Jesus’ announcement. “One of the twelve” and official force fill the garden.

Reformed note: providence is punctual. The hour Jesus named is populated by Judas and the crowd God will overrule.

Matthew 26:48

48Now the betrayer had given them a sign, saying, “The one I will kiss is the man; seize him.”

Arminian

A kiss as signal: intimacy weaponized. Judas plans the mark of friendship for seizure.

Arminian warning: religious gesture can cloak rebellion. Responsible evil often borrows holy forms.

Reformed

Prearranged sign exposes calculated treachery. The kiss is strategy, not impulse alone.

Reformed sobriety: Scripture records the scheme without softening it. God is not surprised; Judas is not excused.

Matthew 26:49

49And he came up to Jesus at once and said, “Greetings, Rabbi!” And he kissed him.

Arminian

Immediate approach, rabbi greeting, kiss. The act is personal and false.

Classical Arminians see culpable hypocrisy under real light. Jesus remains greetable; Judas chooses the traitor’s intimacy.

Reformed

The kiss lands on the Son of Man. Greeting and betrayal coincide.

Reformed grief and glory: the Holy One receives a traitor’s kiss on the way to the cross written for Him.

Matthew 26:50

50Jesus said to him, “Friend, do what you came to do.” Then they came up and laid hands on Jesus and seized him.

Arminian

“Friend, do what you came to do.” Jesus neither panics nor flatters; He permits the deed Judas came for. Hands seize Him.

Arminian reading: patience toward the betrayer is not approval. Grace had been real; judgment now lets the chosen path run its course.

Reformed

Jesus authorizes the moment’s advance: do what you came to do. Seizure follows His word as much as their grip.

Reformed confession: He is not finally overpowered; He hands Himself into the hour. Friend-address and sovereignty meet.

Matthew 26:51

51And behold, one of those who were with Jesus stretched out his hand and drew his sword and struck the servant of the high priest and cut off his ear.

Arminian

Sword for the Messiah’s defense—zeal without understanding. A disciple meets spiritual crisis with steel.

Arminian counsel: sincere loyalty can still choose the wrong means. Grace calls for Jesus’ way, not our reflex violence.

Reformed

Fleshly defense erupts. The servant’s ear becomes a sign of misguided zeal.

Reformed diagnosis: the kingdom Jesus brings will not be secured by that sword. His rebuke is coming.

Matthew 26:52

52Then Jesus said to him, “Put your sword back into its place. For all who take the sword will perish by the sword.

Arminian

Put it back. Jesus rejects sword-logic for this mission. Taking the sword as the kingdom’s method invites perishing by it.

Arminian discipleship: responsible followers must obey this restraint. The cross path is chosen, not a holy war of panic.

Reformed

Command and proverb: sheath the sword. Jesus governs His arrest without revolutionary steel.

Reformed clarity: the Passion will proceed by Scripture and submission, not by disciple blades.

Matthew 26:53

53Do you think that I cannot appeal to my Father, and he will at once send me more than twelve legions of angels?

Arminian

Jesus could appeal and receive legions at once. Restraint is voluntary power, not helplessness.

Classical Arminians stress free, loving refusal of rescue that would abort redemption. He stays for us.

Reformed

Angel armies are available; the Son does not call them. The Father’s will holds the legions back because the cup must be drunk.

Reformed wonder: omnipotence deferred is not impotence. Providence keeps the cross on course.

Key contrast Both deny a trapped, powerless Jesus. Arminians typically emphasize His free choice to forgo rescue; Reformed readers typically emphasize the Father’s will and purpose that withhold the legions for the appointed atonement.

Matthew 26:54

54But how then should the Scriptures be fulfilled, that it must be so?”

Arminian

Scripture must be fulfilled—that it must be so. Jesus interprets His path as obedience to written promise, not fate without meaning.

Arminian note: “must” is moral-theological necessity of God’s saving plan embraced, not bare coercion that erases love.

Reformed

Fulfillment language is decisive. The arrest serves what God promised.

Reformed certainty: Scripture’s “must” stands under God’s decree. The story cannot be rewritten by a sword.

Matthew 26:55

55At that hour Jesus said to the crowds, “Have you come out as against a robber, with swords and clubs to capture me? Day after day I sat in the temple teaching, and you did not seize me.

Arminian

He exposes their theater: treated like a robber though He taught openly. Night force confesses fear of light.

Arminian edge: rejecting public truth often prefers shadows. Responsibility grows with how much teaching they already heard.

Reformed

Temple teaching versus night clubs: their method indicts them. Jesus’ public ministry left them without excuse.

Reformed note: the hour hides what daylight would have complicated—yet fulfills the written path.

Matthew 26:56

56But all this has taken place that the Scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled.” Then all the disciples left him and fled.

Arminian

Prophetic fulfillment, then total flight. Jesus frames the night; disciples abandon Him as He said they would.

Arminian pastoral: their fleeing is failure under pressure—real and later restorable. Grace had warned; they still must own the scatter.

Reformed

Fulfillment summary, then empty garden of friends. The sheep scatter as written.

Reformed comfort: their flight fulfills the word and does not cancel Galilee. The Shepherd remains, though alone among sinners’ hands.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
