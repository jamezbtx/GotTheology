Passage

Matthew 23

Verses 16–22 · ESV · Voices

Jesus continues the woes against casuistry that ranks oaths by gold and gifts while sidelining temple, altar, and God. Blind guides invent loopholes; He restores the sacred order that ends at the throne.

Matthew 23:16

16"Woe to you, blind guides, who say, 'If anyone swears by the temple, it is nothing, but if anyone swears by the gold of the temple, he is bound by his oath.'

Arminian

Blind guides invent oath rankings: temple oath “nothing,” gold oath binding. Technical religion excuses broken word.

Classical Arminians hear a call to integrity. Grace frees people from loophole ethics; truthfulness before God is not optional theater.

Reformed

Woe on blind guides and their oath casuistry. Gold elevated above the temple reverses God’s order.

Reformed diagnosis: false teaching twists holy things into human systems. Christ judges the blindness that leads others by invented rules.

Matthew 23:17

17You blind fools! For which is greater, the gold or the temple that has made the gold sacred?

Arminian

Fools miss the obvious: the temple consecrates the gold. Priority belongs to God’s dwelling, not the metal.

Arminian pastoral clarity: repent of clever inversions. God invites renewed sight that honors what He makes sacred.

Reformed

Blind fools—sharp prophetic speech. The temple makes the gold sacred, not the reverse.

Reformed emphasis: God defines holiness. Human valuations that flip His order stand under rebuke.

Matthew 23:18

18And you say, 'If anyone swears by the altar, it is nothing, but if anyone swears by the gift that is on the altar, he is bound by his oath.'

Arminian

Same pattern with altar and gift. The offering is treated as more binding than the altar that receives it.

Classical Arminians warn against religious deals that protect property-language over worship. Grace calls for honest speech without graded escapes.

Reformed

Altar oath dismissed; gift oath enforced. Again the human gift outranks God’s appointed place.

Reformed reading: casuistry serves self-interest. The Lord’s woe strips the cover from manipulative piety.

Matthew 23:19

19You blind men! For which is greater, the gift or the altar that makes the gift sacred?

Arminian

Blind men: the altar makes the gift sacred. Worship’s furniture of God outranks the donor’s present.

Arminian note: humility returns gifts to their place. God enables clearer seeing when pride’s loopholes are abandoned.

Reformed

The altar consecrates the gift. God’s means of holiness set the rank.

Reformed comfort and warning: truth corrects blindness. Christ restores the grammar of sacredness His opponents scrambled.

Matthew 23:20

20So whoever swears by the altar swears by it and by everything on it.

Arminian

No partial oath: altar includes everything on it. Words before holy things pull the whole into the vow.

Classical Arminians urge simple truthfulness (cf. Matt 5:33–37). Grace trains speech that needs no clever carve-outs.

Reformed

Swearing by the altar binds the swearer to altar and contents. Jesus collapses their distinctions.

Reformed emphasis: God’s holiness is indivisible here. The Word exposes fragmented ethics as false.

Matthew 23:21

21And whoever swears by the temple swears by it and by him who dwells in it.

Arminian

Temple oath reaches Him who dwells there. Every religious vow finally faces God Himself.

Arminian pastoral weight: take God seriously when you speak. Grace invites reverence that refuses empty formulas.

Reformed

Temple oath is oath by the Dweller. God is the true referent of sacred speech.

Reformed confession: the living God inhabits His house by promise. Casuistry that ignores Him is atheism in religious dress.

Matthew 23:22

22And whoever swears by heaven swears by the throne of God and by him who sits upon it."

Arminian

Heaven leads to God’s throne and the One seated there. No “safe” oath escapes His presence.

Classical Arminians close the loophole path: all roads of sacred speech end at God. The woe summons repentant integrity under the King.

Reformed

Heaven = throne of God and Him who sits upon it. Ultimate accountability is settled.

Reformed climax: sovereignty frames every vow. Christ drives ethics back to the enthroned Lord—judgment and truth meet at the throne.

Key contrast Both reject oath casuistry that sidelines God. Arminians typically emphasize a sincere call to truthful speech and repentant integrity; Reformed readers typically emphasize God’s throne as the sovereign end of every sacred oath Christ exposes.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Jesus continues the woes against casuistry that ranks oaths by gold and gifts while sidelining temple, altar, and God—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Matthew 23:16–22.

Arminian Notable voices

“Whosoever shall swear by the gold - The covetous man, says one, still gives preference to the object of his lust; gold has still the first place in his heart. A man is to be suspected when he recommends those good works most from which he receives most advantage.”
Adam Clarke · Commentary on the New Testament (on Matt 23:16–22)

Reformed Notable voices

“Woe to you, blind guides, As ambition is almost always connected with hypocrisy, so the superstitions of the people are usually encouraged by the covetousness and rapacity of pastors. The world has, indeed, a natural propensity to errors, and even draws down upon itself, as if on purpose, every kind of deceit and imposture; but improper modes of worship come to gain a footing only when they are confirmed by the rulers themselves.”
John Calvin · Commentary on a Harmony of the Evangelists (on Matt 23:16–22)
