Passage

Matthew 23

Verses 23–28 · ESV

Mid-woes: tithing herbs while neglecting justice, mercy, and faithfulness; cleaning cups and tombs while greed and lawlessness fill the inside. Jesus demands weightier obedience and inward purity, not cosmetic religion.

Matthew 23:23

23"Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you tithe mint and dill and cumin, and have neglected the weightier matters of the law: justice and mercy and faithfulness. These you ought to have done, without neglecting the others.

Arminian

Herb tithes without justice, mercy, and faithfulness. Jesus affirms both: do the weightier matters and do not neglect the lesser.

Classical Arminians hear a call to whole-law love. Grace enables prioritizing mercy without excusing stinginess toward God; hypocrisy picks easy piety and skips costly righteousness.

Reformed

Weightier matters named: justice, mercy, faithfulness. Lesser tithes remain, but rank is restored.

Reformed preaching: God’s Law has center and circumference. Christ exposes selective obedience that keeps mint and loses Micah’s heart.

Matthew 23:24

24You blind guides, straining out a gnat and swallowing a camel!

Arminian

Comic and devastating: gnat strained, camel swallowed. Scruple on the tiny, blindness on the massive.

Arminian pastoral wit with tears: grace reorders sight. Turn from micro-religion to the camel-sized commands of love.

Reformed

Blind guides—image of inverted discernment. The proverb seals the indictment.

Reformed sobriety: false teachers invert magnitudes. Christ, the true Guide, restores scale under God’s Word.

Matthew 23:25

25"Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you clean the outside of the cup and the plate, but inside they are full of greed and self-indulgence.

Arminian

Outside polished; inside full of greed and self-indulgence. Ritual cleanliness masks grasping hearts.

Classical Arminians urge heart repentance. Grace cleans what scrubbing the rim cannot; God invites surrender of greed, not only better presentation.

Reformed

External wash, internal plunder. Hypocrisy is a full cup of appetite.

Reformed diagnosis: the heart’s corruption is the issue (cf. Matt 15). Only God regenerates what polish cannot reach.

Matthew 23:26

26You blind Pharisee! First clean the inside of the cup and the plate, that the outside also may be clean.

Arminian

Order of cleansing: inside first, then outside. Real holiness starts at the heart and shows.

Arminian hope: God enables inward cleansing that bears outward fruit. The summons is sincere—seek purity where it begins.

Reformed

First the inside. Outer cleanness follows true inward work.

Reformed order of grace: God cleanses the heart; visible righteousness is fruit, not the root. Christ’s imperative exposes blind method.

Matthew 23:27

27"Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you are like whitewashed tombs, which outwardly appear beautiful, but within are full of dead people's bones and all uncleanness.

Arminian

Whitewashed tombs: beautiful outside, death within. Religion as façade over uncleanness.

Classical Arminians warn without caricature: appearance can deceive neighbors and self. Grace calls for honesty before God who sees the bones.

Reformed

Tombs whitewashed—Passover image of concealed defilement. Beauty as cover for death.

Reformed gravity: God is not fooled by paint. The metaphor prepares the charge of hypocrisy and lawlessness in the next verse.

Matthew 23:28

28So you also outwardly appear righteous to others, but within you are full of hypocrisy and lawlessness."

Arminian

Outward righteousness for others; within, hypocrisy and lawlessness. The audience is people; the Judge is God.

Arminian pastoral edge: stop performing. Grace offers real righteousness in Christ that remakes the inside—turn while the woe still teaches.

Reformed

Appear righteous; are full of hypocrisy and anomia. Verdict stated.

Reformed confession: only imputed and imparted righteousness in Christ answers this charge. Human appearance-righteousness is empty before the Holy One.

Key contrast Both reject cosmetic religion. Arminians typically emphasize a sincere call to inward repentance grace enables; Reformed readers typically emphasize the heart’s corruption and God’s provision of true righteousness in Christ against mere appearance.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
