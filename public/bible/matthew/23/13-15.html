Sample passage

Matthew 23

Verses 13–15 · ESV

After exposing honor-seeking religion, Jesus opens the seven woes. The first strike hypocrisy that blocks the kingdom and mission that multiplies ruin. (ESV omits v. 14, present in some manuscripts as a widows’-houses woe.)

Matthew 23:13

13"But woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you shut the kingdom of heaven in people's faces. For you neither enter yourselves nor allow those who would enter to go in.

Arminian

Woe names grief and judgment. They shut the kingdom in people’s faces—neither entering nor letting others enter who would.

Classical Arminians hear a tragic abuse of influence: grace’s door is blocked by false teaching and example. Those who “would enter” show real inclination met with obstruction; leaders bear heavy guilt for hindering sincere seekers.

Reformed

Woe from the King exposes hypocrites who shut heaven’s kingdom. They refuse entry and obstruct others.

Reformed gravity: Christ alone opens and shuts with ultimate authority. False shepherds oppose His kingdom; their barrier is real sin under His judicial word, and the woe is not empty rhetoric.

Key contrast Both treat the shutting of the kingdom as culpable hypocrisy. Arminians typically emphasize blocked seekers who truly would enter; Reformed readers typically emphasize Christ’s judicial woe against leaders who oppose His kingdom under God’s righteous verdict.

Matthew 23:15

15Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you travel across sea and land to make a single proselyte, and when he becomes a proselyte, you make him twice as much a child of hell as yourselves.

Arminian

Zealous mission without truth: sea and land for one convert, then a disciple twice a child of hell. Evangelistic energy can deepen ruin when the gospel is twisted.

Arminian pastoral warning: grace cares about converts’ destiny. Mission must lead people to Christ, not into a harder hypocrisy. The woe summons repentance for teachers and converts alike.

Reformed

Proselytizing zeal ends in doubled Gehenna-children. Activity is not faithfulness.

Reformed sobriety: God weighs mission by its fruit under truth. False religion reproduces itself; Christ’s woe judges both the makers and the system that multiplies heirs of wrath.

About this sample. Commentary here is written for product demonstration. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
