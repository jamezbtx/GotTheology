Passage

Matthew 3

Verses 7–12 · ESV · Voices

John confronts Pharisees and Sadducees seeking baptism without fruit: viper brood, Abrahamic presumption, axe at the root, and a Coming One who baptizes with Spirit and fire and separates wheat from chaff.

Matthew 3:7

7But when he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to his baptism, he said to them, “You brood of vipers! Who warned you to flee from the wrath to come?

Arminian

Religious leaders arrive, and John does not flatter them. “Brood of vipers” and “wrath to come” strip pedigree of safety. Fleeing wrath is wise only if fruit follows.

Arminian urgency: the warning is sincere. Wrath is real, and hearers are summoned to flee it by true repentance—not by a baptism that leaves the heart unchanged.

Reformed

John exposes counterfeit approach to baptism. Wrath is coming; religious labels do not cancel it.

Reformed sobriety: God’s judgment is not a scare tactic without substance. The prophet’s harshness is mercy that tells the truth before the axe falls.

Matthew 3:8

8Bear fruit in keeping with repentance.

Arminian

Repentance has a matching harvest. Fruit “in keeping with” means a life that fits the turn claimed at the water.

Classical Arminians preach this as real moral change under grace. Confession without fruit is incomplete; grace enables a new walk that can and should appear.

Reformed

Fruit authenticates repentance. John refuses empty profession.

Reformed accent: new life shows. Fruit is evidence of a heart God has turned—not a wage that buys pardon, yet never optional window dressing.

Key contrast Both demand fruit with repentance. Arminians typically stress grace-enabled responsibility to produce a matching life; Reformed readers typically stress fruit as necessary evidence of a repentance God has wrought.

Matthew 3:9

9And do not presume to say to yourselves, ‘We have Abraham as our father,’ for I tell you, God is able from these stones to raise up children for Abraham.

Arminian

Abrahamic descent is no shelter from wrath. God can raise children from stones—heritage cannot replace faith and fruit.

Arminian pastoral cut: privilege without response is presumption. The invitation remains open, but ancestry does not save.

Reformed

Covenant privilege without faith is empty. God is free to create a people for Abraham beyond bloodlines.

Reformed reading: election and promise are God’s to fulfill. Stones-language humbles every boast in flesh while magnifying God’s power to raise a true seed.

Matthew 3:10

10Even now the axe is laid to the root of the trees. Every tree therefore that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire.

Arminian

The axe is already at the root—judgment is near, not theoretical. Fruitless trees face fire.

Arminian plea: repent while the axe has not yet swung. The warning aims at conversion, not despair alone.

Reformed

“Even now” marks present crisis. Fruitless trees are cut down and burned.

Reformed honesty: final judgment is rooted in God’s righteousness. The axe image is gospel seriousness that drives sinners to the Coming One, not to self-repair.

Matthew 3:11

11“I baptize you with water for repentance, but he who is coming after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.

Arminian

John’s water baptism is for repentance; the Coming One is mightier and baptizes with Spirit and fire. John decreases; Jesus surpasses.

Arminian Christology of grace: the Spirit’s work is offered in the Mightier One. Fire can purify or judge; either way, Jesus’ baptism eclipses John’s.

Reformed

John’s humility frames Jesus’ supremacy. Spirit-and-fire baptism is the Messiah’s own work.

Reformed accent: the Spirit is given by the Son according to the Father’s purpose. John’s ministry is preparatory; Christ’s is decisive and effectual.

Matthew 3:12

12His winnowing fork is in his hand, and he will clear his threshing floor and gather his wheat into the barn, but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire.”

Arminian

Winnowing separates wheat and chaff. Jesus gathers and burns; the invitation to be wheat is urgent under unquenchable fire.

Arminian evangelism: flee chaff’s end by true repentance and faith in the Coming One. The fork is in His hand—respond while gathering is still offered.

Reformed

The threshing floor is His; wheat and chaff meet different ends. Gathering and burning are the Messiah’s acts.

Reformed confidence: Christ clears His floor completely. Judgment and salvation are not left unfinished; the barn and the fire both display His lordship.

Key contrast Both preach real separation under Christ. Arminians typically emphasize the sincere call to repent and become wheat; Reformed readers typically emphasize Christ’s effectual clearing of His floor—gathering His wheat and judging the chaff.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on repentance, wrath, and fruit—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside John’s warning.

Arminian Notable voices

“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
