Passage

Proverbs 2

Verses 10–22 · WEB

When wisdom enters the heart, discretion and understanding guard the son from the way of evil men who speak perversely and from the seductive woman who has abandoned her covenant. Her path leads to death, while the upright will remain in the land and the wicked will be uprooted. Both traditions read this as practical and covenantal guidance, with broad common ground; they differ mainly in how they read the strong language about those who go to her not returning.

Proverbs 2:10

10For wisdom will enter into your heart. Knowledge will be pleasant to your soul.

Arminian

Wisdom entering the heart and becoming pleasant marks a real inward change. Arminian readers see the fruit of the son’s search: what was sought now becomes delight.

Wisdom becomes a joy, not just a duty.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an inward transformation in which God’s wisdom becomes the soul’s delight (cf. Ps 19:10).

New affections make obedience glad rather than grudging.

Proverbs 2:11

11Discretion will watch over you. Understanding will keep you,

Arminian

Discretion and understanding watch over the son. Arminian readers note that wisdom, once received, functions as a guard in daily choices.

Good judgment protects from harm.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wisdom as a means God uses to keep his people. The protection described in verse 8 now works through internalized understanding.

Grace forms character that guards the life.

Proverbs 2:12

12to deliver you from the way of evil, from the men who speak perverse things,

Arminian

Wisdom delivers from the way of evil and from men who speak perverse things. Arminian readers note the emphasis on speech as the entry point of corruption.

Like the gang of chapter 1, these men tempt through words.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the twisted speech of those who invert good and evil. Wisdom’s discernment exposes it.

Deliverance here is protection from being drawn into evil company.

Proverbs 2:13

13who forsake the paths of uprightness, to walk in the ways of darkness,

Arminian

These men forsake the paths of uprightness to walk in darkness. Arminian readers stress that they have left a right path, a real turning away.

Darkness is chosen in place of light.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the light and darkness imagery that runs through Scripture (cf. John 3:19). Forsaking uprightness is a willful departure.

The verse describes people who love darkness.

Proverbs 2:14

14who rejoice to do evil, and delight in the perverseness of evil,

Arminian

They rejoice in doing evil. Arminian readers note how habitual sin can twist delight itself.

Sin is not only done but enjoyed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the corruption of the affections: evil delights the wicked (cf. Rom 1:32).

The heart, not only the deed, is depraved.

Proverbs 2:15

15who are crooked in their ways, and wayward in their paths,

Arminian

They are crooked and wayward in their paths. Arminian readers note the contrast with the straight and good paths named in verse 9.

The imagery prepares the son to recognize such company.

Reformed

Reformed readers see crookedness as moral distortion, the opposite of the upright way God guards.

The path language unifies the chapter.

Proverbs 2:16

16to deliver you from the strange woman, even from the foreigner who flatters with her words,

Arminian

Wisdom also delivers from the strange woman who flatters. Arminian readers note that this figure returns in Proverbs 5–7 and represents real sexual temptation.

Flattery is again the means of temptation.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise read the strange or foreign woman first as a real adulteress. Many also see her as the counterpart of Lady Wisdom, picturing the pull of folly.

Temptation comes through attractive speech.

Proverbs 2:17

17who forsakes the friend of her youth, and forgets the covenant of her God;

Arminian

She forsakes the companion of her youth and forgets her God’s covenant. Arminian readers note that marriage is treated as a covenant before God (cf. Mal 2:14).

Her sin is betrayal of both her husband and God.

Reformed

Reformed readers also see marriage as a covenant witnessed by God (Mal 2:14). Adultery is covenant-breaking.

The verse ties sexual faithfulness to faithfulness toward God.

Proverbs 2:18

18for her house leads down to death, her paths to the departed spirits.

Arminian

Her house leads down to death and the departed spirits. Arminian readers stress the deadly consequences of following her.

The pleasure offered hides a path to death (cf. Prov 9:18).

Reformed

Reformed readers see the deadly end of the way of folly. The imagery anticipates the warnings in chapters 5, 7, and 9.

Sin’s attraction conceals its end.

Proverbs 2:19

19None who go to her return again, neither do they attain to the paths of life.

Arminian

None who go to her return. Arminian readers read the line as a strong warning that this path can lead past the point of return. They often take it as proverbial emphasis rather than an absolute statement that no adulterer can repent.

The warning is meant to keep the son from taking the first steps.

Reformed

Reformed readers also take the line as a strong warning about the deadly grip of this sin. Many read it as proverbial emphasis rather than a denial that God can restore a repentant sinner (cf. Ps 51).

The point is the danger of the path, not a limit on grace.

Proverbs 2:20

20Therefore walk in the way of good men, and keep the paths of the righteous.

Arminian

Therefore walk in the way of good men. Arminian readers note the positive counterpart to the warnings: choose good company and a righteous path.

Wisdom is not only avoidance but direction.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the call to follow the godly. Good examples are among God’s means for shaping his people (cf. Heb 13:7).

The righteous path is a shared way.

Proverbs 2:21

21For the upright will dwell in the land. The perfect will remain in it.

Arminian

The upright will dwell in the land. Arminian readers note the covenant promise of the land tied to faithful living (cf. Deut 30:20).

The promise encourages perseverance.

Reformed

Reformed readers hear the covenant promise of the land (Ps 37:9, 11, 29), which Jesus echoes in promising that the meek will inherit the earth (Matt 5:5).

The upright are secure in God’s inheritance.

Proverbs 2:22

22But the wicked will be cut off from the land. The treacherous will be rooted out of it.

Arminian

The wicked will be cut off from the land. Arminian readers see the end that follows a chosen way.

The chapter closes with a clear contrast between two paths and two destinies.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s justice removing the wicked from the inheritance (Ps 37:9).

The two-ways teaching of Proverbs ends this discourse with a sober contrast.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
