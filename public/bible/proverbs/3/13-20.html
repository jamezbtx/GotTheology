Proverbs 3

Verses 13–20 · WEB

The father praises wisdom as more precious than silver, gold, or rubies, a tree of life bringing long life, honor, pleasantness, and peace. The same wisdom by which the LORD founded the earth and established the heavens is offered to those who seek it. Both traditions share the conviction that God’s wisdom orders creation and that human wisdom flourishes by aligning with it.

Proverbs 3:13

13Happy is the man who finds wisdom, the man who gets understanding.

Arminian

Happy is the one who finds wisdom and gains understanding. Arminian readers see wisdom as a treasure to be sought and gained through effort and openness. The blessing invites the son to pursue wisdom as the path to a flourishing life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wisdom as a gift found by those who seek it, ultimately coming from God. The beatitude highlights the joy of possessing understanding. Both traditions see this as an invitation to value wisdom above other goods.

Proverbs 3:14

14For her good profit is better than getting silver, and her return is better than fine gold.

Arminian

Wisdom’s profit is better than silver and gold. Arminian readers see a call to rank priorities rightly, valuing wisdom above material gain. The comparison challenges the assumption that wealth is the highest good.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wisdom’s surpassing worth compared with the most prized commodities. The proverb reorders desire toward what truly enriches life. Both traditions see shared ground in this call to value understanding above riches.

Proverbs 3:15

15She is more precious than rubies. None of the things you can desire are to be compared to her.

Arminian

Wisdom is more precious than rubies, and nothing desirable compares to her. Arminian readers see the incomparable value of wisdom, which should shape what the son seeks. Desires must be directed toward what truly lasts.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wisdom as surpassing every earthly treasure. The personification of wisdom as a woman makes her worth vivid and personal. Both traditions see this as shared encouragement to prize wisdom above all other desires.

Proverbs 3:16

16Length of days is in her right hand. In her left hand are riches and honor.

Arminian

Wisdom holds long life in one hand and riches and honor in the other. Arminian readers see wisdom bringing a range of blessings, read as general patterns rather than guarantees. The image presents wisdom as generous to those who embrace her.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wisdom’s gifts portrayed in her hands, describing the good fruit of wise living. Proverbs presents these outcomes as typical rather than absolute. Both traditions agree that the verse encourages pursuit of wisdom without promising wealth to every wise person.

Proverbs 3:17

17Her ways are ways of pleasantness. All her paths are peace.

Arminian

Wisdom’s ways are pleasant and her paths are peace. Arminian readers see the wise life as satisfying rather than burdensome. Walking in wisdom brings inner peace and harmonious relationships.

Reformed

Reformed readers see peace and pleasantness as the fruit of living according to God’s order. Wisdom’s paths lead to wholeness. Both traditions see this verse as an appealing portrait of the wise life, offered as encouragement rather than as a guarantee against trouble.

Proverbs 3:18

18She is a tree of life to those who lay hold of her. Happy is everyone who retains her.

Arminian

Wisdom is a tree of life to those who grasp her, and those who hold her fast are blessed. Arminian readers see the need to take hold and keep holding, reflecting continued commitment. The tree of life image recalls the life God intended for humanity.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the tree of life image echoing Eden, presenting wisdom as a source of life and blessing. Those who retain wisdom are happy. Both traditions see the verse highlighting life-giving wisdom and the importance of holding it firmly.

Proverbs 3:19

19By wisdom the LORD founded the earth. By understanding, he established the heavens.

Arminian

By wisdom the LORD founded the earth and established the heavens. Arminian readers see wisdom woven into creation itself. The same wisdom offered to the son is the wisdom by which God made the world, so living wisely aligns with the order of creation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s wisdom displayed in the structure of creation. The world reflects divine understanding, not chaos. Both traditions share this conviction, seeing human wisdom as participation in the order God has built into creation.

Proverbs 3:20

20By his knowledge, the depths were broken up, and the skies drop down the dew.

Arminian

By God’s knowledge the depths broke open and the skies drop dew. Arminian readers see God’s ongoing care sustaining creation, from deep waters to gentle dew. Wisdom governs both the grand and the ordinary features of the world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s knowledge at work in shaping and sustaining the natural order. The imagery moves from creation to continuing provision. Both traditions see shared ground in God’s wise governance of the world he made.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
