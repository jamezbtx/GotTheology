Proverbs 27

Verses 23–27 · WEB

Proverbs 27:23–27 is a short, unified poem on stewardship: know the condition of your flocks and herds, because riches and even crowns do not last, while living resources renew with the seasons and provide clothing, land, and food for the whole household. Read as a unit, it commends patient, attentive care over trust in stored wealth. Arminian and Reformed readers find it almost entirely shared ground, seeing God’s provision through ordinary, diligent work.

Proverbs 27:23

23Know well the state of your flocks, and pay attention to your herds,

Arminian

Arminian readers see a call to diligent attention to one’s flocks and herds. Responsible stewardship requires knowing the condition of what has been entrusted to us.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see faithful stewardship of God-given resources. Both traditions see shared ground in this opening of the short poem: careful, attentive management is part of wise and godly living.

Proverbs 27:24

24for riches are not forever, nor does the crown endure to all generations.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the reason for diligence: wealth and even crowns do not last. Because riches are fleeting, careful stewardship of living resources is wiser than trusting accumulated wealth.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same reminder of the transience of earthly security. Both traditions read the verse as practical wisdom that also hints at the limits of all human possessions and power.

Proverbs 27:25

25The hay is removed, and the new growth appears, the grasses of the hills are gathered in.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the cycle of seasons: hay is removed, new growth appears, and grasses are gathered. Nature’s rhythm invites timely, attentive work.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the providential order of creation sustaining the farmer’s work. Both traditions see shared ground in a picture of seasonal provision met by human diligence.

Proverbs 27:26

26The lambs are for your clothing, and the goats are the price of a field.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the tangible benefits of careful husbandry: lambs provide clothing and goats can buy a field. Diligent stewardship yields lasting, practical provision.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same blessing of provision through ordinary labor. Both traditions read the verse as part of the poem’s portrait of sufficiency for those who tend what they have.

Proverbs 27:27

27There will be plenty of goats’ milk for your food, for your family’s food, and for the nourishment of your servant girls.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the poem concluding with abundant provision for the whole household, including servants. Faithful stewardship blesses everyone within its care.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s generous provision through faithful work, extending to family and servants alike. Both traditions see shared ground in this closing image of a well-kept household sustained by patient care.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
