Passage

Proverbs 18

Verses 1–12 · WEB

This unit contrasts the closed, self-absorbed fool with the person who finds refuge in the LORD. It warns about isolation, opinionated speech, partial judging, gossip and laziness, then sets two strong towers side by side: the LORD’s name, where the righteous are safe, and the rich man’s wealth, which only seems unscalable. Pride comes before destruction, humility before honor. Both traditions read this as shared wisdom.

Proverbs 18:1

1A man who isolates himself pursues selfishness, and defies all sound judgment.

Arminian

The person who cuts himself off pursues his own desires and rejects sound counsel. Wisdom grows in community.

Arminians see isolation as a chosen path away from correction.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the self-centered heart resisting the means God gives for growth, including counsel and fellowship (cf. Hebrews 10:24–25).

Shared ground: wisdom needs others.

Proverbs 18:2

2A fool has no delight in understanding, but only in revealing his own opinion.

Arminian

The fool does not want to understand, only to air his opinions. His mouth outruns his mind.

Arminians see a refusal to learn.

Reformed

Reformed readers see pride shutting out wisdom.

Both agree that listening must come before speaking (cf. v. 13).

Proverbs 18:3

3When wickedness comes, contempt also comes, and with shame comes disgrace.

Arminian

Wickedness brings contempt; shame brings disgrace. Sin carries social consequences.

Arminians and Reformed readers alike see sin’s natural fruit.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s moral order at work in the world.

Shared ground: sin dishonors.

Proverbs 18:4

4The words of a man’s mouth are like deep waters. The fountain of wisdom is like a flowing brook.

Arminian

Words can be deep waters; the source of wisdom is a flowing stream. The line contrasts or compares hidden depth with refreshing clarity; interpreters differ on how the two lines relate.

Arminians see the power of wise speech to refresh.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wisdom’s source ultimately in God.

Shared ground: wise words give life.

Proverbs 18:5

5To be partial to the faces of the wicked is not good, nor to deprive the innocent of justice.

Arminian

Favoring the wicked or denying justice to the innocent is wrong. This echoes 17:15.

Arminians see God’s concern for fair judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judges accountable to God.

Shared ground: partiality in judgment is evil.

Proverbs 18:6

6A fool’s lips come into strife, and his mouth invites beatings.

Arminian

A fool’s lips bring quarrels and invite blows. Careless speech has consequences.

Arminians see responsibility for words.

Reformed

Reformed readers see speech revealing the heart.

Shared ground: foolish talk brings trouble.

Proverbs 18:7

7A fool’s mouth is his destruction, and his lips are a snare to his soul.

Arminian

The fool’s own mouth destroys him; his lips trap him.

Arminians and Reformed readers alike see self-inflicted ruin.

Reformed

Reformed readers see folly turning back on the fool.

Shared ground: words can ensnare the speaker.

Proverbs 18:8

8The words of a gossip are like dainty morsels: they go down into a person’s innermost parts.

Arminian

Gossip tastes sweet and goes down deep (repeated in 26:22). Its appeal is its danger.

Arminians see the need to refuse gossip.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the corrupt appetite of the heart.

Shared ground: gossip is attractive and harmful.

Proverbs 18:9

9One who is slack in his work is brother to him who is a master of destruction.

Arminian

The lazy worker is brother to the destroyer. Neglect and destruction end in the same place.

Arminians see diligence as responsibility.

Reformed

Reformed readers see work as a calling from God.

Shared ground: slackness is destructive.

Proverbs 18:10

10The LORD’s name is a strong tower: the righteous run to him, and are safe.

Arminian

“The LORD’s name is a strong tower: the righteous run to him, and are safe.” God’s revealed character is a refuge for those who trust him.

Arminians see the active response of running to God in trust.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness to his people as their sure protection.

Shared ground: the LORD himself is the safe place.

Proverbs 18:11

11The rich man’s wealth is his strong city, like an unscalable wall in his own imagination.

Arminian

The rich trust their wealth as a strong city, but only “in his own imagination.” This deliberately contrasts with v. 10.

Arminians see misplaced trust as a choice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see idolatry of wealth (cf. 1 Timothy 6:17).

Shared ground: wealth is a false refuge.

Proverbs 18:12

12Before destruction the heart of man is proud, but before honor is humility.

Arminian

Pride precedes destruction; humility precedes honor (cf. 15:33; 16:18).

Arminians see humility as a chosen posture.

Reformed

Reformed readers see humility as a work of grace, and God exalting the humble (cf. James 4:6).

Shared ground: God opposes pride and honors humility.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
