Passage

Proverbs 18

Verses 13–24 · WEB

The closing unit turns to listening, judging and relationships: hear before answering, hear both sides, the lot that ends disputes, the hard-to-win offended brother, the power of the tongue, the favor of a good wife, and the friend who sticks closer than a brother. Both traditions read these as shared practical wisdom grounded in the fear of the LORD.

Proverbs 18:13

13He who answers before he hears, that is folly and shame to him.

Arminian

Answering before listening is folly and shame. Hearing comes first.

Arminians see the responsibility to listen carefully.

Reformed

Reformed readers see humility in listening (cf. James 1:19).

Shared ground: listen before you speak.

Proverbs 18:14

14A man’s spirit will sustain him in sickness, but a crushed spirit, who can bear?

Arminian

A strong spirit can carry someone through sickness, but a crushed spirit is almost unbearable.

Arminians see the importance of inner resilience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God sustaining the spirit and caring for the brokenhearted (Psalm 34:18).

Shared ground: inner strength matters, and a crushed spirit needs care.

Proverbs 18:15

15The heart of the discerning gets knowledge. The ear of the wise seeks knowledge.

Arminian

The discerning heart and the wise ear seek knowledge. Wisdom is always learning.

Arminians see seeking as active responsibility.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a heart God has made hungry for truth.

Shared ground: the wise keep learning.

Proverbs 18:16

16A man’s gift makes room for him, and brings him before great men.

Arminian

A gift opens doors and gets one an audience. This is observation; it differs from the bribe condemned in 17:23, though the line between gift and bribe needs care.

Arminians see the proverb describing how the world often works.

Reformed

Reformed readers see generosity used wisely, distinguished from corruption.

Shared ground: gifts have influence, which calls for discernment.

Proverbs 18:17

17He who pleads his cause first seems right— until another comes and questions him.

Arminian

The first speaker seems right until cross-examined. Hear both sides.

Arminians see fairness in judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see wisdom in testing claims (cf. Deuteronomy 19:15–18).

Shared ground: listen to both sides before judging.

Proverbs 18:18

18The lot settles disputes, and keeps strong ones apart.

Arminian

Casting lots settles disputes between powerful parties. Proverbs 16:33 says the decision of the lot is from the LORD.

Arminians see God’s providence working through an accepted means of settling disputes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereign providence over even apparently random events (16:33).

Shared ground: God can use ordinary means to bring peace.

Proverbs 18:19

19A brother offended is more difficult than a fortified city. Disputes are like the bars of a fortress.

Arminian

An offended brother is harder to win back than a fortified city. Disputes lock people in like bars.

Arminians see the need to avoid giving offense.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the hardness that sin produces in relationships.

Shared ground: broken relationships are hard to restore.

Proverbs 18:20

20A man’s stomach is filled with the fruit of his mouth. With the harvest of his lips he is satisfied.

Arminian

A person lives on the fruit of his words, whether good or bad.

Arminians see consequences of speech.

Reformed

Reformed readers see speech bearing fruit from the heart (cf. Matthew 12:37).

Shared ground: words have results.

Proverbs 18:21

21Death and life are in the power of the tongue; those who love it will eat its fruit.

Arminian

Death and life are in the tongue’s power; those who love to use it will eat its fruit. Speech can wound or heal.

Arminians see responsibility for every word.

Reformed

Reformed readers see speech as a gift to be governed by grace (cf. James 3).

Shared ground: words carry life and death.

Proverbs 18:22

22Whoever finds a wife finds a good thing, and obtains favor of the LORD.

Arminian

Finding a wife is finding a good thing, a sign of the LORD’s favor (cf. 19:14; 31:10).

Arminians and Reformed readers alike see marriage as God’s good gift.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s providence in marriage.

Shared ground: a good marriage is a blessing from the LORD.

Proverbs 18:23

23The poor plead for mercy, but the rich answer harshly.

Arminian

The poor plead; the rich answer harshly. Proverbs exposes how wealth can harden.

Arminians see the call to compassion.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s concern for the poor and judgment on harshness.

Shared ground: wealth must not harden the heart.

Proverbs 18:24

24A man of many companions may be ruined, but there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother.

Arminian

Many companions can bring ruin, but a true friend sticks closer than a brother. The first line is difficult in Hebrew; the main point is quality of friendship over quantity.

Arminians see the value of choosing faithful friends.

Reformed

Reformed readers often see Christ as the friend who sticks closer than a brother, though the proverb first describes human friendship.

Shared ground: one faithful friend outweighs many shallow ones.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
