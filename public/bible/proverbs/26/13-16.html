Passage

Proverbs 26

Verses 13–16 · WEB

A short cluster about the sluggard: he invents dangers to avoid going out, turns in bed like a door on its hinges, is too lazy to lift food to his mouth, and still thinks himself wiser than seven sensible people. Both traditions read this as practical wisdom about laziness and self-deception, with shared ground throughout.

Proverbs 26:13

13The sluggard says, “There is a lion in the road! A fierce lion roams the streets!”

Arminian

The sluggard claims a lion is in the road. Arminian readers see laziness disguised as caution (cf. 22:13).

Excuses can sound reasonable while hiding an unwillingness to act.

Reformed

Reformed readers also note 22:13. The sluggard’s imagination supplies reasons to avoid work.

Sloth often clothes itself in fear.

Proverbs 26:14

14As the door turns on its hinges, so does the sluggard on his bed.

Arminian

The sluggard turns on his bed like a door on hinges. Arminian readers note the humor: motion without progress.

The image recalls warnings about too much sleep (Prov 6:9–11).

Reformed

Reformed readers see laziness as a failure to use the time and gifts God gives (Prov 24:30–34).

The sluggard moves but goes nowhere.

Proverbs 26:15

15The sluggard buries his hand in the dish. He is too lazy to bring it back to his mouth.

Arminian

The sluggard buries his hand in the dish and won’t bring it to his mouth. Arminian readers note the absurdity of laziness that undermines even self-interest (cf. 19:24).

The image is exaggeration for effect.

Reformed

Reformed readers also see 19:24. Sloth can paralyze even simple, beneficial action.

The saying uses humor to rebuke.

Proverbs 26:16

16The sluggard is wiser in his own eyes than seven men who answer with discretion.

Arminian

The sluggard thinks himself wiser than seven discreet people. Arminian readers note how laziness and pride reinforce each other.

Seven suggests completeness: he thinks he knows better than everyone.

Reformed

Reformed readers link this to verse 12. Self-deception keeps the sluggard from correction.

Pride is the root that keeps sloth unrepentant.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
