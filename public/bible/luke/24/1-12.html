Passage

Luke 24

Verses 1–12 · WEB

At dawn on the first day the women find the stone rolled away and the body gone. Two men in dazzling clothing announce that Jesus is risen, as he had said. The apostles dismiss the report until Peter runs to see the tomb. Both traditions affirm the bodily resurrection; their emphases differ little here.

Luke 24:1

1But on the first day of the week, at early dawn, they and some others came to the tomb, bringing the spices which they had prepared.

Arminian

Early on the first day the women come with spices. Arminian readers note their love and devotion, expecting only to honor a corpse.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same devotion. The first day of the week becomes the day of resurrection, later the Lord’s Day for the church.

Luke 24:2

2They found the stone rolled away from the tomb.

Arminian

They find the stone rolled away. Arminian readers note the surprise: God has acted before anyone arrives.

Reformed

Reformed readers emphasize God’s initiative. The resurrection is God’s work, not the disciples’ achievement.

Luke 24:3

3They entered in, and didn’t find the Lord Jesus’ body.

Arminian

They enter and do not find the Lord Jesus’ body. Arminian readers note that Luke calls him “the Lord Jesus,” already pointing to his risen authority.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same title. Both affirm the empty tomb as a real, bodily fact.

Luke 24:4

4While they were greatly perplexed about this, behold, two men stood by them in dazzling clothing.

Arminian

Two men in dazzling clothing appear while the women are perplexed. Arminian readers see God meeting confusion with revelation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same. The two witnesses recall the two who appeared at the transfiguration (Luke 9:30), and Luke elsewhere calls such figures angels (Luke 24:23).

Luke 24:5

5Becoming terrified, they bowed their faces down to the earth. The men said to them, “Why do you seek the living among the dead?

Arminian

“Why do you seek the living among the dead?” Arminian readers find a gentle rebuke that redirects the women’s grief toward hope.

Reformed

Reformed readers find the same. Jesus is “the living one,” not merely revived but alive in a new and lasting way.

Luke 24:6

6He isn’t here, but is risen. Remember what he told you when he was still in Galilee,

Arminian

“He isn’t here, but is risen. Remember what he told you.” Arminian readers stress the call to remember Jesus’ words, since faith grows from his promises.

Reformed

Reformed readers stress that the resurrection fulfills what Jesus had foretold. Both see memory of Christ’s word as central to faith.

Luke 24:7

7saying that the Son of Man must be delivered up into the hands of sinful men and be crucified, and the third day rise again?”

Arminian

The Son of Man must be delivered, crucified, and rise on the third day. Arminian readers see the divine necessity of the plan of salvation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same “must” as reflecting God’s sovereign purpose. Both agree the cross and resurrection were not accidents but God’s intended way of salvation.

Key contrast Both read the “must” as divine purpose. Arminian readers typically ground it in God’s redemptive plan freely carried out through foreknown events; Reformed readers typically ground it in God’s eternal decree.

Luke 24:8

8They remembered his words,

Arminian

They remember his words. Arminian readers note that remembrance leads to understanding and response.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that understanding comes as God illumines what was already spoken. Both see Scripture and Jesus’ words as the way into faith.

Luke 24:9

9returned from the tomb, and told all these things to the eleven and to all the rest.

Arminian

They return and tell the eleven and the rest. Arminian readers honor the women as the first messengers of the resurrection.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise honor their witness. Both note that God chose women, whose testimony was often discounted, as the first heralds.

Luke 24:10

10Now they were Mary Magdalene, Joanna, and Mary the mother of James. The other women with them told these things to the apostles.

Arminian

Luke names Mary Magdalene, Joanna, and Mary the mother of James. Arminian readers note the named witnesses as a mark of historical concreteness.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same. Named witnesses invite checking and anchor the story in real people.

Luke 24:11

11These words seemed to them to be nonsense, and they didn’t believe them.

Arminian

The apostles think their words are nonsense. Arminian readers see honest unbelief, which Jesus will graciously meet.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the slowness of the human heart, even among apostles. Both note that the disciples did not invent a resurrection they expected.

Luke 24:12

12But Peter got up and ran to the tomb. Stooping and looking in, he saw the strips of linen lying by themselves, and he departed to his home, wondering what had happened.

Arminian

Peter runs to the tomb, sees the linen strips, and goes home wondering. The WEB includes this verse without a footnote, though a few manuscripts lack it. Arminian readers see Peter’s curiosity and hope beginning to stir.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the evidence of the grave cloths is real but does not yet produce full faith. Both see faith growing step by step through evidence and the risen Lord’s own appearance.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
