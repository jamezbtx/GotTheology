Passage

Luke 6

Verses 39–49 · WEB

Jesus closes with parables: the blind cannot lead the blind, disciples become like their teachers, and hypocrites must remove the beam from their own eye before correcting others. A tree is known by its fruit, and the mouth speaks from the heart. Calling Jesus Lord without obeying is empty; the one who hears and does is like a house built on rock that stands in the flood. Both traditions see real faith as obedient; they explain the link between heart, fruit, and perseverance differently.

Luke 6:39

39He spoke a parable to them. “Can the blind guide the blind? Won’t they both fall into a pit?

Arminian

Can the blind lead the blind? Arminian readers see warning about false guides. Followers need a teacher who sees clearly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see need for true teaching. Those who will teach others must first be taught by Jesus.

Luke 6:40

40A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone when he is fully trained will be like his teacher.

Arminian

A disciple becomes like his teacher. Arminian readers see formation. Disciples become like the one they follow, so the choice of teacher matters.

Reformed

Reformed readers see discipleship. Being fully trained means being shaped into Jesus’ likeness.

Luke 6:41

41Why do you see the speck of chaff that is in your brother’s eye, but don’t consider the beam that is in your own eye?

Arminian

Why see the speck in your brother’s eye but not the beam in your own? Arminian readers see Jesus using humor to expose blindness to one’s own faults.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a call to self-examination. Judging others while ignoring our own sin is self-deception.

Luke 6:42

42Or how can you tell your brother, ‘Brother, let me remove the speck of chaff that is in your eye,’ when you yourself don’t see the beam that is in your own eye? You hypocrite! First remove the beam from your own eye, and then you can see clearly to remove the speck of chaff that is in your brother’s eye.

Arminian

Remove the beam first. Arminian readers see humility. Correction is not forbidden, but it must begin with humility.

Reformed

Reformed readers see repentance before correction. Clear sight comes through honest repentance.

Luke 6:43

43“For there is no good tree that produces rotten fruit, nor again a rotten tree that produces good fruit.

Arminian

No good tree produces rotten fruit. Arminian readers see character shown by actions. Fruit reveals what kind of tree is producing it.

Reformed

Reformed readers see renewed nature producing good fruit. Good works flow from a changed nature, not the other way around.

Key contrast Both affirm that good fruit flows from a good heart. Arminian readers often stress cooperating with grace to bear fruit. Reformed readers often stress that God must first renew the heart.

Luke 6:44

44For each tree is known by its own fruit. For people don’t gather figs from thorns, nor do they gather grapes from a bramble bush.

Arminian

Each tree is known by its fruit; figs do not grow on thorns. Arminian readers see actions revealing hearts, and they call disciples to bear good fruit.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same principle: character is revealed over time in what a person produces, because fruit follows nature.

Luke 6:45

45The good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings out that which is good, and the evil man out of the evil treasure of his heart brings out that which is evil, for out of the abundance of the heart, his mouth speaks.

Arminian

Out of the heart the mouth speaks. Arminian readers see inner life shaping words. Words reveal what is stored in the heart.

Reformed

Reformed readers see heart renewal essential. Only a heart changed by grace produces lasting good.

Luke 6:46

46“Why do you call me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ and don’t do the things which I say?

Arminian

Why call me Lord and not obey? Arminian readers see obedience required. Calling Jesus Lord means doing what He says.

Reformed

Reformed readers see empty profession exposed. True faith confesses Jesus as Lord and shows it in obedience.

Luke 6:47

47Everyone who comes to me, and hears my words and does them, I will show you who he is like.

Arminian

Everyone who comes, hears, and does. Arminian readers see coming, hearing, and doing belonging together as a continuing pattern of discipleship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the marks of true disciples: they come to Jesus, hear Him, and obey.

Luke 6:48

48He is like a man building a house, who dug and went deep and laid a foundation on the rock. When a flood arose, the stream broke against that house, and could not shake it, because it was founded on the rock.

Arminian

Built on rock, the house stands. Arminian readers see perseverance through obedience. The house that stands is the one built deep on Jesus’ words.

Reformed

Reformed readers see security in Christ. The foundation is Christ’s word received and obeyed, which keeps the believer in the storm.

Luke 6:49

49But he who hears and doesn’t do, is like a man who built a house on the earth without a foundation, against which the stream broke, and immediately it fell; and the ruin of that house was great.”

Arminian

Built without foundation, the house falls. Arminian readers see danger of disobedience. Hearing without doing leaves a person without a foundation when trouble comes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see judgment on profession without reality. The great ruin shows how serious it is to hear Jesus and not obey.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
