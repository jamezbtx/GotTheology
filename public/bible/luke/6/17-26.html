Passage

Luke 6

Verses 17–26 · WEB

On a level place, before disciples and crowds from Judea, Jerusalem, Tyre, and Sidon, Jesus heals and then delivers blessings and woes. The poor, hungry, weeping, and persecuted are blessed; the rich, full, laughing, and praised receive warnings. Both traditions see the reversal of values in God’s kingdom; they differ little here.

Luke 6:17

17He came down with them and stood on a level place, with a crowd of his disciples and a great number of the people from all Judea and Jerusalem and the sea coast of Tyre and Sidon, who came to hear him and to be healed of their diseases,

Arminian

A great crowd gathers on a level place. Arminian readers see Jesus available to all. People come from Jewish and Gentile regions alike to hear and be healed.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Luke’s Sermon on the Plain parallels Matthew’s Sermon on the Mount. The crowd’s breadth hints at the gospel’s later reach to the nations.

Luke 6:18

18as well as those who were troubled by unclean spirits; and they were being healed.

Arminian

Those troubled by unclean spirits are healed. Arminian readers see deliverance. Freedom from evil spirits accompanies teaching.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus’ authority. Luke repeatedly shows Jesus releasing those under spiritual oppression.

Luke 6:19

19All the multitude sought to touch him, for power came out of him and healed them all.

Arminian

Power comes out and heals all. Arminian readers see grace for everyone. Everyone who came received help, a sign of how far His compassion reaches.

Reformed

Reformed readers see divine power. Luke again notes power going out from Jesus, compare 8:46.

Luke 6:20

20He lifted up his eyes to his disciples, and said: “Blessed are you who are poor, for God’s Kingdom is yours.

Arminian

Blessed are you poor. Arminian readers see God’s favor toward the poor. Luke records poor without Matthew’s in spirit, emphasizing real material need.

Reformed

Reformed readers see blessing to disciples who are poor, perhaps in both material and spiritual sense. The kingdom is given, not earned, to those who come to Jesus in need.

Luke 6:21

21Blessed are you who hunger now, for you will be filled. Blessed are you who weep now, for you will laugh.

Arminian

Blessed are you who hunger and weep. Arminian readers see future reversal. Present suffering is not the last word for Jesus’ followers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see eschatological hope. The promises echo Isaiah 61 and the hope of God’s coming reign.

Luke 6:22

22Blessed are you when men hate you, and when they exclude and mock you, and throw out your name as evil, for the Son of Man’s sake.

Arminian

Blessed when hated for the Son of Man’s sake. Arminian readers see faithful suffering. Disciples should expect rejection for following Jesus.

Reformed

Reformed readers see persecution as a mark of belonging. Suffering for the Son of Man unites disciples with Him.

Luke 6:23

23Rejoice in that day and leap for joy, for behold, your reward is great in heaven, for their fathers did the same thing to the prophets.

Arminian

Rejoice, for your reward is great. Arminian readers see reward for faithfulness. Joy in persecution rests on God’s future vindication.

Reformed

Reformed readers see reward as gracious. The prophets’ example shows that rejection is the common lot of God’s messengers.

Luke 6:24

24“But woe to you who are rich! For you have received your consolation.

Arminian

Woe to the rich. Arminian readers see warning. Wealth can become a substitute for God and leave nothing more to hope for.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the danger of wealth. Those whose comfort is found in riches have already received all they will get.

Luke 6:25

25Woe to you, you who are full now, for you will be hungry. Woe to you who laugh now, for you will mourn and weep.

Arminian

Woe to those who are full now and laugh now. Arminian readers see a warning of reversal: present ease can hide spiritual poverty.

Reformed

Reformed readers see coming judgment on complacency. Those satisfied with this age’s comforts will find themselves empty in the age to come.

Luke 6:26

26Woe, when men speak well of you, for their fathers did the same thing to the false prophets.

Arminian

Woe when all speak well of you. Arminian readers see danger of popularity. Approval from all may signal compromise with the truth.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a warning against seeking universal approval. False prophets were praised for telling people what they wanted to hear.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
