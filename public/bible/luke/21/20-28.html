Passage

Luke 21

Verses 20–28 · WEB

Jesus gives concrete warnings about Jerusalem’s siege and flight, then speaks of cosmic distress and the Son of Man in glory. Both traditions affirm divine judgment, compassion for the vulnerable, and hope in redemption. How the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70 relates to the later imagery and the future coming of Christ is debated within both traditions; these eschatological differences should not be confused with disagreements over election or perseverance.

Luke 21:20

20“But when you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then know that its desolation is at hand.

Arminian

The surrounding armies make Jerusalem’s impending desolation concrete. Arminian readers recognize the warning’s local setting without making their view of salvation determine every prophetic horizon.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise attend to Jerusalem and its siege. The relation between this warning, AD 70, and later coming imagery remains an eschatological question within both traditions.

Luke 21:21

21Then let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains. Let those who are in the middle of her depart. Let those who are in the country not enter therein.

Arminian

Jesus gives practical instructions to flee rather than treat danger as a test of daring. Arminian readers see responsible action as compatible with trust in God.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise affirm prudent response to divine warning. Confidence in providence does not require remaining in avoidable danger or entering the threatened city.

Luke 21:22

22For these are days of vengeance, that all things which are written may be fulfilled.

Arminian

Jesus describes judgment as fulfilling what is written. Arminian readers affirm God’s faithfulness and justice without treating every sufferer’s fate as an individual verdict on salvation.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see divine judgment rather than random history. This corporate catastrophe should not be flattened into a claim that every victim had an identical spiritual condition.

Luke 21:23

23Woe to those who are pregnant and to those who nurse infants in those days! For there will be great distress in the land and wrath to this people.

Arminian

Jesus voices grief for pregnant women and nursing mothers amid distress. Arminian readers hear compassion for vulnerability, not a command to meet suffering with contempt.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise recognize that announcing judgment does not cancel pity. The church should attend tenderly to those whose circumstances make flight and survival especially difficult.

Luke 21:24

24They will fall by the edge of the sword, and will be led captive into all the nations. Jerusalem will be trampled down by the Gentiles until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled.

Arminian

Death, captivity, and Gentile domination describe Jerusalem’s humiliation. Arminian readers should not convert this warning into hostility toward Jewish people or a settled calendar of restoration.

Reformed

Reformed readers share the need to reject contempt and speculative certainty. Interpretations of the Gentile times differ internally and are not determined by unconditional election or conditional security.

Luke 21:25

25“There will be signs in the sun, moon, and stars; and on the earth anxiety of nations, in perplexity for the roaring of the sea and the waves;

Arminian

Cosmic signs and distressed nations widen the discourse’s horizon. Arminian readers distinguish debates over prophetic imagery from the shared summons to trust Christ amid upheaval.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise differ over literal, symbolic, and historically focused interpretations. No single handling of these signs defines the Reformed account of salvation.

Luke 21:26

26men fainting for fear and for expectation of the things which are coming on the world, for the powers of the heavens will be shaken.

Arminian

The world’s terror contrasts with the disciples’ coming invitation to lift their heads. Arminian readers seek hope in Christ rather than spiritual motivation built on panic.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise affirm Christ’s rule when familiar securities are shaken. These images warrant sober hope, not confident identification of every alarming event as the final one.

Luke 21:27

27Then they will see the Son of Man coming in a cloud with power and great glory.

Arminian

The Son of Man appears in power and glory. Arminian readers affirm Jesus’ vindication while distinguishing arguments about AD 70 and the future return from their theology of grace.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise confess Christ’s victorious authority. How this language relates to Jerusalem’s fall and the final coming is disputed within both traditions, not settled by election doctrine.

Luke 21:28

28But when these things begin to happen, look up and lift up your heads, because your redemption is near.”

Arminian

Jesus calls his disciples to hope rather than share the surrounding terror. Arminian readers see redemption as the object of persevering trust in God’s promised deliverance.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise locate confidence in Christ’s promised redemption. Both traditions can lift their heads in hope without agreeing on every chronological relation among the preceding signs.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
