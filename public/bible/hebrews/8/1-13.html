Passage

Hebrews 8

Verses 1–13 · WEB

The writer states his main point: believers have a high priest seated at the right hand of God, serving in the true tabernacle set up by the Lord. The earthly priests serve a copy and shadow. Christ’s ministry is far better, because he mediates a better covenant enacted on better promises. The writer quotes Jeremiah 31 in full: God will make a new covenant, write his laws on hearts, be their God, and remember their sins no more, so that all will know him. The new covenant makes the first obsolete. The two traditions read the new covenant promises differently, especially the scope of “they shall all know me.”

Hebrews 8:1

1Now in the things which we are saying, the main point is this: we have such a high priest, who sat down on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens,

Arminian

We have such a high priest, seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven. Arminian readers see Christ’s exalted ministry as the hope of all who come to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the seated priest as the sign of finished atonement and effective intercession.

Hebrews 8:2

2a servant of the sanctuary and of the true tabernacle which the Lord pitched, not man.

Arminian

A minister in the holy places, in the true tent that the Lord set up. Arminian readers see Christ ministering in God’s very presence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the heavenly reality of which the earthly tabernacle was a copy.

Hebrews 8:3

3For every high priest is appointed to offer both gifts and sacrifices. Therefore it is necessary that this high priest also have something to offer.

Arminian

Every high priest offers gifts and sacrifices, so this priest must also have something to offer. Arminian readers see Christ’s self-offering as the true sacrifice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ offering himself, both priest and sacrifice.

Hebrews 8:4

4For if he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all, seeing there are priests who offer the gifts according to the law,

Arminian

If he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all. Arminian readers see Christ’s priesthood belonging to a higher order.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the heavenly character of Christ’s priesthood.

Hebrews 8:5

5who serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things, even as Moses was warned by God when he was about to make the tabernacle, for he said, “See, you shall make everything according to the pattern that was shown to you on the mountain.”

Arminian

They serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things. Arminian readers see the old system pointing beyond itself.

Reformed

Reformed readers see typology built into the old covenant by God’s design.

Hebrews 8:6

6But now he has obtained a more excellent ministry, by as much as he is also the mediator of a better covenant, which on better promises has been given as law.

Arminian

Christ’s ministry is more excellent, as the covenant he mediates is better, enacted on better promises. Arminian readers see better promises of grace offered to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see better promises that God himself guarantees to fulfill.

Hebrews 8:7

7For if that first covenant had been faultless, then no place would have been sought for a second.

Arminian

If the first covenant had been faultless, there would have been no occasion for a second. Arminian readers see the old covenant as preparatory.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the fault lying with the people, as verse 8 says, not with God’s law.

Hebrews 8:8

8For finding fault with them, he said, “Behold, the days are coming”, says the Lord, “that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah;

Arminian

God finds fault with them: “The days are coming when I will establish a new covenant.” Arminian readers see God’s plan to deal with human failure through a better covenant.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God promising to do sovereignly what the people could not do.

Hebrews 8:9

9not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day that I took them by the hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt; for they didn’t continue in my covenant, and I disregarded them,” says the Lord.

Arminian

Not like the covenant made with their fathers, because they did not continue in it. Arminian readers see that the old covenant people could break covenant, which shows covenant faithfulness matters.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the contrast: the old covenant could be broken, but the new covenant will not be, because God guarantees it.

Hebrews 8:10

10“For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days,” says the Lord: “I will put my laws into their mind; I will also write them on their heart. I will be their God, and they will be my people.

Arminian

I will put my laws into their minds and write them on their hearts, and I will be their God. Arminian readers see the Spirit’s inward renewal enabling obedience, received by faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God unilaterally transforming hearts. The new covenant promises regeneration, not just an offer.

Key contrast Both see the new covenant writing God’s law on hearts. Arminian readers see this renewal received and continued by faith. Reformed readers see God sovereignly accomplishing it in all who belong to the new covenant.

Hebrews 8:11

11They will not teach every man his fellow citizen and every man his brother, saying, ‘Know the Lord,’ for all will know me, from their least to their greatest.

Arminian

They shall all know me, from the least to the greatest. Arminian readers see personal knowledge of God opened to all in the new covenant.

Reformed

Reformed readers see every true member of the new covenant knowing God, which some take as evidence that its members are the regenerate.

Key contrast Both see a new intimacy with God. Arminian readers see personal knowledge of God available to all. Reformed readers see all genuine new covenant members actually knowing God, which shapes their view of who belongs to it.

Hebrews 8:12

12For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness. I will remember their sins and lawless deeds no more.”

Arminian

I will be merciful toward their iniquities, and I will remember their sins no more. Arminian readers see full forgiveness for all who enter the covenant by faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see complete forgiveness secured for all covenant members by Christ’s sacrifice.

Hebrews 8:13

13In that he says, “A new covenant”, he has made the first obsolete. But that which is becoming obsolete and grows aged is near to vanishing away.

Arminian

In speaking of a new covenant, he makes the first obsolete. Arminian readers see the old order passing away.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the old administration giving way to the new, within one covenant of grace.
