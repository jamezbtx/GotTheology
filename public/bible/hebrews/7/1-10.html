Passage

Hebrews 7

Verses 1–10 · WEB

The writer explains the mysterious Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, who met Abraham, blessed him, and received a tenth of the spoils. His name means king of righteousness, and his title means king of peace. Scripture records no genealogy for him, which makes him a fitting picture of the Son, who has a priesthood without end. Because the lesser is blessed by the greater, and Levi in a sense paid tithes through Abraham, Melchizedek’s priesthood is superior to Levi’s. The passage is common ground for both traditions.

Hebrews 7:1

1For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of God Most High, who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him,

Arminian

Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, met Abraham and blessed him. Arminian readers see God working through a priest outside Abraham’s line, which shows his concern for the nations.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Melchizedek as a type of Christ, uniting the offices of king and priest.

Hebrews 7:2

2to whom also Abraham divided a tenth part of all (being first, by interpretation, “king of righteousness”, and then also “king of Salem”, which means “king of peace”,

Arminian

Abraham gave him a tenth; his name means king of righteousness, and he is king of peace. Arminian readers see righteousness and peace joined in Christ for all who come to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see righteousness as the ground of peace, which Christ secures through his work (Romans 5:1).

Hebrews 7:3

3without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, but made like the Son of God), remains a priest continually.

Arminian

Without father or mother or genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, resembling the Son of God, he remains a priest forever. Arminian readers see Scripture’s silence about his origins used as a picture of Christ’s eternal priesthood.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise read the verse typologically. A few have suggested Melchizedek was a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, though most do not.

Hebrews 7:4

4Now consider how great this man was, to whom even Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth out of the best plunder.

Arminian

See how great this man was, to whom Abraham gave a tenth. Arminian readers see the honor Abraham showed him as pointing to Christ’s greatness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the patriarch acknowledging a greater priest, which foreshadows the superiority of Christ.

Hebrews 7:5

5They indeed of the sons of Levi who receive the priest’s office have a commandment to take tithes from the people according to the law, that is, of their brothers, though these have come out of the body of Abraham,

Arminian

The Levitical priests receive tithes from their brothers under the law. Arminian readers see the old priesthood as limited to Israel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Levitical order as a temporary administration within God’s covenant.

Hebrews 7:6

6but he whose genealogy is not counted from them has accepted tithes from Abraham, and has blessed him who has the promises.

Arminian

Melchizedek, not of their descent, received tithes from Abraham and blessed him who had the promises. Arminian readers see God’s blessing given beyond Israel’s formal structures.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the bearer of the promise blessed by a greater priest, which shows the superiority of Christ’s order.

Hebrews 7:7

7But without any dispute the lesser is blessed by the greater.

Arminian

The lesser is blessed by the greater. Both traditions see the principle establishing Melchizedek’s superiority. Arminian readers apply it to Christ’s superiority over every earthly mediator.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ, the greater priest, blessing his people.

Hebrews 7:8

8Here people who die receive tithes, but there one receives tithes of whom it is testified that he lives.

Arminian

Mortal men receive tithes, but there it is testified that he lives. Arminian readers see a living priesthood that never ends.

Reformed

Reformed readers see in this an image of Christ’s endless life, which makes his priesthood permanent.

Hebrews 7:9

9We can say that through Abraham even Levi, who receives tithes, has paid tithes,

Arminian

Levi, who receives tithes, paid tithes through Abraham. Arminian readers see the old priesthood honoring the greater one.

Reformed

Reformed readers see corporate representation: Levi acting in Abraham. Some connect this principle with federal headship in Adam and Christ.

Hebrews 7:10

10for he was yet in the body of his father when Melchizedek met him.

Arminian

He was still in the loins of his ancestor when Melchizedek met him. Arminian readers see the writer’s argument from the unity of the family line.

Reformed

Reformed readers see support for the idea that one person can act for those who come after, as in the doctrine of representation.
