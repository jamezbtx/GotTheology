Passage

Hebrews 7

Verses 20–28 · WEB

Jesus’ priesthood was confirmed with God’s oath, which makes him the guarantor of a better covenant. The old priests were many because death prevented them from continuing, but Jesus holds his priesthood permanently because he lives forever. So he is able to save completely those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to intercede for them. He is holy, innocent, and exalted, and he does not need to offer daily sacrifices, because he offered himself once for all. Verse 25 is treasured by both traditions as a promise of Christ’s saving power.

Hebrews 7:20

20Inasmuch as he was not made priest without the taking of an oath

Arminian

Jesus was not made priest without an oath. Arminian readers see the oath as God’s pledge that this priesthood will not fail.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the oath as marking the new covenant’s superiority and permanence.

Hebrews 7:21

21(for they indeed have been made priests without an oath), but he with an oath by him that says of him, “The Lord swore and will not change his mind, ‘You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek.’”

Arminian

“The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind: you are a priest forever.” Arminian readers see God’s unchanging commitment to Christ’s priesthood.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the covenant of redemption between Father and Son sealed by this oath.

Hebrews 7:22

22By so much, Jesus has become the guarantee of a better covenant.

Arminian

Jesus has become the guarantor of a better covenant. Arminian readers see Christ guaranteeing the covenant’s promises to all who enter by faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as surety, who has taken responsibility to fulfill the covenant for his people.

Hebrews 7:23

23Many, indeed, have been made priests, because they are hindered from continuing by death.

Arminian

The former priests were many, because death prevented them from continuing. Arminian readers see the limits of human priests.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the impermanence of the old covenant’s mediators.

Hebrews 7:24

24But he, because he lives forever, has his priesthood unchangeable.

Arminian

He holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever. Arminian readers see an unbroken priesthood that always supports believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s unchanging priesthood as the ground of his people’s security.

Hebrews 7:25

25Therefore he is also able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, seeing that he lives forever to make intercession for them.

Arminian

He is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to intercede for them. Arminian readers see complete salvation offered to all who keep drawing near. Wesley read “to the uttermost” as full salvation from sin.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s intercession as always effective for those he represents. Since he always lives to intercede, none of them can be lost.

Key contrast Both find great assurance here. Arminian readers stress salvation for those who keep drawing near, and Wesleyans read “to the uttermost” as full salvation from sin. Reformed readers stress that Christ’s unfailing intercession guarantees the final salvation of all he represents.

Hebrews 7:26

26For such a high priest was fitting for us: holy, guiltless, undefiled, separated from sinners, and made higher than the heavens;

Arminian

It was fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners. Arminian readers see Christ’s sinlessness qualifying him to save.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s perfect righteousness as essential, both for his sacrifice and for the righteousness credited to believers.

Hebrews 7:27

27who doesn’t need, like those high priests, to offer up sacrifices daily, first for his own sins, and then for the sins of the people. For he did this once for all, when he offered up himself.

Arminian

He has no need to offer daily sacrifices; he did this once for all when he offered himself. Arminian readers see the completeness of Christ’s work.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the once-for-all sacrifice as final and unrepeatable.

Hebrews 7:28

28For the law appoints men as high priests who have weakness, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son forever who has been perfected.

Arminian

The law appoints weak men as high priests, but the word of the oath appoints a Son made perfect forever. Arminian readers see the Son as the perfect high priest for all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son perfected for his office, so that his priestly work is complete and eternal.
