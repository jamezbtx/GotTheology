Passage

Hebrews 7

Verses 11–19 · WEB

If perfection could have come through the Levitical priesthood, there would have been no need for another priest after the order of Melchizedek. A change of priesthood requires a change of law. Jesus came from Judah, not Levi, and became a priest by the power of an indestructible life, not by a legal requirement about descent. The former commandment is set aside because of its weakness and uselessness, since the law made nothing perfect, and a better hope is introduced, through which we draw near to God. Both traditions agree on the substance. They differ on how far the law is changed.

Hebrews 7:11

11Now if perfection was through the Levitical priesthood (for under it the people have received the law), what further need was there for another priest to arise after the order of Melchizedek, and not be called after the order of Aaron?

Arminian

If perfection had been attainable through the Levitical priesthood, why would another priest be needed? Arminian readers see the old covenant as unable to bring people fully to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Levitical priesthood as unable to perfect, pointing beyond itself to Christ.

Hebrews 7:12

12For the priesthood being changed, there is of necessity a change made also in the law.

Arminian

When the priesthood changes, there is necessarily a change in the law as well. Arminian readers see the ceremonial system replaced by the new covenant.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the ceremonial law fulfilled and set aside, while the moral law continues as a rule of life.

Key contrast Both see the law changed along with the priesthood. Many Arminian and Reformed readers alike distinguish ceremonial from moral law. Reformed readers in particular insist that the moral law continues, as in the Westminster Confession’s treatment of the law.

Hebrews 7:13

13For he of whom these things are said belongs to another tribe, from which no one has officiated at the altar.

Arminian

The one these things are spoken of belonged to another tribe, from which no one served at the altar. Arminian readers see God bringing in something genuinely new.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God fulfilling his promise to David through a priest-king from Judah.

Hebrews 7:14

14For it is evident that our Lord has sprung out of Judah, about which tribe Moses spoke nothing concerning priesthood.

Arminian

Our Lord was descended from Judah. Arminian readers see Christ fulfilling the promises to Judah.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s royal descent joined with his priestly office, fulfilling Psalm 110.

Hebrews 7:15

15This is yet more abundantly evident, if after the likeness of Melchizedek there arises another priest,

Arminian

This becomes clearer when another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek. Arminian readers see Christ as the priest promised in Scripture.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the typology of Melchizedek fulfilled in Christ.

Hebrews 7:16

16who has been made, not after the law of a fleshly commandment, but after the power of an endless life;

Arminian

He became a priest not by a legal requirement about bodily descent but by the power of an indestructible life. Arminian readers see Christ’s endless life as the source of his saving power.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the resurrection life of Christ as the guarantee of his effective priesthood.

Hebrews 7:17

17for it is testified, “You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek.”

Arminian

“You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek.” Arminian readers see an eternal priesthood open to all who come through Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an eternal priesthood that secures his people.

Hebrews 7:18

18For there is an annulling of a foregoing commandment because of its weakness and uselessness

Arminian

The former commandment is set aside because of its weakness and uselessness. Arminian readers see the old system’s inability to give life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the weakness lying in sinful human flesh, not in the law itself (Romans 8:3).

Hebrews 7:19

19(for the law made nothing perfect), and a bringing in of a better hope, through which we draw near to God.

Arminian

The law made nothing perfect, but a better hope is introduced, through which we draw near to God. Arminian readers see the better hope as an open invitation to approach God through Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the better hope as the new covenant’s sure promise, grounded in Christ’s work.
