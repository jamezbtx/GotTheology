Passage

Hebrews 2

Verses 1–4 · WEB

This is the first of the warning passages in Hebrews. Having shown that the Son is greater than the angels, the writer draws the practical conclusion: listeners must pay closer attention to what they have heard, so they do not drift away. If the message delivered through angels at Sinai carried just penalties, there will be no escape for those who neglect so great a salvation, first spoken by the Lord and confirmed by those who heard him and by God’s signs. Both traditions take the warning seriously. They differ on whether drifting away could end in final loss for a true believer.

Hebrews 2:1

1Therefore we ought to pay greater attention to the things that were heard, lest perhaps we drift away.

Arminian

We must pay closer attention to what we have heard, lest we drift away. Arminian readers see drifting as a real danger for believers, who can slowly move away from Christ through neglect.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the warning as one of God’s means to keep his people attentive. Those who truly belong to Christ heed such warnings.

Key contrast Both see drifting as a serious danger. Arminian readers believe a true believer could drift to final loss. Reformed readers believe God uses the warning to keep his people from doing so, while those who drift away entirely show they never truly belonged.

Hebrews 2:2

2For if the word spoken through angels proved steadfast, and every transgression and disobedience received a just penalty,

Arminian

The message spoken through angels was reliable, and every transgression received a just penalty. Arminian readers see God’s consistent justice under both covenants.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the law given at Sinai, mediated by angels, as carrying real sanctions. That sets up the argument from lesser to greater.

Hebrews 2:3

3how will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation—which at the first having been spoken through the Lord, was confirmed to us by those who heard,

Arminian

How will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation? It was declared by the Lord and confirmed by those who heard. Arminian readers draw from this that neglecting salvation is a real path to judgment, open to anyone who has heard.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the greatness of the salvation as heightening the guilt of neglecting it. The Lord’s own preaching makes the message weightier than the law.

Hebrews 2:4

4God also testifying with them, both by signs and wonders, by various works of power, and by gifts of the Holy Spirit, according to his own will?

Arminian

God bore witness by signs, wonders, miracles, and gifts of the Spirit distributed according to his will. Arminian readers see God confirming the gospel so that hearers can respond with confidence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit distributing gifts according to his will, not human choice. Many Reformed readers see these signs as tied especially to the apostolic founding of the church.
