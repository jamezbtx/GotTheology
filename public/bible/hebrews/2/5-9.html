Passage

Hebrews 2

Verses 5–9 · WEB

The writer turns to Psalm 8, which marvels that God made humanity a little lower than the angels and put all things under its feet. We do not yet see everything subject to humanity, but we do see Jesus, made lower than the angels for a little while and now crowned with glory because he suffered death, so that by God’s grace he might taste death for everyone. Verse 9 is a central text in the debate over the extent of the atonement.

Hebrews 2:5

5For he didn’t subject the world to come, of which we speak, to angels.

Arminian

God did not subject the world to come to angels. Arminian readers see God’s purpose for humanity, restored in Christ, as greater than the angels’ role.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the coming world under the rule of Christ and his people, which is God’s design for redeemed humanity.

Hebrews 2:6

6But one has somewhere testified, saying, “What is man, that you think of him? Or the son of man, that you care for him?

Arminian

“What is man, that you think of him?” Arminian readers see God’s care for all humanity, which the incarnation fulfills.

Reformed

Reformed readers see humanity’s original dignity, lost in Adam and restored in Christ, the second Adam.

Hebrews 2:7

7You made him a little lower than the angels. You crowned him with glory and honor.

Arminian

You made him a little lower than the angels and crowned him with glory and honor. Arminian readers see God’s design for human beings as image-bearers, renewed in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Psalm 8 fulfilled first in Christ, who achieves what Adam failed to achieve.

Hebrews 2:8

8You have put all things in subjection under his feet.” For in that he subjected all things to him, he left nothing that is not subject to him. But now we don’t yet see all things subjected to him.

Arminian

You put all things under his feet. We do not yet see all things subject to him. Arminian readers see the tension between God’s purpose and present reality, resolved in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the already and not yet. Christ’s reign is real but not yet fully revealed.

Hebrews 2:9

9But we see him who has been made a little lower than the angels, Jesus, because of the suffering of death crowned with glory and honor, that by the grace of God he should taste of death for everyone.

Arminian

We see Jesus, crowned with glory because he suffered death, so that by God’s grace he might taste death for everyone. Arminian readers see “for everyone” as plain evidence that Christ died for every person without exception. Wesley used texts like this against limited atonement.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the context speaks of the “many sons” brought to glory (2:10) and the “children” God gave Christ (2:13). They read “everyone” as all of Christ’s people, or as every kind of person, while affirming the sufficiency of his death for all.

Key contrast Both affirm that Christ tasted death by God’s grace. Arminian readers read “everyone” as every person without exception. Reformed readers read it in light of the “many sons” and “children” of the following verses, as all whom Christ came to save.
