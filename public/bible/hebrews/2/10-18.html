Passage

Hebrews 2

Verses 10–18 · WEB

The writer explains why the Son had to share in human nature and suffering. It was fitting for God to make the founder of salvation perfect through suffering, as he brings many sons to glory. Christ is not ashamed to call them brothers. He shared flesh and blood so that through death he might destroy the devil and free those enslaved by fear of death. He became like his brothers in every respect so that he could be a merciful and faithful high priest, making atonement for the people’s sins, and able to help those who are tempted. Both traditions treasure the passage. They read its references to “children” and “the people” differently.

Hebrews 2:10

10For it became him, for whom are all things and through whom are all things, in bringing many children to glory, to make the author of their salvation perfect through sufferings.

Arminian

It was fitting that God, in bringing many sons to glory, should make the founder of their salvation perfect through suffering. Arminian readers see the many sons as all who come to God through faith in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the “many sons” as the elect whom God has determined to bring to glory. Christ was perfected in the sense of being fully qualified as Savior through suffering.

Hebrews 2:11

11For both he who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are all from one, for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brothers,

Arminian

He who sanctifies and those who are sanctified all have one source, so he is not ashamed to call them brothers. Arminian readers see Christ’s solidarity with humanity and with believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the union between Christ and his people, whom he sanctifies by his blood.

Hebrews 2:12

12saying, “I will declare your name to my brothers. Among the congregation I will sing your praise.”

Arminian

“I will tell of your name to my brothers.” Arminian readers see Christ leading his people in worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as the leader of the worship of the redeemed, fulfilling Psalm 22.

Hebrews 2:13

13Again, “I will put my trust in him.” Again, “Behold, here I am with the children whom God has given me.”

Arminian

“Here I am, and the children God has given me.” Arminian readers often read “the children God has given” as those who come to Christ in faith, given to him as they believe.

Reformed

Reformed readers connect this with John 6:37 and 17:6. They see the children as a definite people given to Christ by the Father before time.

Key contrast Both see believers as children given to Christ. Arminian readers understand them as those who come in faith. Reformed readers understand them as a people given to Christ by the Father in eternal election.

Hebrews 2:14

14Since then the children have shared in flesh and blood, he also himself in the same way partook of the same, that through death he might bring to nothing him who had the power of death, that is, the devil,

Arminian

Since the children share in flesh and blood, he also shared in them, so that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death. Arminian readers see Christ’s victory over the devil opening freedom to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ taking on human nature specifically for the children. His death defeats Satan on their behalf.

Hebrews 2:15

15and might deliver all of them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.

Arminian

He frees those who all their lives were enslaved by fear of death. Arminian readers see freedom offered to everyone who trusts Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see actual deliverance from bondage for those Christ came to save.

Hebrews 2:16

16For most certainly, he doesn’t give help to angels, but he gives help to the offspring of Abraham.

Arminian

He helps not angels but the offspring of Abraham. Arminian readers see the offspring of Abraham as all who share Abraham’s faith (Galatians 3:7).

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s saving help given to Abraham’s spiritual offspring, God’s covenant people.

Hebrews 2:17

17Therefore he was obligated in all things to be made like his brothers, that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make atonement for the sins of the people.

Arminian

He had to become like his brothers in every respect, to be a merciful and faithful high priest and make propitiation for the sins of the people. Arminian readers see propitiation made for sins in a way that makes salvation available to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see propitiation made for “the people,” meaning those Christ represents. They read this as actual satisfaction of God’s wrath on their behalf.

Hebrews 2:18

18For in that he himself has suffered being tempted, he is able to help those who are tempted.

Arminian

Because he suffered when tempted, he is able to help those who are tempted. Arminian readers see Christ’s help as essential to persevering through trials.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s sympathy and help as one way he preserves his people.
