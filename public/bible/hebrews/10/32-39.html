Passage

Hebrews 10

Verses 32–39 · WEB

The writer balances the warning with encouragement. He reminds the readers of their early days, when they endured suffering and public shame, showed compassion to prisoners, and joyfully accepted the loss of property, knowing they had a better possession. So they must not throw away their confidence but endure, because the coming one will not delay. The righteous will live by faith, and God has no pleasure in anyone who shrinks back. The writer concludes that “we” are not of those who shrink back to destruction but of those who believe and preserve their souls. Both traditions read the closing verse as confidence. They differ on whether it is a certainty or an expectation.

Hebrews 10:32

32But remember the former days, in which, after you were enlightened, you endured a great struggle with sufferings:

Arminian

Remember the former days, when after being enlightened you endured a hard struggle. Arminian readers see past faithfulness as encouragement to continue, while recognizing it does not guarantee the future.

Reformed

Reformed readers see past endurance as evidence of genuine grace, which gives hope for the future.

Hebrews 10:33

33partly, being exposed to both reproaches and oppressions, and partly, becoming partakers with those who were treated so.

Arminian

They were publicly exposed to reproach and stood with others who suffered. Arminian readers see real costly faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the fruit of saving faith in suffering and solidarity.

Hebrews 10:34

34For you both had compassion on me in my chains and joyfully accepted the plundering of your possessions, knowing that you have for yourselves a better possession and an enduring one in the heavens.

Arminian

They had compassion on prisoners and joyfully accepted the plundering of their property, knowing they had a better and lasting possession. Arminian readers see the joy of faith that values heavenly reward.

Reformed

Reformed readers see hope in an inheritance that God secures for his people.

Hebrews 10:35

35Therefore don’t throw away your boldness, which has a great reward.

Arminian

Do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. Arminian readers see confidence as something believers can lose, which calls for vigilance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an exhortation that God uses to keep believers trusting.

Hebrews 10:36

36For you need endurance so that, having done the will of God, you may receive the promise.

Arminian

You need endurance, so that after doing God’s will you may receive what is promised. Arminian readers see endurance as necessary for receiving the promise.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that endurance is necessary, and see God working it in his people.

Hebrews 10:37

37“In a very little while, he who comes will come and will not wait.

Arminian

In a little while, the coming one will come and will not delay. Both traditions see the hope of Christ’s return as a motive for endurance. Arminian readers stress readiness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the certainty of Christ’s coming as encouragement to persevere.

Hebrews 10:38

38But the righteous one will live by faith. If he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him.”

Arminian

My righteous one will live by faith; if he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him. Arminian readers note that the same “righteous one” is warned against shrinking back, which suggests that a righteous person could do so.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the warning addressed to the righteous as a means of keeping them in faith. True faith perseveres.

Key contrast Both read the verse as joining faith and endurance. Arminian readers see the righteous one as capable of shrinking back. Reformed readers see the warning as God’s means of ensuring the righteous will continue in faith.

Hebrews 10:39

39But we are not of those who shrink back to destruction, but of those who have faith to the saving of the soul.

Arminian

We are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who believe and preserve their souls. Arminian readers see pastoral confidence that the readers will continue, without denying the real possibility of falling.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the writer’s assurance that true believers will persevere, since genuine faith leads to final salvation.
