Passage

Hebrews 10

Verses 32–39 · ESV · Voices

Paired with the warning: recall the former days after enlightenment—endurance in suffering, compassion, joy under loss—then do not throw away confidence; you need endurance to receive the promise. Yet a little while; the righteous one shall live by faith; we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls.

Hebrews 10:32

32But recall the former days when, after you were enlightened, you endured a hard struggle with sufferings,

Arminian

But recall the former days when, after you were enlightened, you endured a hard struggle with sufferings. Wesley: enlightened with the knowledge of God and of his truth.

Paired with the warning: memory of real grace under fire. Both camps urge endurance after 10:26–31. Arminian pastoral: you once stood; do not now draw back. Enlightenment was real; endurance must continue.

Reformed

Calvin: in order to stimulate them, he reminds them of evidences of piety already manifested. Remembrance of past warfare is useful not as a pretext for sloth but to render us more active in finishing the course. Enlightened: through God's favor they believed when light from above shone upon them.

Reformed pastoral: recall as fuel for perseverance. Early courage under Christ makes present fainting more shameful. The warning drove fear; this drives hope.

Hebrews 10:33

33sometimes being publicly exposed to reproach and affliction, and sometimes being partners with those so treated.

Arminian

Publicly exposed to reproach and affliction; partners with those so treated. Solidarity in shame was part of their enlightenment's fruit.

Both camps: suffering with and for the brethren. Encouragement after the warning includes remembering shared reproach—not neglecting to meet, but standing together again.

Reformed

Calvin: made gazingstocks by reproaches and afflictions—exposed as on a public theater. They became companions of the godly in persecutions; whatever one suffers, the rest ought to transfer to themselves, or they separate from Christ himself.

Reformed accent: partnership in affliction is communion with Christ and his body. Do not forget how you once shared the shame.

Hebrews 10:34

34For you had compassion on those in prison, and you joyfully accepted the plundering of your property, since you knew that you yourselves had a better possession and an abiding one.

Arminian

Compassion on prisoners; joyfully accepted plunder, knowing a better and abiding possession. Wesley: ye sympathized with suffering brethren and received joyfully the loss of your own goods.

Arminian pastoral: joy under loss sprang from knowing heaven's substance. That same knowledge must now hold them after the fearful warning. Both camps: better possession, abiding—live accordingly.

Reformed

Calvin: poverty caused grief as men, yet sorrow did not prevent the joy of which the Apostle speaks. Looking at the heavenly recompense, they forgot the grief of present calamity. Knowing that you have in heaven a better and enduring substance—without persuasion that the inheritance belongs to you, knowledge is cold and useless.

Reformed comfort: joy accepted plunder because faith saw the abiding possession. Meditate often on that felicity; then poverty and shame cannot overwhelm.

Hebrews 10:35

35Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward.

Arminian

Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. Wesley: cast not away this your confidence—your faith and hope; which none can deprive you of but yourselves.

Arminian accent: "none but yourselves"—confidence can be thrown away. After the warning, the charge is personal and urgent. Both camps: great reward; do not discard what you held in the former days.

Reformed

Calvin: what especially makes us strong to persevere is retaining confidence; when that is lost, we lose the recompense. Confidence is the foundation of a godly life. Mentioning reward diminishes nothing from gratuitous salvation; the faithful rest on mercy alone even as their labor is not vain in the Lord.

Reformed accent: do not throw away what God uses to carry you to the end. Reward and grace are not rivals in Scripture's grammar.

Hebrews 10:36

36For you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised.

Arminian

You have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised. Wesley: the promise—perfect love; eternal life.

Both camps urge endurance after the warning. Arminian pastoral: need of endurance means the race is not finished at enlightenment; doing the will and receiving the promise require continuing faith.

Reformed

Calvin: patience is necessary not only to endure to the end but because Satan has innumerable arts; without extraordinary patience we would be broken before half the course. The inheritance is certain, but life is a race; Christians will never advance without fainting unless sustained by patience.

Reformed pastoral: need of endurance is how the certain promise is pursued. Doing God's will and receiving the promise belong together under patience.

Hebrews 10:37

37For, "Yet a little while, and the coming one will come and will not delay;

Arminian

Yet a little while, and the coming one will come and will not delay. Wesley: he that cometh—to reward every man according to his works.

Both camps: short wait relative to eternity. The coming one anchors endurance after fear. Do not shrink in the little while that remains.

Reformed

Calvin: that enduring may not be grievous, he reminds them the time will not be long. Nothing sustains faint minds like hope of a speedy termination. He who comes will come and will not tarry—God comes to our aid as promised, in due time, not later than he ought.

Reformed comfort: Habakkuk's word applied—the Lord will not delay beyond what is expedient. Wait without inventing another timeline of despair.

Hebrews 10:38

38but my righteous one shall live by faith, and if he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him."

Arminian

My righteous one shall live by faith; if he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him. Wesley: the just—the justified person—shall live in God's favour by faith as long as he retains that gift; if he draw back—make shipwreck of his faith—I abhor him; I cast him off.

Arminian reading: living by faith can cease by drawing back; God's pleasure is not promised to the shrinker. Fair and serious—paired with the warning. Both camps still urge: live by faith now.

Reformed

Calvin: patience is born of faith; we shall never carry on contests unless sustained by faith. All counted just before God live by faith. Drawing back is contrary to faith; pride and self-security drive away fear of God. My soul shall not delight in him—the character of impiety over against faith that draws a man unto submission.

Reformed reading: the righteous live by faith; shrinking marks the path of perdition. The warning stands; God keeps his righteous ones in the faith by which they live.

Key contrast Both confess that the righteous one lives by faith and that shrinking back forfeits God's pleasure—urgent after 10:26–31. Arminians typically hear that a justified person may make shipwreck of faith and be cast off. Reformed readers typically hear that true righteousness lives by persevering faith, while shrinking back reveals the path unto perdition from which God preserves his own through such warnings.

Hebrews 10:39

39But we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls.

Arminian

But we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls. Wesley: not of them who draw back to perdition, but of them that believe to the end, so as to attain eternal life.

Arminian pastoral: a confident pastoral identification that summons them to keep believing to the end. After the fearful warning, both camps say: endure; do not shrink; preserve your souls by faith. No culture war—only holy fear and holy hope together.

Reformed

Calvin: we are not of them which draw back unto perdition, but of them that believe to the saving of the soul. We have been called that we might not draw back. Faith and drawing back are set in opposition; preservation of the soul over against perdition. We who have the light of the Gospel should advance in obedience and draw nearer to God—this is the real preservation of the soul.

Reformed pastoral: "we are not of those" is assurance joined to calling. Perseverance of the saints is not laxity; it is faith that endures after the warning, preserves the soul, and refuses to shrink back to destruction.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on recalling former endurance, not casting away confidence, living by faith, and refusing to shrink back—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Hebrews 10:32–39.

Arminian Notable voices

“Cast not away therefore this your confidence—your faith and hope; which none can deprive you of but yourselves. Now the just—the justified person—shall live by faith as long as he retains that gift of God. But if he draw back—if he make shipwreck of his faith—my soul hath no pleasure in him: that is, I abhor him; I cast him off. We are not of them who draw back to perdition, but of them that believe to the end, so as to attain eternal life.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Hebrews 10:32–39)

Reformed Notable voices

“Call to remembrance the former days: the remembrance of past warfare is useful to render us more active in finishing the remaining part of our course. Cast not away your confidence, which hath great recompense of reward. Ye have need of patience, that after ye have done the will of God ye might receive the promise. The just shall live by faith. But we are not of them which draw back unto perdition, but of them that believe to the saving of the soul: we have been called that we might not draw back.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Hebrews (on Hebrews 10:32–39)
