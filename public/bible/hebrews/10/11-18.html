Passage

Hebrews 10

Verses 11–18 · WEB

The writer contrasts the standing priests of the old covenant, offering the same sacrifices daily, with Christ, who offered one sacrifice for sins for all time and sat down at God’s right hand. By that single offering he has perfected forever those who are being sanctified. The Holy Spirit confirms it through Jeremiah’s new covenant promise: God will write his laws on hearts and remember sins no more. Where there is forgiveness, there is no longer any offering for sin. Verse 14 is a key text for the Reformed doctrine of definite atonement and is read differently by Arminians.

Hebrews 10:11

11Every priest indeed stands day by day serving and offering often the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins,

Arminian

Every priest stands daily offering the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins. Arminian readers see the futility of the old system, which Christ’s work replaces.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the standing priests as a picture of unfinished work, in contrast with Christ, who sits.

Hebrews 10:12

12but he, when he had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, sat down on the right hand of God,

Arminian

Christ offered one sacrifice for sins for all time and sat down at God’s right hand. Arminian readers see the completeness of Christ’s offering, which leaves nothing to add.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ sitting down as the sign that atonement is finished and accepted by the Father.

Hebrews 10:13

13from that time waiting until his enemies are made the footstool of his feet.

Arminian

He waits until his enemies are made a footstool for his feet. Arminian readers see Christ’s present reign moving toward final victory.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the certainty of Christ’s triumph, which flows from his finished work.

Hebrews 10:14

14For by one offering he has perfected forever those who are being sanctified.

Arminian

By a single offering he has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified. Arminian readers see the perfecting as the provision Christ made, applied to those who are being sanctified as they continue in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the verse as strong evidence that Christ’s death actually secures the eternal perfection of a definite people. Those for whom he died are perfected forever.

Key contrast Both affirm that Christ’s single offering is complete. Arminian readers see it perfecting those who receive and continue in its benefits. Reformed readers see it as actually and permanently securing the salvation of those for whom it was made.

Hebrews 10:15

15The Holy Spirit also testifies to us, for after saying,

Arminian

The Holy Spirit also bears witness to us. Arminian readers see the Spirit confirming the new covenant to believers through Scripture.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit as the author of Scripture, testifying to the finished work of Christ.

Hebrews 10:16

16“This is the covenant that I will make with them after those days,” says the Lord, “I will put my laws on their heart, I will also write them on their mind;” then he says,

Arminian

“I will put my laws on their hearts and write them on their minds.” Arminian readers see the Spirit’s inward work enabling real obedience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the new covenant as God’s sovereign promise to renew the hearts of his people.

Hebrews 10:17

17“I will remember their sins and their iniquities no more.”

Arminian

“I will remember their sins and their lawless deeds no more.” Arminian readers see full forgiveness given to all who enter the new covenant by faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s complete and permanent forgiveness of his people, grounded in Christ’s satisfaction.

Hebrews 10:18

18Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin.

Arminian

Where there is forgiveness, there is no longer any offering for sin. Arminian readers see this as the reason that turning away from Christ leaves no other sacrifice (10:26).

Reformed

Reformed readers see the sufficiency of Christ’s sacrifice, which excludes any repeated offering. Reformation polemics against the Mass drew on this verse.
