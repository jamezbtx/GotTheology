Passage

Hebrews 10

Verses 1–10 · WEB

The writer brings his argument about sacrifice to a climax. The law was only a shadow of the good things to come, and its repeated sacrifices could never perfect worshipers or take away sins. They served as a yearly reminder of sin. Quoting Psalm 40, he shows Christ coming to do God’s will in a prepared body, setting aside the old sacrifices to establish the new. By that will believers have been sanctified through the offering of Jesus’ body once for all. Both traditions affirm the sufficiency of Christ’s single sacrifice. They differ on what it actually secures, and for whom.

Hebrews 10:1

1For the law, having a shadow of the good to come, not the very image of the things, can never with the same sacrifices year by year, which they offer continually, make perfect those who draw near.

Arminian

The law was a shadow of the good things to come and could never perfect those who draw near. Arminian readers see the old covenant as preparing for a fuller grace available in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the law as a shadow fulfilled in Christ, within the one covenant of grace across both testaments.

Hebrews 10:2

2Or else wouldn’t they have ceased to be offered, because the worshipers, having been once cleansed, would have had no more consciousness of sins?

Arminian

If the sacrifices had cleansed worshipers, they would have ceased. Arminian readers see repeated sacrifice as showing that the old system could not give a clear conscience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the repetition as proof of the old sacrifices’ insufficiency, in contrast with Christ’s finished work.

Hebrews 10:3

3But in those sacrifices there is a yearly reminder of sins.

Arminian

In these sacrifices there is a reminder of sins every year. Arminian readers see the law keeping sin before the conscience until Christ came.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the yearly reminder pointing toward the one sacrifice that would remove sin for good.

Hebrews 10:4

4For it is impossible that the blood of bulls and goats should take away sins.

Arminian

It is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins. Both traditions agree that the animal sacrifices were never the true ground of forgiveness. Arminian readers see old covenant believers forgiven through faith in God’s promise.

Reformed

Reformed readers see old covenant believers saved by Christ’s future sacrifice, which the animal sacrifices signified.

Hebrews 10:5

5Therefore when he comes into the world, he says, “You didn’t desire sacrifice and offering, but you prepared a body for me.

Arminian

When Christ came into the world he said, “Sacrifices and offerings you did not desire, but a body you prepared for me.” Arminian readers see the incarnation as God’s provision of a true sacrifice for the world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s willing entrance into the world to fulfill the covenant of redemption between Father and Son.

Hebrews 10:6

6You had no pleasure in whole burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin.

Arminian

In burnt offerings and sin offerings you took no pleasure. Arminian readers see God desiring obedience from the heart over ritual.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God taking no final pleasure in sacrifices that could not satisfy his justice.

Hebrews 10:7

7Then I said, ‘Behold, I have come (in the scroll of the book it is written of me) to do your will, O God.’”

Arminian

“Behold, I have come to do your will, O God.” Arminian readers see Christ’s perfect obedience as the heart of his sacrifice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s active obedience to the Father’s will, which is credited to his people along with his death.

Hebrews 10:8

8Previously saying, “Sacrifices and offerings and whole burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin you didn’t desire, neither had pleasure in them” (those which are offered according to the law),

Arminian

He said that God did not desire the sacrifices offered according to the law. Arminian readers see the old order giving way to the new.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the ceremonial law fulfilled and set aside in Christ.

Hebrews 10:9

9then he has said, “Behold, I have come to do your will.” He takes away the first, that he may establish the second,

Arminian

He abolishes the first in order to establish the second. Arminian readers see the new covenant replacing the old system of sacrifice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the administration of the covenant changing from shadow to substance, while God’s gracious purpose stays the same.

Hebrews 10:10

10by which will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.

Arminian

By that will we have been sanctified through the offering of Jesus’ body once for all. Arminian readers see a sacrifice offered once for all time, whose benefits are received by faith and kept by continuing in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see “we have been sanctified” as a completed, definitive work for those Christ represents. His sacrifice actually secures their consecration to God.

Key contrast Both affirm a once-for-all sacrifice. Arminian readers see its benefits received and kept through faith. Reformed readers see it definitively accomplishing the sanctification of those for whom it was offered.
