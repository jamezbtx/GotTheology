Passage

Hebrews 10

Verses 19–25 · WEB

Having shown that Christ’s single sacrifice has accomplished what the old sacrifices never could, the writer turns to exhortation. Believers have confidence to enter God’s presence by the blood of Jesus through a new and living way, and they have a great priest over God’s house. So they should draw near with a true heart, hold fast their confession without wavering, and stir one another to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together. Both traditions treasure this passage. They differ mainly on how the call to hold fast relates to God’s faithfulness.

Hebrews 10:19

19Having therefore, brothers, boldness to enter into the holy place by the blood of Jesus,

Arminian

Believers have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus. Arminian readers see free access to God opened for all who come through Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see confidence grounded wholly in Christ’s finished sacrifice, not in the worshiper’s worthiness.

Hebrews 10:20

20by the way which he dedicated for us, a new and living way, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh,

Arminian

He opened a new and living way through the curtain, that is, his flesh. Arminian readers see a way open to all who will walk in it.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s body given in death as the opening of God’s presence to his people.

Hebrews 10:21

21and having a great priest over God’s house,

Arminian

We have a great priest over the house of God. Arminian readers see Christ’s continuing ministry supporting those who come to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s intercession as securing his people. His priesthood never fails.

Hebrews 10:22

22let’s draw near with a true heart in fullness of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and having our body washed with pure water,

Arminian

Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith. Arminian readers see assurance as the experience of present faith, which Wesley emphasized as the witness of the Spirit.

Reformed

Reformed readers see assurance resting on Christ’s work and confirmed by the Spirit. Many link the washing with baptism as a sign.

Hebrews 10:23

23let’s hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering; for he who promised is faithful.

Arminian

Hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering, for he who promised is faithful. Arminian readers stress that holding fast is a real responsibility; believers must continue in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the command fulfilled because God is faithful. He who promised will keep his people holding fast.

Key contrast Both read the command and the promise together. Arminian readers stress the believer’s duty to keep holding fast. Reformed readers stress that God’s faithfulness ensures his people will do so.

Hebrews 10:24

24Let’s consider how to provoke one another to love and good works,

Arminian

Consider how to stir one another to love and good works. Arminian readers see mutual encouragement as a real safeguard against falling away.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church as one of the means God uses to preserve his people.

Hebrews 10:25

25not forsaking our own assembling together, as the custom of some is, but exhorting one another, and so much the more as you see the Day approaching.

Arminian

Do not neglect meeting together, but encourage one another, as the Day approaches. Arminian readers see neglect of fellowship as an early step toward the apostasy warned of next.

Reformed

Reformed readers see public worship and fellowship as ordained means of grace, all the more needed as the Day draws near.
