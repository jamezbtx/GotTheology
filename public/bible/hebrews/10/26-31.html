Passage

Hebrews 10

Verses 26–31 · WEB

This is the fourth and sharpest warning in Hebrews. If someone sins deliberately after receiving the knowledge of the truth, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins, only a fearful expectation of judgment. The writer argues from the lesser to the greater: if rejecting Moses’ law brought death, how much worse for one who has trampled the Son of God, profaned the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified, and insulted the Spirit of grace. This passage is central to the debate over whether true believers can finally fall away.

Hebrews 10:26

26For if we sin willfully after we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remains no more a sacrifice for sins,

Arminian

If we go on sinning deliberately after receiving the knowledge of the truth, no sacrifice for sins remains. Arminian readers see deliberate, persistent rejection of Christ by someone who truly knew him. Most do not read this as any single sin, but as apostasy.

Reformed

Reformed readers also see apostasy, not ordinary sin. Many read it as describing people who had knowledge of the truth without being regenerated.

Key contrast Both see the passage as describing apostasy, a deliberate turning away from Christ. Arminian readers believe a true believer can commit it. Reformed readers believe it describes those who had knowledge but not saving faith, or a warning God uses to keep true believers from falling.

Hebrews 10:27

27but a certain fearful expectation of judgment, and a fierceness of fire which will devour the adversaries.

Arminian

Only a fearful expectation of judgment remains. Arminian readers see the real eternal danger of turning away from the only sacrifice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the warning as genuine. Outside Christ there is no hope, and the warning drives believers toward him.

Hebrews 10:28

28A man who disregards Moses’ law dies without compassion on the word of two or three witnesses.

Arminian

Anyone who set aside Moses’ law died without mercy on the testimony of two or three witnesses. Arminian readers see the greater accountability of those who have received the new covenant.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the argument from lesser to greater. Rejecting a greater revelation brings greater judgment.

Hebrews 10:29

29How much worse punishment do you think he will be judged worthy of who has trodden under foot the Son of God, and has counted the blood of the covenant with which he was sanctified an unholy thing, and has insulted the Spirit of grace?

Arminian

How much worse for one who has trampled the Son of God and profaned the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified. Arminian readers see “by which he was sanctified” as describing a real believer, cleansed by Christ’s blood, who later turns away.

For them this is one of the strongest texts against unconditional perseverance.

Reformed

Reformed readers offer several readings. Some say “sanctified” refers to outward setting apart within the covenant community. Others take “he” to refer to Christ, who was set apart by his own blood. Still others read the warning as hypothetical.

Key contrast Both take the warning with full seriousness. Arminian readers read “sanctified” as describing a true believer who later apostatizes. Reformed readers read it as outward covenant membership, a reference to Christ, or a warning that God uses to preserve his elect.

Hebrews 10:30

30For we know him who said, “Vengeance belongs to me. I will repay,” says the Lord. Again, “The Lord will judge his people.”

Arminian

“Vengeance belongs to me; I will repay.” “The Lord will judge his people.” Arminian readers see God judging those within his covenant people who turn away.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s holiness in judging his people, which includes purging hypocrites from the visible church.

Hebrews 10:31

31It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.

Arminian

It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God. Arminian readers see a sober warning that believers must heed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the warning as one of God’s means to keep his people from falling.
