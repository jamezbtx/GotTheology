Passage

Hebrews 13

Verses 1–6 · WEB

The final chapter turns to practical exhortations. Brotherly love must continue. Believers are to show hospitality, remember prisoners and the mistreated, honor marriage and keep it pure, and stay free from the love of money, content with what they have. God has promised never to leave or forsake them, so they can say with confidence that the Lord is their helper. Both traditions read these instructions as the fruit of grace. They find different comfort in God’s promise in verse 5.

Hebrews 13:1

1Let brotherly love continue.

Arminian

Let brotherly love continue. Arminian readers see love as something that must be kept up, since it can grow cold.

Reformed

Reformed readers see brotherly love as a lasting mark of the regenerate, nurtured by exhortation.

Hebrews 13:2

2Don’t forget to show hospitality to strangers, for in doing so, some have entertained angels without knowing it.

Arminian

Do not neglect hospitality, for some have entertained angels unawares. Arminian readers see practical love toward strangers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see hospitality as a duty rooted in God’s welcome of sinners.

Hebrews 13:3

3Remember those who are in bonds, as bound with them, and those who are ill-treated, since you are also in the body.

Arminian

Remember prisoners as though in prison with them. Arminian readers see empathy for suffering believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the unity of Christ’s body, sharing one another’s suffering.

Hebrews 13:4

4Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the bed be undefiled; but God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers.

Arminian

Let marriage be held in honor among all, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers. Arminian readers see holiness in sexual life as part of sanctification.

Reformed

Reformed readers see marriage as a creation ordinance to be honored by all.

Hebrews 13:5

5Be free from the love of money, content with such things as you have, for he has said, “I will in no way leave you, neither will I in any way forsake you.”

Arminian

Keep your life free from love of money, for he has said, “I will never leave you nor forsake you.” Arminian readers see God’s faithful presence with those who trust him, a promise to cling to.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an unconditional promise. God will never abandon his people, which grounds their contentment and security.

Key contrast Both find deep comfort in God’s promise. Arminian readers see his faithful presence with all who continue trusting him. Reformed readers see an unconditional guarantee that God will never abandon those who are his.

Hebrews 13:6

6So that with good courage we say, “The Lord is my helper. I will not fear. What can man do to me?”

Arminian

So we can confidently say, “The Lord is my helper; I will not fear.” Arminian readers see confidence grounded in God’s help.

Reformed

Reformed readers see fearless confidence resting on God’s sovereign care.
