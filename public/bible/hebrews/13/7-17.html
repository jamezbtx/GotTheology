Passage

Hebrews 13

Verses 7–17 · WEB

The readers are to remember their former leaders and imitate their faith, because Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever. They should not be carried away by strange teachings about foods, since the heart is strengthened by grace. Believers have an altar the old priests cannot share. As Jesus suffered outside the gate to sanctify the people by his blood, so believers should go to him outside the camp, bearing his reproach, seeking the city to come. They are to offer a sacrifice of praise and do good, and to obey their present leaders, who watch over their souls. Both traditions draw rich teaching here, with differences on the sacraments and church order.

Hebrews 13:7

7Remember your leaders, men who spoke to you the word of God, and considering the results of their conduct, imitate their faith.

Arminian

Remember your leaders who spoke God’s word to you; imitate their faith. Arminian readers see faithful examples encouraging believers to persevere.

Reformed

Reformed readers see godly leaders as a gift of God for the church’s edification.

Hebrews 13:8

8Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever.

Arminian

Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever. Both traditions treasure this verse as an affirmation of Christ’s unchanging nature. Arminian readers see his constant faithfulness to all who come to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s immutability as the security of his people.

Hebrews 13:9

9Don’t be carried away by various and strange teachings, for it is good that the heart be established by grace, not by foods, through which those who were so occupied were not benefited.

Arminian

Do not be led away by strange teachings, for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods. Arminian readers see grace, not ritual, as the strength of the believer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace strengthening the heart, in contrast with ceremonial observances.

Hebrews 13:10

10We have an altar from which those who serve the holy tabernacle have no right to eat.

Arminian

We have an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat. Arminian readers see Christ’s sacrifice as the believer’s altar.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the altar as the cross. They reject reading it as a sacrificial altar for the Mass.

Hebrews 13:11

11For the bodies of those animals, whose blood is brought into the holy place by the high priest as an offering for sin, are burned outside of the camp.

Arminian

The bodies of animals whose blood was brought into the holy place were burned outside the camp. Arminian readers see the sin offering as a picture of Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Day of Atonement fulfilled in Christ.

Hebrews 13:12

12Therefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people through his own blood, suffered outside of the gate.

Arminian

Jesus also suffered outside the gate to sanctify the people through his own blood. Arminian readers see Christ’s blood available to sanctify all who come to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s blood actually sanctifying “the people,” those he represents.

Key contrast Both see Jesus sanctifying the people through his blood. Arminian readers see that sanctification offered to all and received by faith. Reformed readers see it effectively accomplished for those Christ represents.

Hebrews 13:13

13Let’s therefore go out to him outside of the camp, bearing his reproach.

Arminian

Let us go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured. Arminian readers see a call to costly identification with Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers leaving behind the old system to follow Christ.

Hebrews 13:14

14For we don’t have here an enduring city, but we seek that which is to come.

Arminian

Here we have no lasting city, but we seek the city that is to come. Arminian readers see pilgrim faith seeking the heavenly city.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the believer’s hope fixed on the city God has prepared.

Hebrews 13:15

15Through him, then, let’s offer up a sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of lips which proclaim allegiance to his name.

Arminian

Through him let us continually offer a sacrifice of praise to God. Arminian readers see praise as the fitting response of grateful believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see praise as the believer’s priestly sacrifice, offered through Christ.

Hebrews 13:16

16But don’t forget to be doing good and sharing, for with such sacrifices God is well pleased.

Arminian

Do not neglect to do good and share, for such sacrifices please God. Arminian readers see good works as pleasing to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see good works as pleasing to God through Christ, not as meriting salvation.

Hebrews 13:17

17Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they watch on behalf of your souls, as those who will give account, that they may do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be unprofitable for you.

Arminian

Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they keep watch over your souls as those who will give an account. Arminian readers see leaders helping believers persevere.

Reformed

Reformed readers see church leaders as shepherds appointed by Christ, who will give account to him.
