Passage

Hebrews 1

Verses 1–4 · WEB

Hebrews opens without a greeting, going straight to its theme: God, who spoke in many ways through the prophets, has now spoken finally in his Son. The Son is heir of all things, agent of creation, the radiance of God’s glory and exact imprint of his nature, who upholds the universe by his word. After making purification for sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high, as superior to angels as his name is greater. Arminian and Reformed readers share this high Christology almost entirely. Their emphases differ only in how they apply the finished purification.

Hebrews 1:1

1God, having in the past spoken to the fathers through the prophets at many times and in various ways,

Arminian

God spoke long ago to the fathers through the prophets at many times and in many ways. Arminian readers see God’s patient self-revelation, given so that people in every age could know and respond to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the unity of revelation. The prophets and the Son speak one message, and the Old Testament is truly God’s word pointing to Christ.

Hebrews 1:2

2has at the end of these days spoken to us by his Son, whom he appointed heir of all things, through whom also he made the worlds.

Arminian

In these last days he has spoken to us by his Son, heir of all things, through whom he made the world. Arminian readers see the Son as God’s final word, addressed to all people.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the finality of revelation in Christ, which supports the sufficiency of Scripture. Nothing beyond the Son’s word is needed for salvation.

Hebrews 1:3

3His Son is the radiance of his glory, the very image of his substance, and upholding all things by the word of his power, who, when he had by himself purified us of our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high,

Arminian

He is the radiance of God’s glory and the exact imprint of his nature, upholding all things by his word; after making purification for sins, he sat down. Arminian readers see a purification provided for sins that is fully adequate for all who receive it.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a purification actually accomplished, signified by the Son sitting down. His upholding of all things also grounds the Reformed doctrine of providence.

Hebrews 1:4

4having become as much better than the angels as the more excellent name he has inherited is better than theirs.

Arminian

He became as much superior to angels as the name he inherited is more excellent. Arminian readers see the exalted Son as worthy of the trust that the readers were tempted to abandon.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s exaltation as the reward of his mediatorial work. The name points to his divine sonship, now publicly displayed.
