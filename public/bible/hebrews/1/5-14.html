Passage

Hebrews 1

Verses 5–14 · WEB

A chain of Old Testament quotations proves that the Son is greater than the angels. God never said to an angel, “You are my Son,” and he commands the angels to worship the firstborn. The angels are servants, but the Son is addressed as God, whose throne is forever and who laid the foundations of the earth and remains unchanged when creation wears out. The Son sits at God’s right hand, while angels are ministering spirits sent to serve those who will inherit salvation. Both traditions use this passage as a key witness to the deity of Christ. They differ slightly on who “those who will inherit salvation” are.

Hebrews 1:5

5For to which of the angels did he say at any time, “You are my Son. Today I have become your father?” and again, “I will be to him a Father, and he will be to me a Son?”

Arminian

“You are my Son; today I have begotten you.” Arminian readers see the Son’s unique relationship to the Father, declared at his resurrection and exaltation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s eternal sonship publicly declared, fulfilling the promise to David.

Hebrews 1:6

6When he again brings in the firstborn into the world he says, “Let all the angels of God worship him.”

Arminian

“Let all God’s angels worship him.” Both traditions see this as clear evidence of the Son’s deity, since worship belongs to God alone. Arminian readers stress that this is the one the readers must not abandon.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son as the proper object of worship, which rules out any view of him as a created being.

Hebrews 1:7

7Of the angels he says, “He makes his angels winds, and his servants a flame of fire.”

Arminian

“He makes his angels winds, and his servants flames of fire.” Arminian readers see angels as glorious but created servants.

Reformed

Reformed readers see angels as ministers of God’s providence, under his command.

Hebrews 1:8

8But of the Son he says, “Your throne, O God, is forever and ever. The scepter of uprightness is the scepter of your Kingdom.

Arminian

“Your throne, O God, is forever and ever.” Arminian readers see the Son addressed as God, sharing the Father’s eternal reign.

Reformed

Reformed readers see one of the clearest New Testament statements of Christ’s deity.

Hebrews 1:9

9You have loved righteousness and hated iniquity; therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows.”

Arminian

“You have loved righteousness and hated wickedness; therefore God has anointed you.” Arminian readers see the Son’s righteousness as the pattern for believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s perfect righteousness as the righteousness credited to his people.

Hebrews 1:10

10And, “You, Lord, in the beginning, laid the foundation of the earth. The heavens are the works of your hands.

Arminian

“You, Lord, laid the foundation of the earth in the beginning.” Both traditions see the Son as creator. Arminian readers see his creative power as the ground of his saving power.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Psalm 102, addressed to the Lord, applied to the Son. That identifies him with the God of Israel.

Hebrews 1:11

11They will perish, but you continue. They all will grow old like a garment does.

Arminian

They will perish, but you remain. Arminian readers see the Son as the unchanging hope amid a passing world.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s immutability as the security of his people.

Hebrews 1:12

12You will roll them up like a mantle, and they will be changed; but you are the same. Your years won’t fail.”

Arminian

You will roll them up like a robe, but you are the same. Arminian readers see creation’s transience contrasted with Christ’s permanence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ unchanged, “the same yesterday and today and forever” (13:8).

Hebrews 1:13

13But which of the angels has he told at any time, “Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies the footstool of your feet?”

Arminian

“Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool.” Arminian readers see the Son’s present reign moving toward final victory.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Son’s session as the sign of finished work and certain triumph.

Hebrews 1:14

14Aren’t they all serving spirits, sent out to do service for the sake of those who will inherit salvation?

Arminian

Are not all angels ministering spirits sent to serve those who will inherit salvation? Arminian readers see angels helping all who are on the way to salvation through faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see angels serving the elect, who will certainly inherit salvation.

Key contrast Both see angels serving God’s people. Arminian readers see those who will inherit salvation as all who believe and persevere. Reformed readers see them as the elect, whose inheritance is certain.
