Passage

Hebrews 11

Verses 23–31 · WEB

Moses’ parents hid him by faith, unafraid of the king’s edict. Moses himself refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter, choosing ill-treatment with God’s people over the passing pleasures of sin, because he considered the reproach of Christ greater wealth than Egypt’s treasures. By faith he left Egypt, kept the Passover, and led the people through the Red Sea. By faith the walls of Jericho fell, and Rahab was spared. Both traditions see faith making real choices. They frame those choices differently.

Hebrews 11:23

23By faith Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that he was a beautiful child; and they were not afraid of the king’s commandment.

Arminian

By faith Moses’ parents hid him, not afraid of the king’s edict. Arminian readers see courageous faith choosing to obey God rather than men.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God preserving his chosen deliverer through the faith of his parents.

Hebrews 11:24

24By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter,

Arminian

By faith Moses refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter. Arminian readers see a real decision that cost him greatly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace enabling Moses to choose God’s people.

Hebrews 11:25

25choosing rather to share ill treatment with God’s people than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a time,

Arminian

He chose to be mistreated with God’s people rather than enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin. Arminian readers see Moses’ choice as a model of free, costly faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a choice that flowed from a heart God had changed.

Key contrast Both see Moses making a real choice. Arminian readers stress the freedom of that choice, enabled by grace. Reformed readers stress that it flowed from a heart renewed by God.

Hebrews 11:26

26considering the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures of Egypt; for he looked to the reward.

Arminian

He considered the reproach of Christ greater wealth than Egypt’s treasures, looking to the reward. Arminian readers see faith motivated by future reward.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Moses identifying with the Messiah, which shows Old Testament faith looking to Christ.

Hebrews 11:27

27By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the wrath of the king; for he endured, as seeing him who is invisible.

Arminian

By faith he left Egypt, enduring as seeing him who is invisible. Arminian readers see endurance sustained by a vision of God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as seeing the unseen God.

Hebrews 11:28

28By faith he kept the Passover and the sprinkling of the blood, that the destroyer of the firstborn should not touch them.

Arminian

By faith he kept the Passover and sprinkled the blood. Arminian readers see obedience to God’s provision of salvation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Passover blood as a type of Christ’s blood, protecting God’s people.

Hebrews 11:29

29By faith they passed through the Red Sea as on dry land. When the Egyptians tried to do so, they were swallowed up.

Arminian

By faith the people crossed the Red Sea as on dry land. Arminian readers see corporate faith acting on God’s word.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s deliverance of his people, which Paul links with baptism (1 Corinthians 10:2).

Hebrews 11:30

30By faith the walls of Jericho fell down after they had been encircled for seven days.

Arminian

By faith the walls of Jericho fell after being encircled seven days. Arminian readers see faith obeying instructions that seemed strange.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s power working through obedient faith.

Hebrews 11:31

31By faith Rahab the prostitute didn’t perish with those who were disobedient, having received the spies in peace.

Arminian

By faith Rahab did not perish with the disobedient, because she welcomed the spies. Arminian readers see a Gentile outsider saved by responding in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s grace reaching an unlikely outsider and bringing her into his people and the line of Christ.
