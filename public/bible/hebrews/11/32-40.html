Passage

Hebrews 11

Verses 32–40 · WEB

The writer runs out of time to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets. Through faith some conquered kingdoms, shut lions’ mouths, and escaped the sword. Others were tortured, mocked, imprisoned, stoned, sawn in two, and killed, refusing release to gain a better resurrection. The world was not worthy of them. All were commended for their faith but did not receive what was promised, because God had provided something better, so that they would not be made perfect apart from us. Both traditions see faith vindicated whether it leads to triumph or to suffering.

Hebrews 11:32

32What more shall I say? For the time would fail me if I told of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets—

Arminian

Time would fail to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets. Arminian readers note that these were flawed people whose faith God honored.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace working through weak and sinful people, so the glory goes to God.

Hebrews 11:33

33who through faith subdued kingdoms, worked out righteousness, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions,

Arminian

Through faith they conquered kingdoms, enforced justice, obtained promises, and stopped lions’ mouths. Arminian readers see faith acting powerfully in history.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God working through the faith of his people.

Hebrews 11:34

34quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, from weakness were made strong, grew mighty in war, and caused foreign armies to flee.

Arminian

They quenched fire, escaped the sword, were made strong out of weakness. Arminian readers see God’s strength given to those who trust him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see strength made perfect in weakness.

Hebrews 11:35

35Women received their dead by resurrection. Others were tortured, not accepting their deliverance, that they might obtain a better resurrection.

Arminian

Women received back their dead; others were tortured, refusing release, to gain a better resurrection. Arminian readers see faith choosing eternal reward over present relief.

Reformed

Reformed readers see confidence in the resurrection sustaining martyrs.

Hebrews 11:36

36Others were tried by mocking and scourging, yes, moreover by bonds and imprisonment.

Arminian

Others suffered mocking, flogging, chains, and imprisonment. Arminian readers see that faith does not guarantee earthly deliverance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see suffering as part of God’s purpose for his people.

Hebrews 11:37

37They were stoned. They were sawn apart. They were tempted. They were slain with the sword. They went around in sheep skins and in goat skins; being destitute, afflicted, ill-treated—

Arminian

They were stoned, sawn in two, killed with the sword, destitute and afflicted. Arminian readers see costly endurance that remained faithful.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God preserving his people’s faith even through death.

Hebrews 11:38

38of whom the world was not worthy—wandering in deserts, mountains, caves, and the holes of the earth.

Arminian

Of whom the world was not worthy, wandering in deserts and caves. Arminian readers see the world’s rejection of the faithful.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s valuation of his people, despised by the world.

Hebrews 11:39

39These all, having been commended for their faith, didn’t receive the promise,

Arminian

All these were commended through their faith, yet did not receive what was promised. Arminian readers see faith that persevered without seeing fulfillment.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise reserved for the time of Christ.

Hebrews 11:40

40God having provided some better thing concerning us, so that apart from us they should not be made perfect.

Arminian

God provided something better for us, that apart from us they should not be made perfect. Arminian readers see one people of God, completed together in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the one church across the ages, perfected together in Christ.
