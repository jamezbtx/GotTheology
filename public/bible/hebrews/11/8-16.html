Passage

Hebrews 11

Verses 8–16 · WEB

Abraham obeyed God’s call to go out, not knowing where he was going, and lived as a foreigner in the land of promise, looking for the city whose designer and builder is God. By faith Sarah received power to conceive, and from one man as good as dead came descendants as many as the stars. These all died in faith without receiving the promises, confessing that they were strangers on the earth, seeking a better, heavenly country. So God is not ashamed to be called their God, for he has prepared a city for them. Both traditions draw from the passage a picture of pilgrim faith.

Hebrews 11:8

8By faith Abraham, when he was called, obeyed to go out to the place which he was to receive for an inheritance. He went out, not knowing where he went.

Arminian

By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out, not knowing where he was going. Arminian readers see Abraham’s obedience as a real response to God’s call.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s call creating the obedience it commands.

Hebrews 11:9

9By faith he lived as an alien in the land of promise, as in a land not his own, dwelling in tents with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise.

Arminian

By faith he lived in the land of promise as a foreigner, in tents. Arminian readers see faith that keeps going without yet possessing the promise.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the patriarchs as pilgrims, sustained by God’s promises.

Hebrews 11:10

10For he was looking for the city which has foundations, whose builder and maker is God.

Arminian

He was looking forward to the city with foundations, whose designer and builder is God. Arminian readers see hope fixed on the heavenly city as the motive for endurance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the heavenly city as God’s work, prepared for his people.

Hebrews 11:11

11By faith even Sarah herself received power to conceive, and she bore a child when she was past age, since she counted him faithful who had promised.

Arminian

By faith Sarah received power to conceive, since she considered him faithful who had promised. Arminian readers see Sarah’s faith growing from initial laughter to trust.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness as the ground of Sarah’s faith.

Hebrews 11:12

12Therefore as many as the stars of the sky in multitude, and as innumerable as the sand which is by the sea shore, were fathered by one man, and him as good as dead.

Arminian

From one man, as good as dead, were born descendants as many as the stars. Arminian readers see God’s power working through faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God fulfilling his covenant promise to Abraham.

Hebrews 11:13

13These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them and embraced them from afar, and having confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.

Arminian

These all died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them from afar. Arminian readers see faith that perseveres to death.

Reformed

Reformed readers see persevering faith as the mark of true believers.

Hebrews 11:14

14For those who say such things make it clear that they are seeking a country of their own.

Arminian

People who speak this way make it clear they are seeking a homeland. Arminian readers see the heavenly orientation of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the believer’s true home in heaven.

Hebrews 11:15

15If indeed they had been thinking of that country from which they went out, they would have had enough time to return.

Arminian

If they had been thinking of the land they left, they would have had opportunity to return. Arminian readers note that returning was a real possibility the patriarchs chose not to take.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God keeping his people from turning back by fixing their hope on heaven.

Key contrast Both note that the patriarchs did not go back. Arminian readers see a real opportunity to return that faith chose to refuse. Reformed readers see God’s grace keeping their hearts fixed on the better country.

Hebrews 11:16

16But now they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God, for he has prepared a city for them.

Arminian

They desire a better country, a heavenly one; so God is not ashamed to be called their God. Arminian readers see God honoring those who keep seeking him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s covenant name, “their God,” as the heart of the promise.
