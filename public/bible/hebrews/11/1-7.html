Passage

Hebrews 11

Verses 1–7 · WEB

Hebrews 11 begins its great roll call of faith. Faith is the assurance of things hoped for and the conviction of things not seen, and by it the people of old received God’s approval. By faith we understand that God made the world by his word. Abel offered a better sacrifice, Enoch was taken up because he pleased God, and Noah built the ark in reverent fear. Without faith it is impossible to please God, because whoever comes to him must believe that he exists and rewards those who seek him. Both traditions celebrate this chapter. They differ on where faith comes from.

Hebrews 11:1

1Now faith is assurance of things hoped for, proof of things not seen.

Arminian

Faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen. Arminian readers see faith as a real trust that people exercise in response to God’s word, enabled by grace.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as a God-given conviction, produced by the Spirit through the word, that rests on God’s promises.

Hebrews 11:2

2For by this, the elders obtained approval.

Arminian

By it the people of old received their commendation. Arminian readers see God honoring the faith of those who trusted him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God approving faith, which he himself worked in them.

Hebrews 11:3

3By faith we understand that the universe has been framed by the word of God, so that what is seen has not been made out of things which are visible.

Arminian

By faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God. Both traditions see creation out of nothing as an article of faith. Arminian readers stress that faith opens understanding.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as the lens through which believers rightly interpret the world.

Hebrews 11:4

4By faith Abel offered to God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, through which he had testimony given to him that he was righteous, God testifying with respect to his gifts; and through it he, being dead, still speaks.

Arminian

By faith Abel offered a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain. Arminian readers see the difference lying in Abel’s faith, not in the offering itself.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Abel accepted because of faith, in contrast with Cain’s outward religion.

Hebrews 11:5

5By faith Enoch was taken away, so that he wouldn’t see death, and he was not found, because God translated him. For he has had testimony given to him that before his translation he had been well pleasing to God.

Arminian

By faith Enoch was taken up, for he pleased God. Arminian readers see Enoch’s close walk with God as a model of holiness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Enoch as an example of faith that pleases God, by grace.

Hebrews 11:6

6Without faith it is impossible to be well pleasing to him, for he who comes to God must believe that he exists, and that he is a rewarder of those who seek him.

Arminian

Without faith it is impossible to please God; whoever comes to him must believe that he exists and rewards those who seek him. Arminian readers see seeking God as a real human response, made possible by prevenient grace.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as necessary to please God, and see seeking God as the fruit of regeneration.

Key contrast Both affirm that faith is necessary to please God. Arminian readers see faith and seeking as responses enabled by grace given to all. Reformed readers see them as gifts produced by the Spirit in the regenerate.

Hebrews 11:7

7By faith Noah, being warned about things not yet seen, moved with godly fear, prepared a ship for the saving of his house, through which he condemned the world and became heir of the righteousness which is according to faith.

Arminian

By faith Noah, warned about things not yet seen, built an ark and became an heir of righteousness. Arminian readers see Noah’s obedient response to God’s warning as the pattern of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Noah as an heir of righteousness by faith, saved by God’s grace (Genesis 6:8).
