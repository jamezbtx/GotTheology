Passage

Hebrews 3

Verses 7–19 · ESV · Voices

Having set Christ as Son over God's house and warned that we are his house if we hold fast, the writer cites Psalm 95: the Holy Spirit still says Today—do not harden your hearts as Israel did in the wilderness. The fathers tested God, went astray in heart, and under oath were barred from his rest. So take care, brothers: an evil unbelieving heart can lead away from the living God; exhort one another daily while it is called today; we have come to share in Christ if we hold our original confidence firm to the end. The generation that left Egypt under Moses rebelled, sinned, fell in the wilderness, and could not enter because of unbelief.

Hebrews 3:7

7Therefore, as the Holy Spirit says, "Today, if you hear his voice,

Arminian

Therefore—seeing Christ is faithful as Son, be not you unfaithful. Wesley: Wherefore—seeing he is faithful, be not ye unfaithful (Ps. 95:7). The citation is the Holy Spirit's present word, not a dead archive. Today still names the open hour in which the voice may be heard.

Arminian pastoral: shared ground—the Spirit speaks; hear him now. No open-theist detour: God is not guessing who will listen. The urgency is real because the call is real and the heart can still harden if it will not hear.

Reformed

Calvin: As the Holy Ghost saith—this touches the heart more than naming David; the words of the prophets are God's, not men's. He proceeds that they were to obey Christ speaking; David is brought so the goad may be sharper without needless offense.

Reformed pastoral: shared—the Spirit still says Today. The same God who spoke in the Psalm addresses the church now. Hear his voice; the warning that follows is not theater.

Hebrews 3:8

8do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness,

Arminian

Do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness. Wesley: As in the provocation—when Israel provoked me by their strife and murmurings. In the day of temptation—when at the same time they tempted me, by distrusting my power and goodness (Exod. 17:7).

Arminian pastoral: shared—hardening is forbidden, and the wilderness pattern is a real caution, not a fake scare. The heart can stiffen against the voice; grace summons a living refusal of that hardness today.

Reformed

Calvin: Harden not your hearts—rebellion flows from willful wickedness that obstructs grace's entrance. We have by nature a heart of stone; yet when we reject God's voice it is spontaneous obstinacy. The Spirit rightly accuses unbelievers that they resist God and are authors of their own perverseness.

Reformed pastoral: shared—do not harden, as in the provocation. The warning is the means God uses; it is not emptied because God keeps his own. Soften while it is called today.

Hebrews 3:9

9where your fathers put me to the test and saw my works for forty years.

Arminian

Where your fathers put me to the test and saw my works for forty years. Wesley: Where your fathers—that hard-hearted and stiff-necked generation; so little cause had their descendants to glory in them. Tempted me—whether I could and would help them. Proved me—put my patience to the proof, even while they saw my glorious works of judgment and mercy, and that for forty years.

Arminian pastoral: shared—long sight of God's works did not save a generation that tested him. Take the wilderness history seriously; privilege without faith is no shelter.

Reformed

Calvin: Tempted is taken badly—to provoke in a proud, insulting manner. Proved: when yet they had proved me and seen my works. So many evidences of divine power, and so bad a progress—marvelous stupidity to esteem God's power as nothing which had been so fully proved. Forty years marks incessant wickedness, not a brief stumble.

Reformed pastoral: shared—the fathers saw and still tested. Seeing miracles is not the same as knowing God's ways. The church must not romanticize that generation.

Hebrews 3:10

10Therefore I was provoked with that generation, and said, 'They always go astray in their heart; they have not known my ways.'

Arminian

God was provoked with that generation: they always go astray in their heart; they have not known my ways. Wesley: Wherefore—to speak after the manner of men. I was grieved—displeased, offended. They always err in their hearts—led astray by stubborn will and vile affections. They have not known my ways—by which I would have led them like a flock.

Arminian pastoral: shared—heart-wandering and not knowing God's ways provoke him. The diagnosis is moral and spiritual, not a secret that the warning never meant them.

Reformed

Calvin: This was God's sentence—they were destitute of a sound mind; they have not known my ways. He regarded them as past hope, without sense and reason. Assuming the character of man after long trials, he declares obstinate madness: they always went astray, and no hope of repentance appeared.

Reformed pastoral: shared—always astray in heart is the Spirit's verdict on that generation. Provocation is just; the rest-warning that follows is earned, not arbitrary.

Hebrews 3:11

11As I swore in my wrath, 'They shall not enter my rest.'"

Arminian

As I swore in my wrath, They shall not enter my rest. Wesley ties the rest here to the promised land—into my rest—in the promised land—while the letter will press a greater rest still. The oath makes exclusion solemn, not theatrical.

Arminian pastoral: shared—wrath and oath barred that generation. Both camps take the wilderness/rest warning as real. Who finally enters God's rest is the later fork; whether God meant what he swore is not.

Reformed

Calvin: It was the punishment of their madness that they were deprived of the rest promised them. The Lord calls the land where they might have dwelt his rest—their perpetual inheritance by promise. Swearing If they shall enter sets forth wrath greatly inflamed; the defective oath-form recommends fear and reverence.

Reformed pastoral: shared—they shall not enter is a real divine sentence. The warning that applies this history to the church is not fake for the Reformed either.

Hebrews 3:12

12Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God.

Arminian

Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God. Wesley: Take heed, lest there be in any of you—as there was in them. An evil heart of unbelief—unbelief is the parent of all evil, and the very essence of unbelief lies in departing from God, as the living God—the fountain of all our life, holiness, happiness.

Arminian pastoral: the possibility named is real—falling away from the living God through an evil unbelieving heart. Grace enables perseverance; the "lest" still summons care. Do not dissolve the warning into a threat that can never touch anyone who once professed.

Reformed

Calvin: Take heed, brethren, lest there be at any time in any of you a wicked heart of unbelief—unbelief connected with depravity if after receiving the knowledge of Christ they departed from his faith. He addressed those imbued with Christianity's elements; hence By departing—the sin of defection is accompanied with perfidy.

Reformed pastoral: the warning is real means, not fake thunder. Those who finally belong do not fall away; an evil unbelieving heart marks departure from a profession, not the failure of God's keeping of his own. Still: take care—presumption is not faith.

Key contrast Both treat the wilderness pattern as a real caution and refuse to pretend the "lest" is empty. Arminians typically hear a genuine possibility of falling away from the living God through an evil unbelieving heart—care and perseverance as living conditions grace enables but does not render optional. Reformed readers typically hear the same urgency as God's means: those who truly belong do not finally fall away; unbelief exposes a heart that never rested in the living God, while the elect heed the warning and endure.

Hebrews 3:13

13But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called "today," that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.

Arminian

Exhort one another every day, as long as it is called today, that none may be hardened by sin's deceitfulness. Wesley: But, to prevent it, exhort one another, while it is called To-day—this to-day will not last for ever. The day of life will end soon, and perhaps the day of grace yet sooner.

Arminian pastoral: shared—mutual daily exhortation is the appointed remedy against hardening. Sin deceives; brothers must speak while Today remains. Community care is not optional piety; it is how none of you is left to harden alone.

Reformed

Calvin: He pointed out the remedy—exhort one another. By nature inclined to evil, we need helps to retain us in God's fear. Unless faith is raised up, it lies prostrate; unless warmed, frozen; unless roused, torpid. Stimulate one another so Satan may not creep in by fallacies. We fall not at the first assault into open rebellion; meet the danger in due time. Watch over every member—neglect no sheep.

Reformed pastoral: shared—exhort one another daily while it is called today. Hardening by deceitful sin is a near danger; the means of perseverance include the flock's mutual goads.

Hebrews 3:14

14For we have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end.

Arminian

We have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end. Wesley: For we are made partakers of Christ—and we shall still partake of him and all his benefits, if we hold fast our faith unto the end. If—but not else; and a supposition made by the Holy Ghost is equal to the strongest assertion. Both the sentiment and the manner of expression are the same as Heb 3:6.

Arminian pastoral: holding firm is the stated condition of sharing in Christ to the end. Original confidence must be kept; the "if indeed" is not polite decoration. Grace enables the hold; the hold remains the path by which we still partake.

Reformed

Calvin: He commends them for having begun well; lest under grace obtained they indulge carnal security, he says there was need of perseverance. Many having only tasted the Gospel stop mid-race or turn aside. Christ has given himself to be enjoyed on this condition: by the same faith by which we were admitted into participation of him, we preserve so great a blessing even to death. Beginning intimates faith only begun; it must make constant progress to the end.

Reformed pastoral: those who finally share in Christ are those who hold firm to the end. The "if" is evidence and means, not a wager that God might lose his own. Hold original confidence—because true partakers do, and because security without perseverance is not the letter's comfort.

Key contrast Both refuse to treat "if indeed we hold... firm to the end" as sham. Arminians typically hear holding original confidence as the real condition of continuing to share in Christ—"if, but not else," as Wesley presses, parallel to 3:6. Reformed readers typically hear the same clause as proving who truly became partakers: those who finally share hold firm; the warning drives perseverance as God's instrument, not as uncertainty whether he will keep his elect.

Hebrews 3:15

15As it is said, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion."

Arminian

The Psalm returns: Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion. The repetition after verse 14 keeps the condition and the caution joined—share in Christ by holding firm, and hear today without the wilderness hardness.

Arminian pastoral: shared—Today is still the Spirit's word. Hearing and not hardening remain the living response. Do not let the second citation feel like filler; it re-applies the same urgent call.

Reformed

Calvin: While it is said—he intimates that the reason for making progress never ceases as long as we live, because God calls us daily. Faith responds to the Gospel's preaching; as preaching continues through life, we ought to continue growing in faith. It is not enough to receive doctrine readily unless we exhibit the same teachableness tomorrow and every following day.

Reformed pastoral: shared—Today and do not harden return on purpose. Ongoing calling means ongoing obedience; yesterday's hearing does not retire today's command.

Hebrews 3:16

16For who were those who heard and yet rebelled? Was it not all those who left Egypt led by Moses?

Arminian

Who heard and yet rebelled? Was it not all those who left Egypt led by Moses? Wesley: Were they not all that came out of Egypt—an awful consideration! The whole elect people of God (a very few excepted) provoked God presently after their great deliverance, continued to grieve his Spirit for forty years, and perished in their sin!

Arminian pastoral: shared—exodus privilege did not prevent rebellion. Hearing under Moses and still rebelling is the writer's mirror for the church. Take that generation seriously; deliverance without perseverance is no boast.

Reformed

Calvin: David spoke as though that whole generation were unbelieving; yet some who truly feared God mingled with the wicked. The apostle modifies the severity so we know the word is preached that all may obey, and the people were justly condemned when the body was torn by the greatest part's defection. Saying some provoked, while they were by far the greatest part, avoids offense and sets believing fathers as examples too.

Reformed pastoral: shared—those who left Egypt under Moses are the cautionary "who." Corporate privilege is not personal faith. The few who believed do not cancel the mass who rebelled.

Hebrews 3:17

17And with whom was he provoked for forty years? Was it not with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?

Arminian

With whom was he provoked for forty years? With those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness. The questions tighten: rebellion, then sin, then corpses in the desert. The history is bodily and final for that generation's march into rest.

Arminian pastoral: shared—sin and divine provocation are linked; wilderness death is the displayed outcome. No camp softens the carcasses into metaphor that never threatened anyone.

Reformed

Calvin: God had never been angry with his people except for just causes (cf. 1 Cor. 10:5–6). As many chastisements as we read, so many grievous sins provoked vengeance. Unbelief was the chief of all evils—mentioned last yet primary; once unbelievers, they never ceased adding sin to sin. Those who through unbelief rejected the land pursued obstinacy in lust, murmuring, adultery, and superstition.

Reformed pastoral: shared—provoked for forty years with those who sinned. Just anger; real fall in the wilderness. The letter will next name disobedience and unbelief as the bar to rest.

Hebrews 3:18

18And to whom did he swear that they would not enter his rest, but to those who were disobedient?

Arminian

To whom did he swear they would not enter his rest? To those who were disobedient. The oath lands on the disobedient—unbelief's active refusal, not a minor stumble. Rest is refused where disobedience hardens the heart against the promise.

Arminian pastoral: shared—disobedience draws the oath. Both camps agree the barred are the disobedient unbelieving. The fork is not whether God swore truly; it is how that history presses perseverance on us who hear Today.

Reformed

Calvin: To whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? Unbelief prevented enjoying God's kindness; contempt of his word led them to sin. As at first they deserved through unbelief that God deprive them of the promised rest, so whatever sin followed flowed from the same fountain. He speaks of the community rather than every individual; many godly were not entangled or soon repented.

Reformed pastoral: shared—the sworn exclusion falls on the disobedient/unbelieving. Rest is God's gift; unbelief is the bar. Apply the oath as warning, not as curiosity about ancient geography.

Hebrews 3:19

19So we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief.

Arminian

So we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief. Wesley: So we see they could not enter in—though afterward they desired it. Desire after the door is shut does not reopen the oath. Unbelief, not lack of miracles, barred them.

Arminian pastoral: shared conclusion—unbelief made entry impossible. For readers: an evil unbelieving heart still threatens falling away from the living God; hold original confidence firm to the end. The wilderness generation is a real mirror, not a scarecrow.

Reformed

Calvin: So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief—unbelief the chief evil and primary cause of the curse. From the beginning it prevented enjoyment of God's kindness. The apostle speaks of the whole community; the statement of the whole for a part is common when a multitude is in view.

Reformed pastoral: shared—unable to enter because of unbelief. Those who finally enter are those who believe and hold firm; unbelief marks those who never truly entered the rest God gives. The warning remains the means that keeps the church soft to Today.

Key contrast Both conclude with the writer: unbelief barred the wilderness generation, and the warning is real. Arminians typically press the mirror toward a real danger of an unbelieving heart and the need to hold firm so as to share in Christ and enter rest. Reformed readers typically press the same unbelief as the mark of those who never truly entered, while those who finally share hold firm—the oath and the exhortation serving as God's means, not as proof that perseverance is optional or that the elect may be lost.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the Spirit's Today, the wilderness hardening, mutual exhortation, and sharing in Christ if we hold firm—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Hebrews 3:7–19.

Arminian Notable voices

“Wherefore—seeing he is faithful, be not ye unfaithful. Take heed, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief—unbelief is the parent of all evil, and the very essence of unbelief lies in departing from God, as the living God—the fountain of all our life, holiness, happiness. Exhort one another, while it is called To-day—this to-day will not last for ever. For we are made partakers of Christ—and we shall still partake of him and all his benefits, if we hold fast our faith unto the end. If—but not else; and a supposition made by the Holy Ghost is equal to the strongest assertion. Were they not all that came out of Egypt—an awful consideration! The whole elect people of God (a very few excepted) provoked God presently after their great deliverance, continued to grieve his Spirit for forty years, and perished in their sin. So we see they could not enter in—though afterward they desired it.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament, Hebrews 3

Reformed Notable voices

“He proceeds in his exhortation, that they were to obey Christ speaking to them; and that he might add more weight to it, he confirms it by the testimony of David. As the Holy Ghost saith—this availed much more to touch their hearts than if he had quoted David by name; the words adduced from the prophets are those of God and not of men. Harden not your hearts—our rebellion against God flows from willful wickedness, by which we obstruct the entrance of his grace. Take heed, brethren, lest there be at any time in any of you a wicked heart of unbelief—he addressed them who had been imbued with the elements of Christianity; the sin of defection is accompanied with perfidy. Exhort one another—unless our faith be now and then raised up, it will lie prostrate; unless it be warmed, it will be frozen. For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence steadfast unto the end—lest under the pretext of the grace they had obtained they should indulge carnal security, he says there was need of perseverance. So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief—unbelief was the chief of all their evils and the primary cause of their curse.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Hebrews 3:7–19
