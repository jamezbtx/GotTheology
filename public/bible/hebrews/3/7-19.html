Passage

Hebrews 3

Verses 7–19 · WEB

Quoting Psalm 95, the writer urges the readers not to harden their hearts as Israel did in the wilderness. That generation saw God’s works for forty years yet tested him, and God swore they would not enter his rest. The readers must take care that none of them has an evil, unbelieving heart that leads them to fall away from the living God, and must exhort one another daily. We have come to share in Christ, if we hold our original confidence firm to the end. Israel failed to enter because of unbelief. This passage is central to the warning theme of Hebrews.

Hebrews 3:7

7Therefore, even as the Holy Spirit says, “Today if you will hear his voice,

Arminian

“Today, if you hear his voice.” Arminian readers see the Spirit addressing every hearer with a genuine call that requires a response now.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit speaking through Scripture with present authority. The call is urgent for everyone who hears.

Hebrews 3:8

8don’t harden your hearts as in the rebellion, in the day of the trial in the wilderness,

Arminian

Do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion. Arminian readers see hardening as a real human choice that can be resisted.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the human responsibility not to harden one’s heart, alongside God’s sovereign hardening of those who persist in unbelief (Romans 9:18).

Hebrews 3:9

9where your fathers tested me and tried me, and saw my deeds for forty years.

Arminian

Your fathers tested me and saw my works for forty years. Arminian readers see the guilt of people who resist God despite clear evidence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that seeing God’s works does not by itself produce faith. Only grace changes the heart.

Hebrews 3:10

10Therefore I was displeased with that generation, and said, ‘They always err in their heart, but they didn’t know my ways.’

Arminian

They always go astray in their hearts; they have not known my ways. Arminian readers see a pattern of persistent unbelief that provokes God’s displeasure.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the corruption of the heart as the root of rebellion.

Hebrews 3:11

11As I swore in my wrath, ‘They will not enter into my rest.’”

Arminian

As I swore in my wrath, they shall not enter my rest. Arminian readers see that people who were delivered from Egypt still failed to enter the land, a picture of believers who might fail to reach final rest.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the wilderness generation as a mixed company. Many were outwardly redeemed but never truly believed.

Hebrews 3:12

12Beware, brothers, lest perhaps there might be in any one of you an evil heart of unbelief, in falling away from the living God;

Arminian

Take care, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God. Arminian readers see “brothers” warned against falling away, which they take as showing it is possible for believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the warning addressed to the visible church, where some may not truly believe. It is also a means God uses to keep true believers faithful.

Key contrast Both take the warning seriously. Arminian readers see brothers in the faith who could truly fall away. Reformed readers see a warning addressed to the mixed visible church and used by God to preserve his elect.

Hebrews 3:13

13but exhort one another day by day, so long as it is called “today”, lest any one of you be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.

Arminian

Exhort one another every day, so that none of you is hardened by sin’s deceitfulness. Arminian readers see mutual encouragement as a real safeguard against apostasy.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church’s daily care as one of God’s means of preserving his people.

Hebrews 3:14

14For we have become partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence firm to the end,

Arminian

We have come to share in Christ, if we hold our original confidence firm to the end. Arminian readers see holding firm as a condition of finally sharing in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the tense: “we have come to share” is past. Holding firm proves that one has already truly shared in Christ.

Key contrast Both see perseverance as necessary. Arminian readers treat it as a condition for remaining a sharer in Christ. Reformed readers read the past tense as showing that perseverance is the evidence of a sharing already begun.

Hebrews 3:15

15while it is said, “Today if you will hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts, as in the rebellion.”

Arminian

“Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts.” Arminian readers see the call repeated because the danger is real and present.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the repetition as stressing urgency. Today is the time to respond.

Hebrews 3:16

16For who, when they heard, rebelled? Wasn’t it all those who came out of Egypt led by Moses?

Arminian

Who heard and rebelled? Was it not all who left Egypt under Moses? Arminian readers see that those who experienced God’s deliverance could still rebel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see outward deliverance without inward faith, which is a warning to the visible church.

Hebrews 3:17

17With whom was he displeased forty years? Wasn’t it with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?

Arminian

With whom was he provoked for forty years? Those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness. Arminian readers see the consequences of persistent sin.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s judgment on unbelief within the covenant community.

Hebrews 3:18

18To whom did he swear that they wouldn’t enter into his rest, but to those who were disobedient?

Arminian

To whom did he swear they would not enter his rest, but to the disobedient? Arminian readers see disobedience as the expression of unbelief.

Reformed

Reformed readers see unbelief and disobedience as inseparable.

Hebrews 3:19

19We see that they weren’t able to enter in because of unbelief.

Arminian

They were unable to enter because of unbelief. Arminian readers see unbelief as the decisive reason people fail to enter God’s rest, a choice for which they are responsible.

Reformed

Reformed readers see unbelief as the root sin that excludes from God’s rest. Faith alone enters.
