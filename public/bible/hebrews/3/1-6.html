Passage

Hebrews 3

Verses 1–6 · WEB

The writer calls “holy brothers” to consider Jesus, the apostle and high priest of their confession. Moses was faithful as a servant in God’s house, but Jesus is faithful as a Son over the house, and is worthy of greater glory as the builder is greater than the house. Believers are his house, if they hold fast their confidence and hope. The conditional “if” in verse 6 introduces a theme that runs through the next chapter. The two traditions read it differently.

Hebrews 3:1

1Therefore, holy brothers, partakers of a heavenly calling, consider the Apostle and High Priest of our confession: Jesus,

Arminian

Holy brothers, sharers in a heavenly calling, consider Jesus. Arminian readers see the heavenly calling as an invitation that believers have answered and must continue to follow.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the heavenly calling as God’s effectual summons, which marks out his people.

Hebrews 3:2

2who was faithful to him who appointed him, as also Moses was in all his house.

Arminian

Jesus was faithful to the one who appointed him, as Moses was faithful in God’s house. Arminian readers see Christ’s faithfulness as the model for believers’ faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s faithfulness as the ground of salvation. He accomplished everything the Father gave him to do.

Hebrews 3:3

3For he has been counted worthy of more glory than Moses, because he who built the house has more honor than the house.

Arminian

Jesus is worthy of more glory than Moses, as the builder has more honor than the house. Arminian readers see Christ’s superiority as a reason not to turn back to the old covenant.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as the divine builder of God’s people. That points to his deity.

Hebrews 3:4

4For every house is built by someone; but he who built all things is God.

Arminian

Every house is built by someone, but the builder of all things is God. Both traditions see God as creator of all. Arminian readers see this as grounding Christ’s authority.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the identification of Christ with God as builder, which affirms his divine nature.

Hebrews 3:5

5Moses indeed was faithful in all his house as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were afterward to be spoken,

Arminian

Moses was faithful as a servant, testifying to what would be spoken later. Arminian readers see Moses’ ministry as pointing forward to Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the old covenant as a testimony to the new, so both testaments form one story of redemption.

Hebrews 3:6

6but Christ is faithful as a Son over his house. We are his house, if we hold fast our confidence and the glorying of our hope firm to the end.

Arminian

Christ is faithful as a Son over God’s house, and we are his house, if we hold fast our confidence. Arminian readers see the “if” as a real condition: believers remain part of God’s house by continuing in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the “if” as evidence, not a cause. Holding fast shows that someone truly belongs to Christ’s house.

Key contrast Both read the condition seriously. Arminian readers see continuing in faith as a condition of remaining in Christ’s house. Reformed readers see continuing in faith as the evidence that one is truly part of it.
