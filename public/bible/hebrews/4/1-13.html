Passage

Hebrews 4

Verses 1–13 · WEB · Voices

The wilderness warning of Psalm 95 still presses: while the promise of entering God's rest stands, fear lest any seem to have failed to reach it. The good news preached to us as to them, yet without faith the message did not benefit. Believers enter that rest, though God swore the unbelieving would not—and the works were finished from the foundation of the world. Creation's seventh-day rest and Canaan under Joshua are not the final rest; Today is still appointed, and a Sabbath rest remains for the people of God. Therefore strive to enter, lest any fall by the same disobedience. The word of God is living and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword, discerning the heart; no creature that is hidden from his sight—all are naked and exposed to him to whom we must give an account.

Hebrews 4:1

1Let’s fear therefore, lest perhaps anyone of you should seem to have come short of a promise of entering into his rest.

Arminian

Therefore—on the wilderness oath and unbelief of chapter 3—since a promise of entering his rest remains, let’s fear that any of us should seem to have come short of it. The promise is not revoked; the danger of missing it is named for the church. Wesley's whole chapter presses that a rest remains, and that falling short is no empty scare.

Both camps take let us fear seriously—the warning is no empty threat, not fake. The fork is not whether God meant the caution, but how far a hearer may fail to reach the rest. Fear here is holy urgency against presumption while the promise still stands.

Reformed

Calvin: Let us therefore fear—not that which shakes the confidence of faith, but such as fills us with concern that we grow not torpid with indifference. Fear lest we be unfaithful to God's grace. No one comes short of the promise left us except he who by rejecting grace has first renounced the promise; by the fall of others we are taught humility and watchfulness.

The promise still stands, and let us fear is earnest means, not theater. Those who finally enter are kept; the fear God commands is the path of perseverance, not a wager that he may lose his own.

Key contrast Both refuse to treat let us fear or failed to reach it as sham while the promise stands. Arminians hear an actual danger that some who hear may seem—and in truth be—short of the rest; holy fear guards against failing to reach what grace still offers. Reformed interpreters hear the same urgency as God's means: the elect enter; fear guards against torpid unfaithfulness, and none finally come short except those who reject the grace they professed.

Hebrews 4:2

2For indeed we have had good news preached to us, even as they also did, but the word they heard didn’t profit them, because it wasn’t mixed with faith by those who heard.

Arminian

The good news came to us as it did to them, but the word they heard did them no good, because it was not mixed with faith in those who heard. Wesley: the word which they heard did not profit them—so far from it, that it increased their damnation. It is then only when it is mixed with faith that it exerts its saving power.

Hearing without faith does not save. Privilege of the gospel is no shelter if the heart will not trust. Unite the message with faith; otherwise the same good news that offers rest leaves the hearer unhelped.

Reformed

Calvin: the doctrine by which God invites us is the same as that delivered to the fathers—so we may know the calling will be no more profitable to us than to them except we make it sure by faith. Hearing is useless except the word be received by faith; the word separated from faith confers no good, though its power does not depend on us.

Gospel preached, yet without faith no benefit. The wilderness hearers are the mirror. Receive the word by faith; bare audition is not entrance into rest.

Hebrews 4:3

3For we who have believed do enter into that rest, even as he has said, “As I swore in my wrath, they will not enter into my rest;” although the works were finished from the foundation of the world.

Arminian

We who have believed do enter into that rest—set against the oath, They will not enter into my rest—though God's works were finished from the foundation of the world. Wesley: we only that have believed enter into the rest; the proposition is that a rest remains for us, proved through the verses that follow, and it is the eternal rest in heaven.

Faith is the door; unbelief drew the oath. Believing is not a casual label; it is how one enters. The finished works of creation do not cancel a rest still held out to faith today.

Reformed

Calvin: we which have believed do enter into rest—an argument from the contrary: unbelief alone shuts us out; faith alone opens an entrance. God angry with the unbelieving swore they should not partake; then they enter where unbelief does not hinder, provided God invites. Speaking in the first person, he allures with sweetness, separating them from aliens.

Believers enter; the oath fell on unbelief. The rest open to faith is God's gift. Those who believe are those who enter—not a different gospel, but the same promise received.

Key contrast Both agree that faith opens entrance and unbelief drew the oath. Arminians stress "we who have believed" as the living condition of entering, with the later warnings still naming a peril of falling short. Reformed readers typically hear the same clause as describing who truly enter: believers do enter; the oath and the exhortations are means that keep the flock believing, not proof that God's elect finally miss his rest.

Hebrews 4:4

4For he has said this somewhere about the seventh day, “God rested on the seventh day from all his works;”

Arminian

Somewhere he has spoken of the seventh day: God rested on the seventh day from all his works. Wesley: long after he had rested from his works, he speaks again (Gen. 2:2)—so the Psalm's rest is not merely that creation rest.

Shared typology, not a camp fork—creation rest is not in doubt, yet the letter will show another rest still ahead. Both camps agree a rest remains beyond the seventh day of Genesis.

Reformed

Calvin: to define what our rest is, he reminds us that God, having finished creation, rested from his works—and finally concludes that the true rest of the faithful, continuing forever, will be when they rest as God did.

Genesis rest is cited to open, not close, the argument. The church is not told that Canaan or creation exhausted God's rest. A fuller rest is in view for the people of God.

Hebrews 4:5

5and in this place again, “They will not enter into my rest.”

Arminian

Again in this passage: They will not enter into my rest. Wesley: in this psalm, of a rest yet to come. The oath and the seventh-day word stand side by side so the reader sees the rest spoken of is not spent.

The repeated exclusion line keeps the warning warm. Rest remains; disobedience still bars. Do not soften the oath into ancient scenery only.

Reformed

Calvin: God who is said to have rested declared long after that he would not give his rest to the unbelieving—he would have so declared to no purpose had he not intended that the faithful should rest after his own example.

"They will not enter" returns on purpose. The God who rested invites and warns. Unbelief still excludes; faith still enters.

Hebrews 4:6

6Seeing therefore it remains that some should enter into it, and they to whom the good news was preached before failed to enter in because of disobedience,

Arminian

It remains that some should enter into it; those who formerly received the good news was preached before failed to enter in because of disobedience. The logic is pastoral: if some must enter, and the first hearers failed through disobedience, the promise has not been emptied—it still summons faith and obedience.

Disobedience barred the former hearers; a remaining entrance is still held out. The wilderness failure is a caution for those who hear good news now.

Reformed

Calvin: it remaineth that some must enter in—for if not to enter is the punishment of unbelief, then an entrance is open to believers. As the fathers' unbelief deprived them of the promised possession, the promise is renewed so that children may possess what fathers neglected.

Some enter; the disobedient formerly failed. The remaining rest is not typology trivia; it is the open door faith must take, under the same warning.

Hebrews 4:7

7he again defines a certain day, “today”, saying through David so long a time afterward (just as has been said), “Today if you will hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts.”

Arminian

Again defines a certain day, today—through David so long a time afterward. Wesley: after so long a time—above four hundred years from Moses and Joshua to David. Today returns; harden not your hearts returns.

Today is still God's appointed day. Hearing without hardening remains the living response. The long gap after Joshua proves the call was not retired when Canaan was entered.

Reformed

Calvin: there is another today appointed for us in the Psalm because the former people had been excluded; the Holy Spirit warns that we may not do the same and incur the same punishment. Were nothing promised today, how could the warning suit—take heed lest the same thing happen to you as to the fathers?

Today through David proves a rest and a call still open. Do not harden; the Spirit's date-stamp is present tense for the church.

Hebrews 4:8

8For if Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken afterward of another day.

Arminian

If Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken afterward of another day later on. Wesley: the rest—all the rest which God had promised. Joshua's gift was true; it was not final.

Not a camp fork on typology. Both agree Canaan under Joshua was not the last rest. Another day later on means a farther rest still stands for the people of God.

Reformed

Calvin: he meant not to deny that David understood by rest the land of Canaan into which Joshua led the people; but he denies this to be the final rest to which the faithful aspire. Canaan was an image and symbol of the spiritual inheritance; when they possessed it they ought to have advanced toward what was spiritual.

Joshua did not exhaust God's rest. The land was gift and shadow; another day later on summons the church beyond Canaan to the rest that remains.

Hebrews 4:9

9There remains therefore a Sabbath rest for the people of God.

Arminian

So then, There remains therefore a Sabbath rest for the people of God. Wesley: therefore—since he still speaks of another day, there must remain a farther, even an eternal, rest for the people of God.

Shared conclusion of the typology—Sabbath rest remains. Not debate over shadows as the fork between camps, but agreement that God's people still have a rest ahead, and must hear Today.

Reformed

Calvin: he draws the conclusion that there is a sabbathizing reserved for God's people—a spiritual rest to which God daily invites us.

There remains therefore a Sabbath rest. The people of God are not left with only past types. Invitation and warning alike press toward that remaining rest.

Hebrews 4:10

10For he who has entered into his rest has himself also rested from his works, as God did from his.

Arminian

Whoever has entered into his rest has himself also rested from his works, as God did from his. Wesley: for they do not yet so rest; therefore a fuller rest remains for them. Entrance means cessation after God's pattern—not mere geography.

True entrance includes resting from one's works as God rested. The fuller rest still ahead keeps striving from becoming idle presumption.

Reformed

Calvin: this defines that perpetual Sabbath in which there is the highest felicity—likeness to God when united to him. The conformation takes place when we rest from our works; man becomes happy by self-denial, ceasing from his own works to give place to God. Though completion cannot be attained in this life, we ought ever to strive for it.

Entering God's rest means resting from our works as God did. Believers enter on condition that by running they continually go forward; the rest does not void holy striving.

Hebrews 4:11

11Let’s therefore give diligence to enter into that rest, lest anyone fall after the same example of disobedience.

Arminian

Let’s therefore give diligence to enter into that rest, lest anyone fall after the same example of disobedience. Wesley: lest any one should fall—into perdition. The wilderness pattern of disobedience is the warned fall; striving is the appointed response while rest remains.

Pastorally: both take strive and "fall" seriously—the warning is in earnest. Striving is not self-salvation; it is the earnest path grace enables so that none falls short by the same disobedience. Do not dissolve the "lest" into a threat that never lands.

Reformed

Calvin: having pointed out the goal, he exhorts us to pursue our course. He sets falling in opposition to entering, alluding to those who fell in the wilderness because they were rebellious. After the same example—punishment for unbelief and obstinacy is set before us as in a picture; a similar end awaits us if the same unbelief be found in us. To fall means to perish as punishment.

Strive to enter; do not fall by the same disobedience. The elect are kept through means; striving is God's instrument of perseverance, not proof that he fails to bring his people in.

Key contrast Both refuse to treat "give diligence to enter" or "fall after the same example of disobedience" as empty. Arminians typically hear a peril of falling—Wesley says into perdition—so striving is urgent lest any fail to reach the remaining rest. Reformed interpreters hear the same commands as means of perseverance: those who finally enter strive and do not fall in unbelief; the wilderness example warns and drives the flock, while God keeps his own through that very striving.

Hebrews 4:12

12For the word of God is living and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing even to the dividing of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow, and is able to discern the thoughts and intentions of the heart.

Arminian

For the word of God is living and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing even to the dividing of soul and spirit, of joints and marrow, and quick to discern the thoughts and intentions of the heart. Wesley: the word—preached (4:2) and armed with threatenings (4:3)—is living and powerful, attended with the power of the living God, conveying either life or death to the hearers; penetrating the heart; a discerner not only of the thoughts but also of the intentions.

Searching judgment, not a camp fork. The same word that offers rest also lays the heart open. Hypocrisy cannot hide; strive and fear under a word that truly discerns.

Reformed

Calvin: he says this of the word's efficacy that they might know it cannot be despised with impunity—whenever the Lord addresses us, he deals seriously to touch all our inmost thoughts and feelings. It is living and full of hidden power, leaving nothing in man untouched; a discerner that brings light where unbelief and hypocrisy had darkness.

The word is living, active, and searching. Consciences are summoned as soon as it sounds. Take the rest-warning as speech God means; it pierces where we would hide.

Hebrews 4:13

13There is no creature that is hidden from his sight, but all things are naked and laid open before the eyes of him to whom we must give an account.

Arminian

No creature that is hidden from his sight; all things are naked and laid open before the eyes of him to whom we must give an account. Wesley: it is God whose word is thus powerful; it is God in whose sight every creature is manifest; his word working on the conscience gives the fullest conviction. All things are naked and opened—alluding to sacrifices flayed and cleft so that everything without and within was exposed to open view.

Accountability before the God who sees. The rest promise and the wilderness warning both stand under his eyes. Give account as those who have heard Today; do not harden as though unseen.

Reformed

Calvin: for confirms the truth—whatever is hid in man is discerned by God's word, from the nature of God himself. No creature is hid from his eyes; nothing so deep in the soul that it cannot be drawn forth by that word which resembles its author. Render: with whom we have to do—it is God who deals with us; therefore whenever his word is set before us, we ought to tremble, for nothing is hid from him.

Naked and exposed to him to whom we must give an account. The living word searches because the living God sees. Fear, strive, and believe under that gaze; the warning of rest is spoken by the One who misses nothing.

“But the word which they heard did not profit them—so far from it, that it increased their damnation. It is then only when it is mixed with faith, that it exerts its saving power. Therefore—since he still speaks of another day, there must remain a farther, even an eternal, rest for the people of God. Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any one should fall—into perdition.
