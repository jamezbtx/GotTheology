Passage

Hebrews 4

Verses 1–13 · WEB

The writer applies the wilderness story to his readers. Since the promise of entering God’s rest still stands, they should fear lest any of them fail to reach it. The good news came to Israel as it has come to us, but it did not benefit them because it was not joined with faith. A Sabbath rest remains for the people of God, so believers must strive to enter it, lest anyone fall by the same disobedience. The passage ends with the word of God, living and active, which exposes the heart to the God to whom all must give account. Both traditions see faith as the way into God’s rest. They differ on the meaning of the warning to fall.

Hebrews 4:1

1Let’s fear therefore, lest perhaps anyone of you should seem to have come short of a promise of entering into his rest.

Arminian

While the promise of entering his rest remains, let us fear lest any of you seem to have failed to reach it. Arminian readers see a healthy fear that keeps believers watchful.

Reformed

Reformed readers see godly fear as a fruit of grace, which keeps believers from presumption.

Hebrews 4:2

2For indeed we have had good news preached to us, even as they also did, but the word they heard didn’t profit them, because it wasn’t mixed with faith by those who heard.

Arminian

The good news came to us as to them, but the message did not benefit them, because it was not united by faith. Arminian readers see faith as the necessary response that makes the message effective.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as necessary and as God’s gift. Hearing without faith brings no benefit.

Hebrews 4:3

3For we who have believed do enter into that rest, even as he has said, “As I swore in my wrath, they will not enter into my rest;” although the works were finished from the foundation of the world.

Arminian

We who have believed enter that rest. Arminian readers see rest entered now by faith and completed at the end.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers already entering God’s rest, which assures them of its completion.

Hebrews 4:4

4For he has said this somewhere about the seventh day, “God rested on the seventh day from all his works;”

Arminian

God rested on the seventh day from all his works. Both traditions see creation rest as the pattern. Arminian readers see God inviting humanity to share his rest.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s rest as the goal of creation, which Christ’s work makes available to his people.

Hebrews 4:5

5and in this place again, “They will not enter into my rest.”

Arminian

“They shall not enter my rest.” Arminian readers see the real exclusion of those who refused to believe.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s oath against unbelief as unchanging.

Hebrews 4:6

6Seeing therefore it remains that some should enter into it, and they to whom the good news was preached before failed to enter in because of disobedience,

Arminian

Some remain to enter it, and those who first heard failed through disobedience. Arminian readers see the offer of rest still open.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s purpose that some will enter, which cannot fail.

Hebrews 4:7

7he again defines a certain day, “today”, saying through David so long a time afterward (just as has been said), “Today if you will hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts.”

Arminian

God again appoints a day, “Today.” Arminian readers see each generation receiving a genuine invitation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the gospel call renewed in every age.

Hebrews 4:8

8For if Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken afterward of another day.

Arminian

If Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken of another day. Arminian readers see the land as a picture of a fuller rest.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the land as a type fulfilled in Christ and the final rest.

Hebrews 4:9

9There remains therefore a Sabbath rest for the people of God.

Arminian

A Sabbath rest remains for the people of God. Arminian readers see the eternal rest awaiting all who persevere in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the rest as secured for God’s people. Many Reformed readers also see in it support for the continuing Lord’s Day.

Hebrews 4:10

10For he who has entered into his rest has himself also rested from his works, as God did from his.

Arminian

Whoever enters God’s rest rests from his works as God did. Arminian readers see rest from striving for acceptance, received by faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see resting from works as a picture of justification by grace.

Hebrews 4:11

11Let’s therefore give diligence to enter into that rest, lest anyone fall after the same example of disobedience.

Arminian

Let us strive to enter that rest, so that no one falls by the same disobedience. Arminian readers see falling as a real possibility that diligence guards against.

Reformed

Reformed readers see striving as the means God uses to bring his people into rest, which they will reach.

Key contrast Both see diligence as necessary. Arminian readers believe someone could truly fall short of God’s rest. Reformed readers see the striving as the God-given means by which his people certainly arrive.

Hebrews 4:12

12For the word of God is living and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing even to the dividing of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow, and is able to discern the thoughts and intentions of the heart.

Arminian

The word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, discerning the thoughts of the heart. Arminian readers see the word exposing the heart and calling for honest response.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the word as the Spirit’s instrument, piercing and convicting.

Hebrews 4:13

13There is no creature that is hidden from his sight, but all things are naked and laid open before the eyes of him to whom we must give an account.

Arminian

No creature is hidden from his sight; all are exposed to the one to whom we must give account. Arminian readers see accountability before an all-seeing God as a reason to persevere.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s omniscience as both warning and comfort, since nothing escapes him.
