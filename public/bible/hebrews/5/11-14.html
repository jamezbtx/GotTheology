Passage

Hebrews 5

Verses 11–14 · WEB · Voices

The writer pauses before explaining Melchizedek, because his readers have become dull of hearing. By now they should be teachers, but they need milk again rather than solid food. Those who live on milk are unskilled in the word of righteousness, while the mature have trained their powers of discernment by constant practice. This rebuke leads into the warning of chapter 6. Both traditions see a call to spiritual growth, and they differ on how serious the danger of stagnation is.

Hebrews 5:11

11About him we have many words to say, and hard to interpret, seeing you have become dull of hearing.

Arminian

About this we have much to say, and it is hard to explain, since you have become dull of hearing. Arminian readers see spiritual dullness as a condition believers can fall into through neglect, which can lead toward greater danger.

Reformed

Reformed readers see dullness as a real problem in the church, which God addresses through rebuke and teaching.

Hebrews 5:12

12For although by this time you should be teachers, you again need to have someone teach you the rudiments of the first principles of the revelations of God. You have come to need milk, and not solid food.

Arminian

By this time you ought to be teachers, but you need someone to teach you the basic principles again. Arminian readers see that believers can regress, and must keep growing to avoid falling back.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the call to mature in the knowledge of God’s word as a normal part of sanctification.

Hebrews 5:13

13For everyone who lives on milk is not experienced in the word of righteousness, for he is a baby.

Arminian

Everyone who lives on milk is unskilled in the word of righteousness, being a child. Arminian readers see immaturity as something believers are responsible to grow out of.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the need for sound teaching in the “word of righteousness,” which many read as the doctrine of justification.

Hebrews 5:14

14But solid food is for those who are full grown, who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern good and evil.

Arminian

Solid food is for the mature, who have trained their powers of discernment by practice. Arminian readers, especially Wesleyans, see maturity as growth in holiness of heart and life through practice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see discernment as the fruit of steady exposure to Scripture and obedience over time.

Key contrast Both see maturity as gained through practice. Wesleyan readers link it with growth toward entire sanctification. Reformed readers link it with progressive sanctification through the word and obedience.

Voices from both camps

Dullness of hearing, milk versus solid food, and senses trained to discern: older Arminian and Reformed writers, each in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“Concerning whom—The apostle here begins an important digression, wherein he reproves, admonishes, and exhorts the Hebrews. We—Preachers of the gospel.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Heb 5:11–14)

Reformed Notable voices

“He reminds them that they were hard or difficult things; not indeed to repel them, but to stimulate them to greater attention. He however states that the cause of the difficulty was not in the subject but in themselves.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Hebrews (on Heb 5:11)
