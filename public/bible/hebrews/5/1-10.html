Passage

Hebrews 5

Verses 1–10 · WEB · Voices

The writer explains what makes a true high priest. A priest is taken from among men, deals gently with the ignorant and wayward because he shares their weakness, and does not take the honor on himself but is called by God. So too Christ was appointed by the Father, who declared him a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek. In the days of his flesh he offered prayers with loud cries and tears, learned obedience through suffering, and, being made perfect, became the source of eternal salvation to all who obey him. Verse 9 is read differently by the two traditions.

Hebrews 5:1

1For every high priest, being taken from among men, is appointed for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins.

Arminian

Every high priest is chosen from among men to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins. Arminian readers see the priest representing all the people before God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the priest acting for those he represents, a picture of Christ’s representative work.

Hebrews 5:2

2The high priest can deal gently with those who are ignorant and going astray, because he himself is also surrounded with weakness.

Arminian

He can deal gently with the ignorant and wayward, since he himself is beset with weakness. Arminian readers see compassion for those who are struggling and may stray.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s sympathy as part of his care for his people, though unlike earthly priests he is without sin.

Hebrews 5:3

3Because of this, he must offer sacrifices for sins for the people, as well as for himself.

Arminian

He must offer sacrifice for his own sins as well as the people’s. Arminian readers see the limits of human priests.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the contrast with Christ, who needed no offering for himself (7:27).

Hebrews 5:4

4Nobody takes this honor on himself, but he is called by God, just like Aaron was.

Arminian

No one takes this honor for himself, but only when called by God. Arminian readers see God’s initiative in appointing the mediator.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereign appointment of the priest, fulfilled in the Father’s appointment of the Son.

Hebrews 5:5

5So also Christ didn’t glorify himself to be made a high priest, but it was he who said to him, “You are my Son. Today I have become your father.”

Arminian

Christ did not exalt himself to be high priest; the Father appointed him. Arminian readers see Christ’s humility as the model for believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the covenant of redemption, in which the Father appoints the Son to his saving office.

Hebrews 5:6

6As he says also in another place, “You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek.”

Arminian

“You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek.” Arminian readers see a permanent priesthood that always supports those who come to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see an eternal priesthood that secures his people forever.

Hebrews 5:7

7He, in the days of his flesh, having offered up prayers and petitions with strong crying and tears to him who was able to save him from death, and having been heard for his godly fear,

Arminian

In the days of his flesh he offered prayers with loud cries and tears, and was heard because of his reverence. Arminian readers see Christ’s real human struggle, especially in Gethsemane.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s true humanity and his perfect submission to the Father’s will.

Hebrews 5:8

8though he was a Son, yet learned obedience by the things which he suffered.

Arminian

Although he was a Son, he learned obedience through what he suffered. Arminian readers see Christ’s obedience tested and proven in real experience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s active obedience, fulfilled through suffering, credited to his people.

Hebrews 5:9

9Having been made perfect, he became to all of those who obey him the author of eternal salvation,

Arminian

Being made perfect, he became the source of eternal salvation to all who obey him. Arminian readers stress “all who obey him”: salvation is for everyone who responds to Christ with the obedience of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see “eternal salvation” as salvation that cannot be lost, and obedience as the mark of those Christ saves.

Key contrast Both join salvation to obedience. Arminian readers stress that salvation belongs to all who obey, which leaves room for continuing response. Reformed readers stress that the salvation is eternal and that obedience identifies those who receive it.

Hebrews 5:10

10named by God a high priest after the order of Melchizedek.

Arminian

He was designated by God a high priest after the order of Melchizedek. Arminian readers see God’s appointment as the guarantee of Christ’s priesthood.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s priesthood as unique and superior to Aaron’s, which the writer develops in chapter 7.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What older interpreters in each camp said about the Son who learned obedience in the days of His flesh and became the source of eternal salvation.

Arminian Notable voices

“Though he were a Son—This is interposed, lest any should be offended at all these instances of human weakness. In the garden, how frequently did he call God his Father!”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Heb 5:8–9)

Reformed Notable voices

“Yet learned he obedience. The proximate end of Christ’s sufferings was thus to habituate himself to obedience; not that he was driven to this by force, or that he had need of being thus exercised, as the case is with oxen or horses when their ferocity is to be tamed, for he was abundantly willing to render to his Father the obedience which he owed.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Hebrews (on Heb 5:8)
