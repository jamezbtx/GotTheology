Passage

Hebrews 9

Verses 23–28 · WEB

The heavenly things required better sacrifices than the earthly copies. Christ entered heaven itself to appear in God’s presence for us, not to offer himself repeatedly as the high priest did each year, but once at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself. As people die once and then face judgment, so Christ was offered once to bear the sins of many, and will appear a second time, not to deal with sin, but to save those eagerly waiting for him. Verse 28’s “many” is read differently by the two traditions.

Hebrews 9:23

23It was necessary therefore that the copies of the things in the heavens should be cleansed with these, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these.

Arminian

The heavenly things themselves needed better sacrifices. Arminian readers see Christ’s sacrifice as fitting the true, heavenly reality.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s sacrifice opening God’s heavenly presence to his people.

Hebrews 9:24

24For Christ hasn’t entered into holy places made with hands, which are representations of the true, but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us;

Arminian

Christ entered heaven itself, now to appear in God’s presence on our behalf. Arminian readers see Christ’s intercession supporting believers who keep coming to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s intercession as effective for all he represents, never failing.

Hebrews 9:25

25nor yet that he should offer himself often, as the high priest enters into the holy place year by year with blood not his own,

Arminian

He did not enter to offer himself repeatedly. Both traditions affirm one sacrifice. Arminian readers see nothing lacking in Christ’s offering.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the single offering as excluding any repeated sacrifice, including the Mass as Rome understood it.

Hebrews 9:26

26or else he must have suffered often since the foundation of the world. But now once at the end of the ages, he has been revealed to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.

Arminian

He appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself. Arminian readers see sin dealt with for the whole world, available to everyone who believes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sin actually put away for those Christ represents.

Hebrews 9:27

27Inasmuch as it is appointed for men to die once, and after this, judgment,

Arminian

It is appointed for people to die once, and after that comes judgment. Both traditions affirm a single life and judgment after death. Arminian readers stress the urgency of responding to grace in this life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the verse as excluding reincarnation and any second chance after death.

Hebrews 9:28

28so Christ also, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin, but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him.

Arminian

Christ was offered once to bear the sins of many and will appear a second time to save those eagerly waiting. Arminian readers see “many” as a Semitic way of saying “all,” as in Isaiah 53.

Reformed

Reformed readers see “many” as the definite number of those whose sins Christ actually bore.

Key contrast Both affirm that Christ bore sins and will return to save. Arminian readers read “many” as an inclusive term meaning all. Reformed readers read it as the definite people whose sins he bore.
