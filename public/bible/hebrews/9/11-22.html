Passage

Hebrews 9

Verses 11–22 · WEB

Christ has come as high priest of the good things that have come. Through a greater tent not made with hands, and by his own blood rather than the blood of animals, he entered the holy places once for all, securing eternal redemption. If animal blood purified the flesh, how much more will Christ’s blood, offered through the eternal Spirit, purify the conscience to serve the living God. He is the mediator of a new covenant, whose death redeems from transgressions so that the called may receive the eternal inheritance. Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. Both traditions see substitutionary atonement here. They differ on what “eternal redemption” secures and for whom.

Hebrews 9:11

11But Christ having come as a high priest of the coming good things, through the greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say, not of this creation,

Arminian

Christ came as high priest of the good things that have come, through the greater tent. Arminian readers see Christ’s ministry in God’s true presence on behalf of all who come to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s heavenly priesthood as the reality the earthly tabernacle pictured.

Hebrews 9:12

12nor yet through the blood of goats and calves, but through his own blood, entered in once for all into the Holy Place, having obtained eternal redemption.

Arminian

He entered once for all by his own blood, securing an eternal redemption. Arminian readers see redemption provided once for all, received by faith and enjoyed by those who remain in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see “securing an eternal redemption” as an accomplished redemption. Christ actually obtained it for those he represents, so it cannot fail.

Key contrast Both affirm that Christ’s blood obtains eternal redemption. Arminian readers see it provided for all and applied to those who believe. Reformed readers see it actually secured for a definite people whose salvation is therefore certain.

Hebrews 9:13

13For if the blood of goats and bulls, and the ashes of a heifer sprinkling those who have been defiled, sanctify to the cleanness of the flesh,

Arminian

The blood of goats and bulls and the ashes of a heifer sanctify for the purification of the flesh. Arminian readers see outward cleansing that pointed to something deeper.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the argument from lesser to greater beginning: if shadows cleansed outwardly, the reality cleanses inwardly.

Hebrews 9:14

14how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without defect to God, cleanse your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?

Arminian

How much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish, purify our conscience. Arminian readers see real inward cleansing that frees believers to serve God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Trinity at work in the atonement: the Son offering himself through the Spirit to the Father.

Hebrews 9:15

15For this reason he is the mediator of a new covenant, since a death has occurred for the redemption of the transgressions that were under the first covenant, that those who have been called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.

Arminian

He is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance. Arminian readers see “the called” as those who answer the gospel call.

Reformed

Reformed readers see “the called” as those effectually called by God, for whom Christ’s death secures the inheritance.

Key contrast Both see Christ’s death bringing the called into their inheritance. Arminian readers see the called as those who respond to the gospel invitation. Reformed readers see them as those whom God effectually calls.

Hebrews 9:16

16For where a last will and testament is, there must of necessity be the death of him who made it.

Arminian

Where a will is involved, the death of the one who made it must be established. Arminian readers see Christ’s death bringing the new covenant’s blessings into effect.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the covenant ratified by death. Some read the word as “will,” others as a covenant sealed by the death of a sacrifice.

Hebrews 9:17

17For a will is in force where there has been death, for it is never in force while he who made it lives.

Arminian

A will takes effect only at death. Arminian readers see the certainty that Christ’s death opened the inheritance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the inheritance guaranteed by a death that has already occurred.

Hebrews 9:18

18Therefore even the first covenant has not been dedicated without blood.

Arminian

Even the first covenant was inaugurated with blood. Arminian readers see continuity: God’s covenants are always sealed by sacrifice.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Sinai covenant’s blood as a type of Christ’s.

Hebrews 9:19

19For when every commandment had been spoken by Moses to all the people according to the law, he took the blood of the calves and the goats, with water and scarlet wool and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people,

Arminian

Moses sprinkled the book and the people with blood. Arminian readers see the people consecrated to God through the covenant.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the covenant people marked out by blood, a picture of Christ’s blood applied to his people.

Hebrews 9:20

20saying, “This is the blood of the covenant which God has commanded you.”

Arminian

“This is the blood of the covenant God commanded for you.” Arminian readers hear Jesus’ words at the Last Supper echoed here.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lord’s Supper as a sign and seal of the new covenant in Christ’s blood.

Hebrews 9:21

21He sprinkled the tabernacle and all the vessels of the ministry in the same way with the blood.

Arminian

He sprinkled the tent and vessels with blood. Arminian readers see everything in worship needing cleansing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the holiness of God requiring that everything used in his worship be purified.

Hebrews 9:22

22According to the law, nearly everything is cleansed with blood, and apart from shedding of blood there is no remission.

Arminian

Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins. Both traditions affirm that forgiveness requires sacrificial death. Arminian readers, following Grotius in part, often stress that the atonement upholds God’s moral government.

Reformed

Reformed readers see penal substitution: Christ bore the penalty of sin in his death, satisfying God’s justice.
