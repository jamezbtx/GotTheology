Passage

3 John 1

Verses 9–15 · ESV · Voices

The elder had written to the church, but Diotrephes, who loves to be first, will not acknowledge apostolic authority: wicked nonsense, refusal to welcome the brothers, stopping those who would, putting them out. Gaius is told not to imitate evil but good—whoever does good is from God; whoever does evil has not seen God. Demetrius has a good testimony from everyone and from the truth. Then pen and ink yield to the hope of face-to-face speech, peace, and friends greeted by name.

3 John 1:9

9I have written something to the church, but Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first, does not acknowledge our authority.

Arminian

The elder had written to the church, but Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first, does not acknowledge our authority. Wesley: probably the church to which the brothers came; Diotrephes perhaps its pastor, who loves to govern all things according to his own will. Receiveth us not—neither them nor me. So did the mystery of iniquity already work.

Pastoral church order: first-place love that will not receive the apostle or his letter. Not a culture-war on “cancel,” modern church politics, or internet blocking. The sin named is preeminence that refuses apostolic authority and, in v. 10, the brothers sent for the Name.

Reformed

Written to the church; Diotrephes puts himself first and will not acknowledge the elder. Calvin did not comment the name; the Johannine analogue is those who will not hear the apostolic “we” (1 John 4:6) and who go out from the fellowship.

Shared: this is order in a real congregation, not a brief on contemporary platforms. First-place is the diagnosis. Authority refused is the elder’s, tied to the truth of vv. 1–8. Keep the scene in that church.

3 John 1:10

10So if I come, I will bring up what he is doing, talking wicked nonsense against us. And not content with that, he refuses to welcome the brothers, and also stops those who want to and puts them out of the church.

Arminian

If I come, I will bring up what he is doing: wicked nonsense against us; he refuses to welcome the brothers, stops those who want to, puts them out of the church. Wesley: he prateth against us—them and me—thereby endeavouring to excuse himself.

The inverse of Gaius (vv. 5–8) and of 2 John 1:10–11: Diotrephes will not house the Name-workers and punishes those who would. That is church discipline pointed the wrong way, not a modern “cancel” parable. The elder will remember the deeds when he comes. Stay with the brothers, the nonsense, and the putting-out.

Reformed

Coming, he will bring up the deeds: wicked talk, refusing the brothers, stopping welcomers, putting them out. Calvin’s pastoral match: a faithful teacher does not connive at impostures; here the imposture is a local first-man blocking apostolic workers.

Shared: not internet blocking, not generic church-politics rant. The works are specific—slander of the elder, closed door to the strangers of vv. 5–8, coercion of the congregation. Order will be restored by the elder’s coming, not by a culture-war script.

3 John 1:11

11Beloved, do not imitate evil but imitate good. Whoever does good is from God; whoever does evil has not seen God.

Arminian

Beloved, do not imitate evil but imitate good. Wesley: follow not that which is evil—in Diotrephes—but that which is good—in Demetrius. Whoever does evil has not seen God—is a stranger to him.

Match 1 John 3: the doer shows the source. Gaius is not to copy the first-place man. Classical Arminians hear a real either/or: doing evil is not a slight slip in a seer of God; it is the mark of a stranger. Imitate Demetrius’s good, not Diotrephes’s evil. The warning can be heeded or refused.

Reformed

Do not imitate evil but good. Whoever does good is from God; whoever does evil has not seen God. Calvin on 1 John 2:29 and 3:6–7: everyone who does righteousness is born of Him; whoever sins has not seen Him nor known Him. He that does righteousness is righteous even as He is righteous.

Match 1 John 3 holiness notes: the practice shows the birth. Reformed: “has not seen God” describes the one whose life is evil, not a believer’s single fault (Calvin will not say the children of God are wholly without sin). Imitate the born-of-God pattern, not Diotrephes. Shared: do not copy evil. The slight fork is whether “has not seen God” can describe a Christian who is imitating Diotrephes, or only the one who was never born of God.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “whoever does evil has not seen God” as a stranger to Him: Gaius must not imitate Diotrephes, and doing that evil is not compatible with seeing God. Reformed readings, with Calvin on 1 John 3, take doing good as the mark of those born of God, and doing evil as not having seen Him—without treating every fault as unseen God, and without making Diotrephes a model a true child of God could finally become.

3 John 1:12

12Demetrius has received a good testimony from everyone, and from the truth itself. We also add our testimony, and you know that our testimony is true.

Arminian

Demetrius has a good testimony from everyone, and from the truth itself; we also testify, and you know our testimony is true. Wesley: from the truth itself—what they testify is the very truth. Yea, we also bear testimony—I and they that are with me.

Close the commendation. Demetrius is the good to imitate (v. 11), likely one of the workers Diotrephes would not welcome. No extra fight. Triple witness: everyone, the truth, the elder.

Reformed

Good testimony from everyone and from the truth itself; we add ours, and you know it is true. The truth is not a vague vibe; it is the same truth Gaius walks in (vv. 3–4).

Shared: no extra fight. Demetrius is commended so Gaius has a face for “imitate good.” The elder’s record matches 1 John’s “we”—apostolic testimony Gaius already knows to be true.

3 John 1:13

13I had much to write to you, but I would rather not write with pen and ink.

Arminian

Much to write, but not with pen and ink. Match 2 John 12: the needful things are on the page; the rest waits for presence.

Close the letter. No extra fight. Ink is enough for Diotrephes, Demetrius, and the ought of vv. 5–8.

Reformed

Much to write; rather not with pen and ink. Same close as 2 John 12.

Shared: no extra fight. What had to be said about first-place, the brothers, and the good example is written. Presence will finish it.

3 John 1:14

14I hope to see you soon, and we will talk face to face.

Arminian

Hope to see you soon, and talk face to face. Wesley’s 2 John parallel: not minded to write only these needful things when coming is better.

No extra fight. Face to face is how the elder will also “remember” Diotrephes’s deeds (v. 10). Joy and order both wait on the visit.

Reformed

Hope of seeing soon, face to face. The coming of v. 10 and the talk of v. 14 are one visit: presence for Gaius, and presence that will not leave Diotrephes’s deeds unspoken.

Shared: close like 2 John. Paper is stopgap. No extra fight.

3 John 1:15

15Peace be to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends, each by name.

Arminian

Peace be to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends, each by name. Wesley: salute the friends by name—in the same manner as if I had named them one by one. The word friend does not often occur in the New Testament, being swallowed up in the more endearing one of brother.

Close the letter like 2 John 12–13. Named people, peace, no extra fight. The household of the truth has names.

Reformed

Peace to you; friends greet; greet the friends by name. Calvin did not comment the line. The close is personal, as 2 John ended with the elect sister’s children.

Shared: no extra fight. Peace is the last word over a letter that had to name Diotrephes. By name keeps the church a set of faces, not a machine.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Diotrephes who loves to be first, imitating good not evil, Demetrius’s testimony, and a letter closed face to face—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside 3 John 1:9–15.

Arminian Notable voices

“I wrote to the church—probably that to which they came. But Diotrephes—perhaps the pastor of it. Who loveth to have the preeminence among them—to govern all things according to his own will. Receiveth us not—neither them nor me. So did the mystery of iniquity already work! He prateth against us—both them and me, thereby endeavouring to excuse himself. Follow not that which is evil—in Diotrephes. But that which is good—in Demetrius. He that doeth evil hath not seen God—is a stranger to him. Salute the friends by name—in the same manner as if I had named them one by one.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on 3 John 9–15)

Reformed Notable voices

“They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would have continued with us. If ye know that he is righteous, ye know that every one that doeth righteousness is born of him. Whosoever sinneth hath not seen him, neither known him. Little children, let no man deceive you: he that doeth righteousness is righteous, even as he is righteous. He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked.”
John Calvin · Commentary on 1 John (on 1 John 2:19, 29; 3:6–7; 2:6)

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named. Calvin left no commentary on 3 John; the Reformed voice here is his comment on doing righteousness, seeing God, and not imitating those who go out, in 1 John 2–3.
