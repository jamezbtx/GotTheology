Passage

2 Peter 1

Verses 12–21 · WEB

Knowing that his death is near, Peter resolves to keep reminding his readers of the truth so they will remember it after he is gone. He insists that the apostles did not invent clever myths; they were eyewitnesses of Christ’s majesty at the transfiguration and heard the Father’s voice. Prophetic Scripture is even more certain, because it did not originate in human will; holy men spoke as the Holy Spirit carried them along.

2 Peter 1:12

12Therefore I will not be negligent to remind you of these things, though you know them and are established in the present truth.

Arminian

Peter will keep reminding them even though they already know the truth. Arminian readers see that established believers still need reminders, because steadfastness must be maintained.

Reformed

Reformed readers see reminders as one of the ordinary means God uses to preserve his people in the truth.

2 Peter 1:13

13I think it right, as long as I am in this tent, to stir you up by reminding you,

Arminian

As long as he lives in “this tent,” Peter will stir them up. Arminian commentators note that the apostle views his remaining life as an opportunity for service.

Reformed

Reformed commentators see the body described as a temporary dwelling, a theme Paul also develops in 2 Corinthians 5.

2 Peter 1:14

14knowing that the putting off of my tent comes swiftly, even as our Lord Jesus Christ made clear to me.

Arminian

Peter knows his death is coming soon, as Jesus showed him. Arminian interpreters recall John 21:18–19, where Jesus foretold how Peter would die.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters see Peter facing death calmly, because its time was set by the Lord.

2 Peter 1:15

15Yes, I will make every effort that you may always be able to remember these things even after my departure.

Arminian

He will make every effort to ensure that they remember after his departure. Many Arminian and Reformed readers alike see here a hint that Peter wanted his teaching preserved in writing.

Reformed

Reformed readers add that the passage shows concern for a settled body of apostolic teaching that the church can return to.

2 Peter 1:16

16For we didn’t follow cunningly devised fables when we made known to you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but we were eyewitnesses of his majesty.

Arminian

The apostles did not follow cleverly devised myths but were eyewitnesses of Christ’s majesty. Arminian expositors note that faith rests on historical events that can be testified to.

Reformed

Reformed expositors stress that the gospel is grounded in God’s acts in history, not in human speculation.

2 Peter 1:17

17For he received from God the Father honor and glory when the voice came to him from the Majestic Glory, “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

Arminian

The voice from the Majestic Glory said, “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.” Arminian readers see the Father openly affirming the Son before witnesses.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Father’s declaration as the public confirmation of the Son’s eternal identity and mission.

2 Peter 1:18

18We heard this voice come out of heaven when we were with him on the holy mountain.

Arminian

Peter heard the voice himself on the holy mountain. Arminian commentators value the personal nature of the testimony.

Reformed

Reformed commentators point to the mountain becoming holy because God revealed himself there.

2 Peter 1:19

19We have the more sure word of prophecy; and you do well that you heed it as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star arises in your hearts,

Arminian

The prophetic word is “more sure,” a lamp in a dark place until the day dawns. Arminian readers take this as encouragement to keep attending to Scripture until Christ returns.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Scripture as the believer’s sure guide in this age, even more settled than personal experience.

2 Peter 1:20

20knowing this first, that no prophecy of Scripture is of private interpretation.

Arminian

No prophecy of Scripture arises from private interpretation. Arminian interpreters usually read this as being about the origin of prophecy: the prophets did not invent their own meanings.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters often draw from this verse the principle that Scripture interprets Scripture, since all of it comes from the one Spirit.

2 Peter 1:21

21For no prophecy ever came by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke, being moved by the Holy Spirit.

Arminian

Holy men spoke as they were moved by the Holy Spirit. Arminian expositors see real human authors whose words were nonetheless carried by God.

Reformed

This is a central text for the Reformed doctrine of inspiration. Reformed expositors see the Spirit carrying the writers along, so that Scripture is God’s word through human speech.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
