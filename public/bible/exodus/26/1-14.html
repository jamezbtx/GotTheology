Passage

Exodus 26

Verses 1–14 · WEB

The instructions turn to the dwelling itself: an inner set of ten embroidered linen curtains with cherubim, a tent of goats’ hair over it, and outer coverings of rams’ skins and sea cow hides. Loops and clasps join each set so the tabernacle forms one whole.

Exodus 26:1

1“Moreover you shall make the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, with cherubim. You shall make them with the work of a skillful workman.

Arminian

The innermost layer of the tabernacle is ten curtains of fine twined linen in blue, purple and scarlet, with cherubim worked into them by a skilled craftsman.

Arminian readers see human skill honored in God’s service. God later fills Bezalel and others with his Spirit for this very work (31:1–6).

Reformed

Cherubim woven into the ceiling curtains surround the priests with images of God’s heavenly court.

Reformed readers note that the skill to build comes from God (31:3–6; 35:30–35). Gifts are given and directed by him for his worship.

Exodus 26:2

2The length of each curtain shall be twenty-eight cubits, and the width of each curtain four cubits: all the curtains shall have one measure.

Arminian

Each curtain is 28 by 4 cubits, all the same size. The WEB footnote gives a cubit as about 18 inches or 46 centimeters.

Arminian readers see order and consistency in the design.

Reformed

Uniform measurements make the curtains fit together precisely.

Reformed readers see the God of order reflected in the exactness of his instructions (cf. 1 Cor 14:33, 40).

Exodus 26:3

3Five curtains shall be coupled together to one another, and the other five curtains shall be coupled to one another.

Arminian

The ten curtains are joined into two sets of five.

Arminian readers see a dwelling assembled from many parts into a single home for God among his people.

Reformed

The two sets will be joined into one whole (v. 6).

Reformed readers do not press symbolism onto the number of curtains.

Exodus 26:4

4You shall make loops of blue on the edge of the one curtain from the edge in the coupling, and you shall do likewise on the edge of the curtain that is outermost in the second coupling.

Arminian

Loops of blue yarn go on the edge of the outermost curtain of each set.

Arminian readers note that even the fastenings use the sanctuary’s colors.

Reformed

Blue loops prepare the two sets for joining.

Reformed readers see consistent care in every part of the work.

Exodus 26:5

5You shall make fifty loops in the one curtain, and you shall make fifty loops in the edge of the curtain that is in the second coupling. The loops shall be opposite one another.

Arminian

Fifty loops on each set line up opposite one another.

Arminian readers see careful planning so the pieces fit exactly.

Reformed

The matching loops ensure a precise join.

Reformed readers note that God’s design leaves nothing to chance.

Exodus 26:6

6You shall make fifty clasps of gold, and couple the curtains to one another with the clasps. The tabernacle shall be a unit.

Arminian

Fifty gold clasps join the curtains: “The tabernacle shall be a unit.”

Arminian readers see one dwelling for one God among one people. Some readers apply the image to the unity of God’s people (Eph 2:21–22; 4:3–4), as an application rather than the text’s direct meaning.

Reformed

The clasps make the tabernacle one. The veil will later hang under these clasps (v. 33).

Reformed readers see the unity of God’s dwelling, fulfilled in the church as one holy temple in the Lord (Eph 2:21–22).

Exodus 26:7

7“You shall make curtains of goats’ hair for a covering over the tabernacle. You shall make eleven curtains.

Arminian

A second layer of eleven goats’ hair curtains forms a tent over the linen dwelling.

Arminian readers note that ordinary materials protect the beautiful interior.

Reformed

The goats’ hair tent covers the inner dwelling. The text distinguishes the dwelling (the linen layer) from the tent over it.

Reformed readers see layers of protection around the holy presence.

Exodus 26:8

8The length of each curtain shall be thirty cubits, and the width of each curtain four cubits: the eleven curtains shall have one measure.

Arminian

Each goats’ hair curtain is 30 by 4 cubits, slightly longer than the linen curtains so it can cover them.

Arminian readers see the outer tent sized to shelter what lies beneath it.

Reformed

The extra length lets the tent hang over and protect the inner curtains.

Reformed readers note the thoughtful design of the whole structure.

Exodus 26:9

9You shall couple five curtains by themselves, and six curtains by themselves, and shall double over the sixth curtain in the forefront of the tent.

Arminian

Five curtains are joined in one set and six in the other, with the sixth doubled over at the front of the tent.

Arminian readers see practical wisdom in the design, giving the tent a finished front.

Reformed

The doubled curtain finishes the front edge of the tent.

Reformed readers do not assign doctrinal meaning to the arrangement.

Exodus 26:10

10You shall make fifty loops on the edge of the one curtain that is outermost in the coupling, and fifty loops on the edge of the curtain which is outermost in the second coupling.

Arminian

Fifty loops go on the edge of each set, as with the linen curtains.

Arminian readers see the same careful pattern repeated.

Reformed

The repeated design ensures the two sets join properly.

Reformed readers note the consistency of God’s instructions.

Exodus 26:11

11You shall make fifty clasps of bronze, and put the clasps into the loops, and couple the tent together, that it may be one.

Arminian

Bronze clasps join this outer tent, “that it may be one.” The linen curtains used gold clasps (v. 6).

Arminian readers note the gradation: gold nearer the presence, bronze farther out.

Reformed

The shift from gold to bronze follows the sanctuary’s pattern of greater holiness toward the center.

Reformed readers see the holiness of God taught through materials as well as words.

Exodus 26:12

12The overhanging part that remains of the curtains of the tent—the half curtain that remains—shall hang over the back of the tabernacle.

Arminian

The extra half curtain hangs over the back of the tabernacle.

Arminian readers see thorough provision: no part of God’s dwelling is left exposed.

Reformed

The overhang ensures the back of the dwelling is covered.

Reformed readers note that the design protects the whole sanctuary.

Exodus 26:13

13The cubit on the one side and the cubit on the other side, of that which remains in the length of the curtains of the tent, shall hang over the sides of the tabernacle on this side and on that side, to cover it.

Arminian

An extra cubit on each side hangs over the sides to cover the tabernacle.

Arminian readers see thorough provision for the dwelling’s protection.

Reformed

The overhang on both sides completes the covering.

Reformed readers see God providing for the protection of his dwelling place.

Exodus 26:14

14You shall make a covering for the tent of rams’ skins dyed red, and a covering of sea cow hides above.

Arminian

Two outer coverings complete the roof: rams’ skins dyed red, and sea cow hides above. As at 25:5, the WEB footnotes the term as possibly “fine leather.”

Arminian readers note the plain, weather-resistant exterior. The glory is inside.

Reformed

From outside, the tabernacle looked ordinary. Its beauty was hidden within.

Some Reformed readers have drawn an analogy with Christ, who had no outward majesty (Isa 53:2) yet was full of glory, though this is an application rather than the text’s stated meaning.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
