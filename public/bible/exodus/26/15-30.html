Passage

Exodus 26

Verses 15–30 · WEB

This section describes the framework of upright acacia boards set in silver sockets, held together by bars and overlaid with gold. It ends by pointing Moses again to what was shown to him on the mountain.

Exodus 26:15

15“You shall make the boards for the tabernacle of acacia wood, standing upright.

Arminian

Upright boards (or frames) of acacia wood form the walls of the tabernacle.

Arminian readers see a sturdy structure prepared for God’s dwelling.

Reformed

The frames give the tabernacle its shape and stability.

Reformed readers note the transition from coverings to structure, all according to the pattern.

Exodus 26:16

16Ten cubits shall be the length of a board, and one and a half cubits the width of each board.

Arminian

Each board is 10 cubits long and 1½ cubits wide.

Arminian readers see a dwelling of real size and substance prepared for God’s presence.

Reformed

The measurements set the height and proportions of the dwelling.

Reformed readers see order in God’s design but do not draw doctrine from the numbers.

Exodus 26:17

17There shall be two tenons in each board, joined to one another: thus you shall make for all the boards of the tabernacle.

Arminian

Two tenons on each board fit into sockets below.

Arminian readers note the careful joinery.

Reformed

The tenons hold the boards firmly in place.

Reformed readers see stability built into God’s dwelling.

Exodus 26:18

18You shall make twenty boards for the tabernacle, for the south side southward.

Arminian

Twenty boards form the south side.

Arminian readers see the walls rising according to a plan given by God, not improvised.

Reformed

The south wall is measured out by its number of boards.

Reformed readers do not press symbolic meaning onto the count.

Exodus 26:19

19You shall make forty sockets of silver under the twenty boards; two sockets under one board for its two tenons, and two sockets under another board for its two tenons.

Arminian

Forty silver sockets, two under each board, hold the frames.

Arminian readers note that the silver for the sockets later comes from the atonement money paid by the people (30:11–16; 38:25–27).

Reformed

Exodus 38:25–27 records that the silver for these sockets came from the half-shekel ransom paid for each counted Israelite (30:11–16).

Reformed readers see the dwelling resting on a foundation tied to ransom. Some draw a typological link to redemption in Christ, while recognizing that the text itself does not draw it.

Exodus 26:20

20For the second side of the tabernacle, on the north side, twenty boards,

Arminian

Twenty boards form the north side.

Arminian readers note the symmetry with the south side.

Reformed

The north side matches the south.

Reformed readers see balance in God’s design.

Exodus 26:21

21and their forty sockets of silver; two sockets under one board, and two sockets under another board.

Arminian

Forty silver sockets again, two under each board.

Arminian readers see the same care given to each side of God’s house.

Reformed

The repetition confirms the plan for the north side.

Reformed readers note the careful consistency of the instructions.

Exodus 26:22

22For the far side of the tabernacle westward you shall make six boards.

Arminian

Six boards form the far (west) side.

Arminian readers note that the entrance faces east, so the most holy place lies at the west end.

Reformed

The west end, far from the entrance, will house the most holy place.

Reformed readers see the approach to God moving from east to west, from the entrance toward his presence.

Exodus 26:23

23You shall make two boards for the corners of the tabernacle in the far side.

Arminian

Two corner boards finish the far side.

Arminian readers see sturdy corners that keep the dwelling stable on the journey.

Reformed

The corner boards strengthen the back of the structure.

Reformed readers do not read doctrine into the corners.

Exodus 26:24

24They shall be double beneath, and in the same way they shall be whole to its top to one ring: thus shall it be for them both; they shall be for the two corners.

Arminian

The corner boards are doubled from bottom to top and joined at one ring. The exact construction is not entirely clear to modern readers.

Arminian readers acknowledge the uncertainty without speculation.

Reformed

Interpreters have proposed different reconstructions of these corners.

Reformed readers, too, admit the technical uncertainty; nothing doctrinal depends on it.

Exodus 26:25

25There shall be eight boards, and their sockets of silver, sixteen sockets; two sockets under one board, and two sockets under another board.

Arminian

Eight boards with sixteen silver sockets make up the west end.

Arminian readers see the far wall completed with the same silver foundation as the sides.

Reformed

Six boards plus two corner boards equal eight, each with two sockets.

Reformed readers note the careful arithmetic of the plan.

Exodus 26:26

26“You shall make bars of acacia wood: five for the boards of the one side of the tabernacle,

Arminian

Bars of acacia wood hold the boards together, five for one side.

Arminian readers see strength and unity in the structure.

Reformed

The bars bind the frames into a stable whole.

Reformed readers see a dwelling made secure by God’s design.

Exodus 26:27

27and five bars for the boards of the other side of the tabernacle, and five bars for the boards of the side of the tabernacle, for the far side westward.

Arminian

Five bars for the other side and five for the west end.

Arminian readers see every wall braced alike, a whole structure made secure.

Reformed

Each wall is braced with its own bars.

Reformed readers note that every side receives the same care.

Exodus 26:28

28The middle bar in the middle of the boards shall pass through from end to end.

Arminian

The middle bar passes through from end to end, binding the boards along the full length.

Arminian readers see unity in the structure.

Reformed

The central bar ties the whole frame together.

Reformed readers note the structural unity without pressing it into symbolism.

Exodus 26:29

29You shall overlay the boards with gold, and make their rings of gold for places for the bars. You shall overlay the bars with gold.

Arminian

The boards and bars are overlaid with gold, with gold rings to hold the bars.

Arminian readers see the walls of the holy place shining with gold around the priests’ service.

Reformed

Gold walls surround the holy and most holy places.

Reformed readers see the splendor of God’s dwelling reflected in its materials.

Exodus 26:30

30You shall set up the tabernacle according to the way that it was shown to you on the mountain.

Arminian

“You shall set up the tabernacle according to the way that it was shown to you on the mountain.” The theme of the divine pattern returns (25:9, 40).

Arminian readers see the importance of faithful obedience in carrying out God’s design.

Reformed

The reminder ties the construction back to revelation on Sinai.

Reformed readers stress that the God who dwells among his people also shows how his dwelling is to be built. Hebrews reads the tabernacle as a copy of the heavenly sanctuary (Heb 8:5; 9:24).

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
