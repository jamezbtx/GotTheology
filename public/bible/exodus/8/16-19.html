Passage

Exodus 8

Verses 16–19 · WEB

The third plague comes without a recorded warning: Aaron strikes the dust, and lice cover people and animals. This time the magicians cannot imitate the sign and confess, “This is God’s finger.” Pharaoh’s heart remains hardened, and the narrative marks the limit of Egypt’s counterfeit power.

Exodus 8:16

16The LORD said to Moses, “Tell Aaron, ‘Stretch out your rod, and strike the dust of the earth, that it may become lice throughout all the land of Egypt.’ ”

Arminian

The LORD tells Aaron to strike the dust so that it becomes lice throughout Egypt. This plague comes without a recorded warning to Pharaoh.

Arminian readers note the change in pattern after Pharaoh’s broken promise. Judgment comes more directly when a warning has been despised.

Reformed

God commands the third sign without first sending Moses to Pharaoh. The sequence of plagues has its own deliberate pattern.

Reformed readers see God shaping the contest as he wills. Both traditions observe the structure of the plague cycle without needing to resolve every reason for its variations.

Exodus 8:17

17They did so; and Aaron stretched out his hand with his rod, and struck the dust of the earth, and there were lice on man, and on animal; all the dust of the earth became lice throughout all the land of Egypt.

Arminian

Aaron strikes the dust and lice cover people and animals everywhere. The dust of the land itself becomes the plague.

Arminian readers see the extent of Egypt’s distress under a stubborn ruler. The plague touches every living creature in the land.

Reformed

Obedience brings immediate fulfilment. The ground beneath Egypt’s feet turns against it.

Reformed readers see the Creator using the humblest material as his instrument. Both traditions read the sign as a demonstration of power over the whole created order.

Exodus 8:18

18The magicians tried with their enchantments to produce lice, but they couldn’t. There were lice on man, and on animal.

Arminian

The magicians try to produce lice with their enchantments, but they cannot. Their imitations reach a limit.

Arminian readers see the failure as clear evidence offered to Pharaoh. Egypt’s experts can no longer give him cover for unbelief.

Reformed

Egypt’s powers fail at the third sign. Whatever they could imitate before, they cannot match this.

Reformed readers see God marking the boundary of counterfeit power. Both traditions agree the narrative is now showing plainly that the LORD alone is at work.

Exodus 8:19

19Then the magicians said to Pharaoh, “This is God’s finger;” but Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he didn’t listen to them, as the LORD had spoken.

Arminian

The magicians tell Pharaoh, “This is God’s finger,” but his heart was hardened and he did not listen, as the LORD had spoken. His own experts confess what he refuses.

Arminian readers see Pharaoh refusing the testimony of his advisers as well as Moses. His resistance becomes more obviously wilful as the evidence mounts.

Reformed

The magicians’ confession is a turning point: even Egypt’s experts recognise divine power. The phrase “finger of God” is echoed in Jesus’ words about casting out demons in Luke 11:20.

Reformed readers see the hardening still running according to God’s word. Both traditions agree that the magicians’ admission leaves Pharaoh without excuse.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
