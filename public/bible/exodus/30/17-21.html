Passage

Exodus 30

Verses 17–21 · WEB

A bronze basin is to stand between the Tent of Meeting and the altar, so that Aaron and his sons can wash their hands and feet before entering the tent or ministering at the altar, lest they die. This is a perpetual statute for the priestly line. Both traditions see the requirement of cleansing for those who draw near to a holy God. Many Christian readers in both traditions hear echoes of washing language in the New Testament (for example, Hebrews 10:22), while recognizing that the passage itself concerns priestly purity.

Exodus 30:17

17The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses about another furnishing. The instructions move to the bronze basin.

Arminian readers note that God provides for every aspect of worship. Nothing is left out.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the section begins again with God’s word. Each element of the tabernacle rests on his command.

Both traditions read this as a continuation of the tabernacle instructions.

Exodus 30:18

18“You shall also make a basin of bronze, and its base of bronze, in which to wash. You shall put it between the Tent of Meeting and the altar, and you shall put water in it.

Arminian

A bronze basin with a bronze base is placed between the Tent and the altar and filled with water. It stands in the path of the priests.

Arminian readers note that cleansing is provided before priests approach. God makes provision for purity.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the basin’s location between the altar and the tent. Washing is required before entering God’s presence.

Both traditions see in the basin a reminder that drawing near to God requires cleansing.

Exodus 30:19

19Aaron and his sons shall wash their hands and their feet in it.

Arminian

Aaron and his sons wash their hands and feet in it. Ministry requires clean hands and feet.

Arminian readers note that ongoing cleansing is needed. Even those consecrated must wash regularly.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the priests were washed fully at their ordination (29:4) and now wash hands and feet regularly. Many interpreters compare John 13:10, though that link is an application.

Both traditions see a distinction between initial cleansing and continual washing.

Exodus 30:20

20When they go into the Tent of Meeting, they shall wash with water, that they don’t die; or when they come near to the altar to minister, to burn an offering made by fire to the LORD.

Arminian

When they enter the Tent or approach the altar, they must wash with water so that they do not die.

Arminian readers note the seriousness of approaching God without cleansing. God’s warnings are real.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the danger of death shows God’s holiness. Approaching him carelessly is perilous.

Both traditions agree that God’s holiness calls for reverence and cleansing.

Exodus 30:21

21So they shall wash their hands and their feet, that they not die. This shall be a statute forever to them, even to him and to his descendants throughout their generations.”

Arminian

Washing hands and feet is a perpetual statute for Aaron and his descendants. The requirement is ongoing.

Arminian readers note that the priests must continue to wash throughout their service. Faithfulness is ongoing.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the statute is for the priestly line under the old covenant. Its meaning is fulfilled in the cleansing provided through Christ.

Both traditions see continued cleansing as part of faithful service before God.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
