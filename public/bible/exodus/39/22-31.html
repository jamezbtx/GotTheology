Passage

Exodus 39

Verses 22–31 · WEB

The robe of blue with pomegranates and golden bells, the linen tunics, turban, sash, and the gold plate inscribed HOLY TO THE LORD complete the priestly garments. Both traditions read these details as provision for reverent ministry before the holy God and a reminder of the people’s calling to holiness, while avoiding speculative symbolism the text does not require.

Exodus 39:22

22He made the robe of the ephod of woven work, all of blue.

Arminian

The robe of the ephod is woven entirely of blue. Arminian readers see another garment completed for the priest’s service, made with the same care as the ephod and breastplate.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see fidelity to the instructions for the priestly wardrobe. Both traditions avoid assigning a fixed meaning to the color while noting its prominence throughout the tabernacle materials.

Exodus 39:23

23The opening of the robe in the middle of it was like the opening of a coat of mail, with a binding around its opening, that it should not be torn.

Arminian

The robe’s opening is bound so that it will not tear. Arminian readers see practical foresight that protects the garment for continued use in ministry before God.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see detailed obedience in the construction. Both read the binding chiefly as a protective measure, with the narrative stressing completeness rather than hidden symbolism.

Exodus 39:24

24They made on the skirts of the robe pomegranates of blue, purple, scarlet, and twined linen.

Arminian

Pomegranates of colored yarn are made on the robe’s hem. Arminian readers see beauty incorporated into the priest’s garments, reflecting the goodness of the God he serves.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see ornament prescribed by God rather than added by human preference. Both traditions acknowledge that interpretations of the pomegranates vary and none is required by the text itself.

Exodus 39:25

25They made bells of pure gold, and put the bells between the pomegranates around the skirts of the robe, between the pomegranates;

Arminian

Golden bells are placed between the pomegranates. Arminian readers see the bells connected, in Exodus 28, with the priest’s movement in the holy place before the LORD.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise note that Exodus 28 links the bells to the priest’s safe ministry in God’s presence. Both see a reminder of the seriousness of approaching the holy God.

Exodus 39:26

26a bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate, around the skirts of the robe, to minister in, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The alternating pattern of bell and pomegranate surrounds the hem, as the LORD commanded. Arminian readers see the people’s obedience completed for a garment intended for ministry.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the refrain marking faithful execution. Both traditions agree that the robe’s purpose is service before God, not display of the priest’s own status.

Exodus 39:27

27They made the tunics of fine linen of woven work for Aaron and for his sons,

Arminian

Linen tunics are made for Aaron and his sons. Arminian readers see the whole priestly family provided for, preparing them for service on behalf of the people.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God providing the garments for those he appointed to the priesthood. Both note that the priests do not design their own clothing but receive what God prescribes.

Exodus 39:28

28the turban of fine linen, the linen headbands of fine linen, the linen trousers of fine twined linen,

Arminian

The turban, headbands, and linen trousers are listed. Arminian readers see modesty and dignity provided for in the priestly garments, as Exodus 28 also emphasizes.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s concern for propriety in worship. Both traditions read the list as part of the careful record showing every item was made.

Exodus 39:29

29the sash of fine twined linen, blue, purple, and scarlet, the work of the embroiderer, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The embroidered sash completes the garments, as the LORD commanded. Arminian readers see skilled embroiderers contributing their particular gifts to the shared work of the community.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see diverse gifts directed toward one obedient purpose. Both agree the refrain once again ties the work to God’s command rather than to the artisans’ preferences.

Exodus 39:30

30They made the plate of the holy crown of pure gold, and wrote on it an inscription, like the engravings of a signet: “HOLY TO THE LORD”.

Arminian

A gold plate on the holy crown is engraved “HOLY TO THE LORD”. Arminian readers see the priest marked as consecrated to God, representing a people called to holiness.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see holiness defining the priest’s role and, through him, the calling of Israel. Both traditions agree that the inscription points to God’s claim on those who serve him.

Exodus 39:31

31They tied to it a lace of blue, to fasten it on the turban above, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

A blue lace fastens the plate to the turban, as the LORD commanded. Arminian readers see the final garment detail completed, so the priest is fully equipped for ministry.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the conclusion of the garment instructions with the familiar refrain. Both note that the plate is secured above the priest’s forehead, keeping the declaration of holiness visible.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
