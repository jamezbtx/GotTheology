Passage

Exodus 39

Verses 32–43 · WEB

The work of the tabernacle is finished, and the people bring every part to Moses. He sees that all was done as the LORD commanded and blesses them. After the golden calf, the community now worships according to God’s pattern rather than its own invention. Both traditions celebrate faithful obedience and the blessing that follows; the inventory is shared ground rather than a site of doctrinal dispute.

Exodus 39:32

32Thus all the work of the tabernacle of the Tent of Meeting was finished. The children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses; so they did.

Arminian

All the work of the tabernacle is finished, and Israel did all that the LORD commanded. Arminian readers see a community responding with complete obedience to God’s gracious provision for his presence among them.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the people carrying out God’s revealed pattern faithfully. Both traditions note the contrast with the golden calf episode, where worship had been shaped by human initiative instead.

Exodus 39:33

33They brought the tabernacle to Moses: the tent, with all its furniture, its clasps, its boards, its bars, its pillars, its sockets,

Arminian

The people bring the tabernacle and its parts to Moses. Arminian readers see the community presenting its finished work for inspection by God’s appointed leader.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see Moses as the mediator receiving the work for comparison with the pattern God showed him. Both agree the presentation emphasizes accountability to God’s instruction.

Exodus 39:34

34the covering of rams’ skins dyed red, the covering of sea cow hides, the veil of the screen,

Arminian

The coverings and the veil are listed among the items brought. Arminian readers see the inventory as a record that the whole structure, including its protective layers, was completed.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see a thorough accounting of the work. Both traditions note the veil’s role in screening the ark, without pressing later debates into this list.

Exodus 39:35

35the ark of the covenant with its poles, the mercy seat,

Arminian

The ark of the covenant, its poles, and the mercy seat are presented. Arminian readers see the center of the sanctuary, where God would meet with Moses, completed and ready.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the ark and mercy seat as the focal point of God’s presence and covenant. Both traditions recognize their importance in Israel’s worship without forcing later theological disputes onto this inventory.

Exodus 39:36

36the table, all its vessels, the show bread,

Arminian

The table, its vessels, and the show bread are listed. Arminian readers see provision for continual offering before God as part of the completed sanctuary.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the table prepared for its appointed service. Both note that the bread is set before the LORD, signaling ongoing fellowship and covenant relationship.

Exodus 39:37

37the pure lamp stand, its lamps, even the lamps to be set in order, all its vessels, the oil for the light,

Arminian

The lamp stand, lamps, vessels, and oil are presented. Arminian readers see preparation for continual light in the holy place, as God commanded for the sanctuary’s daily service.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see each furnishing completed according to the pattern. Both traditions recognize the lamp stand’s role in the ordered worship of the tabernacle.

Exodus 39:38

38the golden altar, the anointing oil, the sweet incense, the screen for the door of the Tent,

Arminian

The golden altar, anointing oil, incense, and door screen are listed. Arminian readers see everything needed for the sanctuary’s worship brought together for Moses’ inspection.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see completeness in the furnishings and materials for consecration. Both note that the anointing oil anticipates the setting apart described in chapter 40.

Exodus 39:39

39the bronze altar, its grating of bronze, its poles, all of its vessels, the basin and its base,

Arminian

The bronze altar, its grating, the basin, and their parts are presented. Arminian readers see provision for sacrifice and cleansing ready for use in Israel’s approach to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the altar and basin as essential to approaching the holy God. Both traditions agree that sacrifice and washing frame the access God provides, while later fulfillment questions are addressed elsewhere.

Exodus 39:40

40the hangings of the court, its pillars, its sockets, the screen for the gate of the court, its cords, its pins, and all the instruments of the service of the tabernacle, for the Tent of Meeting,

Arminian

The court’s hangings, pillars, sockets, screen, cords, and pins are listed. Arminian readers see even the smallest instruments counted, honoring every contribution to the work.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see a full inventory confirming that nothing was omitted. Both note that the court defines the boundary of the sacred space around God’s dwelling.

Exodus 39:41

41the finely worked garments for ministering in the holy place, the holy garments for Aaron the priest, and the garments of his sons, to minister in the priest’s office.

Arminian

The holy garments for Aaron and his sons are presented. Arminian readers see the priesthood equipped to serve, completing the preparation for ministry on behalf of the people.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the garments brought for the priests God appointed. Both traditions read the list’s conclusion as a sign that both sanctuary and ministers are ready.

Exodus 39:42

42According to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so the children of Israel did all the work.

Arminian

Israel did all the work according to all that the LORD commanded Moses. Arminian readers see the people’s faithful response celebrated as genuine obedience that God values.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the people’s obedience as the fruit of God’s instruction and gifting. Both traditions agree the summary affirms complete conformity to God’s word.

Exodus 39:43

43Moses saw all the work, and behold, they had done it as the LORD had commanded. They had done so; and Moses blessed them.

Arminian

Moses inspects the work, finds it done as commanded, and blesses the people. Arminian readers see God’s servant affirming the community’s faithful labor with blessing.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see blessing following obedience done according to God’s word. Some readers in both traditions note echoes of the creation account, where completed work is seen and blessed, though the parallel is an interpretive observation.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
