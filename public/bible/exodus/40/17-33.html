Passage

Exodus 40

Verses 17–33 · WEB

In the second year, Moses sets up the tabernacle step by step, placing each furnishing and beginning its service. The refrain as the LORD commanded Moses appears seven times, and the section ends with Moses finishing the work. Both traditions see orderly worship grounded in God’s word and cleansing required for those who draw near.

Exodus 40:17

17In the first month in the second year, on the first day of the month, the tabernacle was raised up.

Arminian

The tabernacle is raised on the first day of the first month of the second year. Arminian readers see God’s instruction fulfilled on the appointed day, marking a milestone in Israel’s journey.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the precise fulfillment of God’s command. Both note that the dating anchors the event in Israel’s history after the exodus. God’s word is kept on his schedule.

Exodus 40:18

18Moses raised up the tabernacle, and laid its sockets, and set up its boards, and put in its bars, and raised up its pillars.

Arminian

Moses raises the tabernacle, setting sockets, boards, bars, and pillars. Arminian readers see the leader personally carrying out the assembly God assigned to him. His hands do the work he received on the mountain.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see orderly construction proceeding by God’s pattern. Both traditions value the narrative’s step-by-step account as testimony to faithful completion. Each element rests on the one before it.

Exodus 40:19

19He spread the covering over the tent, and put the roof of the tabernacle above on it, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The covering is spread over the tent as the LORD commanded. Arminian readers see the refrain resuming, linking each stage of assembly to God’s instruction.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the repeated formula affirming exact obedience. Both note that the refrain as the LORD commanded Moses appears seven times between 40:19 and 40:32.

Exodus 40:20

20He took and put the covenant into the ark, and set the poles on the ark, and put the mercy seat above on the ark.

Arminian

The covenant is placed in the ark, with poles and mercy seat set in place. Arminian readers see God’s covenant word kept at the heart of the sanctuary.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the covenant document central to Israel’s relationship with God. Both traditions agree that God’s presence and his covenant word belong together in the tabernacle.

Exodus 40:21

21He brought the ark into the tabernacle, and set up the veil of the screen, and screened the ark of the covenant, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The ark is brought in and screened with the veil. Arminian readers see holiness guarded while God’s presence is established among his people. Nearness and reverence are held together in the placing of the ark.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the veil marking the sanctity of the place where God meets Israel. Both note the refrain confirming Moses acted as commanded.

Exodus 40:22

22He put the table in the Tent of Meeting, on the north side of the tabernacle, outside of the veil.

Arminian

The table is placed on the north side outside the veil. Arminian readers see orderly placement of furnishings that supports the regular service of the priests.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see precise arrangement according to God’s instructions. Both traditions read the placement as part of the ordered worship space God designed. Even location is received rather than invented.

Exodus 40:23

23He set the bread in order on it before the LORD, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The bread is set in order before the LORD. Arminian readers see continual provision presented before God as an expression of covenant fellowship. The bread rests before God as a sign of continuing relationship.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the bread set before the LORD in appointed order. Both note the refrain once more tying the action to God’s command.

Exodus 40:24

24He put the lamp stand in the Tent of Meeting, opposite the table, on the south side of the tabernacle.

Arminian

The lamp stand is placed opposite the table on the south side. Arminian readers see the holy place arranged so that light and provision stand together before God.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see careful arrangement following the pattern. Both traditions avoid overreading spatial details while appreciating the order of the sanctuary. The room is ordered for service, not display.

Exodus 40:25

25He lit the lamps before the LORD, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The lamps are lit before the LORD. Arminian readers see the sanctuary beginning its active service, with light kindled in God’s presence. The first lamplight marks a sanctuary that is now in use for worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the lamps lit according to God’s word. Both note that worship moves from construction to actual use as each furnishing is set in place.

Exodus 40:26

26He put the golden altar in the Tent of Meeting before the veil;

Arminian

The golden altar is placed in the tent before the veil. Arminian readers see the altar of incense positioned close to the place of God’s presence.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see placement exactly as instructed in 40:5. Both traditions read the location as significant for the ordered approach to God. Approach to God follows the order he gives.

Exodus 40:27

27and he burned incense of sweet spices on it, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

Incense of sweet spices is burned on the altar. Arminian readers see worship beginning as Moses offers incense before the LORD. Moses himself performs the first act of worship before the priests begin their regular duties.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the first offering of incense carried out by command. Both note that later Scripture sometimes associates incense with prayer, though this verse itself simply records the act.

Exodus 40:28

28He put up the screen of the door to the tabernacle.

Arminian

The screen is put up at the tabernacle door. Arminian readers see the entrance defined, marking where the people approach the dwelling of God. The doorway invites approach on God’s terms.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the sanctuary’s boundaries set according to the pattern. Both traditions see the screen as part of the regulated access God provides.

Exodus 40:29

29He set the altar of burnt offering at the door of the tabernacle of the Tent of Meeting, and offered on it the burnt offering and the meal offering, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The altar of burnt offering is set at the door, and burnt and meal offerings are made. Arminian readers see sacrifice beginning as part of the people’s restored worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see sacrifice inaugurated at God’s command. Both traditions agree that the altar’s use marks the start of the ordered sacrificial worship described further in Leviticus.

Exodus 40:30

30He set the basin between the Tent of Meeting and the altar, and put water therein, with which to wash.

Arminian

The basin is placed with water for washing. Arminian readers see the provision for cleansing installed, preparing the priests for regular ministry. The water is ready before any priest steps inside.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the basin put to its appointed purpose. Both note that washing is integral to service in God’s presence. Cleansing precedes ministry, and the provision for it comes from God himself.

Exodus 40:31

31Moses, Aaron, and his sons washed their hands and their feet there.

Arminian

Moses, Aaron, and his sons wash their hands and feet. Arminian readers see those who serve submitting to the cleansing God requires before ministering. No one is exempt from the washing.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see even Moses and the priests requiring purification. Both traditions agree that approaching the holy God calls for cleansing he himself provides.

Exodus 40:32

32When they went into the Tent of Meeting, and when they came near to the altar, they washed, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

They wash whenever they enter the tent or approach the altar, as commanded. Arminian readers see ongoing, habitual obedience rather than a single act of preparation.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see continual cleansing as part of God’s ordinance for priestly service. Both note the refrain confirming that this practice follows God’s instruction.

Exodus 40:33

33He raised up the court around the tabernacle and the altar, and set up the screen of the gate of the court. So Moses finished the work.

Arminian

Moses raises the court and finishes the work. Arminian readers see the completion of a long process of obedience, ready now for God’s response. The finished work awaits God’s presence rather than replacing it.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the work brought to completion according to the pattern. Some readers in both traditions observe that the phrasing recalls God finishing his work at creation, an interpretive observation rather than an explicit statement.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
