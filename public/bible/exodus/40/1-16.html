Passage

Exodus 40

Verses 1–16 · WEB

God instructs Moses to raise the tabernacle on the first day of the first month, arrange its furnishings, anoint everything to make it holy, and wash, clothe, and anoint Aaron and his sons for priestly service. Moses does all that is commanded. Both traditions see consecration as God’s act through appointed means and recognize the priesthood within the Sinai covenant; later questions about its fulfillment are addressed elsewhere in Scripture.

Exodus 40:1

1The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses again, introducing instructions for setting up the tabernacle. Arminian readers see God continuing to guide his people step by step in their relationship with him.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s initiative directing the establishment of his dwelling. Both traditions agree the tabernacle is raised at God’s word, not at human initiative.

Exodus 40:2

2“On the first day of the first month you shall raise up the tabernacle of the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

The tabernacle is to be raised on the first day of the first month. Arminian readers see a fresh beginning for the redeemed community marked by God’s presence among them.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God appointing the timing of the sanctuary’s establishment. Both note the significance of a new year beginning with the dwelling place of God set up among his people.

Exodus 40:3

3You shall put the ark of the covenant in it, and you shall screen the ark with the veil.

Arminian

The ark is placed inside and screened by the veil. Arminian readers see the ark at the center of the sanctuary, symbolizing God’s covenant presence with Israel.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the veil marking the holiness of God’s presence. Both traditions agree that access to God is real yet regulated, with fuller reflection on the veil offered later in Scripture.

Exodus 40:4

4You shall bring in the table, and set in order the things that are on it. You shall bring in the lamp stand, and light its lamps.

Arminian

The table is set in order and the lamp stand lit. Arminian readers see the holy place prepared for ongoing service that expresses continued fellowship with God.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see orderly worship appointed by God. Both note that the instructions emphasize arrangement and use, preparing for the regular ministry of the priests.

Exodus 40:5

5You shall set the golden altar for incense before the ark of the covenant, and put the screen of the door to the tabernacle.

Arminian

The golden altar for incense is set before the ark, and the door screen is hung. Arminian readers see worship and prayer associated with approaching God’s presence.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the incense altar positioned in relation to the ark as God commanded. Both traditions note the ordered progression of furnishings toward the place of God’s presence.

Exodus 40:6

6“You shall set the altar of burnt offering before the door of the tabernacle of the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

The altar of burnt offering is set before the door of the tabernacle. Arminian readers see sacrifice positioned at the entrance as the way God provides for approach.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see access to God framed by sacrifice. Both traditions agree that the altar’s place at the entrance signals that sin must be dealt with before drawing near.

Exodus 40:7

7You shall set the basin between the Tent of Meeting and the altar, and shall put water therein.

Arminian

The basin is placed between the tent and the altar and filled with water. Arminian readers see provision for cleansing so the priests may serve in God’s presence.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see washing required for those who minister before the holy God. Both note that the basin’s placement links sacrifice and service in a single ordered approach.

Exodus 40:8

8You shall set up the court around it, and hang up the screen of the gate of the court.

Arminian

The court is set up and its gate screened. Arminian readers see sacred space established around God’s dwelling, inviting reverence from the whole community. The whole camp can see where God dwells.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the boundaries of holiness defined by God. Both traditions agree that the court frames the tabernacle as distinct, set apart for God’s presence.

Exodus 40:9

9“You shall take the anointing oil, and anoint the tabernacle and all that is in it, and shall make it holy, and all its furniture, and it will be holy.

Arminian

The tabernacle and everything in it are to be anointed and made holy. Arminian readers see God setting apart the place and objects for his service through the means he appoints.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see consecration as God’s act through the appointed anointing. Both traditions agree holiness here derives from God’s designation rather than from the materials themselves.

Exodus 40:10

10You shall anoint the altar of burnt offering, with all its vessels, and sanctify the altar, and the altar will be most holy.

Arminian

The altar of burnt offering and its vessels are anointed and become most holy. Arminian readers see special holiness attached to the place of sacrifice, highlighting its seriousness.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the altar set apart as most holy in God’s ordering. Both traditions note the gravity of the sacrificial system within the covenant God established.

Exodus 40:11

11You shall anoint the basin and its base, and sanctify it.

Arminian

The basin and its base are anointed and sanctified. Arminian readers see even the instruments of cleansing consecrated, showing that every part of worship belongs to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see comprehensive consecration according to God’s instruction. Both agree that the verse completes the anointing of the sanctuary’s furnishings before the priests are consecrated.

Exodus 40:12

12“You shall bring Aaron and his sons to the door of the Tent of Meeting, and shall wash them with water.

Arminian

Aaron and his sons are brought to the door and washed. Arminian readers see cleansing as preparation for those called to serve God on behalf of others.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God requiring purity for those who minister before him. Both traditions note that Leviticus 8 describes the carrying out of this ordination.

Exodus 40:13

13You shall put on Aaron the holy garments; and you shall anoint him, and sanctify him, that he may minister to me in the priest’s office.

Arminian

Aaron is clothed, anointed, and sanctified to minister as priest. Arminian readers see a calling to service enabled by God’s consecration, not seized by personal ambition.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God appointing and setting apart the priest. Both traditions note that Hebrews relates Aaron’s priesthood, as a pattern, to Christ’s greater priestly ministry.

Exodus 40:14

14You shall bring his sons, and put tunics on them.

Arminian

Aaron’s sons are brought and clothed in tunics. Arminian readers see the priestly calling shared with the family, establishing ongoing service for the community. No one in the line appoints himself.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God providing for continuing ministry through Aaron’s line. Both note that the sons, like their father, receive garments prepared according to God’s command.

Exodus 40:15

15You shall anoint them, as you anointed their father, that they may minister to me in the priest’s office. Their anointing shall be to them for an everlasting priesthood throughout their generations.”

Arminian

The sons are anointed for an everlasting priesthood throughout their generations. Arminian readers see God’s provision for lasting priestly service within the Sinai covenant. The ordinance is given as a gift for Israel’s worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see a permanent ordinance for Israel’s priesthood. Both traditions relate this to later discussion in Hebrews about Christ’s priesthood, a question approached through covenant theology rather than by Arminian–Reformed lines.

Exodus 40:16

16Moses did so. According to all that the LORD commanded him, so he did.

Arminian

Moses did all that the LORD commanded him. Arminian readers see the leader modeling faithful obedience that matches the people’s obedience in chapter 39. Leader and people alike respond to God’s word.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see Moses carrying out God’s word exactly. Both traditions agree the summary prepares for the detailed account that follows. Moses’ obedience is the hinge between instruction and fulfillment.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
