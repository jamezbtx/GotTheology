Passage

Exodus 40

Verses 34–38 · WEB

The cloud covers the tent and the LORD’s glory fills the tabernacle so fully that even Moses cannot enter. From then on the cloud guides Israel’s journeys by day and fire by night. Both traditions share the central confession of this climax: the holy God, who redeemed Israel and forgave the golden calf, now dwells among his people and leads them. Later developments of that theme should not be forced onto every detail.

Exodus 40:34

34Then the cloud covered the Tent of Meeting, and the LORD’s glory filled the tabernacle.

Arminian

The cloud covers the tent and the LORD’s glory fills the tabernacle. Arminian readers see God graciously coming to dwell among a people who had recently sinned with the golden calf.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s glory taking up residence as the climax of Exodus. Both traditions share joy that God chooses to dwell with his people, a theme Scripture develops further through the temple and in Christ.

Exodus 40:35

35Moses wasn’t able to enter into the Tent of Meeting, because the cloud stayed on it, and the LORD’s glory filled the tabernacle.

Arminian

Moses cannot enter because the cloud remains and the glory fills the tabernacle. Arminian readers see the overwhelming holiness of God’s presence, which even Moses must respect.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the majesty of God’s glory that no one can approach on their own terms. Both note that Leviticus begins with God calling Moses from the tent, showing access granted by God.

Exodus 40:36

36When the cloud was taken up from over the tabernacle, the children of Israel went onward, throughout all their journeys;

Arminian

When the cloud lifts, Israel travels onward. Arminian readers see God guiding his people continually, with the people responding in trust to his leading. Departure follows God’s signal, not the people’s impatience.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God directing his people’s journey by his presence. Both traditions agree that the tabernacle is a moving sanctuary that accompanies Israel rather than a fixed shrine.

Exodus 40:37

37but if the cloud wasn’t taken up, then they didn’t travel until the day that it was taken up.

Arminian

If the cloud stays, they do not travel. Arminian readers see the people learning patient dependence, moving only as God leads them. Staying put becomes its own act of trust in God’s timing.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s sovereign direction governing the timing of the journey. Both note that Numbers 9 expands this pattern of waiting and moving at the LORD’s command.

Exodus 40:38

38For the cloud of the LORD was on the tabernacle by day, and there was fire in the cloud by night, in the sight of all the house of Israel, throughout all their journeys.

Arminian

The cloud by day and fire by night remain over the tabernacle in Israel’s sight. Arminian readers see God’s visible faithfulness to the whole community throughout their wanderings.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s abiding presence with his people through all their journeys. Both traditions close Exodus with shared confidence that the God who redeemed Israel now dwells with and leads them.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
