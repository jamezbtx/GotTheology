Passage

Exodus 4

Verses 27–31 · WEB

God sends Aaron to meet Moses at the mountain of God, and the brothers return to Egypt together. Aaron speaks the words God gave Moses and performs the signs before the elders of Israel. The people believe, and on hearing that the LORD has seen their affliction they bow and worship, fulfilling God’s word in 3:18 that they would listen.

Exodus 4:27

27The LORD said to Aaron, “Go into the wilderness to meet Moses.” He went, and met him on God’s mountain, and kissed him.

Arminian

God sends Aaron to meet Moses at God’s mountain, and the brothers are reunited with a kiss. Arminian readers note God coordinating human actions toward his purpose. Both traditions see the fulfillment of God’s word in 4:14 that Aaron would come to meet Moses.

Reformed

Reformed readers see providence orchestrating the meeting at the very place where Moses was called. God’s promise in 4:14 is fulfilled. Both traditions agree that God guides both brothers and that the location at God’s mountain ties the mission to the place of revelation.

Exodus 4:28

28Moses told Aaron all the LORD’s words with which he had sent him, and all the signs with which he had instructed him.

Arminian

Moses shares with Aaron all of God’s words and the signs. Arminian readers see faithful communication and partnership. Both traditions agree that Moses now accepts his role and passes on God’s message without alteration.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the faithful transmission of revelation as the commissioned prophet entrusts God’s words to his spokesman. Shared ground: Moses’ earlier reluctance has given way to obedience, and God’s plan in 4:15–16 is being carried out.

Exodus 4:29

29Moses and Aaron went and gathered together all the elders of the children of Israel.

Arminian

Moses and Aaron gather Israel’s elders, as God commanded in 3:16. Arminian readers note obedience to the details of God’s instructions. Both traditions agree that the elders are the proper first audience and that the mission proceeds in good order.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the fulfillment of God’s directive in 3:16. The structure of the community is honored. Both traditions agree that obedience to God’s instructions marks this stage of the story.

Exodus 4:30

30Aaron spoke all the words which the LORD had spoken to Moses, and did the signs in the sight of the people.

Arminian

Aaron speaks and performs the signs before the people. Arminian readers see the signs functioning as God intended: as evidence to support faith. Both traditions agree that the partnership between Moses and Aaron works as God planned in 4:15–16.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s word and signs confirming each other, as God promised. The signs serve the word rather than replacing it. Shared ground: God’s provision of Aaron and the signs proves effective in the first encounter with Israel.

Exodus 4:31

31The people believed, and when they heard that the LORD had visited the children of Israel, and that he had seen their affliction, then they bowed their heads and worshiped.

Arminian

The people believe and worship when they hear that the LORD has seen their affliction. Arminian readers see a genuine response of faith to God’s word and signs. Both traditions agree that this fulfills God’s promise in 3:18 that they would listen.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as the fruit of God’s word and work; God’s promise in 3:18 is fulfilled. The people’s worship anticipates the goal of the exodus. Both traditions agree that the moment is joyful, though the narrative soon shows how fragile Israel’s faith will be.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
