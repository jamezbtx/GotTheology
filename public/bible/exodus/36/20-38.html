Passage

Exodus 36

Verses 20–38 · WEB

The account continues with the boards, sockets, bars, veil, and entrance screen, closely matching 26:15–37. The details describe a structure carefully assembled according to God’s instructions. Both traditions see faithful obedience, skilled labor, and the holiness of God’s presence here, and both share the New Testament use of the veil and tabernacle in reference to Christ.

Exodus 36:20

20He made the boards for the tabernacle of acacia wood, standing up.

Arminian

Upright boards of acacia wood are made for the tabernacle. Arminian readers see durable wood chosen for the frame. The work follows 26:15. The frame gives shape to everything else.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see faithful execution of the structural plan. Both traditions read the verse descriptively. Acacia was a hardy desert wood suited for a portable structure carried through the wilderness.

Exodus 36:21

21Ten cubits was the length of a board, and a cubit and a half the width of each board.

Arminian

Each board is ten cubits long and a cubit and a half wide. Arminian readers see uniform dimensions for a stable frame. Exact modern equivalents remain approximate.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see precise conformity to the pattern. Both traditions treat measurement details without forcing theological meaning. Modern conversions of these measures are approximate, since cubit lengths varied in the ancient world.

Exodus 36:22

22Each board had two tenons, joined to one another. He made all the boards of the tabernacle this way.

Arminian

Each board has two tenons, joined together. Arminian readers see craftsmanship that allows the frame to stand firmly. Practical design serves holy purpose. The tenons fit into the silver sockets described next.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see skilled joinery in obedience to instruction. Both traditions value careful workmanship. The repeated phrase that he made all the boards this way stresses consistency throughout the frame.

Exodus 36:23

23He made the boards for the tabernacle, twenty boards for the south side southward.

Arminian

Twenty boards are made for the south side. Arminian readers see the structure taking shape side by side. The numbers repeat 26:18. The south side is built first.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see orderly construction. Both traditions read the verse as straightforward inventory. Each side corresponds to the dimensions God specified, so the parts fit together into one dwelling.

Exodus 36:24

24He made forty sockets of silver under the twenty boards: two sockets under one board for its two tenons, and two sockets under another board for its two tenons.

Arminian

Forty silver sockets support the twenty boards, two per board. Arminian readers see a firm foundation for the frame. Silver bases support the dwelling. The sockets anchor the boards securely.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise note the silver sockets; 38:25–27 connects the silver with the census offering. Both traditions read this descriptively. The precise count of two sockets for each board shows the methodical character of the work.

Exodus 36:25

25For the second side of the tabernacle, on the north side, he made twenty boards

Arminian

Twenty boards are made for the north side. Arminian readers see symmetry with the south side. The work proceeds systematically. The north wall mirrors the south wall in number and design.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see orderly obedience. Both traditions note no contrast. The north wall is built exactly like the south, showing that the whole structure follows one consistent design from God.

Exodus 36:26

26and their forty sockets of silver: two sockets under one board, and two sockets under another board.

Arminian

Forty silver sockets support the north boards. Arminian readers see consistency between sides of the structure. The method repeats faithfully. Every board on the north side rests on its own foundation.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see conformity to the pattern. Both traditions read this as descriptive detail. The repetition of the socket pattern underlines that the builders did not improvise on God’s design.

Exodus 36:27

27For the far part of the tabernacle westward he made six boards.

Arminian

Six boards are made for the rear on the west. Arminian readers see the back wall completing the enclosure. The rear faces west, with the entrance to the east.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the orientation following God’s design. Both traditions read the verse without a theological fork. The orientation placed the most holy place at the rear, farthest from the entrance.

Exodus 36:28

28He made two boards for the corners of the tabernacle in the far part.

Arminian

Two boards are made for the rear corners. Arminian readers see special attention to the corners for stability. The design addresses structural needs. Corners are where a frame most needs strength.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see careful execution. Both traditions treat the detail as descriptive. The corner boards ensure that the rear wall joins the side walls securely, as 26:23 instructed.

Exodus 36:29

29They were double beneath, and in the same way they were all the way to its top to one ring. He did this to both of them in the two corners.

Arminian

The corner boards are doubled from bottom to top, joined at one ring. Arminian readers see reinforcement at critical points. The exact construction is not fully clear from the text, and interpreters reconstruct it differently.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise acknowledge uncertainty in reconstructing the corners. Both traditions should avoid presenting a reconstruction as certain. The narrative’s point is faithful execution rather than a technical blueprint for modern readers.

Exodus 36:30

30There were eight boards and their sockets of silver, sixteen sockets—under every board two sockets.

Arminian

The rear has eight boards with sixteen silver sockets. Arminian readers see the totals confirming the design. Every board has its foundation. The tally closes the description of the frame.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see completeness in execution. Both traditions read the verse as shared inventory. The tally confirms that the rear wall matches the pattern, with each board resting on two silver sockets.

Exodus 36:31

31He made bars of acacia wood: five for the boards of the one side of the tabernacle,

Arminian

Five acacia bars are made for one side. Arminian readers see bars binding the boards together. Stability comes through connection. The bars keep the upright boards from shifting.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see orderly construction following 26:26. Both traditions read this descriptively. The bars hold the upright boards in line, giving the portable structure strength without permanent foundations.

Exodus 36:32

32and five bars for the boards of the other side of the tabernacle, and five bars for the boards of the tabernacle for the hinder part westward.

Arminian

Five bars are made for the other side and five for the rear. Arminian readers see the frame bound on every side. The structure is unified.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see conformity to the plan. Both traditions note no contrast. The same number of bars on each wall gives the frame balanced strength, following the instructions closely.

Exodus 36:33

33He made the middle bar to pass through in the middle of the boards from the one end to the other.

Arminian

The middle bar runs through the boards from end to end. Arminian readers see a central support uniting the whole wall. Its exact placement is described briefly.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see structural unity. Both traditions read the detail without forced symbolism. Interpreters discuss whether this bar passed through the boards or along them; the text’s point is a frame firmly held together.

Exodus 36:34

34He overlaid the boards with gold, and made their rings of gold as places for the bars, and overlaid the bars with gold.

Arminian

The boards and bars are overlaid with gold, with gold rings for the bars. Arminian readers see beauty and value marking God’s dwelling. Gold signals the holiness of the place.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see glory fitting God’s presence. Both traditions share the sense that the dwelling reflects God’s worth. The gold overlay unites strength and splendor: acacia gives structure, while gold marks the place as holy to the LORD.

Exodus 36:35

35He made the veil of blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen, with cherubim. He made it the work of a skillful workman.

Arminian

The veil is made of colored yarn and linen with cherubim. Arminian readers see the separation between the holy place and the most holy place. Christians in both traditions read Hebrews 10:20 and the torn veil of Matthew 27:51 in light of Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the veil guarding the most holy place and point to its fulfillment in Christ’s opening of access to God. This typology is shared ground, not a point of division.

Exodus 36:36

36He made four pillars of acacia for it, and overlaid them with gold. Their hooks were of gold. He cast four sockets of silver for them.

Arminian

Four gold-covered acacia pillars with gold hooks and silver sockets hold the veil. Arminian readers see the veil supported with honor. The structure reflects the holiness of what lies behind it.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see careful execution of 26:32. Both traditions read the verse descriptively. The pillars combine acacia, gold, and silver, reflecting the honored place of the veil before the most holy place.

Exodus 36:37

37He made a screen for the door of the tent, of blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen, the work of an embroiderer;

Arminian

An embroidered screen of colored yarn and linen is made for the tent door. Arminian readers see the entrance marked with beauty. The door invites approach on God’s terms.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the screen as the appointed entry into the tent. Some Christian readers connect entry imagery with Christ, as in John 10:9; such readings are shared.

Exodus 36:38

38and the five pillars of it with their hooks. He overlaid their capitals and their fillets with gold, and their five sockets were of bronze.

Arminian

Five pillars with hooks, gold-overlaid capitals and bands, and bronze sockets support the screen. Arminian readers see bronze at the entrance, gold within. The gradation of materials continues.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the completed entrance following the pattern. Both traditions see God dwelling among his people in a place made with care, a theme fulfilled in John 1:14.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
