Passage

Exodus 36

Verses 8–19 · WEB

The narrative now describes the making of the tabernacle’s curtains and coverings, closely repeating the instructions of 26:1–14. The repetition stresses that the work followed the pattern God gave. Both traditions read the tabernacle as God’s provision for dwelling among his people, and both share the New Testament reading of it as a shadow fulfilled in Christ (Hebrews 8–9; John 1:14).

Exodus 36:8

8All the wise-hearted men among those who did the work made the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine twined linen, blue, purple, and scarlet. They made them with cherubim, the work of a skillful workman.

Arminian

The skilled workers make ten linen curtains with cherubim. Arminian readers see beauty and craftsmanship devoted to God’s dwelling. The work follows the instructions given in 26:1.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see obedience to the pattern God revealed. The cherubim recall guardians of God’s holy presence, as in Genesis 3:24. Both traditions share this reading.

Exodus 36:9

9The length of each curtain was twenty-eight cubits, and the width of each curtain four cubits. All the curtains had one measure.

Arminian

Each curtain measures twenty-eight by four cubits. Arminian readers see precise measurements reflecting careful obedience. The WEB footnote estimates a cubit at about 18 inches, though ancient cubits varied.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see precision in following God’s design. Exact modern equivalents of ancient measures are uncertain. Both traditions read the uniform size as part of an ordered structure.

Exodus 36:10

10He coupled five curtains to one another, and the other five curtains he coupled to one another.

Arminian

Five curtains are joined together, and the other five likewise. Arminian readers see the structure assembled in ordered sections. The detail repeats 26:3. Building in sets makes a large structure manageable.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see orderly construction according to God’s instructions. Both traditions read the verse as descriptive, without a theological fork. The narrator follows the earlier instructions almost word for word.

Exodus 36:11

11He made loops of blue on the edge of the one curtain from the edge in the coupling. Likewise he made in the edge of the curtain that was outermost in the second coupling.

Arminian

Blue loops are made on the edges of the joined sets. Arminian readers see small details enabling the whole to function. Faithfulness reaches even fastenings.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see care in executing every part of the pattern. Both traditions value the thoroughness of the work. The loops anticipate the clasps that will join the two sets into one tabernacle.

Exodus 36:12

12He made fifty loops in the one curtain, and he made fifty loops in the edge of the curtain that was in the second coupling. The loops were opposite to one another.

Arminian

Fifty loops face each other on the two sets. Arminian readers see symmetry and order in the design. The repetition stresses obedience. The loops are placed so that the sets meet precisely.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see precision matching the instructions in 26:5. Both traditions read the verse as straightforward description. The narrator repeats the numbers to show that the work matched what God required.

Exodus 36:13

13He made fifty clasps of gold, and coupled the curtains to one another with the clasps: so the tabernacle was a unit.

Arminian

Fifty gold clasps join the curtains so the tabernacle is one. Arminian readers see unity as the goal of the craftsmanship. Many parts become one dwelling.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the tabernacle as a unified whole. Some Christian readers reflect on unity in God’s dwelling with his people; that reflection is shared, not disputed.

Exodus 36:14

14He made curtains of goats’ hair for a covering over the tabernacle. He made them eleven curtains.

Arminian

Eleven goats’ hair curtains form a covering over the tabernacle. Arminian readers see protection layered over the inner beauty. Humbler material guards the finer work.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the covering as part of the structure’s design in 26:7. Both traditions read it descriptively. The inner curtains of fine linen are thus protected by a sturdier outer layer.

Exodus 36:15

15The length of each curtain was thirty cubits, and four cubits the width of each curtain. The eleven curtains had one measure.

Arminian

Each goats’ hair curtain measures thirty by four cubits. Arminian readers see the outer layer slightly larger to cover the inner curtains. The measurements reflect careful planning.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see precise execution of the pattern. As with verse 9, exact modern equivalents are uncertain. Both traditions read the verse as shared description.

Exodus 36:16

16He coupled five curtains by themselves, and six curtains by themselves.

Arminian

Five curtains are joined, and six are joined. Arminian readers see the arrangement following the instructions in 26:9. Even the asymmetry is part of the plan.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see faithful conformity to the design. Both traditions note no theological contrast here. The arrangement allows the extra curtain to be folded at the front, as 26:9 describes.

Exodus 36:17

17He made fifty loops on the edge of the curtain that was outermost in the coupling, and he made fifty loops on the edge of the curtain which was outermost in the second coupling.

Arminian

Fifty loops are made on the outer edges of each set. Arminian readers see repetition of the earlier method for the outer covering. Consistency marks the work.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see orderly execution. Both traditions read the verse as part of the descriptive inventory. The same pattern of loops and clasps is applied to the second covering.

Exodus 36:18

18He made fifty clasps of bronze to couple the tent together, that it might be a unit.

Arminian

Fifty bronze clasps join the tent into one. Arminian readers see bronze used for the outer layer, gold for the inner. Materials differ with proximity to the holiest place.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see a gradation of materials reflecting degrees of holiness. Interpreters discuss this pattern, but it is shared rather than disputed between the traditions.

Exodus 36:19

19He made a covering for the tent of rams’ skins dyed red, and a covering of sea cow hides above.

Arminian

Coverings of red-dyed rams’ skins and hides go over the tent. Arminian readers see further protection for the dwelling. The WEB’s sea cow hides render an uncertain Hebrew term.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the outer coverings as practical protection in the wilderness. Both traditions should acknowledge uncertainty about the hides rather than build doctrine on them.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
