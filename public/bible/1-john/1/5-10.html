Passage

1 John 1

Verses 5–10 · ESV · Voices

The message heard from Him is that God is light, and in Him is no darkness at all. Fellowship-talk while walking in darkness is a lie; walking in the light brings fellowship and cleansing by the Son’s blood. If we say we have no sin we deceive ourselves; if we confess, He is faithful and just to forgive and to cleanse. Claiming we have not sinned makes Him a liar.

1 John 1:5

5This is the message we have heard from him and proclaim to you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all.

Arminian

This is the sum of the message: God is light, and in Him is no darkness at all. Wesley: light of wisdom, love, holiness, glory; no contrary principle; pure, unmixed light.

Shared confession: God is light as 4:8 will say God is love. Arminian hearing: the God who is light still invites walkers out of darkness. The message is from the Son, not a later party platform.

Reformed

Calvin: God is light, and is in the light—not to be taken too strictly. There is nothing in Him but what is bright, pure, unalloyed; He makes all things manifest by His brightness. No union between light and darkness, so no fellowship while we walk in darkness.

Reformed hearing: God hates an evil conscience, pollution, and whatever pertains to darkness. Shared: in Him is no darkness at all.

1 John 1:6

6If we say we have fellowship with him while we walk in darkness, we lie and do not practice the truth.

Arminian

If we say we have fellowship while we walk in darkness, we lie. Wesley: say with tongue or heart; darkness is sin of any kind; our actions prove the truth is not in us.

Arminian honesty: fellowship-talk is not a covering for an unholy walk. Grace that saves still refuses the lie. Walking in darkness while naming Christ is not a weak stage of the same grace; it is falsehood.

Reformed

Calvin: an argument from what is inconsistent. God sanctifies all who are His. What God communicates is not a vain fiction; the power of this fellowship must shine in our life, otherwise possession of the gospel is fallacious.

Reformed honesty: we do not act truthfully. Both camps refuse a fellowship that will not walk. The lie is named so that the next verses’ confession can be real.

1 John 1:7

7But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin.

Arminian

If we walk in the light as He is in the light, we have fellowship, and the blood of Jesus His Son cleanses us from all sin. Wesley: in all holiness; the blood, with the grace purchased thereby, cleanses from all sin, original and actual, taking away guilt and power.

Arminian cleansing: walk and blood belong together. Cleansing is real—guilt and power. This is not perfection already arrived; it is the blood presently cleansing those who walk in the light they have.

Reformed

Calvin: proof of union with God if we are conformable to Him—not that purity conciliates us as prior cause, but union is made evident by the effect. He who aspires to God’s likeness, however distant, is said to be like God. Integrity of conscience distinguishes light from darkness.

Reformed cleansing: the blood cleanses from all sin as a daily, perpetual benefit in the Church. Remission cannot be separated from repentance. The whole cleansing is assigned to Christ’s blood, not half to works.

1 John 1:8

8If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.

Arminian

If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves. Wesley: any child of man, before His blood has cleansed us; no sin to be cleansed from, instead of confessing.

Both: real sin. Classical Arminians will not let holiness language become a claim to present sinlessness. The child of God still has sin to confess. Self-deception is the alternative to 1:9.

Reformed

Calvin commends grace from its necessity: no one is free from sin; we are all lost except the Lord comes with pardon. Sin here is not only vicious inclination but the fault that renders us guilty. A universal declaration: none of the saints are exempt. Augustine fitly refuted Pelagians with this verse.

Both: real sin. Reformed readers deny a perfection that needs no pardon. The confession of guilt is required lest we by lying deceive ourselves.

1 John 1:9

9If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

Arminian

If we confess, He is faithful and just to forgive and to cleanse. Wesley: with a penitent and believing heart. Faithful because He promised; just—and for this very reason He will pardon, because when the debt is paid it is equity to cancel the bond. Forgive takes away guilt; cleanse purifies from every kind and degree.

Both: real confession, real forgiveness. Arminian pastoral: the door is open as often as we come. Justice here is gospel justice—the paid debt—not a threat hanging over the penitent. Cleansing is both pardon and purification.

Reformed

Calvin: God will be propitious provided they acknowledge themselves sinners. Hell reigns where there is no peace with God. He is called just because He has bound Himself by His word; He would not have Himself deemed just except He forgives. Confession to God must be sincere and includes true repentance. A twofold fruit: He forgives, and He renews.

Both: real confession, real forgiveness. Reformed pastoral: facility of mercy is not an enticement to sin. Cleansing is begun and goes on daily until completed. No one in the flesh is yet fully cleansed as to reformation; John refers to what God performs.

1 John 1:10

10If we say we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.

Arminian

If we say we have not sinned, we make Him a liar. Wesley: retain even to our lives’ end a deep sense of past sins. God says all have sinned; His word is not in us if we will not receive it.

Arminian humility: finished cleansing in glory does not license a present boast of never having sinned. The word must have place in the heart. 1:8–10 together: sin is real, confession is real, pardon is real.

Reformed

Calvin: they who claim purity blaspheme God. He represents the whole race as guilty. To escape the charge is to war with God and accuse Him of falsehood. His word is not in us: we reject the great truth that all are under guilt.

Reformed humility: due progress in the word is to groan under the burden of sins and flee to mercy. 1:8–10 together: no saint is innocent; confession meets a faithful, just Forgiver.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on God who is light, real sin, real confession, and real forgiveness—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside 1 John 1:5–10.

Arminian Notable voices

“God is light—the light of wisdom, love, holiness, glory. If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. But if with a penitent and believing heart we confess our sins, he is faithful—because he had promised this blessing—and just. For this very reason he will pardon: when the debt is paid, it is the part of equity to cancel the bond. Both to forgive us our sins—to take away all the guilt—and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness—to purify our souls from every kind and every degree of it.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on 1 John 1:5, 8–9)

Reformed Notable voices

“If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves. As no one is free from sin, we are all lost and undone, except the Lord comes to our aid with the remedy of pardon. By the word sin is meant here not only corrupt inclination, but the fault which really renders us guilty. If we confess, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. This confession, as it is made to God, must be in sincerity; and the heart cannot speak to God without newness of life. It then includes true repentance.”
John Calvin · Commentary on 1 John (on 1 John 1:8–9)
