Passage

1 John 5

Verses 1–12 · ESV · Voices

Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God, and love of the Father includes love of those born of Him. His commandments are not burdensome; faith is the victory that overcomes the world. Jesus Christ came by water and blood; the Spirit testifies. The testimony is that God gave us eternal life, and this life is in His Son: whoever has the Son has life.

1 John 5:1

1Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God, and everyone who loves the Father loves whoever has been born of him.

Arminian

Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God; everyone who loves the Father loves whoever has been born of Him. Wesley: the paragraph’s sum is 5:13—know that you who believe have eternal life. Faith is first and last with John also. Loving the Begetter, a natural affection to all His brethren.

Arminian birth: believing and being born belong together in the text’s order as lived reality. Love of God spills to the begotten. The new birth is known in faith that receives Jesus as the Christ.

Reformed

Calvin: faith and brotherly love are united; God regenerates us by faith, so He must be loved as Father, and this love embraces all His children. All born of God believe that Jesus is the Christ; Christ alone is the object of faith. To believe that He is the Christ is to hope from Him all that was promised of the Messiah. All who really believe have been born of God; faith is above the human mind; we must be drawn by the Father. The singular “begotten” includes all the faithful, not Christ only (against some ancients).

Reformed birth: no one has faith except born of God. Begin with the godly in love, and this exercise teaches love to all. Shared: believe, born, love the begotten.

1 John 5:2

2By this we know that we love the children of God, when we love God and obey his commandments.

Arminian

By this we know we love the children of God: when we love God and obey His commandments. Wesley: a plain proof that we love the children of God as His children.

Arminian proof: love to the children is not a mood detached from love to God and kept commandments. The two loves test each other.

Reformed

Calvin: true love is that which is towards God. Men are rightly loved when God holds the primacy. Mutual love is to be cultivated so that God may be honored. To love of God he joins keeping the law: reverence connected with love.

Reformed proof: it often happens that we love men apart from God. Shared: love of the children is known in love of God and obedience.

1 John 5:3

3For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments. And his commandments are not burdensome.

Arminian

This is the love of God, that we keep His commandments; and they are not burdensome. Wesley: the only sure proof. Not grievous to any that are born of God.

Arminian not burdensome: new birth makes the commands a fitting yoke, not a grind. Love is not idle. Keeping is the proof, enabled by the birth of v. 1.

Reformed

Calvin: if constrained only by fear we obey, we are far from true obedience. Hearts devoted in willing reverence, then life formed to the law. Not grievous has been added lest difficulties damp zeal. The difficulty arises not from the nature of the law but from corrupt flesh. He confines “not grievous” to God’s children: it comes through the power of the Spirit. Facility also because they are sustained by pardon and do not despond when they come short.

Reformed not burdensome: we must fight; the world hinders. He only keeps the law who resists the world. Shared: love keeps; commands are not grievous to the born.

1 John 5:4

4For everyone who has been born of God overcomes the world. And this is the victory that has overcome the world—our faith.

Arminian

Everyone born of God overcomes the world; this is the victory that has overcome the world—our faith. Wesley: the most unlimited universality. Conquers whatever allures or frights from keeping commandments. The grand means: our faith; all things possible to him that believes.

Arminian victory: faith is the means of overcoming, present tense as a fight faith can win. Born of God is the who; faith is the how. The world of 2:15 is beatable.

Reformed

Calvin: whence comes this victory? He makes it depend on faith. Past time more emphatical: that has overcome, as though the enemy already put to flight. Warfare continues; we are already partakers of victory. This confidence does not introduce indifference. World includes whatever is adverse to the Spirit: corruption of nature, lusts, crafts of Satan. He does not teach that God only brings some help so that we, aided, are able; victory depends on faith alone, and faith receives from another that by which it overcomes.

Reformed victory: they take away from God who sing triumph to their own power. Shared: the victory is our faith.

1 John 5:5

5Who is it that overcomes the world except the one who believes that Jesus is the Son of God?

Arminian

Who overcomes the world except the one who believes that Jesus is the Son of God? Wesley: superior to all worldly care, desire, fear? Every believer, and none else.

Arminian who: the overcomer is the believer. No other candidate. The Son of God is the object that makes the world lose its mastery.

Reformed

Calvin: a reason for the previous sentence. We conquer by faith because we derive strength from Christ—“I can do all things through him that strengtheneth me.” He only can conquer Satan and the world who, diffident as to himself, recumbs on Christ’s power alone. Faith is a real apprehension of Christ, applying His power to ourselves.

Reformed who: not self-trust. Shared: the believer in the Son overcomes.

1 John 5:6

6This is he who came by water and blood—Jesus Christ; not by the water only but by the water and the blood. And the Spirit is the one who testifies, because the Spirit is the truth.

Arminian

This is He who came by water and blood—Jesus Christ; not by the water only but by the water and the blood. The Spirit testifies, because the Spirit is the truth. Wesley: not by the water only wherein He was baptized, but by the water and the blood which He shed when He finished the work; when all was finished, blood and water came out of His side. The Spirit testifies by Moses, prophets, the Baptist, apostles, and the New Testament writings. The Spirit is truth—the very God of truth.

Arminian came: baptism’s water and the cross’s blood together; Jesus is demonstrated to be the Christ by completing what He undertook. The Spirit’s witness is public in Scripture and inward as truth.

Reformed

Calvin: the real substance of the law’s shadows appears in Him. Under water and blood, the whole perfection of holiness and righteousness: water washed filth, blood made expiation. The law adumbrated; Messiah performed. Reference to baptism as the water is not probable; he thinks of the water and blood from Christ’s side, that the faithful may know cleansing and the sprinklings of blood are fulfilled in Him. The Spirit bears witness: a full firmness produced by His testimony. He calls the Spirit truth because His authority is indubitable.

Reformed came: not only some part of salvation in Christ, but the whole. Shared: water and blood, and the Spirit who is truth. (ESV, with the Greek majority, does not include the later heavenly-witness expansion in v. 7; Wesley argued for it, Calvin was inclined to receive it but noted Greek copies disagreed. The notes follow the ESV text.)

1 John 5:7

7For there are three that testify:

Arminian

For there are three that testify. In the ESV, this line introduces the three of v. 8. Wesley, reading the longer text, treated a heavenly three as recapitulation of the Father, Word, and Spirit.

Arminian handling: whatever one makes of the later clause, the apostolic point in this range is the agreeing testimony to Jesus as the Christ. Do not let a textual dispute bury the three that follow.

Reformed

Calvin noted that the whole heavenly-witness verse has been by some omitted; Greek copies did not agree; he was inclined to receive it as found in copies he approved, taking “these three are one” as consent, not a separate essence-proof. He also showed how the passage flows if one refers only to witnesses on earth.

Reformed handling: the ESV text is the three of v. 8. The doctrinal confession of Father, Word, and Spirit does not depend on this clause. The testimony that follows is enough.

1 John 5:8

8the Spirit and the water and the blood; and these three agree.

Arminian

The Spirit and the water and the blood; and these three agree. Wesley (on the earthly three): the Spirit in the word, confirmed by miracles; the water of baptism, typifying purity and inward purifying; the blood represented in the Lord’s supper and applied to consciences. These three harmoniously agree in one testimony: Jesus Christ is the divine, complete, only Savior of the world.

Arminian agree: one testimony, not three gospels. Savior of the world is Wesley’s own width here, matching 2:2.

Reformed

Calvin: water and blood applied so that rejecters of Christ have no excuse. He adds the Spirit, who holds first place: without Him water and blood would flow without benefit. He seals the testimony on hearts, makes the fruit of Christ’s death come to us, makes Christ with all His blessings ours. Three agree in one.

Reformed agree: faith does not lay hold on a bare or empty Christ. Cleansing and expiation are distinguished for our infirmity, as the law had both washings and sacrifices. Shared: three witnesses, one Christ.

1 John 5:9

9If we receive the testimony of men, the testimony of God is greater, for this is the testimony of God that he has borne concerning his Son.

Arminian

If we receive the testimony of men, God’s testimony is greater: this is the testimony God has borne concerning His Son. Wesley: we receive men’s testimony continually; God’s is of higher authority—this very testimony which God the Father, together with the Word and the Spirit, hath testified of the Son as the Saviour of the world.

Arminian greater: refusing this testimony is not a small historical shrug. Saviour of the world again names the width of what God has testified.

Reformed

Calvin: from the less to the greater. If in worldly affairs we stand to the words of men who may lie, how unreasonable that God should have less credit when sitting as it were on His own throne. Our corruption alone prevents us from receiving Christ. The testimony of God is not only what the Spirit imprints but also what we derive from water and blood: that power was heavenly.

Reformed greater: the blood of Christ is not estimated in a common human manner. Shared: God’s testimony concerning the Son is greater than men’s.

1 John 5:10

10Whoever believes in the Son of God has the testimony in himself. Whoever does not believe God has made him a liar, because he has not believed in the testimony that God has borne concerning his Son.

Arminian

Whoever believes in the Son has the testimony in himself; whoever does not believe God has made Him a liar. Wesley: the believer has the dear evidence in himself; the unbeliever supposes that to be false which God has expressly testified.

Arminian in himself: faith has inward evidence, and unbelief is an accusation against God’s truthfulness. The testimony is there to be believed.

Reformed

Calvin: the faithful know themselves beyond the danger of erring, because they have God as foundation. The ungodly are guilty of extreme blasphemy, charging God with falsehood. Nothing is more valued by God than His truth. This is why unbelief is so severely condemned: the glory of God is implicated. A man may be like an angel in other parts of life; if he rejects Christ, his sanctity is diabolical.

Reformed in himself: every one of the godly feels within that God is the author of his faith. Beginning of true religion: obediently embrace this doctrine.

1 John 5:11

11And this is the testimony, that God gave us eternal life, and this life is in his Son.

Arminian

This is the testimony: God gave us eternal life, and this life is in His Son. Wesley: a title to, and the real beginning of, eternal life; purchased by and treasured up in His Son, who has all the springs and fullness to communicate to His body the church, first in grace then in glory.

Arminian life: given, located in the Son, begun now. Title and beginning together. The church receives from His fullness.

Reformed

Calvin: having set forth the benefit, he invites us to believe. God freely offers life; ingratitude is intolerable if we do not receive a doctrine so sweet. Three things: we are given up to death until God restores us—life is a gift, not acquired by merits; this life is conferred by the gospel; we cannot become partakers otherwise than by believing in Christ. No other way appointed.

Reformed life: in the Son, not elsewhere. Shared: eternal life given, and in the Son.

1 John 5:12

12Whoever has the Son has life; whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life.

Arminian

Whoever has the Son has life; whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life. Wesley: he that hath the Son living and reigning in him by faith hath this life; he that hath not, no part or lot therein. Former clause: the Son, because believers know Him; latter: the Son of God, that unbelievers may know how great a blessing they fall short of.

Arminian have: having is by faith, a living reign. Not having is not a secret exception for heroic pagans in this verse’s pastoral aim; it is the church’s public line: life is in the Son.

Reformed

Calvin: confirmation. Life is in none but in Christ. He excludes all from the hope of life who seek it not in Christ. To have Christ is to possess Him by faith. All separated from the body of Christ are without life. Specious virtues from an impure heart have an ill odor to God. Purity and genuine religion come from the Spirit of Christ. Righteousness of men is in the remission of sins; Christ alone reconciles the Father.

Reformed have: God is propitious to none but in Christ. Shared: have the Son, have life; have not, have not.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on new birth and faith, a victory over the world, and the Spirit, water, and blood that testify—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside 1 John 5:1–12.

Arminian Notable voices

“The scope and sum of this whole paragraph appears from the conclusion: these things have I written to you who believe, that ye may know that ye who believe have eternal life. Whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world—conquers whatever it can lay in the way, either to allure or fright the children of God from keeping his commandments. And this is the victory—even our faith. This is he who came by water and blood—not by the water only, wherein he was baptized, but by the water and the blood which he shed when he had finished the work. And this is the sum of that testimony, that God hath given us a title to, and the real beginning of, eternal life; and that this is purchased by, and treasured up in, his Son.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on 1 John 5:1–6, 11)

Reformed Notable voices

“Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is born of God. Faith cannot be separated from love. All they who really believe have been born of God; for faith is far above the reach of the human mind, so that we must be drawn to Christ by our heavenly Father. This is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith. He makes the victory over the world to depend on faith. This is he that came by water and blood. Under these two the Apostle denotes the whole perfection of holiness and righteousness: by water was all filth washed away, and by blood was expiation made. The Spirit renders the faithful certain. This is the record, that God hath given to us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. He that hath the Son hath life; and he that hath not the Son of God hath not life.”
John Calvin · Commentary on 1 John (on 1 John 5:1–12)
