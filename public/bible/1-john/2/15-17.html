1 John 2

Verses 15–17 · ESV · Voices

Do not love the world or the things in it. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him. All that is in the world—desires of the flesh, desires of the eyes, pride of life—is not from the Father. The world is passing away with its desires; whoever does the will of God abides forever.

1 John 2:15

15Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.

Arminian

Do not love the world or the things in it. If anyone loves the world, the Father’s love is not in him. Wesley, to all ages: pursue your victory by overcoming the world. Seek happiness in visible things and you do not love God.

Arminian either/or: two loves cannot reign. This is not hatred of people or of creation as gift (4:1–5 of 1 Timothy already fenced that). It is the world as rival happiness. Grace that can be received can also be spent on visible things instead of the Father.

Reformed

Calvin: the only rule for living religiously is to love God; but occupied with vain love of the world we turn thoughts another way. This vanity must first be torn away. The world: everything connected with present life apart from the kingdom and the hope of eternal life—corruptions of every kind, allurements by which man withdraws from God.

Reformed either/or: we necessarily forget God when we regard nothing so much as the earth. A corrupt lust that thinks no more of immortal life than mute animals cannot be of God. Shared: the Father’s love and the world’s love do not cohabit.

1 John 2:16

16For all that is in the world—the desires of the flesh and the desires of the eyes and pride of life—is not from the Father but is from the world.

Arminian

All that is in the world: desires of the flesh, of the eyes, pride of life—not from the Father. Wesley: outward senses; pleasures of imagination, grand, new, or beautiful; pomp in clothes, houses, living, which procures honor and gratifies pride—including desire of praise and, remotely, covetousness. All from the prince of this world.

Arminian diagnosis: three mouths of the same hunger. The issue is source: not from the Father. Creation remains good; this appetite is the world’s, and it can be refused.

Reformed

Calvin: three kinds parenthetically, examples of the pursuits of men who live for the world. Lust of the flesh: worldly men intent on living softly, only on their own advantages. Lust of the eyes: libidinous looks and vanity that delights in pomps. Pride of life: haughtiness, ambition, contempt of others, blind love of self.

Reformed diagnosis: as soon as the world presents itself, corrupt-heart lusts are captivated like unbridled beasts. Various lusts, all adverse to God, bear rule. Shared anatomy of the world; the quarrel is not whether these are deadly.

1 John 2:17

17And the world is passing away along with its desires, but whoever does the will of God abides forever.

Arminian

The world is passing away with its desires; whoever does the will of God abides forever. Wesley: all that can gratify those desires passes with it. He that loves God, not the world, abides in the enjoyment of what he loves, forever.

Arminian forever: the will of God is doable by grace, and the abiding is promised to the doer. Passing world is pastoral urgency, not a slogan. Train the loves toward what lasts.

Reformed

Calvin: nothing in the world but what is fading. True happiness God offers is eternal; it is shameful to be entangled with what will vanish. Doing the will of God: not perfect keeping of the law, but the obedience of faith, imperfect yet approved. Christ comes to the despairing, regenerates, and makes the endeavor obtain the praise of perfect righteousness.

Reformed forever: the law makes the will known, but no one satisfies it; happiness is not hoped from it as a completed score. Shared: the world passes; the doer of God’s will abides. The how of that doing is where camps usually part—here both refuse despair and refuse a world-love.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on not loving the world, three lusts, and a will of God that abides—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside 1 John 2:15–17.

Arminian Notable voices

“Love not the world—pursue your victory by overcoming the world. If any man love the world—seek happiness in visible things, he does not love God. The desire of the flesh—of the pleasure of the outward senses. The desire of the eye—of the pleasures of imagination. The pride of life—all that pomp which generally procures honour from the bulk of mankind. The world passeth away, and the desire thereof. But he that doeth the will of God—that loves God, not the world—abideth for ever.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on 1 John 2:15–17)

Reformed Notable voices

“By the world understand everything connected with the present life, apart from the kingdom of God and the hope of eternal life. If any man love the world, the love of the Father is not in him. He proves how necessary it is to cast away the love of the world if we wish to please God. The lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life: as soon as the world presents itself, our lusts are captivated by it. The world passeth away: they who seek their happiness from it make a wretched provision for themselves. He that doeth the will of God abideth for ever: he means the obedience of faith, which, however imperfect, is yet approved by God.”
John Calvin · Commentary on 1 John (on 1 John 2:15–17)
