Passage

1 John 2

Verses 7–14 · ESV · Voices

The commandment is old—from the beginning—and new, true in Him and in them, because the darkness is passing and the true light is shining. Love of the brother abides in the light; hatred walks blind. John writes to children, fathers, and young men: sins forgiven, the One from the beginning known, the evil one overcome, the Father known, the word abiding.

1 John 2:7

7Beloved, I am writing you no new commandment, but an old commandment that you had from the beginning. The old commandment is the word that you have heard.

Arminian

No new commandment, but old—the word heard from the beginning. Wesley: not any new one; they had it even from their forefathers.

Arminian continuity: love is not a novelty John invented. The same word that began with them still binds. Newness will be in the next verse’s fulfillment, not in a replacement law.

Reformed

Calvin: he taught nothing respecting love but what had been heard from the beginning. Novelty is disliked; an unwonted yoke is not easily taken. Old here is first elements of the gospel, taught from their new entrance into religious life.

Reformed continuity: this proceeded from Christ Himself. Antiquity is not superstition’s age but Christ’s own first word to them.

1 John 2:8

8At the same time, it is a new commandment that I am writing to you, which is true in him and in you, because the darkness is passing away and the true light is already shining.

Arminian

At the same time it is new, true in Him and in you, because darkness is passing and the true light already shines. Wesley: new with regard to loving one another; exemplified in Him and now fulfilled by you as never before. The darkness of that dispensation is passed; Christ the true light shines in your hearts.

Arminian newness: the command is ancient and freshly true where Christ lives in a people. Light already shining is present grace, not only a future dawn.

Reformed

Calvin: new because God renews it daily so the faithful practice it through whole life. It does not grow old. This one command respecting love constitutes the whole truth of Christ as to conduct. The knowledge of Christ alone is sufficient to dissipate darkness.

Reformed newness: they stood as at the goal; the body of the Church has no other perfection than holy love continually among them. Daily progress remains; each faith has its dawn before noonday.

1 John 2:9

9Whoever says he is in the light and hates his brother is still in darkness.

Arminian

Whoever says he is in the light and hates his brother is still in darkness. Wesley: in Christ, united to Him; the very name brother shows the love due; void of Christ and of all true light.

Arminian test: light-talk with hatred is remaining night. Union with Christ will not sit beside contempt of the brother.

Reformed

Calvin: all are blind who are strangers to love. Love of God and love of brethren involve no contradiction: the latter is testimony of the former. Love is the rule of life.

Reformed test: fictitious sanctity dazzles while love is neglected. The saying without the loving is darkness until now.

1 John 2:10

10Whoever loves his brother abides in the light, and in him there is no cause for stumbling.

Arminian

Whoever loves his brother abides in the light, and in him there is no cause for stumbling. Wesley: for Christ’s sake; of God; he that hates is an occasion of stumbling to himself, while the lover has a free journey.

Arminian abiding: love is the road, not a decoration on it. Stumbling is what hatred does to the hater first.

Reformed

Calvin: he who acts in love will never stumble. Taken with Psalm 119:165: no stumbling-block to him.

Reformed abiding: love is not a side virtue. It is where the light is stayed in, and the walk is unentangled.

1 John 2:11

11But whoever hates his brother is in the darkness and walks in the darkness, and does not know where he is going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes.

Arminian

Whoever hates his brother is in darkness, walks in it, and does not know where he is going. Wesley: he must hate if he does not love; there is no medium. In sin, perplexity, entanglement; he knows not that he is on the high road to hell.

Arminian warning: no middle between love and hate in this letter’s moral grammar. Darkness is not a mood; it is a road.

Reformed

Calvin: whatever specious appearance of excellency you show, there is nothing but what is sinful if love is absent. Comparable to 1 Corinthians 13. This doctrine is not understood by the world because most are dazzled by disguises.

Reformed warning: hatred blinds. The walk does not know its end because darkness has taken the eyes.

1 John 2:12

12I am writing to you, little children, because your sins are forgiven for his name's sake.

Arminian

Little children: your sins are forgiven for His name’s sake. Wesley: this is the sum of what I have now written; he then proceeds to things built on this foundation.

Arminian foundation: forgiveness first, then the addresses to ages. Name’s sake locates pardon in Christ, not in the child’s achievement. The rest of the letter builds on this, it does not replace it.

Reformed

Calvin: little children means men of all ages here. Lest the exhortation obscure free remission, he again inculcates the doctrine that belongs to faith: salvation is laid up in Christ alone. For His name’s sake: the material cause, lest we seek other means to reconcile us to God.

Reformed foundation: holiness must be urged, but faith must retain primacy. Pass by free remission and you build without a foundation. Christ only, not other names.

1 John 2:13

13I am writing to you, fathers, because you know him who is from the beginning. I am writing to you, young men, because you have overcome the evil one. I write to you, children, because you know the Father.

Arminian

Fathers know Him who is from the beginning; young men have overcome the evil one; children know the Father. Wesley: the address is first proposed here, then enlarged. Fathers know the eternal God in a manner others do not; young men have overcome in many battles by faith; little children know the Father by the Spirit witnessing they are children of God.

Arminian ages: the same gospel fits every stage. Overcoming is by the power of faith, not by having finished the war. Knowing the Father is already a children’s gift.

Reformed

Calvin: he enumerates ages so no one exempts himself. The old may think they have exceeded the age of learning; children refuse as not yet old enough; the middle are occupied. He accommodates the gospel to all. Fathers: Christ was from the beginning, co-eternal with the Father. Young men: true strength is spiritual, from Christ; they have conquered because the Head has conquered. Children: they find the Father there.

Reformed ages: what is particular is also general. We are all like orphans until adoption by the gospel. Each is told what he most needs, that all stand in need of the doctrine.

1 John 2:14

14I write to you, fathers, because you know him who is from the beginning. I write to you, young men, because you are strong, and the word of God abides in you, and you have overcome the evil one.

Arminian

Fathers again: you know Him from the beginning. Young men: you are strong, the word abides, you have overcome. Wesley: he speaks briefly to fathers who needed not much, having deep acquaintance with God; young men are strong in faith, the word deeply rooted, the adversary often foiled.

Arminian strength: abiding word is how young faith stays in the fight. Overcoming is repeated because the battle is not theoretical. The word in the heart is the difference.

Reformed

Calvin notes the repetition; some thought copyists filled a number, but the better reading keeps the three twice for emphasis. Young men are strong because the word abides and they have overcome.

Reformed strength: we are conquerors before we engage, for our Head has once for all conquered. The word abiding is God’s means, not a second savior.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on an old-and-new commandment, brother-love, and a word to every age in the church—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside 1 John 2:7–14.

Arminian Notable voices

“I write not a new commandment—I do not speak of any new one. But the old commandment which ye had even from your forefathers. Again, I do write a new commandment to you—namely, with regard to loving one another. A commandment which, though it also was given long ago, yet is truly new in him and in you. It was exemplified in him, and is now fulfilled by you, in such a manner as it never was before. He that loveth his brother abideth in the light. I have written to you, beloved children, because your sins are forgiven you.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on 1 John 2:7–12)

Reformed Notable voices

“John reminds us that he taught nothing respecting love but what had been heard by the faithful from the beginning. He says new, because God, as it were, renews it by daily suggesting it, so that the faithful may practice it through their whole life. He that saith he is in the light, and hateth his brother, is in darkness even until now. I write unto you, little children, because your sins are forgiven you for his name’s sake. Holiness of life ought indeed to be urged, but still we ought ever to take heed, lest the doctrine of faith be smothered—that doctrine which teaches that Christ is the only author of salvation.”
John Calvin · Commentary on 1 John (on 1 John 2:7–12)
