Passage

1 Timothy 4

Verses 6–16 · ESV · Voices

Timothy will be a good servant of Christ Jesus if he puts these things before the brothers, trained in the words of the faith and good doctrine. He is to refuse irreverent myths, train for godliness, and set his hope on the living God, the Savior of all people, especially of those who believe. He must command and teach, be an example despite youth, devote himself to public reading, exhortation, and teaching, not neglect the gift given by prophecy with the elders’ hands, practice these things, and watch himself and the teaching—so saving himself and his hearers.

1 Timothy 4:6

6If you put these things before the brothers, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, being trained in the words of the faith and of the good doctrine that you have followed.

Arminian

Put these things before the brothers: the warning of 4:1–5 is not for Timothy’s private notebook. A good servant of Christ Jesus is trained in the words of the faith and the good doctrine he has already followed.

Arminian ministry: serving well is handing on what you have received, not inventing a brand. Training is ongoing; followed is already a history of grace embraced.

Reformed

A good minister of Jesus Christ, nourished in the words of faith and of good doctrine: Calvin heard a servant fed by the same gospel he serves.

Reformed charge: put these things before the church. Doctrine already followed is still to be set out. The minister is a steward, not a source.

1 Timothy 4:7

7Have nothing to do with irreverent, silly myths. Rather train yourself for godliness;

Arminian

Refuse irreverent, silly myths—speculative tales that feel religious and starve godliness. Train yourself: Wesley’s exercise thyself unto godliness. Holiness is not accidental.

Arminian energy: grace that is resistible is also grace that must be used. Training is cooperation with the Spirit, not self-salvation. Myths are a counterfeit workout.

Reformed

Refuse profane and old wives’ fables; exercise unto godliness: Calvin opposed curious tales with disciplined piety.

Reformed training: Godliness is God’s work in the believer, and the believer is commanded to train. Means of grace are not optional hobbies. Myths are rejected because they are not God’s Word.

1 Timothy 4:8

8for while bodily training is of some value, godliness is of value in every way, as it holds promise for the present life and also for the life to come.

Arminian

Bodily training has some value; godliness has value in every way—this life and the life to come. Wesley: profitable for all things, having the promise of the life that now is and of that which is to come.

Arminian hope: godliness is not grim postponement. Present life is already promised good, and the life to come is not a maybe. Train because both horizons are packed with promise.

Reformed

Bodily exercise profits a little; godliness is profitable unto all things: Calvin kept the comparison without despising the body.

Reformed promise: godliness carries promise now and later because God is faithful, not because training purchases heaven. The life to come is secured in Christ; present piety is its fitting walk.

1 Timothy 4:9

9The saying is trustworthy and deserving of full acceptance.

Arminian

The trustworthy saying formula returns (as in 3:1). Full acceptance: do not half-receive the promise of v. 8.

Both camps can sit here without a fight. The saying is God’s, to be taken whole.

Reformed

Faithful is the saying, worthy of all acceptation: Calvin treated the formula as a seal on what precedes and what follows.

Reformed hearing: full acceptance is the church’s amen, not a bargaining posture.

1 Timothy 4:10

10For to this end we toil and strive, because we have our hope set on the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of those who believe.

Arminian

We toil and strive because hope is set on the living God. Savior of all people, especially of those who believe: Wesley heard the living God who is willing that all should be saved, and in a special sense the Savior of believers.

Arminian soteriology: all is not a cipher for the elect. God is Savior toward all—sufficient provision, sincere willingness—and believers are those who actually receive that salvation. Especially marks the difference between offered rescue and embraced rescue, not between a secret few and a pretended all.

Reformed

Savior of all men, especially of those that believe: Calvin denied that all includes the reprobate as saved. All denotes men of every kind and rank, or God’s common kindness toward all, and a peculiar salvation for believers.

Reformed soteriology: God is Preserver of all creatures and Redeemer of the elect. Especially of those who believe names the only ones for whom Christ is Savior in the saving sense. Universal language here is not universal atonement as Arminians preach it; it is God’s wide providence and a gospel that goes to all sorts, with actual salvation restricted to those given faith.

Key contrast The live fork is how “Savior of all people, especially of those who believe” is heard. Arminian readers take all as a real saving willingness toward every person, with especially marking those who believe and are actually saved. Reformed readers take all as every kind of person (or common preservation), and especially as the elect who alone are saved in Christ.

1 Timothy 4:11

11Command and teach these things.

Arminian

Command and teach: Timothy is not only to model; he is to speak with authority these things—the doctrine of 4:1–10.

Arminian charge: teaching is a means of grace for hearers who can still receive or refuse. Command is pastoral courage, not a personality cult.

Reformed

These things command and teach: Calvin heard an apostolic charge with teeth.

Reformed ministry: the same gospel is both taught and commanded. God uses the minister’s mouth as a means; the authority is Christ’s.

1 Timothy 4:12

12Let no one despise you for your youth, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity.

Arminian

Youth is not a license to be ignored, and not an excuse to be unserious. Example in speech, conduct, love, faith, purity: Wesley’s let no one despise thy youth is answered by a life that cannot easily be despised.

Arminian pattern: love, faith, and purity are graces to be exercised in public. Timothy’s age is fenced by holiness, not by bluster.

Reformed

Let no man despise thy youth: be an example of the believers. Calvin located the cure for contempt in a visible pattern, not in demanding respect.

Reformed example: speech, walk, love, faith, purity are the minister’s first sermon. God often uses a young servant; the servant must not give despisers easy material.

1 Timothy 4:13

13Until I come, devote yourself to the public reading of Scripture, to exhortation, to teaching.

Arminian

Until I come: public reading, exhortation, teaching. The church’s gathered diet is Scripture read aloud, then pressed and taught. Wesley: give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to teaching.

Arminian means of grace: the public Word is how God ordinarily converts and builds. Devote yourself is a minister’s labor, offered to hearers who must still receive.

Reformed

Give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine until I come: Calvin made the public Scripture the spine of the wait.

Reformed worship: God addresses His people by the read Word and by teaching that stays tied to it. Exhortation is not a substitute for reading; it rides on it.

1 Timothy 4:14

14Do not neglect the gift you have, which was given you by prophecy when the council of elders laid their hands on you.

Arminian

A gift given by prophecy with the elders’ hands: Timothy must not neglect what was recognized and conferred in the church. Hands and prophecy do not replace the Spirit; they mark His giving in the open.

Arminian stewardship: gifts can be neglected. Grace is not a stored battery that runs without use. Lay hold; stir up; the laying on of hands was a real moment in a real council.

Reformed

Neglect not the gift that is in thee, given by prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the presbytery: Calvin treated the gift as God’s, recognized by the church’s act.

Reformed ordination: the hands do not create the gift; they attest it. Neglect is still possible as a ministerial sin, and the warning is a means God uses to keep His servant at the work.

1 Timothy 4:15

15Practice these things, immerse yourself in them, so that all may see your progress.

Arminian

Practice, immerse, so that all may see your progress. Growth is meant to be visible, not advertised as a brand.

Arminian progress: sanctification is real change over time, cooperated with, seen by the church. Immerse yourself is the opposite of a gift left on a shelf.

Reformed

Meditate upon these things; give thyself wholly to them, that thy profiting may appear to all: Calvin wanted progress that the flock can actually see.

Reformed growth: God grants increase, and the minister is told to sink himself into the work. Visible progress encourages the church that the same grace is at work in them.

1 Timothy 4:16

16Keep a close watch on yourself and on the teaching. Persist in this, for by so doing you will save both yourself and your hearers.

Arminian

Watch yourself and the teaching; persist. By so doing you will save both yourself and your hearers: Wesley took the double watch as the minister’s path of salvation and of saving others through the gospel he handles.

Arminian double care: a sound life with unsound doctrine, or sound doctrine with a neglected life, both ruin. Persist because falling away is a live danger, and because hearers really may be saved through a watched ministry. Salvation here is not a minister meriting heaven; it is God using means—self-watch and true teaching—toward Timothy and toward the flock.

Reformed

Take heed to thyself and to the doctrine; continue in them, for in doing this thou shalt both save thyself and them that hear thee: Calvin refused to let the verse become a minister’s self-salvation by works, and still let the means stand.

Reformed double care: God saves His people through a watched life and watched doctrine. Yourself and your hearers names instrument, not meritorious cause. Persistence is commanded because this is how the Savior of v. 10 ordinarily keeps pastor and people.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on training for godliness, the Savior of all people, and a young minister’s example—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside 1 Timothy 4:6–16.

Arminian Notable voices

“Exercise thyself unto godliness. For bodily exercise profiteth a little, but godliness is profitable for all things, having the promise of the life that now is, and of that which is to come. We trust in the living God, who is the Saviour of all men—Who is willing that all should be saved. Especially of those that believe. Let no one despise thy youth. Give attendance to reading, to exhortation, to teaching. Take heed to thyself and to the doctrine. For in so doing thou shalt save both thyself and them that hear thee.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on 1 Tim 4:7–16)

Reformed Notable voices

“Exercise thyself unto godliness. Godliness is profitable unto all things. We trust in the living God, who is the Saviour of all men, especially of those that believe. The word all does not include the reprobate, but denotes men of all kinds and ranks; or it means that God is the Saviour of all by the kind of goodness which is common, and of believers in a peculiar way. Take heed to thyself and to the doctrine; for in doing this thou shalt both save thyself and them that hear thee.”
John Calvin · Commentary on 1 Timothy (on 1 Tim 4:7–16)
