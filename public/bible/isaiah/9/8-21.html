Passage

Isaiah 9

Verses 8–21 · ESV · Voices

The Lord has sent a word against Jacob; it will fall on Israel. Ephraim and Samaria, proud, say: bricks have fallen, we will build with dressed stones; sycamores down, we will put cedars. The Lord raises Rezin’s adversaries. Syrians east, Philistines west, devour with open mouth. His anger has not turned away; his hand is stretched out still. They did not turn to him who struck them. Head and tail cut off in one day: honored man and lying prophet. Guides lead astray; the guided are swallowed. No compassion on fatherless and widows; everyone godless. Wickedness burns. The land is scorched; brother will not spare brother. Manasseh, Ephraim, and both against Judah. His hand is stretched out still.

Isaiah 9:8

8The Lord has sent a word against Jacob, and it will fall on Israel;

Arminian

The Lord has sent a word against Jacob, and it will fall on Israel. Wesley: consolation for the faithful is behind him (9:1–7); he returns to the rebellious Israelites. It fell—it shall certainly be accomplished.

Arminian sent: a real word against historic Jacob. It will fall. Not a newspaper, not open theism: the sentence is already out.

Reformed

Calvin: Jacob and Israel are the same; the diversity is elegant. The wicked gain nothing by trying to turn the judgment away. To send is to appoint. The word must dwell in him; it cannot vanish without effect. It hath fallen: certainty of the result. God hath spoken, who cannot be deceived and cannot change.

Reformed sent: the word does what it was sent to do. Shared: Lord, word against Jacob, fall on Israel.

Isaiah 9:9

9and all the people will know, Ephraim and the inhabitants of Samaria, who say in pride and in arrogance of heart:

Arminian

All the people will know, Ephraim and the inhabitants of Samaria, who say in pride and in arrogance of heart. Wesley: they shall know whether my word be true or false; Ephraim the proudest of the ten tribes; Samaria the strongest place, seat of king and court.

Arminian know: they will find out. Historic Samaria’s pride, not a slogan. The word is still to be tested by the event.

Reformed

Calvin: people here is Israel, not Judah. Know: actual experience, an indirect reproof of unbelief. Fortified cities, thinking themselves beyond danger, are more insolent. He attacks obstinacy: chastised once and again, they reckoned losses as gain and became more hardened. The insult proceeds from haughtiness of heart.

Reformed know: too late, but they will know. Shared: Ephraim, Samaria, pride, arrogance of heart.

Isaiah 9:10

10“The bricks have fallen, but we will build with dressed stones; the sycamores have been cut down, but we will put cedars in their place.”

Arminian

The bricks have fallen, but we will build with dressed stones; the sycamores have been cut down, but we will put cedars in their place. Wesley: we have received some damage; we doubt not we shall quickly repair it with advantage.

Arminian bricks: a real boast after a real blow. Historic Ephraim’s rebuild, not a newspaper recovery plan. They still say it.

Reformed

Calvin: obstinate men who despise the calamity as if it were an advantage, an occasion to adorn houses and fields more splendidly. Not the fault of a single age: luxury increases after chastisements; men go on in crimes more shamelessly than ever. What then but heavier blows?

Reformed bricks: loss treated as upgrade. Shared: bricks, dressed stones, sycamores, cedars.

Isaiah 9:11

11But the Lord raises the adversaries of Rezin against him, and stirs up his enemies.

Arminian

The Lord raises the adversaries of Rezin against him, and stirs up his enemies. Wesley: to chastise pride and defeat hopes; the Assyrians; Rezin named because confederate with Ephraim; his—Ephraim’s, not Rezin’s.

Arminian raises: historic reversal. The ally’s foes become Ephraim’s. The Lord does it.

Reformed

Calvin: proud of alliance with Syria, they hoped everything would succeed. He threatens a change that takes away all hope. After Rezin slain, that country ruined. He will mingle various enemies. The army of that monarchy was collected out of various nations.

Reformed raises: the Lord turns the league inside out. Shared: adversaries of Rezin, his enemies.

Isaiah 9:12

12The Syrians on the east and the Philistines on the west devour Israel with open mouth. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still.

Arminian

The Syrians on the east and the Philistines on the west devour Israel with open mouth. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still. Wesley: though Rezin was destroyed, the nation survived, submitted to Assyria, and invaded Israel; east and west; devour like wild beasts.

Arminian still: a real refrain. Historic Syrians and Philistines, not a modern pincer. The hand is still out because they have not yet turned (v. 13). Not open theism: the next stroke is already His.

Reformed

Calvin: former friends invade on every side. Alliances we thought useful prove our destruction. God does not expend all His scourges at once; when we proceed obstinately He multiplies them. The severest line: they think a single punishment has exhausted God. His hand is stretched out still: He has not dispensed with the rods.

Reformed still: more remains. Shared: Syrians, Philistines, devour, anger not turned away, hand stretched out still.

Isaiah 9:13

13The people did not turn to him who struck them, nor inquire of the Lord of hosts.

Arminian

The people did not turn to him who struck them, nor inquire of the Lord of hosts. Wesley: him—to God.

Arminian did not turn: a real refusal under a real blow. They could have turned and inquired. The smiting was meant to bring them back. Not a fake warning: they did not. Match the hub: a King who strikes and still waits to be sought, leaving this not-turning as theirs.

Reformed

Calvin: the most heinous of all, when in spite of chastisements they grow hardened and kick. Desperate wickedness. God inflicts punishment to bring back the wanderers; by terrifying He humbles that they may return. The beginning of conversion is to seek God. Hypocrites plead prayers, fastings, tears; God chooses to be sought when the sinner, truly subdued, willingly takes the yoke he had shaken off.

Reformed did not turn: they would not be drawn. Shared: did not turn, him who struck, nor inquire, Lord of hosts.

Key contrast Both confess the word has fallen, and that they did not turn to the Lord who struck them, so His hand is stretched out still (9:12–13). Historic Ephraim, not a newspaper, and not open theism: the sentence stands. Arminians typically hear a real not-turning under a blow meant to bring them back—they could have inquired (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear desperate hardness: He strikes to bring wanderers home, but they will not be drawn; seeking is conversion when the sinner is truly subdued and takes the yoke (Calvin).

Isaiah 9:14

14So the Lord cut off from Israel head and tail, palm branch and reed in one day—

Arminian

So the Lord cut off from Israel head and tail, palm branch and reed in one day. Wesley: high and low; mighty and noble; weakest and meanest; all together, one as well as another.

Arminian cut off: the whole range in one day. Historic Israel, not a chart. The so follows the not-turning.

Reformed

Calvin: vengeance universal; the whole nation corrupted; no part sound. When iniquity abounds, each flatters himself by the crowd. Branch: stronger; reed: feeblest. Neither the strong nor the weak spared.

Reformed cut off: no rank is cover. Shared: head, tail, palm branch, reed, one day.

Isaiah 9:15

15the elder and honored man is the head, and the prophet who teaches lies is the tail;

Arminian

The elder and honored man is the head, and the prophet who teaches lies is the tail. Wesley: false prophets perish, not as a loss of good men, but to show the calamity reaches all, and to beat down vain hopes they had fed.

Arminian tail: lying prophecy is the rear of the beast. Historic court prophets, not a newspaper pundit war.

Reformed

Calvin: heads are princes who sat at the helm; the tail is false prophets who deceive by falsehoods—not because they were mean, but the lowest part of the body. He presses hardest on those whose rank led others into sin.

Reformed tail: the teachers of lies go with the nobles. Shared: elder, honored, head, prophet who teaches lies, tail.

Isaiah 9:16

16for those who guide this people have been leading them astray, and those who are guided by them are swallowed up.

Arminian

Those who guide this people have been leading them astray, and those who are guided by them are swallowed up. Wesley: leaders—false prophets; by false doctrines and evil counsels; they that are led of them are destroyed—shall certainly perish.

Arminian guided: real leading, real swallowing. The led still perish. Not a works-gospel: the word was there to inquire (v. 13). Not a fake warning: swallowed up is still the end of this road.

Reformed

Calvin: magistrates whose duty was to restrain allowed all to indulge; they ought to be reckoned seducers. Yet this affords no excuse: if the blind lead the blind, both fall. None are ruined by wicked leaders but those who of their own accord wish to be led astray.

Reformed guided: they wanted the leash. Shared: guide, leading astray, guided, swallowed up.

Isaiah 9:17

17Therefore the Lord does not rejoice over their young men, and has no compassion on their fatherless and widows; for everyone is godless and an evildoer, and every mouth speaks folly. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still.

Arminian

The Lord does not rejoice over their young men, and has no compassion on their fatherless and widows; for everyone is godless and an evildoer, and every mouth speaks folly. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still. Wesley: shall not rejoice over them to do them good; fatherless are His special care, much less others. Every one—not precisely: seven thousand elect among them in Elijah’s day, but the body of the people. Hypocrite—they professed to worship, yet had forsaken Him.

Arminian still: the refrain again. A real body gone godless, and a real leftover He still knows. Historic Israel, not a millennial camp.

Reformed

Calvin: no sex or age exempt. All hypocrites and evil-doers: no true fear of God; inward contempt seated in the heart, consciences stupefied. Wickedness has taken the mind, then the members; they boast of crimes without shame. Repeat the refrain: a single punishment has not exhausted Him.

Reformed still: the body is the point; the hand is not done. Shared: young men, fatherless, widows, godless, folly, anger not turned away, hand stretched out still. Wesley names Elijah’s seven thousand; Calvin names the body’s contempt.

Isaiah 9:18

18For wickedness burns like a fire; it consumes briers and thorns; it kindles the thickets of the forest, and they roll upward in a column of smoke.

Arminian

Wickedness burns like a fire; it consumes briers and thorns; it kindles the thickets of the forest, and they roll upward in a column of smoke. Wesley: shall burn you; thorns—the low and mean; forest—tall trees entangled, ready to catch and spread; smoke like a vast furnace.

Arminian burns: their wickedness is the fire. Historic thicket, not a newspaper blaze.

Reformed

Calvin: the wicked blame God; he shows their sins are the wood and fuel. They may not remonstrate as if He had dealt cruelly. Gradation: a fire gathers strength, spreads, ascends. At first He proceeds gently; if light chastisement fail, He doubles; if obstinate, wrath burns to the uttermost.

Reformed burns: they are the fuel. Shared: wickedness, fire, briers, thorns, thickets, smoke.

Isaiah 9:19

19Through the wrath of the Lord of hosts the land is scorched, and the people are like fuel for the fire; no one spares another.

Arminian

Through the wrath of the Lord of hosts the land is scorched, and the people are like fuel for the fire; no one spares another. Historic civil fire: the land itself burns, and brother will not spare brother.

Arminian scorched: His wrath, their fuel. Not a newspaper civil war. They still refuse to spare.

Reformed

Calvin: God is a righteous avenger, gracious and merciful, but just, and cannot endure the wicked. Darkness/scorch from the smoke of that fire. No man shall spare his brother: when no enemies are seen, He will arm us for our destruction. Intestine broils. He has blinded them and given them up (Rom 1:28).

Reformed scorched: the hand uses their own hands. Shared: wrath of the Lord of hosts, land, fuel, no one spares another.

Isaiah 9:20

20They slice meat on the right, but are still hungry, and they devour on the left, but are not satisfied; each devours the flesh of his own arm,

Arminian

They slice meat on the right, but are still hungry, and they devour on the left, but are not satisfied; each devours the flesh of his own arm. Insatiable: they eat and stay empty, even of their own.

Arminian devour: historic famine-rage in Israel. Not a slogan. The hunger does not turn them to the Lord of hosts (v. 13).

Reformed

Calvin: snatch at all hands: insatiable covetousness or cruelty. They suck a brother’s blood as if it were their own flesh. The bitterness is heightened because this is Abraham’s seed. A dreadful proof of heavenly punishment when brothers inflict mutual wounds.

Reformed devour: the chosen race at its own arm. Shared: right, hungry, left, not satisfied, flesh of his own arm.

Isaiah 9:21

21Manasseh devours Ephraim, and Ephraim Manasseh; together they are against Judah. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still.

Arminian

Manasseh devours Ephraim, and Ephraim Manasseh; together they are against Judah. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still. Wesley: though nearer than any other tribe, both sons of Joseph.

Arminian still: the refrain the third time. Historic brothers, then both against Judah. The word of v. 8 has fallen; they still have not turned. Not a millennial camp, not a newspaper map. The child of 9:6 is not this fire; this is Ephraim under a stretched-out hand.

Reformed

Calvin: tribes from one patriarch, Joseph, will destroy themselves by mutual conflict, then unite against Judah. For all this: still more severe chastisements when, after being frequently invited, they refuse to be reconciled. He does not cease to afflict till He destroy, as Moses: seven times more (Lev 26). His hand stretched out: rods prepared, that He may immediately strike. Not a woman’s passion; wrath followed by revenge.

Reformed still: invited, refused, struck again. Shared: Manasseh, Ephraim, against Judah, anger not turned away, hand stretched out still.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a word that falls on Ephraim, pride that rebuilds with cedar, and a hand still stretched out—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 9:8–21.

Arminian Notable voices

“The Lord—having inserted consolation for the faithful, he returns to commination against the rebellious Israelites. It fell—it shall certainly be accomplished. Know—whether my word be true or false. Ephraim—the ten tribes, the proudest. Stones—we have received some damage, but we shall quickly repair it with advantage. Set up—the Assyrians. Syrians—though Rezin was destroyed, the nation survived, submitted to Assyria, and invaded Israel. Him—to God. Head and tail—high and low, mighty and mean, all together. The prophet that teacheth lies—false prophets who fed vain hopes shall perish. The leaders—false prophets; by false doctrines they cause them to err; they that are led are destroyed. Every one—not precisely; there were seven thousand elect among them, as in Elijah’s day, but the body of the people. Hypocrite—they professed to worship God, yet had forsaken him. Manasseh—though nearer than any other tribe, both sons of Joseph.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 9:8–21)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Lord hath sent a word: it must dwell and rest in Jacob; it cannot vanish without effect. Who say in pride: once and again chastised, they reckoned their losses gain and became more hardened. The bricks are fallen: we shall build more magnificently. The Lord will set up the adversaries of Rezin: they who were on our side will, in the smallest portion of time, be turned against us. His anger is not turned away: if they had suffered much, they have more to endure; they think a single punishment has exhausted God. His hand is stretched out still: he has not dispensed with the rods. The people hath not turned: the most heinous of all, when in spite of chastisements they grow hardened. God inflicts punishment to bring back the wanderers. The beginning of conversion is to seek God. Hypocrites plead prayers and fastings; God chooses to be sought when the sinner, truly subdued, willingly takes the yoke he had shaken off. The rulers are seducers: yet this affords no excuse; none are ruined by wicked leaders but those who of their own accord wish to be led astray. All are hypocrites and evil-doers: no true fear of God; inward contempt seated in the heart. Wickedness burneth as the fire: their own sins are the fuel; they may not remonstrate as if he had dealt cruelly.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 9:8–18)
