Passage

Isaiah 9

Verses 1–7 · ESV · Voices

No gloom for her who was in anguish. Former time: Zebulun and Naphtali brought into contempt; latter time: the way of the sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the nations, made glorious. A people who walked in darkness see a great light. The nation is multiplied, joy increased like harvest and spoil. The yoke, staff, and rod are broken as on the day of Midian; war-boots and bloody garments burn. For to us a child is born, a son given: Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. Of his government and peace no end, on David’s throne, forever. The zeal of the Lord of hosts will do this.

Isaiah 9:1

1But there will be no gloom for her who was in anguish. In the former time he brought into contempt the land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali, but in the latter time he has made glorious the way of the sea, the land beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the nations.

Arminian

No gloom for her who was in anguish. Former time: Zebulun and Naphtali brought into contempt; latter time: the way of the sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the nations, made glorious. Wesley: the storm fell most heavily here; afterward Shalmaneser carried Israel away; Galilee of the Gentiles because it bordered them.

Arminian no gloom: historic north first under Assyria, then honoured. Matthew’s sea-road is this road. Not a newspaper, not a millennial map.

Reformed

Calvin: consolation after 8:22’s darkness. This chastisement lighter than Tiglath and Shalmaneser because a promise is attached; without the word the smallest stream drowns, with it the open sea is survivable. Galilee of the Gentiles: mixed with Gentiles since Solomon granted land to Hiram.

Reformed no gloom: the promise is the seasoning. Shared: Zebulun, Naphtali, way of the sea, Galilee of the nations.

Isaiah 9:2

2The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light; those who dwelt in a land of deep darkness, on them has light shone.

Arminian

The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light; those who dwelt in a land of deep darkness, on them has light shone. Wesley: Israel and Judah; darkness comprehends calamity and ignorance, idolatry and profaneness. Have seen—shall see at the coming of the Messiah.

Arminian light: a real shining on a real people. Historic Galilee first, then the Messiah they shall see. They still walk; the light still comes.

Reformed

Calvin: past tense for future, to set it before their eyes. Even in death, God can restore. Matthew applied it to Christ in Galilee correctly: the return from Babylon is a prelude that runs till Christ. Captivity was death; Jews there differed little from Gentiles, so the light reaches both. We lie buried till Christ shine by the doctrine of his word (Eph 5:14).

Reformed light: the word that raises the dead. Shared: walked in darkness, great light, deep darkness, light shone. Not a millennial timetable.

Isaiah 9:3

3You have multiplied the nation; you have increased its joy; they rejoice before you as with joy at the harvest, as they are glad when they divide the spoil.

Arminian

You have multiplied the nation; you have increased its joy; they rejoice before you as at harvest, as when they divide the spoil. Wesley: the promise to Abraham made good by gathering Gentiles to the Jews; before thee—in thy presence and the place of thy worship.

Arminian multiplied: a real joy in a real presence. Harvest and spoil are still the measure. Historic nation first, then the ingathering.

Reformed

Calvin: this later, smaller remnant yields greater joy than the old full kingdom, because the face of God shines. True joy, not transitory. Before thee: Moses’ phrase; they behold Him by faith as the author of salvation. Christ brings a joy we cannot be robbed of.

Reformed multiplied: spiritual happiness, not head-count. Shared: multiplied, joy, harvest, spoil.

Isaiah 9:4

4For the yoke of his burden, and the staff for his shoulder, the rod of his oppressor, you have broken as on the day of Midian.

Arminian

The yoke of his burden, the staff for his shoulder, the rod of his oppressor, you have broken as on the day of Midian. Wesley: burdensome yoke, staves for loads, rod of all oppressors, especially sin and the devil. Midian: three hundred men.

Arminian broken: a real breaking. Midian is the pattern: God can do it with few. Historic oppressor first (Assyria), then sin’s rod. They still wore the yoke; He still breaks it.

Reformed

Calvin: the cause of the joy—rescued from shameful slavery. Midian: God sometimes works alone so His hand is not hidden (Judg 7). Not the law as yoke, not Vespasian, not Hezekiah (that was danger, not slavery). From Babylon till Christ, one deliverance.

Reformed broken: His arm, not their numbers. Shared: yoke, staff, rod, Midian.

Isaiah 9:5

5For every boot of the tramping warrior in battle tumult and every garment rolled in blood will be burned as fuel for the fire.

Arminian

Every boot of the tramping warrior in battle tumult and every garment rolled in blood will be burned as fuel for the fire. Wesley: ordinary battle is noise and slaughter; this victory consumes the adversaries as by fire.

Arminian burned: war-gear on the pile. Historic end of that fight, not a newspaper ceasefire. The child of v. 6 is why the boots can burn.

Reformed

Calvin: ordinary conquest hews enemies down; here the Lord conquers by His own hand, as lightning. The copulative is but: not the confusion of battle, but burning. Extends to the kingdom of Christ, not merely Cyrus.

Reformed burned: He needs no pile of corpses. Shared: boot, tumult, garment rolled in blood, fuel for the fire.

Isaiah 9:6

6For to us a child is born, to us a son is given; and the government shall be upon his shoulder, and his name shall be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.

Arminian

To us a child is born, to us a son is given; the government shall be upon his shoulder; his name shall be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. Wesley: the ground of the light, joy, and victory. Us—unto us Jews, of whom Christ was born, and to whom he was primarily sent. Child—Messiah, not Hezekiah: the titles cannot without blasphemy be given to any mere man. Wonderful counsellor—of his church in all ages; all the treasures of wisdom (Col 2:3). Mighty God—God as well as man; El never used in the singular of a creature. Father of eternity—though as man then unborn, from everlasting to everlasting.

Arminian born: shared christology—this child is Mighty God, Wonderful Counselor, God and man. Given to us: a real gift to a people who may receive Him. Government on His shoulder is still a burden He bears. Not a millennial camp fight, not Hezekiah, not a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: not Hezekiah, already born. Son without addition is the Son of God (2 Sam 7:14; Ps 2:7). Given to us: little avail that Christ was born if He were not our own; this giving is a chief article of faith. Government on the shoulder like Eliakim’s key, not the cross. Wonderful: excellencies we perceive by faith; His grace exceeds miracles. Counsellor: from the Father’s bosom, the perfect teacher; wisdom from His gospel. Mighty God: El plus gibbor; if He were not God, glorying in Him would be cursed (Jer 17:5); the same reason as Immanuel (7:14). Father of the age: Author who keeps the Church through all ages. Prince of Peace: peace with God (Rom 5:1), then composure of mind.

Reformed given: He is ours, or the birth avails little. Shared: child born, son given, government, Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.

Key contrast Both confess the child is the Messiah, Mighty God and Wonderful Counselor, not Hezekiah: God with us as at 7:14 (Wesley; Calvin). Shared christology; not a millennial camp fight, and not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear a son given to us Jews, of whom He was born and to whom primarily sent, whose titles describe a nature we may trust and a government we may receive (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear a giving that makes Him our own: little avail that He was born if not given; the Church’s salvation is in His person (Calvin).

Isaiah 9:7

7Of the increase of his government and of peace there will be no end, on the throne of David and over his kingdom, to establish it and to uphold it with justice and with righteousness from this time forth and forevermore. The zeal of the Lord of hosts will do this.

Arminian

Of the increase of his government and of peace there will be no end, on the throne of David and over his kingdom, to establish it and to uphold it with justice and with righteousness from this time forth and forevermore. The zeal of the Lord of hosts will do this. Wesley: peaceable government to the ends of the earth; the throne promised to David and his seed for ever; from the beginning of it to all eternity. Zeal—almighty God, fervent affection to His own glory, the honour of His son, and His people.

Arminian no end: David’s throne in this child, forever. The Lord will do it; they still live under that increase. Not a millennial camp as the split: both camps sit this son on David’s seat without end.

Reformed

Calvin: this government alone is unchangeable; earthly kingdoms slip. Do not judge stability from appearances. Throne of David: nothing new, the old promise. Judgment and righteousness: the kingdom is spiritual, established by the Spirit; inner man regenerated first; we are not Christ’s unless we follow what is good and just and bear the impress sealed by the Spirit. Zeal: God inflamed with amazing love of believers; if they cannot measure the promise, still hope—the manner is wonderful.

Reformed no end: His zeal performs what appearances deny. Shared: increase, no end, throne of David, justice, righteousness, forevermore, zeal of the Lord of hosts. Not a millennial timetable as Arminian versus Reformed.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a light in Galilee, a broken Midian-yoke, and a child called Mighty God—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 9:1–7.

Arminian Notable voices

“The people—Israel and Judah. Darkness—calamity and ignorance, idolatry and profaneness. Have seen—shall see at the coming of the Messiah. Thou hast multiplied the nation—made good thy promise to Abraham by gathering in the Gentiles to the Jews. The rod—of all his oppressors, but especially of sin and the devil. As in the day of Midian—when God destroyed the Midianites by three hundred men. For—the ground of the light, joy, and victory. Us—unto us Jews, of whom Christ was born, and to whom he was primarily sent. A child—the Messiah by the consent of interpreters, not only Christian but Jewish; later Jews wrest it to Hezekiah, which the following titles confute. Wonderful counsellor—the counsellor of his church in all ages; in him all the treasures of wisdom (Col 2:3). Mighty God—this title can agree to no man but Christ, who was God as well as man; El is never used in the singular of any creature. The father—of eternity; though as man then unborn, from everlasting to everlasting. No end—his peaceable government extended to all the ends of the earth. The throne—promised to David and his seed for ever. The zeal—this great work by almighty God, out of fervent affection to his own glory, to the honour of his son, and to his people.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 9:2–7)

Reformed Notable voices

“The people walking in darkness hath seen a great light: even in darkness, nay, in death itself, there is good ground of hope. Matthew applied this to Christ preaching in Galilee correctly. The deliverance from Babylon was but a prelude, intended to last till Christ brought true salvation to bodies and souls. We lie buried in darkness till Christ shine on us by the doctrine of his word. Unto us a child is born: the Jews impudently torture this as Hezekiah, who had been born before this prediction. Son, without addition, can mean none else than the Son of God. A Son hath been given to us: it would have been of little avail that Christ was born, if he had not likewise been our own. This giving is one of the chief articles of our faith. The government upon his shoulder: not an allusion to the cross; like the key of David on Eliakim. Wonderful: not his mysterious essence, but excellencies we perceive by faith; the grace exhibited in Christ exceeds all miracles. Counsellor: from the bosom of the Father, the highest and most perfect teacher. The mighty God: El is a proper name; if Christ had not been God, it would have been unlawful to glory in him. He is called the mighty God for the same reason that he was called Immanuel. Father of the age: Author who preserves the Church through all ages. Prince of Peace: justified by faith, we have peace with God. Of the increase there shall be no end: this government alone is unchangeable. We are not Christ’s unless we follow what is good and just, and bear the impress of that righteousness sealed by the Holy Spirit. The zeal of the Lord of hosts will perform this: God inflamed with amazing love of believers.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 9:2–7)
