Passage

Isaiah 63

Verses 15–19 · ESV · Voices

The prophet now pleads for historic Israel: look down from heaven; where is your zeal and your might; the yearning of your heart and your compassion are held back. You are our Father though Abraham does not know us. Why do you make us wander from your ways and harden our heart? Return for the sake of your servants; we have become like those you never ruled. This is a covenant plea after the Exodus recital—not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. The same Father still hears the church that is called by his name.

Isaiah 63:15

15Look down from heaven and see, from your holy and beautiful habitation. Where are your zeal and your might? The stirring of your inner parts and your compassion are held back from me.

Arminian

Wesley: now the prophet begins to expostulate with God, from the goodness of his nature and the greatness of his works. Look down—his throne is in heaven; behold with regard thy poor people. Where—what is become of that love which of old would not let thee suffer thy people to be wronged? The sounding of his bowels is spoken of God after the manner of men.

Arminian plea: real prayer that the Father would show the zeal he seems to hold back. Not open theism: God is not ignorant, surprised, or learning. Historic Israel first; the church still asks him to look down. Shared ground with Calvin: zeal and compassion belong to him.

Reformed

Calvin: although he seems shut up in heaven, look down from the habitation of his holiness. His power is not diminished. Believers acknowledge a powerful and kind God even when they perceive no tokens of it; he never ceases to care about his people. God is always compassionate, because he does not change his nature; they expostulate familiarly for shutting up his bowels, yet impute the severity to their sins.

Reformed zeal: anthropopathism, not a finite God holding back from ignorance. Prayer that he would show the face he seems to hide. Same plea as Wesley; the accent is that he is always like himself.

Isaiah 63:16

16For you are our Father, though Abraham does not know us, and Israel does not acknowledge us; you, O Lord, are our Father, our Redeemer from of old is your name.

Arminian

Wesley: Abraham was our father after the flesh, though he be dead, and so ignorant of our condition. Redeemer—another argument for pity, because their Father was their Redeemer. From everlasting—thou hast been our Redeemer of old.

Arminian Father: he is Father though ancestry cannot help. Historic Israel’s plea; the gospel church still names him Father and Redeemer. Shared ground: you are our Father; Redeemer from of old is your name. Not a millennial bloodline chart.

Reformed

Calvin: he calls God Father in the name of the Church; it is the Church’s peculiar privilege. In no other way than through Christ can God be called Father. Though our fathers deny us, yet God will reckon us as children; sooner shall the rights of nature perish than the sacred adoption founded on his unchangeable decree. They rely on God alone, not on the intercessions of Abraham or Jacob.

Reformed Father: God as Father of the Church beyond blood. Same Father as Wesley; the accent is unchangeable adoption, not a newspaper ancestry.

Isaiah 63:17

17O Lord, why do you make us wander from your ways and harden our heart, so that we fear you not? Return for the sake of your servants, the tribes of your heritage.

Arminian

Wesley: made us—suffered us to err. Hardened—suffered it to be hardened. Thy fear—the fear of thee. Servants’ sake—for our sakes, that little remnant that are thy servants. Inheritance—the land of Canaan, which God gave them as an inheritance.

Arminian hardening: judicial permission after they wandered; God suffered the heart to harden, and the remnant may still return. Not open theism: he is not surprised into hardening, and they do not charge him with authoring their sin. Historic tribes first; the church still prays, Return. Shared ground: return for the sake of your servants.

Reformed

Calvin: they acknowledge their own fault; they do not free themselves from blame. God is said to harden and to blind when he gives men up to Satan, the minister and executioner of his wrath. Yet God is not the author of sin: he punishes ingratitude by blinding. Return on account of thy servants—Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as ministers of the covenant, not living intercessors.

Reformed hardening: just judgment and secret counsel, not God learning their wander. Same prayer to return; the accent is righteous vengeance they confess, not a surprise in heaven.

Key contrast Both refuse open theism and a millennial timetable. Arminians typically hear permission: God suffered them to err and suffered the heart to be hardened; a remnant of servants can still return. Reformed readers typically hear judicial hardening: God gives them up to be blinded, yet is not the author of sin; they trace the wandering to wrath they deserved. Shared ground: return for the sake of your servants; he is not ignorant or surprised.

Isaiah 63:18

18Your holy people held possession for a little while; our adversaries have trampled down your sanctuary.

Arminian

Wesley: people—the people set apart for his servants. A little while—comparatively to the promise, which was for ever. Sanctuary—the temple.

Arminian sanctuary: historic temple trampled; the forever promise still stands. Gospel church still grieves a profaned house. Shared ground with Calvin: adversaries have trodden down what he consecrated. Not a millennial temple chart.

Reformed

Calvin: fourteen hundred years was a little time compared with the everlasting inheritance promised to Abraham’s seed. Heavier still: wicked men had profaned the land the Lord consecrated. The end of redemption is a people that worships him in a right manner.

Reformed sanctuary: short beside eternity; the complaint is for his name in the holy place. Same trampled sanctuary; hope rests on the promise, not a newspaper ruin.

Isaiah 63:19

19We have become like those over whom you have never ruled, like those who are not called by your name.

Arminian

Wesley: thine—we continue so; we are in covenant, which they never were; and thus it is an argument they use with God to look upon them. Never—not in that manner thou didst over us. They—neither owned thee, nor were owned by thee.

Arminian name: we are still thine, though we look like the unruled. Historic Israel’s last plea in the unit; the church still asks to be owned. Shared close with Calvin: his name is called upon his people.

Reformed

Calvin: they complain that they seem not to differ from unbelievers, receiving no relief in adversity. The Church is distinguished by this mark, that his name is called upon her. Unbelievers cannot call upon him; there is no access but through the word.

Reformed close: calling on his name is the Church’s mark. Same plea as Wesley; they ask him to show how wide a difference there is between his people and strangers.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on looking down from heaven, the Father who is Redeemer, and hearts that wander—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 63:15–19.

Arminian Notable voices

“Now the prophet begins to expostulate with God, and to argue both from the goodness of his nature, and from the greatness of his works. Behold—Not barely see, but behold with regard, and respect thy poor people. Where—What is become of that love, which of old would not let thee suffer thy people to be wronged? The founding—This is spoken of God after the manner of men. Abraham—He who was our father after the flesh, though he be dead, and so ignorant of our condition. Redeemer—This is urged as another argument for pity; because their Father was their Redeemer. Made us—Suffered us to err. Hardened—Suffered it to be hardened. Servants sake—For our sakes, that little remnant that are thy servants. Thine—We continue so; we are in covenant, which they never were.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 63:15–19)

Reformed Notable voices

“Although we do not see thee, O Lord, and although thou hast withdrawn from us as if thou wert shut up in heaven… yet look down from heaven… behold our distresses. Believers must differ from unbelievers in acknowledging a powerful and kind God, even when they perceive no tokens of his power or kindness; for God never ceases to care about his people. They do indeed hold by this principle, that God is always compassionate, because he does not change his nature. He calls God a Father in the name of the Church; for all cannot call him thus, but it is the peculiar privilege of the Church. Though our fathers deny us, yet God will reckon us as children… the sacred adoption… founded on thy unchangeable decree. When they trace their sins to the wrath of God, they do not intend to free themselves from blame. God himself is said to harden and to blind, when he gives up men to be blinded by Satan, who is the minister and executioner of his wrath. And yet it cannot be said that God is the author of sin, because he punishes the ingratitude of men by blinding them in this manner.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 63:15–17)
