Passage

Isaiah 63

Verses 7–14 · ESV · Voices

The prophet will recount the Lord’s steadfast love to the house of Israel: he said they were his people, became their Savior, and the angel of his presence saved them. This is a recital of Exodus mercy—Moses, the divided waters, the Holy Spirit, rest in the valley—not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. They rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit; then he remembered the days of old. Historic then gospel: the same Lord who carried them all the days of old still leads his people to make for himself a glorious name.

Isaiah 63:7

7I will recount the steadfast love of the Lord, the praises of the Lord, according to all that the Lord has granted us, and the great goodness to the house of Israel that he has granted them according to his compassion, according to the abundance of his steadfast love.

Arminian

Wesley: here begins a new matter, the prophet’s prayer through chap. 64. He starts by mentioning the great kindnesses God had shewn the Jews, emphatically, with the greatest advantages.

Arminian recital: recount the Lord’s steadfast love—historic Exodus mercy first, then the gospel church in the same story. Shared ground with Calvin: compassion and abundant steadfast love, not our merits. Not a newspaper, and not a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: Isaiah brings consolation by calling the distressed to remember God’s ancient benefits. He cannot forsake his Church. The foundation of our salvation is laid on mercy; this excludes the merits of men. Blessings on Moses and others are ascribed not to their merits but to the mercy of God.

Reformed mercy: remember, do not invent a new God. Same recital as Wesley; the accent is undeserved compassion toward the house of Israel.

Isaiah 63:8

8For he said, "Surely they are my people, children who will not deal falsely." And he became their Savior.

Arminian

Wesley: when he made a covenant with our fathers and brought them out of Egypt. Not lie—that will keep my covenant. So he—not Cyrus, Zerubbabel, or Nehemiah, but Christ himself.

Arminian people: he called them children who would keep faith; the Savior is Christ, sincerely their Savior. They could have remained true. Historic Exodus first; the gospel church is still his people. Shared ground: he became their Savior.

Reformed

Calvin: he mentions the election of the people—a peculiar people who should call upon him. He does not treat of the secret decree here, but speaks after the manner of men of mutual consent; yet the foundation stands sure that none of the elect shall perish. They did not keep their promise; still he became their Savior. Hope of pardon if they fly to God and repent.

Reformed elect: my people is electing speech. The end of calling is a holy life; their lie does not cancel the Savior. Same Savior as Wesley; the fork is why they are his.

Key contrast Both refuse open theism and a millennial timetable. Arminians typically hear covenant children expected to keep faith, and Christ himself as Savior—not Cyrus or a later restorer. Reformed readers typically hear electing speech toward a peculiar people: none of the elect perish; will not deal falsely is the end of calling, and their failure is ingratitude. Shared ground: he said they are my people, and he became their Savior.

Isaiah 63:9

9In all their affliction he was afflicted, and the angel of his presence saved them; in his love and in his pity he redeemed them; he lifted them up and carried them all the days of old.

Arminian

Wesley: the angel is the same that conducted them through the wilderness—the Lord Jesus Christ, who appeared to Moses in the bush. Saved them from the house of bondage. He carried them in the arms of his power and on the wings of his providence, of old, to remember his ancient kindness for many generations.

Arminian angel: Christ himself saved and carried. Real pity, not a finite God learning their pain or changing his mind from ignorance. Historic Exodus; the church is still carried. Shared ground with Calvin: the angel of his presence is Christ.

Reformed

Calvin: the Lord accommodates himself to the manner of men, attributing to himself a father’s affection; not that he can in any way endure anguish, but by a customary figure of speech he assumes human passions. The angel of his face saved them—Calvin has no doubt the office of Savior is ascribed to Christ, the angel of highest rank. The cause was his love and undeserved kindness. He carried them all the days of old; God is not wearied in doing good.

Reformed compassion: fatherly, not passible like a creature. Same Christ-angel as Wesley; the accent is undeserved love, not God becoming finite.

Isaiah 63:10

10But they rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit; therefore he turned to be their enemy, and himself fought against them.

Arminian

Wesley does not pause here; v. 8’s covenant children and v. 9’s Christ still govern. They rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit—real covenant grief, not God taken by surprise or learning their sin.

Arminian grief: the people who could have kept faith did not. He turned to be their enemy. Historic wilderness judgments; the church may still grieve the Spirit (Eph 4:30). Not open theism: the Spirit is not finite. Shared ground: they rebelled, and he fought against them.

Reformed

Calvin: they were rebellious and drove away his goodness. God is by nature disposed to show kindness; he became an enemy because they revolted. We irritate the Holy Spirit by our wickedness—this form of speech, after the manner of men, is to produce stronger abhorrence against sin. The same Spirit performs our salvation; our sins break the bond of union (Eph 4:30).

Reformed grief: not God changing his mind from ignorance. Condescension of language; Spirit withdrawn in judgment. Same rebellion; the accent is that we accuse ourselves, not that God was surprised.

Isaiah 63:11

11Then he remembered the days of old, of Moses and his people. Where is he who brought them up out of the sea with the shepherds of his flock? Where is he who put in the midst of them his Holy Spirit,

Arminian

Wesley: this relates either to the people in Babylon (he shall remember) or to the wilderness: Where is he who delivered his people of old, to do the like for us now? A like phrase as God recollecting himself: Where am I with my former bowels? Shepherds—Moses and Aaron. Holy Spirit—those abilities and gifts wherewith God furnished Moses, as properly proceeding from the Spirit.

Arminian remember: the church still asks for the same Exodus God. Historic Moses first; gospel help is the same mercy. Shared ground: he put his Holy Spirit in their midst. Not a millennial replay.

Reformed

Calvin: chastisement is designed to rouse the people from lethargy. The true meaning is that the wretchedly oppressed Jews thought of the times of old. The shepherd is Moses, endowed with a remarkable grace of the Holy Spirit for the benefit of the whole people; the power of the Spirit was seen in the midst of the whole people.

Reformed memory: affliction teaches them to ask where God is. Same Moses and Spirit; the accent is God’s gift of the Spirit, not a newspaper crossing only.

Isaiah 63:12

12who caused his glorious arm to go at the right hand of Moses, who divided the waters before them to make for himself an everlasting name,

Arminian

Wesley does not pause here; v. 11’s Moses and Spirit still govern. Glorious arm at Moses’ right hand, waters divided, everlasting name.

Arminian arm: the Lord used Moses, yet the name is his. Historic Red Sea; the church still lives under that name. Not a newspaper crossing, and not a millennial sea-chart.

Reformed

Calvin: two things connected—the right hand of Moses and the arm of God’s majesty. The Lord employs men’s ministry so that his praise is not diminished. God performed miracles to gain himself an everlasting name; it is not lawful to transfer even the smallest portion of praise to man.

Reformed name: Moses is instrument; the arm is the Lord’s. Same dividing of the waters; all glory his.

Isaiah 63:13

13who led them through the depths? Like a horse in the desert, they did not stumble.

Arminian

Wesley: as an horse—with as much ease and tenderness as a horse led by the bridle. Not stumble—though the sea were but newly divided, it was dried and smoothed by the wind God sent, as it were to prepare the way.

Arminian depths: he led them gently. Historic Red Sea; gospel leading is still tender. Shared ground: they did not stumble.

Reformed

Calvin: he led out his people as gently as if one were leading a horse into a plain. He made them walk through the depths.

Reformed path: the deep was no obstacle. Same ease as Wesley; the miracle is his, not their footing.

Isaiah 63:14

14Like livestock that go down into the valley, the Spirit of the Lord gave them rest. So you led your people, to make for yourself a glorious name.

Arminian

Wesley: a laden beast goeth warily and gently down the hill. Rest—led them easily, that they should not be over-travelled; God gave them rest from their enemies, drowning them in the sea, and led them till he found a place for resting (Num 10:33)—or to Canaan the place of their rest.

Arminian rest: the Spirit of the Lord gave them rest. Historic valley and Canaan; the church still asks for that Spirit. Shared close with Calvin: he led his people to make a glorious name. Not a millennial rest chart.

Reformed

Calvin: the Red Sea was no obstacle; they marched through the depths as on level ground. A glorious name—the people argue that if he once wished to obtain a glorious name, he must not now throw away all care about it.

Reformed close: lead us still, for your name’s sake. Same Spirit-given rest; hope rests on his name, not a newspaper valley.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the Lord’s steadfast love, the angel of his presence, and the Spirit who gave them rest—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 63:7–14.

Arminian Notable voices

“Here begins a new matter, which contains the prophet’s prayer, to the end of chap. 64, wherein he begins with mentioning the great kindnesses that God had shewn the Jews, and that emphatically, setting them forth with the greatest advantages. He said—When he made a covenant with our fathers, and brought them out of Egypt. Not lie—That will keep my covenant. So he—Not Cyrus, Zerubbabel, or Nehemiah, but Christ himself. The angel—The same that conducted them through the wilderness; the Lord Jesus Christ, who appeared to Moses in the bush. Saved them—From the house of bondage. Carried—He carried them in the arms of his power, and on the wings of his providence.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 63:7–9)

Reformed Notable voices

“Isaiah brings consolation to his people in distressed and calamitous circumstances… call to remembrance God’s ancient benefits… he cannot forsake his Church. The Prophet extols the mercy of God… the foundation of our salvation and of all blessings is laid on it; for this excludes the merits of men. He mentions the election of the people… the foundation standeth sure, that none of the elect shall perish, because the Lord knoweth who are truly his. The Lord accommodates himself to the manner of men, by attributing to himself all the affection, love, and compassion which a father can have… not that he can in any way endure anguish, but, by a very customary figure of speech, he assumes and applies to himself human passions. I have no doubt that the office of Savior is ascribed to Christ… the angel of highest rank, by whose guidance, safeguard, and protection, the Church has been preserved. They were rebellious… we ourselves, by our rebellion, refuse and even drive away his goodness. We are said to irritate the Holy Spirit by our wickedness; and this form of expression, after the manner of men, is intended to produce in us stronger abhorrence against sin.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 63:7–10)
