Passage

Isaiah 63

Verses 1–6 · ESV · Voices

A watchman asks who comes from Edom in crimsoned garments from Bozrah, marching in the greatness of his strength. The Speaker answers: it is I, speaking in righteousness, mighty to save; he has trodden the winepress alone, and no one was with him. Historic Edom is Esau’s land, the church’s old enemy; the winepress is the Lord’s own judgment, not a newspaper. Canonical reading often hears the same Lord in Christ who treads (Rev 19 borrows the image)—one warrior, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 63:1

1Who is this who comes from Edom, in crimsoned garments from Bozrah, he who is splendid in his apparel, marching in the greatness of his strength? "It is I, speaking in righteousness, mighty to save."

Arminian

Wesley: the church asks with admiration who appears in this habit. Edom is Idumea, where Esau dwelt; it is put for all the enemies of the church. Bozrah is Edom’s capital—Edom meaning red, Bozrah a vintage. Here Christ gives an answer: he will faithfully perform what he hath promised, and truly execute justice. Mighty—I have power to accomplish salvation.

Arminian warrior: the One who comes is Christ, Savior who also judges. Historic Edom first; then all the church’s enemies. Shared ground: he is mighty to save. Not a millennial Armageddon chart.

Reformed

Calvin: this chapter has been violently distorted as if it related to Christ wet with his own blood on the cross. The Prophet speaks of God himself. The Lord comes forth with red garments that all may know he is their protector and avenger. Judgment on the Edomites shows the Church is the object of his care. The Lord himself replies: I speak justly; I claim no ordinary power to save.

Reformed avenger: God himself, not a crucified figure in this verse. Historic Edom’s cruelty is punished. Same Speaker who is mighty to save; the fork is whether the crimson is Christ’s passion or the Lord’s slaughter of persecutors.

Key contrast Both refuse a millennial timetable. Arminians typically hear Christ answering the church: he comes from Edom as Savior who also judges the church’s enemies. Reformed readers typically hear God himself as the Church’s protector and avenger—Calvin refuses to make the crimson the Savior’s own blood on the cross. Shared ground: the Speaker is mighty to save, and Edom does not escape.

Isaiah 63:2

2Why is your apparel red, and your garments like his who treads in the winepress?

Arminian

Wesley does not pause here; v. 1’s Christ still answers. Apparel red like one who treads the winepress—Bozrah’s vintage now blood, not grape-juice.

Arminian stain: the question is wonder, not doubt that he saves. Historic Edom’s winepress; gospel judgment on the church’s enemies. Not a later battlefield photo.

Reformed

Calvin: he continues to put questions, that he may arouse their minds. This sprinkling of blood is remarkable and extraordinary. Bozrah lay in a vine-bearing district. There will be other vintages than those which are customary; for blood shall be shed instead of the juice of the grapes.

Reformed vintage: the red is the Lord’s judgment, not chance. Same winepress image as Wesley; the stain is the enemy’s blood, not a millennial headline.

Isaiah 63:3

3"I have trodden the winepress alone, and from the peoples no one was with me; I trod them in my anger and trampled them in my wrath; their lifeblood spattered on my garments, and stained all my apparel.

Arminian

Wesley: I have destroyed the enemies of my people, I have crushed them as grapes are crushed—an usual metaphor for the utter destruction of a people.

Arminian winepress: Christ treads alone. Historic Edom first; then the church’s enemies. He needs no helper. Shared solitude with Calvin: no one was with him. Not a newspaper army, and not a millennial fight as the fork.

Reformed

Calvin: the Lord was stained with blood because he will take vengeance on the Edomites and other enemies who treated his people cruelly. It would be absurd to say these things relate to Christ because he alone redeemed us; it means God will punish the Edomites with no need of human assistance. Peoples may rise to destroy Edom, yet the work of God shall be separate from them.

Reformed alone: God is first cause; instruments do not own the judgment. The Edomites shall not remain unpunished for persecuting the Church. Same solitude; the accent is his wrath, not our aid.

Isaiah 63:4

4For the day of vengeance was in my heart, and my year of redemption had come.

Arminian

Wesley: of vengeance—to take vengeance on the enemies of my church.

Arminian year: the day in his heart is judgment on those enemies; the year of redemption is their deliverance. Historic Edom first; the church still waits on the same Lord who both judges and saves. Do not invent a millennial year as the camp split.

Reformed

Calvin: God does not cease to discharge his office though he delays till a seasonable time. It does not belong to us to prescribe when he ought to judge. Day and year mark the long captivity, that they may not despair. He punishes wicked men for the purpose of delivering the godly and of redeeming his Church; by their slaughter he opens a way for his grace.

Reformed year: vengeance is just judgment on persecutors, executed when he pleases, for the Church’s sake. Same pair as Wesley—vengeance and redemption. Not a millennial clock.

Isaiah 63:5

5I looked, but there was no one to help; I was appalled, but there was no one to uphold; so my own arm brought me salvation, and my wrath upheld me.

Arminian

Wesley: none to help—not that he needed it, but to see what men would do, in regard his people needed it. Standing or not standing by his people is standing or not standing by him. Uphold is a staff one leans on.

Arminian help: he looks for helpers among men because his people needed them. None came. His own arm still saves. The church may still stand with him; absence is unbelief, not his lack of power.

Reformed

Calvin: though the Jews were destitute of all assistance, the arm of the Lord is alone sufficient to punish enemies and set his people at liberty. From God alone they ought to expect salvation, eyes wholly fixed on him, who has no need of the assistance of others. He contrasts every human aid with his own arm.

Reformed arm: he will be seen to be their Savior. Do not gaze around. Same solitude as Wesley; the accent is that his arm needs no staff.

Isaiah 63:6

6I trampled down the peoples in my anger; I made them drunk in my wrath, and I poured out their lifeblood on the earth."

Arminian

Wesley: drunk—they go as it were to and fro, not knowing what to do with themselves. Bring down—whatever it is wherein their strength lies, he will bring to the very dust, to nothing.

Arminian close: the peoples are made drunk in wrath; their strength is dust. Historic Edom first; the church’s enemies cannot keep their footing. Shared end with Calvin: he treads them down. Not a millennial last-battle chart.

Reformed

Calvin: God’s wrath is sufficiently powerful to destroy the wicked without calling for others’ assistance, that the Jews may not be deterred by the strength arrayed against them. Make them drunk means fill them to satiety. He produces the Edomites as example, nearer and most injurious. Afflicted Jews must not call their salvation into question as if God hated them.

Reformed close: revolution of affairs is his. Strength cast to the earth is his sentence, not chance. Same trampling; hope rests on his wrath against persecutors, not a newspaper timetable.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the warrior from Edom, stained garments, and the winepress trodden alone—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 63:1–6.

Arminian Notable voices

“The church makes enquiry, and that with admiration, who it is that appears in such a habit or posture? Edom—Idumea, where Esau dwelt. It is put for all the enemies of the church. Bozrah—the capital city of Idumea. Here is also an allusion to the garments of this conqueror, Edom signifying red, and Bozrah a vintage. Here Christ gives an answer, wherein he both asserts his fidelity, that he will faithfully perform what he hath promised, and that he will truly execute justice. Mighty—I have power to accomplish salvation. I have destroyed the enemies of my people, I have crushed them as grapes are crushed. Of vengeance—to take vengeance on the enemies of my church.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 63:1, 3–4)

Reformed Notable voices

“This chapter has been violently distorted by Christians, as if what is said here related to Christ, whereas the Prophet speaks simply of God himself; and they have imagined that here Christ is red, because he was wet with his own blood which he shed on the cross. But the Prophet meant nothing of that sort. The obvious meaning is, that the Lord comes forth with red garments in the view of his people, that all may know that he is their protector and avenger. It would be absurd to say that these things relate to Christ, because he alone and without human aid redeemed us; for it means that God will punish the Edomites in such a manner that he will have no need of the assistance of men. The Lord therefore punishes and destroys wicked men for the purpose of delivering the godly and of redeeming his Church.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 63:1, 3–4)
