Isaiah 14

Verses 24–27 · ESV · Voices

The LORD of hosts has sworn: as he has planned, so shall it be, and as he has purposed, so shall it stand. He will break the Assyrian in his land and on his mountains trample him underfoot; the yoke shall depart from them, and the burden from their shoulder. This is the purpose that is purposed concerning the whole earth, and this is the hand that is stretched out over all the nations. The LORD of hosts has purposed: who will annul it? His hand is stretched out: who will turn it back?

Isaiah 14:24

24The LORD of hosts has sworn: “As I have planned, so shall it be, and as I have purposed, so shall it stand,

Arminian

Wesley: Saying—this verse does not only concern Babylon’s destruction, but also the overthrow of Sennacherib and the Assyrian host, which was a pledge of the destruction of the city and empire of Babylon. As I have planned, so shall it be; as I have purposed, so shall it stand. Shared: the LORD of hosts has sworn.

Arminian pastoral: the sworn purpose is sure. Sennacherib’s host is Wesley’s historic pledge, not a newspaper. The oath stands in the land; it does not erase the armies he uses as means. Not a millennial decoder.

Reformed

Calvin: for more full confirmation an oath was necessary. There is nothing of which it is more difficult to convince us than that wicked men will immediately be ruined, when we see them flourishing. On this account he employs an oath. As I have thought, so shall it be.

Reformed pastoral: what he has purposed shall stand. The oath is the Lord’s, over historic Assyria, not America. Shared: the LORD of hosts has sworn.

Isaiah 14:25

25that I will break the Assyrian in my land, and on my mountains trample him underfoot; and his yoke shall depart from them, and his burden from their shoulder.”

Arminian

Wesley: My land—in Judea, which is my land in a peculiar manner. Mountains—in my mountainous country, for such Judea was, especially about Jerusalem. I will break the Assyrian in my land, and on my mountains trample him underfoot; his yoke shall depart, and his burden from their shoulder.

Arminian pastoral: the Assyrian is broken in Judea, on those mountains. Historic Assyria, not a modern state. The yoke departing is a real relief in the land he calls his. Do not copy the trampling.

Reformed

Calvin: some think this relates to Sennacherib’s army, which the hand of God destroyed by means of an angel when he besieged Jerusalem. The Lord will put an end to the tyranny of the Assyrians; the breaking does not refer so much to persons as to the empire. It even includes the redemption obtained through Christ, of which that deliverance was a forerunner. Jerusalem among the mountains was despised; this contempt magnifies the power of God.

Reformed pastoral: the Assyrian yoke is broken in his land. Shared: trampled on his mountains. Historic Assyria, not a twenty-first-century map.

Key contrast Both hear the Assyrian broken in the LORD’s land and trampled on his mountains—historic Assyria in Judea, not a twenty-first-century map. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s Judea, his land in a peculiar manner, the mountainous country about Jerusalem. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: the breaking is of the empire more than of persons, and that deliverance was a forerunner of the redemption obtained through Christ.

Isaiah 14:26

26This is the purpose that is purposed concerning the whole earth, and this is the hand that is stretched out over all the nations.

Arminian

Wesley: The earth—upon this vast empire, now in the hands of the Assyrians, and shortly to come into the hands of the Babylonians. The hand—the providence of God executing his purpose. This is the purpose that is purposed concerning the whole earth, and this is the hand that is stretched out over all the nations.

Arminian pastoral: whole earth here is that historic empire, not a millennial chart. Providence executes what he has purposed; the armies and the land are means under the oath. Shared: the LORD has purposed.

Reformed

Calvin: why the whole earth and all the nations, when he is speaking about Babylon? The Babylonian empire, after swallowing up Nineveh, extended nearly through the whole of the east; the devastation of that empire was also the destruction of the whole world. He declares the will and purpose of God, and his power. His hand ought never to be separated from his mouth; the power is active, not unemployed.

Reformed pastoral: the stretched-out hand cannot be turned back. Shared: purpose concerning the whole earth. Historic empire, not a newspaper.

Key contrast Both hear a purpose over the whole earth and a hand stretched out over the nations—the historic empire, not a millennial decoder. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s vast empire, now Assyrian and soon Babylonian, and the hand as the providence of God executing his purpose. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: purpose and power are not to be separated; as soon as he has decreed he stretches out his hand, and execution must follow.

Isaiah 14:27

27For the LORD of hosts has purposed, and who will annul it? His hand is stretched out, and who will turn it back?

Arminian

Wesley is silent on this verse; nearest: the hand is the providence of God executing his purpose. For the LORD of hosts has purposed, and who will annul it? His hand is stretched out, and who will turn it back?

Arminian pastoral: no one annuls the sworn purpose. Providence executing it does not make the oath uncertain, and it does not erase the historic means. Shared: who will turn it back? Historic Assyria’s fall, not a culture-war speech.

Reformed

Calvin: who shall disannul his purpose? Who shall turn back his hand? He speaks disdainfully of all the creatures. As soon as the Lord has decreed, he stretches out his hand, and when his hand is stretched out, the execution of the work must follow. There can be no repentance or change in God; no occurrence can thwart his purpose.

Reformed pastoral: what he has purposed shall stand. Shared: his hand is stretched out. The LORD of hosts, over Assyria, not a millennial decoder.
