Passage

Isaiah 14

Verses 12–23 · ESV · Voices

The taunt against the king of Babylon continues: the Day Star, son of Dawn, is fallen from heaven and cut down to the ground; he said in his heart he would ascend and make himself like the Most High, but he is brought down to Sheol, to the far reaches of the pit. Those who see him stare and ponder: is this the man who made the earth tremble? Kings of the nations lie in their tombs; he is cast out like a loathed branch, denied burial for destroying his land and slaying his people. The LORD of hosts cuts off from Babylon name and remnant and sweeps it with the broom of destruction.

Isaiah 14:12

12How you are fallen from heaven, O Day Star, son of Dawn! How you are cut down to the ground, you who laid the nations low!

Arminian

Wesley: Fallen—from the height of thy glory. Lucifer—which properly is a bright star, that ushers in the morning; but is here metaphorically taken for the mighty king of Babylon. Son—the title of son is given not only to one begotten, but to anything related, as the sons of Arcturus. Shared: Day Star is that historic king.

Arminian pastoral: fallen from heaven is fallen from glory. The taunt already begun against the king of Babylon continues here. Do not invent a satanic-versus-king fight the notes do not make. Historic pride-fall, not a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: he compares him to Lucifer, the Son of the Dawn, on account of his splendor and brightness above others. The exposition which some have given, as if it referred to Satan, has arisen from ignorance; the context shows the king of the Babylonians. It was gross ignorance to imagine Lucifer the king of devils; pass by those inventions as useless fables.

Reformed pastoral: shared with Wesley: this is the king of Babylon. Cut down to the ground is the Lord’s doing. Day Star in the ESV is that monarch’s brightness, not a millennial decoder and not America.

Isaiah 14:13

13You said in your heart, “I will ascend to heaven; above the stars of God I will set my throne on high; I will sit on the mount of assembly in the far reaches of the north;

Arminian

Wesley: I—I will advance myself above the state of a weak man. Above—above all other kings and potentates; or, above the most eminent persons of God’s church. North—a more exact description of the place of the temple; it stood upon mount Moriah, northward from the hill of Zion strictly so called.

Arminian pastoral: the “I will” is that king’s heart. Shared pride: he said he would ascend. Historic Babylon’s boast toward Zion’s north, not a satanic-versus-king camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: yet thou saidst in thy heart; to say means to resolve in one’s mind. He ridicules the pride of the Babylonian monarch. I will sit on the mountain of the testimony: by this effrontery he attempted to make himself equal to God. The blasphemy is proved in that he attacked heaven itself rather than an earthly place.

Reformed pastoral: the mount of assembly is the covenant site. The king’s heart aimed at God’s presence with his people. Historic Zion, not a millennial decoder.

Key contrast Both hear the king of Babylon saying in his heart he will ascend and set his throne on high—the pride-fall of that monarch, not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s advance above a weak man, above kings, or above the eminent of God’s church, with north as Moriah by Zion. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: he attacked heaven itself and the mountain of the testimony, making himself equal to God.

Isaiah 14:14

14I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will make myself like the Most High.”

Arminian

Wesley: Like—in the uncontrolableness of my power, and the universal extent of my dominion. I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will make myself like the Most High. Shared: the boast is the king of Babylon’s.

Arminian pastoral: likeness to the Most High is uncontrolable dominion in his own mouth. The warning stood while he still said “I will.” Not a culture-war speech and not a chart of another age.

Reformed

Calvin: all who ascribe more to themselves than God allows are chargeable with exalting themselves against God; where pride is, contempt of God must be. He is not accused of exalting himself above angels, but of endeavoring to crush the Church of God. Satan’s “Ye shall be as gods” is named as the same pattern of pride, not as a reading that this verse is the devil.

Reformed pastoral: the tyrant who assaults Zion assaults the Lord who dwells there. Shared: I will ascend / like the Most High is still that king’s heart. Historic Babylon, not America.

Isaiah 14:15

15But you are brought down to Sheol, to the far reaches of the pit.

Arminian

Wesley is silent on this verse; nearest: fallen from the height of glory, and like the Most High in uncontrolable power. But you are brought down to Sheol, to the far reaches of the pit. Shared: I will ascend / brought down to Sheol.

Arminian pastoral: the pit answers the boast. The king of Babylon is the man brought down. Historic Sheol-poetry, not a millennial decoder.

Reformed

Calvin: he contrasts the king’s intention—his throne above the clouds—with the sides of the pit, some corner of a sepulcher into which he shall be thrown. The Lord from on high laughs at the pride of the ungodly; in the twinkling of an eye he overturns their schemes.

Reformed pastoral: brought down to Sheol is the Lord’s contrast, not chance. Shared: the oppressor is brought down to Sheol. The far reaches of the pit are that king’s end.

Isaiah 14:16

16Those who see you will stare at you and ponder over you: “Is this the man who made the earth tremble, who shook kingdoms,

Arminian

Wesley is silent on this verse; nearest: fallen from glory, the mighty king of Babylon cut down. Those who see you will stare and ponder: is this the man who made the earth tremble, who shook kingdoms?

Arminian pastoral: staring is the taunt’s punch. Historic onlookers over a fallen king, not a headline and not a culture-war speech.

Reformed

Calvin: they first ask if it be possible that he who, by the slightest expression of his will, made the earth to tremble, should be so quickly and easily laid low. Tyrants, with all their cruelty, are like clouds that threaten to destroy everything and are scattered in a moment.

Reformed pastoral: “Is this the man” is astonishment under God’s hand. Shared: the man is the king of Babylon. The earth trembled at that historic scepter.

Isaiah 14:17

17who made the world like a desert and overthrew its cities, who did not let his prisoners go home?”

Arminian

Wesley: Cried not—whereby he signifies both his irresistible power, and his continued cruelty. Who made the world like a desert and overthrew its cities, who did not let his prisoners go home.

Arminian pastoral: cruelty was chosen under that scepter. Prisoners kept is historic Babylon’s manner, not a modern labor slogan. The warning stood.

Reformed

Calvin: he expresses the cruel and savage disposition of the tyrant: he brought desolation on the world, overthrew cities, did not release prisoners. Tyrants choose rather to be feared than to be loved. They think the only way to reign is to strike terror by inexorable cruelty.

Reformed pastoral: the Judge gives like for like. Shared: this is that king’s world-made-desert. Not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 14:18

18All the kings of the nations lie in glory, each in his own tomb;

Arminian

Wesley: All—that is, other things most commonly do. Lie—are buried in their own sepulchres, having stately monuments erected to their memory. All the kings of the nations lie in glory, each in his own tomb.

Arminian pastoral: glory in a tomb is still a burial. The contrast is coming: this king will not lie as they lie. Historic tombs, not a decoder.

Reformed

Calvin: he contrasts the king of Babylon with other kings, that after death he will be more wretched than the rest. He does not venture to limit this to Nebuchadnezzar alone, because it does not appear from history that he was denied burial. He therefore describes not a single man but a whole dynasty.

Reformed pastoral: lying in glory is the ordinary honor of dead kings. The taunt is still against Babylon’s house. Shared: kings of the nations in their tombs.

Isaiah 14:19

19but you are cast out, away from your grave, like a loathed branch, clothed with the slain, those pierced by the sword, who go down to the stones of the pit, like a dead body trampled underfoot.

Arminian

Wesley: Cast out—or, cast from thy grave or burying-place. Which very probably happened to Belshazzar, when his people had neither opportunity nor heart to bestow an honourable interment upon him, and the conquerors would not suffer them to do it. Like—like a rotten twig of a tree, which he that prunes the trees casts away. Trodden—neglected, like such a carcase. And this might literally happen to Belshazzar’s dead body.

Arminian pastoral: a loathed branch, clothed with the slain: honest disgrace, not sensational gore. Wesley’s hook is Belshazzar, historic, not a newspaper. Do not copy the trampling.

Reformed

Calvin: the kings of Babylon will be loaded with such disgrace that they will even be cast out of the sepulcher. As hurtful trees are rooted out, the king of Babylon does not deserve a place among men. Their bloody bodies are thrown into a ditch with their rotten garments.

Reformed pastoral: denied burial is judgment on that tyranny, not a license to desecrate. Shared: cast out, away from the grave. Historic Babylon’s king, not a modern tyrant.

Isaiah 14:20

20You will not be joined with them in burial, because you have destroyed your land, you have slain your people. May the offspring of evildoers nevermore be named!

Arminian

Wesley: Joined—not buried as they are. Slain—thou hast exercised great tyranny and cruelty, not only to thine enemies, but even to thine own subjects. The seed—such as Belshazzar was, being descended from that Nebuchadnezzar who had made such horrid slaughters. Renowned—or, shall not be renowned for ever: although I have long borne with thee and thy family.

Arminian pastoral: he destroyed his land and slain his people. The offspring of evildoers: that royal house, not a living people now. The long bearing was real; the cutting off is real.

Reformed

Calvin: he who has laid the earth desolate does not deserve that the earth shall receive him into its bosom. The Lord punishes the pride of wicked men who wish to leave a perpetual remembrance; he blots out their name. The righteous shall be held in perpetual remembrance; the remembrance of the wicked must be destroyed.

Reformed pastoral: not joined in burial because of blood on his own land. Shared: the king of Babylon’s house is the seed named here. Not a culture-war speech.

Isaiah 14:21

21Prepare slaughter for his sons because of the guilt of their fathers, lest they rise and possess the earth, and fill the face of the world with cities.

Arminian

Wesley: Children—cut off all the branches of the royal family. Rise—not recover their former power. Prepare slaughter for his sons because of the guilt of their fathers, lest they rise and possess the earth and fill the face of the world with cities.

Arminian pastoral: be honest—this is judgment on a royal house so it does not rise again. We do not sensationalize slaughter. We do not aim this verse at a living people. Historic Babylon’s sons, not a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: he does not speak of innocent children, but of flagitious and unprincipled children who perhaps even exceeded their parents; they were justly associated with their parents. God never punishes those who do not deserve it. The children of the reprobate, whom the curse of God pursues, are liable to the same sentence.

Reformed pastoral: name the judgment without relish. The LORD cuts off a house so it does not fill the world with cities. Shared: slaughter prepared for Babylon’s sons. Not a permit to imitate it.

Key contrast Both hear slaughter prepared for the sons because of the fathers’ guilt, lest they rise and fill the world—historic Babylon’s house, not a modern map. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s cutting off the branches of the royal family so they do not recover former power. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: not innocent children but the seed of the reprobate, involved in the parents’ sins, whom the curse pursues.

Isaiah 14:22

22“I will rise up against them,” declares the LORD of hosts, “and will cut off from Babylon name and remnant, descendants and posterity,” declares the LORD.

Arminian

Wesley: Remnant—the remembrance of those that are dead, and the persons of those who yet survive. I will rise up against them, declares the LORD of hosts, and will cut off from Babylon name and remnant, descendants and posterity.

Arminian pastoral: name, remnant, descendants, posterity: the house is ended by the Lord’s rising. Historic Babylon, not America. The warning was a real word while a remnant still could have been named.

Reformed

Calvin: the Lord now declares that he will do what he had commanded others to do. Men are instruments; he is, strictly speaking, the Author of the work. I will cut off from Babylon the name and remnant, son and grandson. He meant not only the posterity but the remembrance.

Reformed pastoral: “I will rise up” is the LORD of hosts. Shared: cut off from Babylon. The Author is God; Medes and others are servants. Not a newspaper.

Isaiah 14:23

23“And I will make it a possession of the hedgehog, and pools of water, and I will sweep it with the broom of destruction,” declares the LORD of hosts.

Arminian

Wesley: Bittern—a great water fowl, which delights in solitary places, as also in watery grounds. Such as those were about Babylon. Pools—the ground about Babylon was of itself very moist, because of the great river Euphrates, kept from overflowing with charge and labour; this being neglected, when the city was destroyed, it was easily turned into pools of water.

Arminian pastoral: hedgehog, pools, broom of destruction: honest emptying of a site, not sensational. Wesley’s Euphrates neglected is historic hydrology. The broom is the Lord’s, over Babylon, not over a modern skyline.

Reformed

Calvin: henceforth it will not be a habitation of men, but will resemble a hideous cavern in which wild beasts shall lurk. He spoke of an animal found near the water; Babylon lay in a moist place, watered by the Euphrates and the Tigris. The Lord threatens to bring a deluge upon it. I will sweep it with the besom of destruction.

Reformed pastoral: the broom is his. Shared: Babylon made a possession of the hedgehog. Pools and sweep are the decree made visible on that ground. Not a millennial decoder.
