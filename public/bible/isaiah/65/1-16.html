Passage

Isaiah 65

Verses 1–16 · ESV · Voices

The Lord answers the plea of chapters 63–64. He was ready to be found by a nation not called by his name; he spread out his hands all day to a rebellious people who sacrificed in gardens, sat in tombs, and said, Keep to yourself. Yet as new wine in the cluster, he will not destroy them all: offspring from Jacob, chosen servants on the mountains, Sharon and Achor for those who sought him. Fortune and Destiny get a sword; servants eat and drink. Historic Judah and the nations first, then the gospel Paul reads here (Rom 10:20–21)—not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 65:1

1I was ready to be sought by those who did not ask for me; I was ready to be found by those who did not seek me. I said, "Here I am, here I am," to a nation that was not called by my name.

Arminian

Wesley: the primary sense is the conversion of the Gentiles upon the rejection of the Jews for contempt and crucifying of Christ; St. Paul, Rom. 10:20, expressly so applies it. Sought—a diligent enquiry in things relating to God. I invited whole nations by the preaching of my gospel, doubling my words, unto a nation not called by my name, with whom I was not in covenant.

Arminian offer: Here I am, here I am is importunity—the gospel sincerely held out to those who had not asked. Historic nations first; then the church gathered from them. Shared ground with Calvin: Paul reads Gentiles here. Not a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: the Gentiles were anticipated by grace and brought no merit; they neither call on the Lord, nor seek him, nor think about him. Behold I, behold I—he has manifested himself so that foreign nations do not doubt he dwells in the midst of them. Paul says Isaiah boldly affirms the Gentiles have been called; we were called by an eternal purpose long before the event.

Reformed calling: grace first, no seeking merit. Same Here I am to a nation not called by his name; the accent is anticipation of the unseeking, not their inquiry.

Key contrast Both take 65:1 with Paul (Rom 10:20) as Gentiles found by the gospel—not a millennial timetable. Arminians typically stress a sincere, doubled invitation to whole nations who had not asked. Reformed readers typically stress that the Gentiles were anticipated by grace with no merit, called by an eternal purpose. Shared ground: Here I am, here I am, to a nation not called by my name.

Isaiah 65:2

2I spread out my hands all the day to a rebellious people, who walk in a way that is not good, following their own devices;

Arminian

Wesley: applied to the Jews, Rom. 10:21. I have stretched out my hands, I have used all means to reduce them: the hands of a passionate orator to persuade them, of a liberal benefactor to load them with my benefits; this I have done continually in the whole course of my providence with them.

Arminian hands: all means, all day, real persuasion. They could have been reduced; they followed their own devices. Historic Judah; the church still sees those hands. Shared ground: spread out all the day to a rebellious people.

Reformed

Calvin: he accuses the Jews of ingratitude; there is no reason they should say the Lord does them wrong if he bestow grace on others. Stretching out the hands is the daily invitation by the word; he even embraces us, so that if we do not comply it is owing entirely to our own fault. Nothing is more displeasing than for men to be self-willed, devoted to their own inclinations.

Reformed hands: the word really near; they alienated themselves through madness. Same stretched hands; the fork is how the invitation lands, not whether it was held out.

Key contrast Both hear real hands spread to Judah (Rom 10:21). Arminians typically stress all means used to persuade and reduce them. Reformed readers typically stress that the daily invitation is the word, and they chose their own inclinations rather than follow God as leader. Shared ground: they walk in a way that is not good; the fault is theirs, not open theism in heaven.

Isaiah 65:3

3a people who provoke me to my face continually, sacrificing in gardens and making offerings on bricks;

Arminian

Wesley: to my face—with the utmost impudence, not taking notice of God’s omnipresence and omniscience. In gardens—directly contrary to the divine rule.

Arminian gardens: historic idolatry in Judah’s gardens, not a living politician’s park. They provoke a God who already sees. Shared ground with Calvin: invented worship to his face.

Reformed

Calvin: they forsook the command and polluted themselves by superstitions. The origin of all superstitions is that men are delighted with their own inventions. To my face—they sin more heinously, having been warned by his word. Gardens and bricks are altars he abhorred, contrary to his word, even if they imitated the appointed form.

Reformed worship: obedience, not invention. Same gardens; the beginning and perfection of lawful worship is a readiness to obey.

Isaiah 65:4

4who sit in tombs, and spend the night in secret places; who eat pig's flesh, and broth of tainted meat is in their vessels;

Arminian

Wesley: they remained among the graves, consulting with devils, who were thought to delight in such places, or to practice necromancy, all forbidden (Deut 18:11). Monuments—idol temples, or caves in which the heathens worshipped. Broth of abominable things—flesh unclean by the law.

Arminian tombs: historic necromancy and unclean meat in Judah. Shared ground: they chose what he forbade. Not a newspaper séance.

Reformed

Calvin: instead of consulting God alone they consulted demons in graves and deserts. How solemnly the Lord had forbidden it appears from Deuteronomy 18:10. God demands nothing more than obedience, which he prefers to slain beasts (1 Sam 15:22). Swine’s flesh is not trivial: nothing the Lord has forbidden ought to be reckoned trivial.

Reformed tombs: disobedience in small-looking things is still revolt. Same graves and pig’s flesh; the issue is his word, not their taste.

Isaiah 65:5

5who say, "Keep to yourself, do not come near me, for I am too holy for you." These are a smoke in my nostrils, a fire that burns all the day.

Arminian

Wesley: thus they esteemed themselves holier than others, though all their holiness lay in rituals, and those too such as God never commanded. These are a smoke in my nostrils, a fire that burneth all the day—a continual provocation; as smoke is an offense to our noses.

Arminian smoke: ritual pride, not holiness. Historic hypocrites; the church still hates that smoke. Shared ground with Calvin: Keep to yourself is disdain, not devotion.

Reformed

Calvin: hypocrites disdainfully repel faithful advisers. Hypocrisy is never free from supercilious disdain; Remain by thyself means Away with thee. They boast of holiness and despise the word. The verse is in close connection with the preceding: not only revolt, but obstinate inventions.

Reformed smoke: they will not be reproved. Same fire all the day; hope of repentance lasts only while the ear is still lent to warnings.

Isaiah 65:6

6Behold, it is written before me: "I will not keep silent, but I will repay; I will indeed repay into their lap

Arminian

Wesley: they may think I take no notice of these things; but I will as certainly remember them as princes that record things in writing which they would not forget.

Arminian writing: he is not silent from ignorance. Not open theism: the record is already before him. Historic repayment; the church still knows he will not keep silent. Shared ground: it is written before me.

Reformed

Calvin: judges keep processes in writing; the Lord testifies these things can never fade into oblivion. We must not abuse God’s patience; all our faults are written before him. To recompense into the bosom is frequent in Scripture; men think sins concealed, but he will recompense into their bosom.

Reformed writing: slow judgment is not forgetfulness. Same repayment into the lap; he is a righteous judge.

Isaiah 65:7

7both your iniquities and your fathers' iniquities together, says the Lord; because they made offerings on the mountains and insulted me on the hills, I will measure into their lap payment for their former deeds."

Arminian

Wesley: when I reckon with them, I will punish them not only for their personal sins, but for the sins of their parents, which they have made their own by imitation. I will not only punish the late sins, but the former sins of this kind which those that went before did commit, and they have continued in.

Arminian measure: imitation makes the fathers’ sins their own. Historic hills; the church still refuses that heritage. Shared ground: offerings on the mountains insult him.

Reformed

Calvin: this treason is ancient; they follow the fathers’ footsteps. The son does not bear the father’s iniquity (Ezek 18:20) as if innocent, but they carry on the crimes and are condemned in the same judgment. Incense on the mountains stands for the whole revolt; pretended worship from human contrivance is abominable. Antiquity is no shield for superstition.

Reformed measure: long obstinacy, not a surprise in heaven. Same lap-payment; length of years does not turn error into law.

Isaiah 65:8

8Thus says the Lord: "As the new wine is found in the cluster, and they say, 'Do not destroy it, for there is a blessing in it,' so I will do for my servants' sake, and not destroy them all.

Arminian

Wesley: these words may be conceived as a gracious answer from God to the prophet, pleading God’s covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Israel. His threatening should be made good upon the generality of this people. Yet as in a vineyard generally unfruitful there may be some vine that brings forth fruit: Destroy it not, for there is a blessing. So will I do for my servants’ sake—Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—that I may not destroy them all.

Arminian cluster: a real remnant for the patriarchs’ sake; not all destroyed. Historic Judah first; the gospel still finds wine in the cluster. Shared ground: do not destroy it all.

Reformed

Calvin: he softens the preceding hardness so believers may not think salvation past hope. As a man finding a grape in a cluster spares it, the Lord will not tear up those in whom he finds flavor. He will punish so as to have regard to his own, and not involve all universally in the same destruction. The remnant will be small compared with the multitude (Isa 1:9).

Reformed cluster: his own, not the whole mass. Same blessing in the cluster; the accent is the elect plants he will not tear up by the roots.

Key contrast Both refuse a millennial camp fight and a newspaper remnant. Arminians typically hear servants Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and a fruit-bearing vine spared so he will not destroy them all. Reformed readers typically hear the Lord sparing his own—the elect—while the generality falls; grace is not for every bearer of the name without distinction. Shared ground: there is a blessing in the cluster; he will not destroy them all.

Isaiah 65:9

9I will bring forth offspring from Jacob, and from Judah possessors of my mountains; my chosen shall possess it, and my servants shall dwell there.

Arminian

Wesley: God farther promises to bring out of Judah an inheritor of his mountains, which refers to the Jews’ return out of the captivity of Babylon to Jerusalem, to worship God in his temple upon mount Zion. Mine elect—my chosen ones; the term signifies such as are dignified with some special favour. The whole nation of the Jews are called a chosen people.

Arminian seed: historic return to Zion first; elect can name the nation he favored. Gospel church still dwells as servants. Shared ground: offspring from Jacob; servants shall dwell there.

Reformed

Calvin: the Lord reserves some seed that shall call upon him; the Church shall exist as the pillar and foundation of truth (1 Tim 3:15). Heirs of my mountains are those who, having returned from captivity, inhabit their native land. This grace is confined to the elect and true worshippers of God, that every one may not apply it to himself without distinction.

Reformed seed: elect possessors, not every Judahite. Same mountains after exile; do not judge the Church from the present ruin.

Key contrast Both start with historic return from Babylon to Judah’s mountains. Arminians typically let mine elect include the nation called chosen. Reformed readers typically confine the possession to the elect and true worshippers. Shared ground: offspring from Jacob; my servants shall dwell there. Neither needs a 1000-year chart.

Isaiah 65:10

10Sharon shall become a pasture for flocks, and the Valley of Achor a place for herds to lie down, for my people who have sought me.

Arminian

Wesley: Sharon was a place of great fruitfulness for pastures. It was become like a wilderness; God here promises that it should again be a place for the flocks.

Arminian pasture: historic Sharon and Achor restored for those who sought him. Gospel people still seek. Shared ground with Calvin: my people who have sought me. Not a millennial ranch.

Reformed

Calvin: the land that was a desert shall be again inhabited; Sharon and Achor fit for flocks and herds. Earthly blessings invite us to heavenly ones. He excludes the reprobate who glory vainly in the name of God. The sure mark that distinguishes lambs from kids is to seek the Lord; it is not enough to shelter under a title.

Reformed pasture: seekers, not mere names. Same Sharon; seeking with a pure conscience is the mark.

Isaiah 65:11

11But you who forsake the Lord, who forget my holy mountain, who set a table for Fortune and fill cups of mixed wine for Destiny,

Arminian

Wesley: to forget God’s holy mountains signifies not to regard the true worship of God. Zion is his holy mountain. That troop—the idols of the ten tribes and of the Assyrians were a troop, whereas the God of Israel was one God. Preparing a table—the feasts they made upon their sacrifices. Number—the multitude of their idols.

Arminian table: historic Fortune and Destiny, not a living politician’s luck. Shared ground: they forget my holy mountain.

Reformed

Calvin: hypocrites must not abuse the restoration promises. Forsakers have forgotten Mount Zion—the rule of a holy life laid down in the word. Gad he takes as a troop or army of false gods; they fill an oblation to the number, honoring many idols. Superstition has no limit.

Reformed table: many gods, one forgotten mountain. Same Fortune and Destiny; no faith without the word (Rom 10:17).

Isaiah 65:12

12I will destine you to the sword, and all of you shall bow down to the slaughter, because, when I called, you did not answer; when I spoke, you did not listen, but you did what was evil in my eyes and chose what I did not delight in."

Arminian

Wesley: a great number of you shall perish by the sword; God saith he will number them, tell them out one by one to the sword. Bow down—as you have bowed down to idols. I called—by my prophets; you did not answer by doing the things which I enjoined. Chuse—you sinned deliberately, choosing sinful courses, the things which I hated.

Arminian call: they could have answered; they chose what he did not delight in. Not open theism: he called, they refused. Historic sword; the church still hears that because. Shared ground: when I called, you did not answer.

Reformed

Calvin: I will number you to the sword—an allusion to the number of their gods. They sinned through deliberate malice rather than ignorance; those to whom the word is proclaimed will be condemned more severely. To hear the Lord is to obey; they did evil before mine eyes, as if to his face. They are condemned not for gross crimes only but for foolish devotions that corrupted worship. Men may take pleasure in these things; the Lord abhors them.

Reformed call: the word was near; they chose. Same slaughter for unanswered calling; no free choice to worship as we think fit.

Isaiah 65:13

13Therefore thus says the Lord God: "Behold, my servants shall eat, but you shall be hungry; behold, my servants shall drink, but you shall be thirsty; behold, my servants shall rejoice, but you shall be put to shame;

Arminian

Wesley has no separate note on vv. 13–14; the heading is judgments on the wicked and blessings on the godly (vv. 11–16). Servants eat, drink, and rejoice; the forsakers hunger and are shamed.

Arminian servants: real split inside historic Judah, then the gospel church. Shared ground with Calvin: my servants versus you. Not a millennial banquet chart.

Reformed

Calvin: he distinguishes hypocrites who held a place in the Church from the true children. Empty titles shall avail them nothing. Eat and drink denote a prosperous condition, yet the kingdom of Christ is described under figures. Contentment does not lie in abundance but in calm peace of mind and spiritual joy; unbelievers have no relish for such things.

Reformed servants: true children eat from the Father’s hand; the wicked, however full, cannot enjoy with a good conscience. Same contrast; prosperity is from God alone.

Isaiah 65:14

14behold, my servants shall sing for gladness of heart, but you shall cry out for pain of heart and shall wail for breaking of spirit.

Arminian

Wesley: still the godly/wicked split of the unit. Gladness of heart is the servants’ song; breaking of spirit is the wail of those who forsook the mountain.

Arminian song: the heart’s gladness is given, not seized. Historic servants; the church still sings. Shared ground: servants sing; you wail.

Reformed

Calvin: he intends to state that contentment is spiritual joy. A persuasion of God’s fatherly love is more delightful than all earthly enjoyments. The wicked have continual fear and trembling; their conscience can never be at ease.

Reformed song: fatherly love tasted; shame is already in the conscience. Same singing servants; the fork is who those servants are, already named as his.

Isaiah 65:15

15You shall leave your name to my chosen for a curse, and the Lord God will put you to death, but his servants he will call by another name,

Arminian

Wesley: they shall use your names as examples of the eminent wrath of God upon sinners. Another name—he will not suffer his own people to be called by a name by which idolaters are known.

Arminian name: the chosen will not wear the forsakers’ label. Historic Judah split; gospel servants receive another name (62:2). Shared ground: his servants he will call by another name. Not a newspaper rebrand.

Reformed

Calvin: God will separate hypocrites from true servants. Their name shall be a curse, a formula of cursing. He will adopt a new people; he is not confined to the Jews so as not to find others whom he shall adorn with the name of his people. Another name is not a curiosity about the word Christian; it is election of another people without being accused of fickleness.

Reformed name: new servants, old name a curse. Same putting to death of false boasting; he keeps his purpose against those who obscure his glory.

Isaiah 65:16

16so that he who blesses himself in the land shall bless himself by the God of truth, and he who takes an oath in the land shall swear by the God of truth; because the former troubles are forgotten and are hidden from my eyes."

Arminian

Wesley: God of truth—because they shall see what God promised is fulfilled. Are hid—that is, they are at an end.

Arminian truth: promises kept; former troubles ended. Historic return’s comfort first; the gospel church blesses itself by the same God of truth. Shared close with Calvin: the former afflictions forgotten. Not a millennial reset.

Reformed

Calvin: the whole world is contrasted with a corner of Judea; God is worshipped in all places (John 4:21; 1 Tim 2:8). Blessing and swearing denote the whole worship of God. The true God is faithful to his promises. Former afflictions surrendered to forgetfulness began when the people came out of Babylon, and is extended to Christ.

Reformed close: worldwide worship of the faithful God. Same forgotten troubles; the blessing is his truth, not a corner’s monopoly.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a nation not called by his name, hands spread to the rebellious, and a cluster he will not destroy—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 65:1–16.

Arminian Notable voices

“This in the primary sense is a prophecy of the conversion of the Gentiles upon the rejection of the Jews; St. Paul, Rom. x. 20, expressly so interprets it. I invited whole nations by the preaching of my gospel to behold me, and that with importunity, doubling my words upon them, unto a nation not called by my name. I have stretched out my hands, I have used all means to reduce them, the hands of a passionate orator to persuade, of a liberal benefactor to load them with my benefits. These words may be conceived as a gracious answer from God to the prophet, pleading God’s covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Israel. As in a vineyard there may be some vine that brings forth fruit: Destroy it not, for there is a blessing. So will I do for my servants’ sake, that I may not destroy them all, for the sake of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Mine elect—the whole nation of the Jews are called a chosen people.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 65:1–2, 8–9)

Reformed Notable voices

“God has good reason for rejecting the Jews, because they have profited nothing by warnings. That they might not think the covenant void, he will have another people which formerly was no people. I have stretched out my hands: the daily invitation by the word; if we do not comply, it is owing entirely to our own fault. To them that asked not: the Gentiles were anticipated by the grace of God, and brought no merit. Paul earnestly contends for the calling of the Gentiles from this passage (Rom 10:20). As if one found a grape in a cluster, Destroy it not; the Lord will punish the sins of his people so as to have regard nevertheless to his own, and not involve all universally in the same destruction. This grace is confined to the elect and true worshippers of God, that every one may not apply it to himself without distinction.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 65:1–2, 8–9)
