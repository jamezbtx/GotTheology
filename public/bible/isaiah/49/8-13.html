Passage

Isaiah 49

Verses 8–13 · ESV · Voices

The LORD answers the Servant in a time of favor and a day of salvation, keeps him and gives him as a covenant to the people to establish the land. Prisoners are told to come out; they shall not hunger or thirst; mountains become a road; they come from afar; heavens and earth sing because the LORD has comforted his people and will have compassion on the afflicted.

Isaiah 49:8

8Thus says the LORD: “In a time of favor I have answered you; in a day of salvation I have helped you; I will keep you and give you as a covenant to the people, to establish the land, to apportion the desolate heritages,

Arminian

In a time of favor I have answered you; in a day of salvation I have helped you; I will keep you and give you as a covenant to the people, to establish the land, to apportion the desolate heritages. Wesley: The Lord—God the Father unto Christ. Heard thee—though not so as to deliver thee from death; yet so as to crown thee with glory and honour. For a covenant—to be the Mediator and surety of that covenant which is made between me and them. Desolate heritages—that Christ may possess the Heathen, who were in a spiritual sense in a most desolate condition.

Arminian covenant: the Father answers the Son and sets him as mediator, not a later scheme of ages. The time of favor is real help held out, not a feint. Shared: covenant to the people; land established; desolate heritages apportioned. Christ is the one given, as Wesley hears, and Calvin will not give the covenant-title to any other. The fork is whether the “you” stops at the Head or, with Calvin, also addresses the body.

Reformed

In a time of good pleasure have I heard thee. Calvin: while he addresses the whole body of the Church, he begins with Christ, who is the head. I will give thee to be a covenant is applicable to no other than Christ. Christ is not so much his own as ours; he neither came, nor died, nor rose again for himself. The good-will of God is the foundation of our salvation; we are saved not through our worthiness or merits, but by his free grace. These predictions belong to the whole Church in all ages: begin with Babylon, go on to the redemption of Christ, proceed to the last day.

Reformed covenant: the Head receives what is meant for the members; the partition-wall broken, Jews and Gentiles one body. Shared: time of favor, day of salvation, covenant of the people. No culture fight about restorations; the point is the Mediator who raises what was waste.

Key contrast Both hear Christ given as the covenant. Wesley takes the Father’s speech as unto Christ personally—Mediator and surety, heard not by being spared death but by being crowned. Calvin refuses to address Christ separately from his body: the Prophet speaks to the whole Church, beginning with the Head; good pleasure is free grace, and the word is not boxed into one age.

Isaiah 49:9

9saying to the prisoners, ‘Come out,’ to those who are in darkness, ‘Appear.’ They shall feed along the ways; on all bare heights shall be their pasture;

Arminian

Saying to the prisoners, Come out; to those in darkness, Appear. They shall feed along the ways; on all bare heights shall be their pasture. Wesley: Prisoners—to the Gentiles who are fast bound by the cords of their sins, and taken captive by the devil at his will. Go forth—come forth to the light, receive divine illumination. In high places—they shall have abundant provision in all places, even those which commonly are unfruitful.

Arminian word: the call is a real come-out, held out to bound Gentiles. Illumination is received; this is not a chart of later captivities. Shared: prisoners hear “Come out”; the dark are told to appear. The Servant who is a light for the nations now speaks that light. Those who stay bound do not stay because the word was never for them, but because they will not come forth to it.

Reformed

That thou mayest say to them that are bound, Go forth. Calvin: before the coming of Christ we are bound under a miserable yoke, and plunged in darkness. The preaching of the Gospel is the means by which we are delivered. If we desire liberty, let us listen to Christ when he speaks. Where then is the liberty of our will? Whosoever claims for himself light, or reason, or understanding, can have no share in this deliverance; liberty is not promised to any but those who acknowledge that they are captives.

Reformed word: the Shepherd’s say is the release. Shared: the bound are addressed; pasture is given. Calvin’s warning is not a sneer at Wesley’s “receive”; it is that the captive must confess captivity, not claim a native light.

Key contrast Both hear Christ actually calling prisoners out. Wesley hears Gentiles bound by sin invited to come forth to the light and receive illumination—a sincere come-out. Calvin hears the Gospel word as the only means, and denies a share to those who claim their own light or free will; deliverance is for those who acknowledge they are captives.

Isaiah 49:10

10they shall not hunger or thirst, neither scorching wind nor sun shall strike them, for he who has pity on them will lead them, and by springs of water will guide them.

Arminian

They shall not hunger or thirst, neither scorching wind nor sun shall strike them; he who has pity on them will lead them, and by springs of water will guide them. Wesley’s adjacent lemma already promised abundant provision even in unfruitful places; the same Gentiles who came out are not abandoned in the way.

Arminian leading: pity that actually leads, springs that actually guide. Not a diet-war, not a later-age chart. Shared: no hunger, no thirst, no scorching; the pitier leads. The nations given light in 49:6 are here kept on the road.

Reformed

They shall not hunger or thirst. Calvin: there is food in the hand of God, so that they shall not want provisions for the journey. He recalls manna in the wilderness and the pillar of cloud against the sun, and the waters from the rock: former benefits magnify the power of God in securing the safety of the Church.

Reformed leading: the Church is fed as Israel was fed; the Shepherd does not run out. Shared: no hunger or thirst; springs of water. Pity is the LORD’s, not a wage.

Isaiah 49:11

11And I will make all my mountains a road, and my highways shall be raised up.

Arminian

I will make all my mountains a road, and my highways shall be raised up. Wesley: A way—I will remove all hindrances, and prepare the way for them, by levelling high grounds, and raising low grounds.

Arminian way: hindrances really removed so the gathered can come. Not a culture fight about roads. Shared: mountains become a road; highways raised. The LORD prepares what he commands.

Reformed

I will place all my mountains. Calvin: he will remove every obstacle that might prevent the return; mountains that appeared impassable he will render passable. When he calls them my mountains, he declares that he will be the leader of the expedition and travel along with the Church, as if consulting his own interests when he assists his people.

Reformed way: God marches with the body he restores. Shared: every hindrance yields. No scheme of ages; the same LORD who gave the Servant as light now levels the road.

Isaiah 49:12

12Behold, these shall come from afar, and behold, these from the north and from the west, and these from the land of Syene.”

Arminian

Behold, these shall come from afar, from the north and from the west, and from the land of Syene. Wesley: These—my people shall be gathered from the most remote parts of the earth. He speaks of the conversion of the Gentiles, with allusion to gathering and bringing back the Jews from all parts where they were dispersed. Sinim—Sinites, or Sin in Egypt for the southern parts.

Arminian far-ones: conversion of the nations is the point, not a dispensational map of later Israels. The allusion to a Jewish return is type, not a second gospel. Shared: they come from afar, north, west, south. The light for the nations in 49:6 now has a geography.

Reformed

Behold, those from afar shall come. Calvin: while Isaiah promises a return from Babylon, he extends this prediction to the time of Christ. The second birth of the Church is here described. Not only shall the Jews return to Jerusalem to build the temple, but they who had formerly been aliens from the Church shall be collected from every corner of the world.

Reformed far-ones: aliens gathered into the one body under the Head. Shared: from afar, every direction. Not a chart of ages; the Church’s increase, Gentiles associated with Jews, as already in 49:6.

Key contrast Both refuse a monopoly of one land or a later-age chart. Wesley hears “my people” gathered from the ends of the earth—conversion of the Gentiles, with the Jewish return as allusion. Calvin hears the second birth of the Church: not Jews only returning to build, but former aliens collected from every corner into the one body.

Isaiah 49:13

13Sing for joy, O heavens, and exult, O earth; break forth, O mountains, into singing! For the LORD has comforted his people and will have compassion on his afflicted.

Arminian

Sing for joy, O heavens, and exult, O earth; break forth, O mountains, into singing! For the LORD has comforted his people and will have compassion on his afflicted. Wesley’s head-note on the stretch: God’s love to his church perpetual. The comfort is the LORD’s; the afflicted are his.

Arminian comfort: heavens are told to sing because the people have actually been pitied, not because a party won a culture fight. Shared: the LORD has comforted; compassion on the afflicted. The Servant’s work in 8–12 lands as comfort, not as a scheme.

Reformed

Praise, O heavens; and rejoice, O earth. Calvin: he confirms the promise which afflictions make doubtful. Though believers behold nothing but death and ruin, they are to sustain the heart by hope of a better condition. He will have compassion on his poor: the Church in this world is liable to many calamities; under the cross we partake of that compassion.

Reformed comfort: the song is commanded while the wound is still open. Shared: heavens, earth, mountains; the LORD has comforted his people. Compassion is on the afflicted Church, the body under the Head.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a time of favor, a covenant of the people, prisoners told to come out, and the LORD who has comforted his people—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 49:8–13.

Arminian Notable voices

“The Lord—God the Father unto Christ. Heard thee—though not so as to deliver thee from death; yet so as to crown thee with glory and honour. For a covenant—to be the Mediator and surety of that covenant, which is made between me and them. To establish—to establish truth and righteousness upon earth. Desolate heritages—that desolate places may be repaired and repossessed. That Christ may possess the Heathen, who were in a spiritual sense in a most desolate condition. Prisoners—to the Gentiles who are fast bound by the cords of their sins, and taken captive by the devil at his will. Go forth—come forth to the light, receive divine illumination. A way—I will remove all hindrances, and prepare the way for them, by levelling high grounds, and raising low grounds. These—my people shall be gathered from the most remote parts of the earth. He speaks here of the conversion of the Gentiles, with allusion to that work of gathering, and bringing back the Jews from all parts where they were dispersed, into their own land. God’s love to his church perpetual.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 49:8–13)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Prophet, while he addresses the whole body of the Church, begins with Christ, who is the head. I will give thee to be a covenant is applicable to no other than Christ. Christ is not so much his own as ours; he neither came, nor died, nor rose again for himself. He has been appointed to be the mediator of the covenant, because the Jews by their sins had revolted from God. The good-will of God is the foundation of our salvation; we are saved not through our worthiness or merits, but by his free grace. Christ was given to be a covenant of the people, because there was no other way to God but by him. The partition-wall having been broken down, Jews and Gentiles have been united in one body. Before the coming of Christ we are bound under a miserable yoke, and plunged in darkness; the preaching of the Gospel is the means by which we are delivered. Liberty is not promised to any but those who acknowledge that they are captives. They shall not hunger or thirst: there is food in the hand of God. He will remove every obstacle; he travels along with the Church. Those from afar shall come: not only that the Jews shall return to Jerusalem, but that they who had formerly been aliens from the Church shall be collected from every corner of the world. Praise, O heavens: Jehovah hath comforted his people, and will have compassion on his poor.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 49:8–13)
