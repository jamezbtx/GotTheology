Passage

Isaiah 5

Verses 18–30 · WEB · Voices

Woe to those who haul sin with cart ropes and dare the Holy One to hurry. Woe to those who swap good and evil, who are wise in their own eyes, heroes at drink, who acquit for a bribe. Fire eats stubble: they have rejected the law. His anger is kindled; the hand is still stretched out. He whistles for nations from the end of the earth. They come swift, unsleeping, hoofs like flint, roaring like lions. On that day, darkness over the land.

Isaiah 5:18

18Woe to those who draw iniquity with cords of falsehood, and wickedness as with cart rope,

Arminian

Woe to those who draw iniquity with cords of falsehood, and wickedness as with cart rope. Wesley: not only drawn by allurements, but active in drawing sin to themselves; cords of lying—vain pretenses; a rope—with all their might, as beasts drawing carts.

Judah hauling its own sin. They still pull. Not a fake warning: the cart is theirs.

Reformed

Calvin: cords are the excuses by which men harden the heart. Conscience would restrain; they break through. They bind themselves to sin and are without excuse.

The rope is self-tied. Both note: woe, cords, cart ropes.

Isaiah 5:19

19who say, “Let him make haste, let him hasten his work, that we may see it; let the counsel of the Holy One of Israel draw near and come, that we may know it!”

Arminian

Who say, Let him make haste, let him hasten his work, that we may see it; let the counsel of the Holy One of Israel draw near, that we may know it. Wesley: God in whose name the prophets threaten; the language of their actions; they scornfully repeated the Holy One’s title.

The mockers taunt God with their own mouths. They could have waited on the word. The delay is His; the dare is theirs.

Reformed

Calvin: utmost contempt—they treat judgment as fable and would be glad to see it, as if God did nothing until He punished. Faith holds us on His mouth when the effect does not immediately appear. Elect rely on the word; the ungodly scorn it and call for works. Peter’s mockers (2 Pet 3:4) speak the same line.

Unbelief demands a spectacle; faith waits. Common confession: quick, speed, Holy One of Israel, that we may know.

Key contrast Both confess the woe on those who dare the Holy One to hurry, and that the coming nations are His answer (5:19, 26). Arminians typically hear a taunt—the language of their actions (Wesley)—under a delay they might have trusted. Calvinists hear the mark of those who will not live by the word: the elect rely on His mouth; the ungodly scorn the word and call for works (Calvin).

Isaiah 5:20

20Woe to those who call evil good, and good evil; who put darkness for light, and light for darkness; who put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!

Arminian

Woe to those who call evil good and good evil, darkness light, bitter sweet. Wesley: they take away the difference between good and evil; justify the wicked and condemn the righteous.

Swapping in Judah’s gates (1:23; 5:23).

Reformed

Calvin: not limited to judges; whoever flatters himself in evil and condemns what is done aright. As if they would change light into darkness—folly that would destroy the principles of nature.

Desperate wickedness that erases the table. Evil good, darkness light, bitter sweet.

Isaiah 5:21

21Woe to those who are wise in their own eyes, and prudent in their own sight!

Arminian

Woe to those who are wise in their own eyes, and prudent in their own sight. The vineyard’s plants now diagnose themselves.

A conceit they can still drop. Beginning of another course of life is to be taught (5:13).

Reformed

Calvin: obstinate scorners who set their own wisdom against God’s warnings. The beginning of piety is willingness to be taught, when we have renounced our own judgment. We must become fools if we desire to be God’s disciples. In their own eyes: in their own conceit.

Self-wisdom is already revolt. Wise in their own eyes, prudent in their own sight.

Isaiah 5:22

22Woe to those who are mighty to drink wine, and champions at mixing strong drink;

Arminian

Woe to those who are mighty to drink wine, and champions at mixing strong drink. Wesley: to mingle—to drink.

The strength of 5:11 now named valor. Valor spent in the cup.

Reformed

Calvin: they waste strength in bacchanalian warfare. Beastly ambition: a man of vigor displays it by swallowing. We eat to support the body and live to obey God.

The might is misused. Both readings hold: heroes, wine, mixing strong drink.

Isaiah 5:23

23who acquit the guilty for a bribe, but deny justice for the innocent!

Arminian

Who acquit the guilty for a bribe, but deny justice for the innocent of his right. Wesley: take away—pronounce sentence against him.

“Acquit”—the cry of 5:7 in the courtroom.

Reformed

Calvin: gifts blind the wise (Exod 23:8). Where gifts are allowed, regard to right is corrupted. He names the vice that most abounded in their seats.

The vineyard’s bloodshed has a price. They acquit the guilty for a bribe and deny the innocent their right.

Isaiah 5:24

24Therefore as the tongue of fire devours the stubble, and as the dry grass sinks down in the flame, so their root shall be as rottenness, and their blossom shall go up as dust, because they have rejected the law of the LORD of Armies, and despised the word of the Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

As fire devours stubble, their root shall be as rottenness, their blossom dust; they have rejected the law of the LORD of Armies, and despised the word of the Holy One of Israel. Wesley: a tree that dies at the roots, past recovery.

The word they dared in v. 19 is the word they despised. Root-rot is the vineyard without rain (5:6).

Reformed

Calvin: contempt of the law is the source and accumulation of all that is evil. They shook off the yoke not by ignorance but by inveterate malice. Nothing worse than that Satan take away reverence for the law.

Rejecting the word is the fire’s fuel. Both agree: stubble, root, blossom, law, Holy One of Israel.

Isaiah 5:25

25Therefore the LORD’s anger burns against his people, and he has stretched out his hand against them and has struck them. The mountains tremble, and their dead bodies are as refuse in the middle of the streets. For all this, his anger is not turned away, but his hand is still stretched out.

Arminian

The LORD’s anger burns against his people; He has stretched out his hand and struck them; the mountains tremble; corpses lie like refuse in the streets. For all this, his anger is not turned away, but his hand is still stretched out still. The past stroke does not empty the next.

Blows already felt, and more coming. They might have turned. The hand is His; the corpses were Judah’s.

Reformed

Calvin: former punishments, and not near an end. Men forget chastisements as soon as they escape. Therefore: not chance. Chosen people treated sharply proves heinous crime. The hand stretched out still: it may yet pursue them.

One plague does not exhaust Him. Agreed: anger, hand, mountains, corpses, stretched out still.

Isaiah 5:26

26He will lift up a banner to the nations from far away, and he will whistle for them from the end of the earth. Behold, they will come speedily and swiftly.

Arminian

He will lift up a banner to the nations from far away, and he will whistle for them from the end of the earth; quickly, speedily they come. Wesley: ensign to the Chaldeans, a far country; that army of several nations; hiss—whistle as shepherds gather sheep; how easily God does this.

Far nations—Assyria, then Babylon. He signals; they still march. The armies march under God’s rule.

Reformed

Calvin: from distant nations they did not expect. He does not need a trumpet; a hiss, a nod, gathers them. Wars are not accidental: God assembles soldiers. The Assyrians aimed at wealth; He employs their agency. They are drawn contrary to their wish; that is no excuse.

The far army is His instrument. Signal, nations far away, whistle, quickly they come.

Isaiah 5:27

27No one shall be weary nor stumble among them; no one shall slumber nor sleep, neither shall the belt of their waist be untied, nor the strap of their sandals be broken,

Arminian

No one is weary or stumbles among them; no one slumbers or sleeps; not a belt is loosened, not a sandal strap broken. Wesley: watchful to execute His judgments; He takes impediments out of their way.

A host that does not nap.

Reformed

Calvin: everything prepared so there is no delay. They will be so far from sleeping they will not even slumber (cf. Ps 121:4, inverted).

He clears the road. Common to both: weary, stumbles, sleeps, sandal strap.

Isaiah 5:28

28whose arrows are sharp, and all their bows bent. Their horses’ hoofs will be like flint, and their wheels like a whirlwind.

Arminian

Arrows sharp, bows bent, horses’ hoofs like flint, wheels like the whirlwind. Wesley: furnished and ready, waiting His command; flint—not broken by a long rough road; whirlwind—swift march and violent chariots.

The arrows describe an army’s equipment. Length of journey will not save Judah.

Reformed

Calvin: provided with weapons. Hoofs like flint: no molestation on a long road. Wheels like a whirlwind: chariots arriving without weariness.

No length of way hinders Him. The arrows, bows, flint hoofs, and whirlwind wheels picture an unstoppable army.

Isaiah 5:29

29Their roaring will be like a lioness. They will roar like young lions. Yes, they shall roar, and seize their prey and carry it off, and there will be no one to deliver.

Arminian

They will roar like young lions. Yes, they growl and seize prey and carry it off, and none can rescue. Wesley: cruelty and eagerness to devour.

The invaders prowl over the vineyard like predators. None can rescue—from them, because He sent them.

Reformed

Calvin: not men of compassion but savage beasts. Strength such that none approach to rescue. God armed them with formidable power so this wayward people might know they have to do with God, into whose hands it is fearful to fall.

The lion is His rod. Both hear: lion, prey, none can rescue.

Isaiah 5:30

30They will roar against them in that day like the roaring of the sea. If one looks to the land, behold, darkness and distress. The light is darkened in its clouds.

Arminian

They will roar against them in that day like the roaring of the sea. If one looks to the land, darkness and distress; the light is darkened in its clouds. Wesley: sorrow; when they look to the heavens as men in distress, they see no light.

“That day”—dark over Judah’s land. They look up; the sky does not help. The vineyard’s song ends in night.

Reformed

Calvin: the attack like a deluge, the whole land shipwrecked. Punishment without alleviation. Up or down, no relief: His creatures are armed against us when He pursues. We may escape men; we cannot escape His hand.

Darkness is the last exhibit of the stretched-out hand.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Arminian and Reformed writers of the past on cords of falsehood, a scoff at delayed counsel, and a whistle that fetches far nations, in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“That draw—not only drawn to sin by allurements, but active in drawing sin to themselves. Cords—vain and deceitful arguments whereby sinners draw themselves to sin. A rope—with all their might, as beasts that draw carts. Let him—God, in whose name the prophets threaten; this was the language of their actions. The Holy One—they scornfully repeated the title. To them—that take away the difference between good and evil. An ensign—to the Chaldeans, a far country; nations, because that army was of several nations. Will hiss—whistle for them by his word, as shepherds gather sheep. How easily and speedily God can do this work.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 5:18–26)

Reformed Notable voices

“Woe unto them that draw iniquity with cords of vanity: allurements by which men harden their heart; they bind themselves to sin. Who say, Let him make speed: they will not acknowledge God unless they have immediate evidence. It is the peculiar excellence of faith to hold us dependent on the mouth of God. The distinction between the elect and the reprobate: the elect simply rely on the word; ungodly men scorn the word and call for works. He will hiss to the nations: wars are not kindled accidentally. God does not need a trumpet; by a mere nod he can accomplish the whole. The Assyrians did not intend to obey God, but he employs their agency; they are drawn contrary to their wish, yet this is no excuse.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 5:18–26)
