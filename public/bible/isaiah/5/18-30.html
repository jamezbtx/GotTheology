Passage

Isaiah 5

Verses 18–30 · ESV · Voices

Woe to those who haul sin with cart ropes and dare the Holy One to hurry. Woe to those who swap good and evil, who are wise in their own eyes, heroes at drink, who acquit for a bribe. Fire eats stubble: they have rejected the law. His anger is kindled; the hand is stretched out still. He whistles for nations from the ends of the earth. They come swift, unsleeping, hoofs like flint, roaring like lions. On that day, darkness over the land.

Isaiah 5:18

18Woe to those who draw iniquity with cords of falsehood, who draw sin as with cart ropes,

Arminian

Woe to those who draw iniquity with cords of falsehood, who draw sin as with cart ropes. Wesley: not only drawn by allurements, but active in drawing sin to themselves; cords of lying—vain pretenses; a rope—with all their might, as beasts drawing carts.

Arminian draw: historic Judah hauling its own sin. They still pull. Not a fake warning: the cart is theirs.

Reformed

Calvin: cords are the excuses by which men harden the heart. Conscience would restrain; they break through. They bind themselves to sin and are without excuse.

Reformed draw: the rope is self-tied. Shared: woe, cords, cart ropes.

Isaiah 5:19

19who say: “Let him be quick, let him speed his work that we may see it; let the counsel of the Holy One of Israel draw near, and let it come, that we may know it!”

Arminian

Who say: Let him be quick, let him speed his work that we may see it; let the counsel of the Holy One of Israel draw near, that we may know it. Wesley: God in whose name the prophets threaten; the language of their actions; they scornfully repeated the Holy One’s title.

Arminian say: a real taunt from real mouths. They could have waited on the word. Historic scoffers in Judah, not a newspaper. Match the hub: the delay is His; the dare is theirs.

Reformed

Calvin: utmost contempt—they treat judgment as fable and would be glad to see it, as if God did nothing until He punished. Faith holds us on His mouth when the effect does not immediately appear. Elect rely on the word; the ungodly scorn it and call for works. Peter’s mockers (2 Pet 3:4) speak the same line.

Reformed say: unbelief demands a spectacle; faith waits. Shared: quick, speed, Holy One of Israel, that we may know.

Key contrast Both confess the woe on those who dare the Holy One to hurry, and that the coming nations are His answer (5:19, 26). Arminians typically hear a real taunt—the language of their actions (Wesley)—under a delay they might have trusted. Reformed readers typically hear the mark of those who will not live by the word: the elect rely on His mouth; the ungodly scorn the word and call for works (Calvin).

Isaiah 5:20

20Woe to those who call evil good and good evil, who put darkness for light and light for darkness, who put bitter for sweet and sweet for bitter!

Arminian

Woe to those who call evil good and good evil, darkness light, bitter sweet. Wesley: they take away the difference between good and evil; justify the wicked and condemn the righteous.

Arminian call: real swapping in Judah’s gates (1:23; 5:23). Not a culture-war slogan: they named bloodshed justice.

Reformed

Calvin: not limited to judges; whoever flatters himself in evil and condemns what is done aright. As if they would change light into darkness—folly that would destroy the principles of nature.

Reformed call: desperate wickedness that erases the table. Shared: evil good, darkness light, bitter sweet.

Isaiah 5:21

21Woe to those who are wise in their own eyes, and shrewd in their own sight!

Arminian

Woe to those who are wise in their own eyes, and shrewd in their own sight. The vineyard’s plants now diagnose themselves.

Arminian wise: a real conceit they can still drop. Beginning of another course of life is to be taught (5:13).

Reformed

Calvin: obstinate scorners who set their own wisdom against God’s warnings. The beginning of piety is willingness to be taught, when we have renounced our own judgment. We must become fools if we desire to be God’s disciples. In their own eyes: in their own conceit.

Reformed wise: self-wisdom is already revolt. Shared: wise in their own eyes, shrewd in their own sight.

Isaiah 5:22

22Woe to those who are heroes at drinking wine, and valiant men in mixing strong drink,

Arminian

Woe to those who are heroes at drinking wine, and valiant men in mixing strong drink. Wesley: to mingle—to drink.

Arminian heroes: the strength of 5:11 now named valor. Historic carousers, not a culture-war on drink as such. Valor spent in the cup.

Reformed

Calvin: they waste strength in bacchanalian warfare. Beastly ambition: a man of vigor displays it by swallowing. We eat to support the body and live to obey God.

Reformed heroes: the might is misused. Shared: heroes, wine, mixing strong drink.

Isaiah 5:23

23who acquit the guilty for a bribe, and deprive the innocent of his right!

Arminian

Who acquit the guilty for a bribe, and deprive the innocent of his right. Wesley: take away—pronounce sentence against him.

Arminian acquit: the cry of 5:7 in the courtroom. Historic bribes in Judah’s gates, not a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: gifts blind the wise (Exod 23:8). Where gifts are allowed, regard to right is corrupted. He names the vice that most abounded in their seats.

Reformed acquit: the vineyard’s bloodshed has a price. Shared: guilty, bribe, innocent, right.

Isaiah 5:24

24Therefore, as the tongue of fire devours the stubble, and as dry grass sinks down in the flame, so their root will be as rottenness, and their blossom go up like dust; for they have rejected the law of the Lord of hosts, and have despised the word of the Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

As fire devours stubble, their root will be as rottenness, their blossom dust; they have rejected the law of the Lord of hosts and despised the word of the Holy One of Israel. Wesley: a tree that dies at the roots, past recovery.

Arminian therefore: the word they dared in v. 19 is the word they despised. Historic law, historic scorn. Root-rot is the vineyard without rain (5:6).

Reformed

Calvin: contempt of the law is the source and accumulation of all that is evil. They shook off the yoke not by ignorance but by inveterate malice. Nothing worse than that Satan take away reverence for the law.

Reformed therefore: rejecting the word is the fire’s fuel. Shared: stubble, root, blossom, law, Holy One of Israel.

Isaiah 5:25

25Therefore the anger of the Lord was kindled against his people, and he stretched out his hand against them and struck them, and the mountains quaked; and their corpses were as refuse in the midst of the streets. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still.

Arminian

The anger of the Lord was kindled against his people; He stretched out His hand and struck; mountains quaked; corpses as refuse in the streets. For all this His anger has not turned away, and His hand is stretched out still. The past stroke does not empty the next.

Arminian still: historic blows already felt, and more coming. They might have turned. The hand is His; the corpses were Judah’s.

Reformed

Calvin: former punishments, and not near an end. Men forget chastisements as soon as they escape. Therefore: not chance. Chosen people treated sharply proves heinous crime. The hand stretched out still: it may yet pursue them.

Reformed still: one plague does not exhaust Him. Shared: anger, hand, mountains, corpses, stretched out still.

Isaiah 5:26

26He will raise a signal for nations far away, and whistle for them from the ends of the earth; and behold, quickly, speedily they come!

Arminian

He will raise a signal for nations far away, and whistle for them from the ends of the earth; quickly, speedily they come. Wesley: ensign to the Chaldeans, a far country; that army of several nations; hiss—whistle as shepherds gather sheep; how easily God does this.

Arminian whistle: historic far nations—Assyria, then Babylon—not a newspaper. He signals; they still march. Real armies under that rule. No millennial camp.

Reformed

Calvin: from distant nations they did not expect. He does not need a trumpet; a hiss, a nod, gathers them. Wars are not accidental: God assembles soldiers. The Assyrians aimed at wealth; He employs their agency. They are drawn contrary to their wish; that is no excuse.

Reformed whistle: the far army is His instrument. Shared: signal, nations far away, whistle, quickly they come.

Isaiah 5:27

27None is weary, none stumbles, none slumbers or sleeps, not a waistband is loose, not a sandal strap broken;

Arminian

None is weary, none stumbles, none slumbers or sleeps; not a waistband loose, not a sandal strap broken. Wesley: watchful to execute His judgments; He takes impediments out of their way.

Arminian none: a real host that does not nap. Historic march, not a myth.

Reformed

Calvin: everything prepared so there is no delay. They will be so far from sleeping they will not even slumber (cf. Ps 121:4, inverted).

Reformed none: He clears the road. Shared: weary, stumbles, sleeps, sandal strap.

Isaiah 5:28

28their arrows are sharp, all their bows bent, their horses' hoofs seem like flint, and their wheels like the whirlwind.

Arminian

Arrows sharp, bows bent, horses’ hoofs like flint, wheels like the whirlwind. Wesley: furnished and ready, waiting His command; flint—not broken by a long rough road; whirlwind—swift march and violent chariots.

Arminian arrows: real kit of a real army. Length of journey will not save Judah.

Reformed

Calvin: provided with weapons. Hoofs like flint: no molestation on a long road. Wheels like a whirlwind: chariots arriving without weariness.

Reformed arrows: no length of way hinders Him. Shared: arrows, bows, flint, whirlwind.

Isaiah 5:29

29Their roaring is like a lion, like young lions they roar; they growl and seize their prey; they carry it off, and none can rescue.

Arminian

Roaring like a lion, like young lions; they growl and seize prey; they carry it off, and none can rescue. Wesley: cruelty and eagerness to devour.

Arminian roar: historic predators over this vineyard. None can rescue—from them, because He sent them.

Reformed

Calvin: not men of compassion but savage beasts. Strength such that none approach to rescue. God armed them with formidable power so this wayward people might know they have to do with God, into whose hands it is fearful to fall.

Reformed roar: the lion is His rod. Shared: lion, prey, none can rescue.

Isaiah 5:30

30They will growl over it on that day, like the growling of the sea. And if one looks to the land, behold, darkness and distress; and the light is darkened by its clouds.

Arminian

They will growl over it on that day, like the growling of the sea. If one looks to the land, darkness and distress; the light is darkened by its clouds. Wesley: sorrow; when they look to the heavens as men in distress, they see no light.

Arminian that day: historic dark over Judah’s land, not a millennial chart. They look up; the sky does not help. The vineyard’s song ends in night.

Reformed

Calvin: the attack like a deluge, the whole land shipwrecked. Punishment without alleviation. Up or down, no relief: His creatures are armed against us when He pursues. We may escape men; we cannot escape His hand.

Reformed that day: darkness is the last exhibit of the stretched-out hand. Shared: growl, sea, land, darkness, distress, clouds.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on cords of falsehood, a scoff at delayed counsel, and a whistle that fetches far nations—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 5:18–30.

Arminian Notable voices

“That draw—not only drawn to sin by allurements, but active in drawing sin to themselves. Cords—vain and deceitful arguments whereby sinners draw themselves to sin. A rope—with all their might, as beasts that draw carts. Let him—God, in whose name the prophets threaten; this was the language of their actions. The Holy One—they scornfully repeated the title. To them—that take away the difference between good and evil. An ensign—to the Chaldeans, a far country; nations, because that army was of several nations. Will hiss—whistle for them by his word, as shepherds gather sheep. How easily and speedily God can do this work.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 5:18–26)

Reformed Notable voices

“Woe unto them that draw iniquity with cords of vanity: allurements by which men harden their heart; they bind themselves to sin. Who say, Let him make speed: they will not acknowledge God unless they have immediate evidence. It is the peculiar excellence of faith to hold us dependent on the mouth of God. The distinction between the elect and the reprobate: the elect simply rely on the word; ungodly men scorn the word and call for works. He will hiss to the nations: wars are not kindled accidentally. God does not need a trumpet; by a mere nod he can accomplish the whole. The Assyrians did not intend to obey God, but he employs their agency; they are drawn contrary to their wish, yet this is no excuse.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 5:18–26)
