Passage

Isaiah 5

Verses 8–17 · WEB · Voices

After the vineyard’s wild grapes, woes fall on those who join house to house until they dwell alone, and on those who rise early for drink and feast without regarding the Lord’s work. Many houses will stand empty; ten acres yield a bath. Therefore the people go into captivity for lack of knowledge; Sheol opens wide. Man is humbled; the LORD of Armies is exalted in justice. Then lambs graze, and nomads eat the ruins of the rich.

Isaiah 5:8

8Woe to those who join house to house, who lay field to field, until there is no room, and you are made to dwell alone in the middle of the land!

Arminian

Woe to those who join house to house, lay field to field, until no room, and you dwell alone in the middle of the land. Wesley: that they alone may be lords, and all others tenants and servants.

The woe falls on Judah’s hunger for land. Adding a field is not the sin; squeezing the neighbor until you sit alone is. The vineyard of 5:7 now eats the next plot.

Reformed

Calvin: not wrong in itself to add field to field; he looks at the mind that never has enough. They would wish other men removed from the earth. A man left alone cannot plough. God linked men so they need one another.

Covetousness, not square footage. House to house, field to field, dwell alone.

Isaiah 5:9

9In my ears, the LORD of Armies says: “Surely many houses will be desolate, even great and beautiful, unoccupied.

Arminian

The LORD of Armies has sworn in my ears: many houses will be desolate, even great and beautiful ones, without inhabitant. Wesley: I heard God speak what I am about to utter.

God swears an oath over the palaces. The joined houses of v. 8 stand empty.

Reformed

Calvin: something must be supplied—the Lord sits as judge. Covetous men do not think He observes. If not: a form of oath. Just punishment: He chastises those who would not be content with a moderate portion. Palaces become receptacles of owls.

None of this escapes His ears. Lord of hosts, many houses, desolate, without inhabitant.

Isaiah 5:10

10For ten acres of vineyard shall yield one bath, and a homer of seed shall yield an ephah.”

Arminian

Ten acres of vineyard shall yield one bath, and a homer of seed but an ephah. Wesley: the bath about eight gallons—an acre not one gallon; an ephah the tenth of a homer; they lose nine parts of their seed.

“Yield”—the fertile hill of 5:1 now starves.

Reformed

Calvin: the same blight on fields as on houses. Ten acres, one bath—soil so barren it yields scarcely a tenth. Men blame the niggardliness of the soil; the curse is on covetousness that swallowed the goodness of God.

He withholds the increase they stored for themselves. Both see: ten acres, one bath, homer, ephah.

Isaiah 5:11

11Woe to those who rise up early in the morning, that they may follow strong drink, who stay late into the night, until wine inflames them!

Arminian

Woe to those who rise up early to run after strong drink, who stay late as wine inflames them. A second woe beside the land-woe.

Judah drinks from dawn until dark. The run and the tarrying that never looks up.

Reformed

Calvin: from morning to twilight they continue carousals. Abundance and luxury join. To rise early is to be earnest—here, earnest for drink, contrary to rising for work (Ps 104:23).

Drunkenness is another abuse of God’s gifts. Early, strong drink, late, wine inflames.

Isaiah 5:12

12The harp, lyre, tambourine, and flute, with wine, are at their feasts; but they don’t respect the work of the LORD, neither have they considered the operation of his hands.

Arminian

Lyre, tambourine, and flute, with wine, are at their feasts; but they don’t respect the work of the LORD, neither have they considered the operation of his hands. Wesley: they give themselves wholly to luxury; the work is His judgments, inflicted and threatened, which required another course of life.

Music is not the charge; refusing to regard is. They could have looked.

Reformed

Calvin: he does not blame music, a science not to be despised; he describes a nation swimming in luxury. They do not consider why the Lord supplies them, or that they were born to obey God, not to wallow. Worse than beasts if they will not direct their minds to the Author of life.

The operation of his hands includes the coming exile. Neither side disputes this: lyre, harp, feasts, not regard, work of His hands.

Isaiah 5:13

13Therefore my people go into captivity for lack of knowledge. Their honorable men are famished, and their multitudes are parched with thirst.

Arminian

Therefore my people go into captivity for lack of knowledge. Their honorable men are famished, and their multitudes are parched with thirst. Wesley: no serious consideration of God’s works, of their own duty and danger; honourable men who thought themselves out of reach of famine.

Captivity follows “lack of knowledge”—Wesley calls it a failure to consider God’s works and their own danger. Arminians read the ignorance as chosen neglect, not mere misfortune.

Reformed

Calvin: not that teachers failed, but voluntary ignorance. They perished because they despised instruction. My people: a nation separated to have God near, shutting its eyes in so much light. Captivity is already in part inflicted. Ignorance is sometimes near, sometimes remote: they refuse to learn, and yet it does not excuse them. The source of calamities is that we do not allow ourselves to be taught by the word of God.

They would not be taught; the exile is His sentence. My people, exile, lack of knowledge, hungry, thirst.

Key contrast Both confess exile comes for lack of knowledge, and that honored Judah still starves (5:13). Arminians hear a failure to consider God’s works, duty, and danger (Wesley). Reformed interpreters hear voluntary ignorance: they despised instruction; the outward light of the law left them without excuse (Calvin).

Isaiah 5:14

14Therefore Sheol has enlarged its desire, and opened its mouth without measure; and their glory, their multitude, their pomp, and he who rejoices among them, descend into it.

Arminian

Therefore Sheol has enlarged its desire and opened its mouth without measure; the nobility of Jerusalem and her crowds go down into it, her revelers and all who celebrate in her. Wesley: he that spends all his days in mirth and jollity.

Death opens wider than the feast. The mouth of the grave answers the open mouth of wine.

Reformed

Calvin: hell as an insatiable beast. He speaks as if the event were already before their eyes. Nobles and low rank together—none escape. Therefore: these things do not happen by chance.

The appetite is His judgment, not fortune. Agreed on both sides: enlarged, mouth, nobility, multitude, revelers.

Isaiah 5:15

15So man is brought low, mankind is humbled, and the eyes of the arrogant ones are humbled;

Arminian

Man is humbled, each one brought low, the eyes of the haughty brought low. The refrain of 2:11 after a new catalogue.

High and low, still their eyes until they fall.

Reformed

Calvin: adam and ish—mean and mighty. Design of chastisement: His justice shines when He punishes. Hypocrites become more insolent when not struck; He will be exalted in their destruction.

“Humbled”—the same day against pride. Undisputed: man, brought low, haughty eyes.

Isaiah 5:16

16but the LORD of Armies is exalted in justice, and God the Holy One is sanctified in righteousness.

Arminian

The LORD of Armies is exalted in justice, and God the Holy One is sanctified in righteousness. Wesley: exalted by this just judgment; sanctified—appears to be a holy God by righteous judgments.

Both traditions confess that the LORD is exalted in justice. His holiness is seen in the sentence. Justice here is the vineyard’s missing fruit now executed.

Reformed

Calvin: the formal cause of His excellence. He will be raised when He shows Himself judge. Unlikely that the wicked continue in prosperity while His glory is set aside. He is holy by nature and must be sanctified; destruction hangs over the wicked that rebellion may be subdued.

He cannot deny Himself. Lord of hosts, justice, Holy God, righteousness.

Isaiah 5:17

17Then the lambs will graze as in their pasture, and strangers will eat the ruins of the rich.

Arminian

Then the lambs will graze as in their pasture, and strangers eat the ruins of the rich. Wesley: when God has finished that judgment; lambs—the poor and harmless left when the rich are carried captive; waste places of the fat ones; strangers—poor Israelites left as vine-dressers (2 Kings 25:12), strangers to that land as not its owners.

Leftover poor on seized ground. The vineyard’s soil feeds someone after the grabbers go.

Reformed

Calvin: consolation for the godly who tremble at the threatenings. He will be a faithful shepherd to His lambs. The haughty who oppressed the poor boasted they were the Church; they are goats. Waste places of the fat ones shall strangers eat: the children of God, banished as foreigners, will regain what tyrants seized.

The flock remains when the fat are gone. Lambs graze, ruins of the rich.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Voices from both camps

Classic comment on houses joined to houses, a feast that will not look, and exile for lack of knowledge, from both sides of the debate.

Arminian Notable voices

“Alone—that they alone may be the lords and owners, and all others only their tenants and servants. In mine ears—I heard God speak what I am about to utter. The harp—they give up themselves wholly to luxury. The work—what God hath lately done, and is yet doing, and about to do; his grievous judgments, which required another course of life. No knowledge—no serious consideration of God’s works, and of their own duty and danger. Honourable men—who thought themselves quite out of the reach of famine. Exalted—by the execution of this just judgment. Sanctified—shall appear to be an holy God, by his righteous judgments. The lambs—the poor and harmless people left in the land when the rich are carried into captivity. Strangers—the poor Israelites left to be vine-dressers, who were strangers to that land as not the proper owners of it.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 5:8–17)

Reformed Notable voices

“Woe to them that join house to house. He looks at the disposition of mind, which cannot be satisfied. It cannot be condemned as a thing in itself wrong if a man add field to field; but they wish to have everything possessed by themselves alone. Therefore my people are gone into captivity because they have no knowledge: they perished because they despised instruction. The source of all our calamities is that we do not allow ourselves to be taught by the word of God. Jehovah of hosts shall be exalted in judgment: so long as men go on in their sins, his justice is in some degree smothered; it shines when he punishes. The lambs shall feed: though it may seem as if God were about to destroy the whole nation, he will show himself a faithful shepherd to his lambs.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 5:8–17)
