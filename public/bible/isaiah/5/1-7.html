Passage

Isaiah 5

Verses 1–7 · ESV · Voices

Isaiah sings for his beloved a song of a vineyard on a fertile hill. The beloved digs, clears stones, plants choice vines, builds a tower, hews a vat, and looks for grapes. Wild grapes come. Jerusalem and Judah are told to judge. What more was there to do? The hedge will be removed, the wall broken, briers will grow, the clouds commanded not to rain. The vineyard is the house of Israel; Judah his pleasant planting. He looked for justice, and behold, bloodshed; for righteousness, an outcry.

Isaiah 5:1

1Let me sing for my beloved my love song concerning his vineyard: My beloved had a vineyard on a very fertile hill.

Arminian

Let me sing for my beloved a love song concerning his vineyard: a vineyard on a very fertile hill. Wesley: a witness for God and against you, as Moses’ song; vineyard—his church; hill—places most commodious for vines.

Arminian song: historic Judah as the beloved’s plot, not a newspaper. A real hill, a real love. The singer is jealous for the bride entrusted to him.

Reformed

Calvin: a poem in behalf of God, that he might expostulate about ingratitude. Beloved: God; Isaiah assumes the character of one jealous for the bride. Vineyard: no dearer possession, and none that demands more constant toil. Hill, son of oil: a fertile situation in the allegory, the people’s prosperous condition under care.

Reformed song: the care is His peculiar regard, not common rain on just and unjust. Shared: beloved, vineyard, fertile hill.

Isaiah 5:2

2He dug it and cleared it of stones, and planted it with choice vines; he built a watchtower in the midst of it, and hewed out a wine vat in it; and he looked for it to yield grapes, but it yielded wild grapes.

Arminian

He dug it, cleared stones, planted choice vines, built a watchtower, hewed a wine vat; he looked for grapes, and it yielded wild grapes. Wesley: he removed all hindrances and gave them all the means of fruitfulness; a tower for the keepers.

Arminian dug: real means actually given. Choice vines were planted; wild grapes still came. The looking is not a fake warning. All the ordinary care of a husbandman was there; the yield was theirs.

Reformed

Calvin: incessant care; he has neglected nothing expected of the best householder. Do not force every clause (Spirit as fence, doctrine as vat). Planted, then everything else necessary. He hoped it would bring forth grapes: God is not deceived like a mortal (Heb 4:13); Isaiah describes how the people ought to have acted. The gospel is proclaimed for the obedience of faith, not because He expects all to be obedient, but that unbelievers are rendered inexcusable by the hearing.

Reformed dug: the looking names their duty, not a gap in His knowledge. Shared: stones, choice vines, tower, vat, wild grapes.

Isaiah 5:3

3And now, O inhabitants of Jerusalem and men of Judah, judge, I pray you, between me and my vineyard.

Arminian

Now, O inhabitants of Jerusalem and men of Judah, judge, I pray you, between me and my vineyard. Historic hearers are made the jury.

Arminian judge: a real appeal to real men of Judah. They can still weigh the case. The vineyard is theirs to recognize.

Reformed

Calvin: the guilty are made judges in a cause so plain they have no evasion. Proof of the strongest confidence: bid them declare if this be not the fact. Immediately the accusation is decided against those to whom he now commits the decision.

Reformed judge: their own mouth will close the plea. Shared: Jerusalem, Judah, judge between me and my vineyard.

Isaiah 5:4

4What more was there to do for my vineyard, that I have not done in it? When I looked for it to yield grapes, why did it yield wild grapes?

Arminian

What more was there to do for my vineyard, that I have not done in it? When I looked for grapes, why wild grapes? Wesley’s “all the means of fruitfulness” stands here: nothing ordinary was withheld.

Arminian what more: a real question over historic Judah. He gave the means; they yielded wild. Not a works-gospel: grapes were the fruit of a gift. Not a fake warning: the yield was theirs. Match the hub: a King who plants and looks, leaving this wildness as a real second cause.

Reformed

Calvin: what could have been expected from the best husbandman which he has not done? They have no excuse. If it be objected that God had the remedy in his hands, if he had turned their hearts, this is an idle evasion; conscience holds them. Though God do not pierce hearts by His Spirit so as to render them obedient, they cannot complain that this was wanting; the outward call takes away ignorance. He does not speak here of His power, but declares He was not obligated to do more than He did.

Reformed what more: the complaint stops their mouth; it does not teach that effectual turning was omitted as if He lacked it. Shared: what more, looked, wild grapes.

Key contrast Both confess the Lord did all a vineyard’s husbandman does, and that Judah’s wild grapes are without excuse (5:4). Arminians typically hear all the means of fruitfulness actually given, and a real looking that wild grapes disappointed (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear no proof that God failed to turn the heart: He does not speak here of His power to pierce; the outward call leaves them inexcusable, and He was not obligated to do more (Calvin).

Isaiah 5:5

5And now I will tell you what I will do to my vineyard. I will remove its hedge, and it shall be devoured; I will break down its wall, and it shall be trampled down.

Arminian

I will remove its hedge, and it shall be devoured; I will break down its wall, and it shall be trampled down. After the verdict, the sentence.

Arminian hedge: historic walls of Judah coming down—Assyria, then Babylon—not a slogan against borders. Protection withdrawn is still His doing; trampling is still armies’.

Reformed

Calvin: vengeance for contempt of grace. They will be deprived of the gifts they abused. When His protection is gone they become prey to all that pass by, men or beasts.

Reformed hedge: He withdraws what He put up. Shared: hedge, devour, wall, trample.

Isaiah 5:6

6I will make it a waste; it shall not be pruned or hoed, and briers and thorns shall grow up; I will also command the clouds that they rain no rain upon it.

Arminian

I will make it a waste; not pruned or hoed; briers and thorns; I will command the clouds that they rain no rain upon it. Wesley: I will remove my ministers who used care to make you fruitful; thorns—give you up to your own lusts; no rain—deprive you of all my blessings.

Arminian waste: real drought and real neglect of the plot. Ministers withdrawn, lusts unpruned. The clouds still hear Him.

Reformed

Calvin: He will not take pains to dig and prune; briars choke; by withholding rain He dries the roots. Weapons for punishing ingratitude. Whenever we are justly deprived of favors freely bestowed, acknowledge His anger.

Reformed waste: the same hand that commanded rain now commands none. Shared: waste, briers, thorns, no rain.

Isaiah 5:7

7For the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah are his pleasant planting; and he looked for justice, but behold, bloodshed; for righteousness, but behold, an outcry!

Arminian

The vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant planting; He looked for justice, behold, bloodshed; for righteousness, behold, an outcry. Wesley: pleasant—in whom God formerly delighted; a cry from the oppressed, to men for help and to God for vengeance.

Arminian vineyard: the riddle named. Historic house of Israel, men of Judah—not a newspaper. Justice was looked for in the gates (1:23; 3:14). Bloodshed and cry are theirs. The looking of v. 2 is now justice and righteousness.

Reformed

Calvin: without metaphor now. Whole nation the vineyard, individuals the plants; no man escapes. He looked for judgment, behold oppression; righteousness, behold a cry—paronomasia in the Hebrew. Piety is described from the second table: where cruelty reigns, religion is extinguished. We who were wild olives grafted in (Rom 11) owe the same fruit.

Reformed vineyard: Israel and Judah, then the church after them. Shared: Lord of hosts, house of Israel, Judah, justice, bloodshed, righteousness, outcry.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a well-dressed vineyard that yielded wild grapes—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 5:1–7.

Arminian Notable voices

“Now—I will record it to be a witness for God, and against you, as Moses did his song. Vineyard—his church. He gathered—he removed all hindrances, and gave them all the means of fruitfulness. A tower—for the residence of the keepers. Nor digged—I will remove my ministers, who used great care and diligence to make you fruitful. Thorns—I will give you up to your own lusts. No rain—I will deprive you of all my blessings. Pleasant—in whom God formerly delighted. A cry—from the oppressed, crying to men for help, and to God for vengeance.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 5:1–7)

Reformed Notable voices

“What more ought to have been done to my vineyard? He inquires what could have been expected from the best husbandman which he has not done. Hence they had no excuse. If it be objected that God had the remedy in his hands, if he had turned the hearts of the people, this is an idle evasion as applied to those men; for their conscience holds them fast. Though God do not pierce the hearts of men by the power of his Spirit, so as to render them obedient to him, yet they will have no right to complain that this was wanting; for every pretense of ignorance is taken away by the outward call. Besides, God does not speak here of his power, but declares that he was not under any obligation to do more than he did. God, to whose eyes all things are naked, is not deceived by his expectation like a mortal man.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 5:2–4)
