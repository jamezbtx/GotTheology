Passage

Isaiah 62

Verses 1–12 · ESV · Voices

The prophet will not keep silent until Zion’s righteousness shines and her salvation burns like a torch. Forsaken names are changed: Hephzibah, Beulah; watchmen take no rest; a highway is cleared; the Holy People are Sought Out, A City Not Forsaken. Historic restoration after exile comes first; the church is the same people under the gospel. Bridegroom joy and a sworn harvest belong to that one story—not a newspaper, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 62:1

1For Zion’s sake I will not keep silent, and for Jerusalem’s sake I will not be quiet, until her righteousness goes forth as brightness, and her salvation as a burning torch.

Arminian

Wesley: Zion and Jerusalem are both put for the church (Heb 12:22). These seem to be the words of the prophet strongly resolving, notwithstanding all difficulties, to solicit God for the church’s happiness, and constantly excite to the belief of it by his preaching. Righteousness—the vindicating of his people’s cause; the lamp is the church conspicuous among the nations, and the revealing of Christ unto the world.

Arminian watch: the church still prays and preaches until righteousness shines. Silence is not obedience. Historic Zion first; the gospel church is the same people. Shared ground with Calvin: he will not rest till her salvation burns. Not a millennial timetable.

Reformed

Calvin: some explain this as relating to prayer, but he refers it rather to doctrine. The prophet will not be slack till he encourage believers in the hope of salvation. Teachers spend themselves for the Church. Righteousness is the Church’s rights restored; salvation is added, because those God justifies regain their salvation.

Reformed word: God will execute his promises at the proper time. We are wretched while his grace is withheld for sin. Same persistence as Wesley; the accent is the effectual word God owns, not a newspaper restoration only.

Isaiah 62:2

2The nations shall see your righteousness, and all the kings your glory, and you shall be called by a new name that the mouth of the Lord will give.

Arminian

Wesley: a new name—the church shall be more renowned than ever, both in respect of her condition, and so called Hephzi-bah, and of her relation, and so called Beulah; and this new name the Lord gives them accordingly (v. 4).

Arminian name: nations and kings still may see. The mouth of the Lord names; the church can still be renamed. Gospel honor, not a later political brand.

Reformed

Calvin: the new name means a crowded assemblage; the scattered people regain a name of which they had been deprived. God is the author of this glory. It is extraordinary, and shines when the Gospel is preached.

Reformed named: he will never forsake his Church. Wicked men may slander; God is not deprived of his right to vindicate those whose names he has written in heaven. Restoration from exile is the shadow; gospel gathering the substance.

Isaiah 62:3

3You shall be a crown of beauty in the hand of the Lord, and a royal diadem in the hand of your God.

Arminian

Wesley: crown of glory—an expression to set forth the dignity of her state. In the hand—preserved and defended by God’s hand. Royal diadem—the same for substance, or the royal priesthood (1 Pet 2:9).

Arminian crown: the church is already a crown in his hand, a people as near to God as priests. Dignity is gift and calling, not a millennial coronation.

Reformed

Calvin: the Church is God’s crown; he wishes his glory to shine in us. By nature filthy and more abominable than mire, yet he adorns us as the diadem of his kingdom. The return was but the forerunner of rank at Christ’s manifestation.

Reformed diadem: look to God for glory hidden from flesh. Be aroused to a holy life, that his image may more and more be formed anew in us.

Isaiah 62:4

4You shall no more be termed Forsaken, and your land shall no more be termed Desolate, but you shall be called My Delight Is in Her, and your land Married; for the Lord delights in you, and your land shall be married.

Arminian

Wesley: Forsaken—as a woman forsaken by her husband. Hephzi-bah—My delight is in her; a new name agreeing with her new condition. Beulah—Married; agreeing to her new relation. Increase of children as the fruit of marriage, which refers to the great enlargement of the church in the gospel days.

Arminian honor: the names are the church’s new condition and relation under the gospel. Forsaken is not the last word. The people may still be delighted in and married. Historic Zion first; gospel enlargement the same mercy.

Reformed

Calvin: this proceeds from the good-pleasure of God—undeserved favor, that nothing may be ascribed to the merits or excellence of men (Hos 2:19). They shall be prosperous for no other reason than that God, out of infinite goodness, will graciously receive into favor those whom he had abandoned.

Reformed beloved: Hephzibah is electing delight, not earned reputation. The divorce shall not be perpetual, but the turn is his good-pleasure, not our recovery of form. Same names as Wesley; the fork is why they stick.

Key contrast Both refuse a millennial name-chart. Arminians typically stress the new names as the church’s gospel honor and enlarged condition (Hephzibah, Beulah) after being forsaken. Reformed readers typically stress that the names proceed from God’s undeserved good-pleasure toward those he had abandoned—nothing ascribed to merits. Shared ground: Zion is not finally Forsaken.

Isaiah 62:5

5For as a young man marries a young woman, so shall your sons marry you, and as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall your God rejoice over you.

Arminian

Wesley does not pause here; v. 4’s marriage still governs. Bridegroom joy is God’s over the restored people, the fruit of Beulah’s new relation.

Arminian joy: sons marrying and God rejoicing are gospel family and joy, already held out. Historic Zion first; the church may still be rejoiced over. Not a later wedding only.

Reformed

Calvin: there is but one Husband, that is, God, fulfilled in Christ; pastors espouse the Church as a chaste virgin (2 Cor 11:2). Unity of faith with the mother Church. Without God, a multitude is not a church but a brothel, as in Popery’s boast of the name.

Reformed marriage: begin with God presiding; then the Church is truly populous. Spiritual marriage, not a newspaper wedding.

Isaiah 62:6

6On your walls, O Jerusalem, I have set watchmen; all the day and all the night they shall never be silent. You who put the Lord in remembrance, take no rest,

Arminian

Wesley: day nor night—there shall be a vigilant and industrious ministry. Ye—his servants in ordinary, his remembrancers, such as put God in mind of his promise, and such as make the Lord to be remembered, putting his people in mind of him.

Arminian watchmen: ministers must not hold their peace. The church’s prayer and preaching take no rest. Shared walls with Calvin; the accent is the duty not to be silent.

Reformed

Calvin: God appoints faithful guards. Christ’s kingdom is not defended by weapons of war; it is spiritual, protected by the sword of the word. Night and day pastors must watch. It is a remarkable kindness when we have faithful pastors; pray for those guards.

Reformed watch: God set them. Angels may watch (Ps 91:11), but the address is to ministers. Not an earthly garrison, and not a millennial wall chart.

Isaiah 62:7

7and give him no rest until he establishes Jerusalem and makes it a praise in the earth.

Arminian

Wesley: a praise—by sending the Messiah, and those labourers into his vineyard, whereby the church may be established on sure foundations, and so become a matter of praise to God. All the nations shall praise him for her.

Arminian praise: give him no rest—keep asking until he sends the Messiah and workers. Jerusalem a praise is missionary, already under the gospel, not a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: add prayer to teaching. Two distinct benefits: faithful pastors, and the Church preserved by their agency—but God alone appoints pastors (Rom 10:15) and grants success. External agency is joined with the Spirit’s efficacy.

Reformed restore: labors are unprofitable unless he gives fruit. Do not lose courage in ruin; pray that the Lord will restore her, which he also promises.

Isaiah 62:8

8The Lord has sworn by his right hand and by his mighty arm: "I will not again give your grain to be food for your enemies, and foreigners shall not drink your wine for which you have labored;

Arminian

Wesley does not pause here; the oath still governs the harvest. Grain and wine no longer for enemies—the church eats what she labored for.

Arminian oath: the Lord has sworn. Historic exile’s plunder ends; the gospel people taste the same mercy. Not a millennial pantry. Shared ground: he will not give the fruit to strangers.

Reformed

Calvin: God swears by his right hand because of our unbelief. If he has any power, he will display it in your salvation. Enemies’ plunder was his permission (Deut 28:33); peaceful enjoyment under vine and fig-tree is his special blessing.

Reformed sworn: the arm of his strength, not chance, secures the harvest. Trust the oath, not a chart.

Isaiah 62:9

9but those who garner it shall eat it and praise the Lord, and those who gather it shall drink it in the courts of my sanctuary."

Arminian

Wesley: but—thou shalt not sow, and another reap, as formerly. Courts—in my courts; holiness being put for God himself.

Arminian harvest: eat and praise. The gatherers who actually thank the Lord keep the fruit. Worship in his courts, not a later temple timetable.

Reformed

Calvin: lawful labor, yet fruitless unless the Lord supplies. All we obtain is his; thank him. He alludes to firstfruits and feasting in God’s presence (Deut 12:18).

Reformed thanks: praise is the mark that the food is from him. Sanctuary courts are gospel worship, the Church eating what God gives.

Isaiah 62:10

10Go through, go through the gates; prepare the way for the people; build up, build up the highway; clear it of stones; lift up a signal over the peoples.

Arminian

Wesley: go through—doubled by way of emphasis. Go meet the Gentiles, whom God purposes to bring into the church. Prepare—let them not have any obstructions. Stones—no stumbling-stone or offence. Standard—an allusion to soldiers, that the army may know where to repair from all quarters.

Arminian highway: clear stones so nations may still come. Prepare the way—missionary work now, as in 57:14 and 40:3. The signal is gospel welcome, not a millennial checkpoint.

Reformed

Calvin: a free passage through gates once shut or ruined. Teachers, and all whose agency the Lord employs, clear the way—Medes and Persians first, then every restorer of the Church. Level, level: every obstacle shall be removed; all shall obey God’s command.

Reformed road: the Church is a populous city again. Peoples assemble when the standard is lifted. God commands the highway; men do not invent it.

Isaiah 62:11

11Behold, the Lord has proclaimed to the end of the earth: Say to the daughter of Zion, "Behold, your salvation comes; behold, his reward is with him, and his recompense before him."

Arminian

Wesley: Zion—to Jerusalem, or the church. Thy salvation—thy saviour. Reward—the reward due to the work.

Arminian Savior: say to the daughter of Zion, your Savior comes. The proclamation is to the end of the earth. Come is present gospel, sincerely held out, not postponed news only.

Reformed

Calvin: the Lord will proclaim so nations know this is his command. Addressed to ministers of the word. Not limited to one age—from the return from Babylon through Christ’s coming, when the Savior was exhibited. His work and reward are before him; he is not idle.

Reformed coming: the Gospel voice from sunrise to sunset. Salvation is God’s work, not merits. Same coming as Wesley; the accent is the Lord who actually arrives.

Isaiah 62:12

12And they shall be called The Holy People, The Redeemed of the Lord; and you shall be called Sought Out, A City Not Forsaken.

Arminian

Wesley does not pause here; v. 4’s names still govern. Holy People, Redeemed, Sought Out, A City Not Forsaken—the same honor after exile, under the gospel.

Arminian close: the church may still be sought and not forsaken. Names are the people’s new condition. Historic then gospel. Do not invent a millennial city fight between the notes.

Reformed

Calvin: a holy people by the covenant of adoption with Abraham. They are a holy people when he appears as Savior and Redeemer. Sought out, not forsaken: contrast between the time God made a divorce from his people and the time he reconciled to himself those he had cast off.

Reformed close: redemption makes the name true. God testified he remembered his heritage. Same people, elect Church—not a newspaper caption, and not a millennial chart as the fork.

Arminian Notable voices

Reformed Notable voices

“Some explain this as relating to prayer, but I choose rather to refer it to doctrine… no inconvenience or annoyance shall wear out his patience… in the office of teaching… concerning the redemption of the Church. This proceeds from the good-pleasure of God; that is, from his undeserved favor, that nothing may be ascribed to the merits or excellence of men. There is but one Husband, that is, God… fulfilled in Christ. They shall be a holy people, when he shall appear as their Savior and Redeemer. Sought out, not forsaken… a contrast between the time when God made a divorce from his people, and the time when he again reconciled to himself those whom he had cast off.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 62:1, 4–5, 12)
