Passage

Isaiah 42

Verses 1–9 · ESV · Voices

After the Cyrus-token of chapter 41, the LORD sets forth a more eminent servant: chosen, upheld, Spirit-endowed, who will bring forth justice to the nations without crying in the street. A bruised reed he will not break, a faintly burning wick he will not quench; he will not grow faint until justice is established, and the coastlands wait for his law. God the LORD, who created the heavens and gives breath to the people, calls this servant in righteousness—a covenant for the people and a light for the nations—to open blind eyes and bring prisoners from darkness. His name is the LORD; his glory he will not give to idols; former things have come to pass, and he declares new things before they spring forth.

Isaiah 42:1

1Behold my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit upon him; he will bring forth justice to the nations.

Arminian

Behold my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit upon him; he will bring forth justice to the nations. Wesley: having given one eminent instance of certain foreknowledge in the deliverance by Cyrus, now a more eminent example by foretelling the Messiah. Expressly interpreted of Christ, Matthew 12:18. To him, and to him only, all the particulars following truly belong. Whom—enable to do and suffer all belonging to his office. Elect—chosen by me to this great work. Delighteth—both for himself and for all his people, being fully satisfied with that sacrifice which he shall offer up to me. Bring forth—publish or shew. Judgment—the law, will, and counsel of God concerning man’s salvation. Gentiles—not only to the Jews, but to the Heathen nations.

Shared christology: the Servant is Christ, and him only. Historic Isaiah: Cyrus in chapter 41 was the prior instance of foreknowledge; this song is the Messiah, not a living politician. Arminian hearing: the Father is satisfied with that sacrifice for the Servant and for all his people, and the justice is published to Jew and Heathen alike. Do not invent a millennial scheme, and do not turn “all his people” into an atonement fight Calvin does not pick here.

Reformed

Behold my servant. Calvin: speaks of Christ as First-born and Head; to no other person could the following statements be applied; the Evangelists place it beyond controversy (Matthew 12:17–21). Servant by eminence: the name belongs especially to Christ because called to a singular incomparable work. The servanthood was a voluntary act (Philippians 2:6–9); human nature assumed to submit to obedience. Behold: look to Christ, not the present despair. Uphold him and put my Spirit on him: assist Christ in all things; necessary that Christ be endued with the Spirit to be Mediator. Elect: excellent; also undeserved love by which he embraced us all in his only-begotten Son. In whom my soul is well pleased: Christ is not only beloved by the Father (Matthew 3:17) but is alone beloved and accepted; no way of obtaining favor from God but through the intercession of Christ. He will exhibit judgment to the Gentiles: well-regulated government, not merely a bench sentence; not only Judea but the whole world. Without him everything disordered. The kingdom is inner: good conscience, newness of life (Romans 6:4).

Reformed hearing: look to Christ, not to despair. Shared with Wesley: the Servant is Christ only; Matthew 12 settles it. The Spirit is for the Mediator’s work; justice to the nations is the gospel’s government, not a later millennial chart. Skip a fork on delight: Wesley’s satisfied sacrifice and Calvin’s alone-beloved intercession both lead with the same Christ.

Isaiah 42:2

2He will not cry aloud or lift up his voice, or make it heard in the street;

Arminian

He will not cry aloud or lift up his voice, or make it heard in the street. Wesley: cry—in a way of contention or ostentation. Heard—as contentious and vain-glorious persons frequently do.

Arminian hearing: the Messiah’s manner is gentleness, not street-noise. Shared: no pomp, no contention. Historic Christ, not a newspaper campaign. The next verse will show what that quietness does for the bruised.

Reformed

Shall not cry. Calvin: without pomp of earthly kings. Not an earthly conception of him. He frequently forbade publishing miracles (Matthew 8:4). Quiet, no great noise.

Reformed hearing: do not measure the Servant by palace kings. Shared with Wesley: he will not cry in ostentation. The meekness is real; it is not emptiness. Match the hub: the Spirit-endowed Servant of v. 1 works without the street’s vanity.

Isaiah 42:3

3a bruised reed he will not break, and a faintly burning wick he will not quench; he will faithfully bring forth justice.

Arminian

A bruised reed he will not break, and a faintly burning wick he will not quench; he will faithfully bring forth justice. Wesley: Christ will not deal rigorously with those that come to him, but use all gentleness, cherishing the smallest beginnings of grace, comforting and healing wounded consciences. Quench—that wick almost extinct, he will not quench but revive and kindle it again. Judgment—the law of God or doctrine of the gospel, truly and faithfully.

Arminian hearing: smallest beginnings of grace are cherished, not crushed. Shared: bruised reed kept, smoking flax trimmed. The justice is still brought forth faithfully; gentleness is not the gospel’s cancellation. Those who come to him meet a healer of wounded consciences.

Reformed

Bruised reed. Calvin: supports the weak; does not crush those half-broken. Smoking flax: does not extinguish but trims. Forbearance; the dove at baptism a token of mildness. Ministers should be meek. But he shall bring forth judgment in truth: meekness is not flattery of vices. Forbearance for the submissive, not the obstinate; an iron rod for despisers (Psalm 2:9).

Reformed hearing: mildness has a limit—truth still comes forth. Shared with Wesley: the weak are not broken. Do not invent a millennial iron-rod as the Arminian/Reformed fork; Calvin’s rod is the same Christ who will not quench the faintly burning. The meekness is for the half-broken; despisers are another case.

Isaiah 42:4

4He will not grow faint or be discouraged till he has established justice in the earth; and the coastlands wait for his law.

Arminian

He will not grow faint or be discouraged till he has established justice in the earth; and the coastlands wait for his law. Wesley: till he has established his law or doctrine among the nations. Isles—countries remote from Judea shall gladly receive his doctrine.

Arminian hearing: remote countries receive the doctrine gladly; this is gospel reach, not a later age-scheme. Shared: he will not faint till justice is established. Historic Isaiah: coastlands wait for his law—the Messiah’s teaching, not a newspaper map of isles.

Reformed

Shall not faint. Calvin: mild yet no softness; the ministry efficacious till he establish judgment. Extends to the whole course of the gospel, not three years only. Isles wait for his law: law is doctrine; Christ appointed also for the Gentiles. Wait: the elect eagerly embrace the gospel (John 10:16).

Reformed hearing: the work runs the whole gospel course, not the years of the flesh only. Shared with Wesley: isles receive his doctrine. Do not turn Gentiles and coastlands into a millennial fight. Calvin’s “elect eagerly embrace” is how the waiting is fulfilled, not a cue to deny that the law is for the nations.

Isaiah 42:5

5Thus says God, the LORD, who created the heavens and stretched them out, who spread out the earth and what comes from it, who gives breath to the people on it and spirit to those who walk in it:

Arminian

Thus says God, the LORD, who created the heavens and stretched them out, who spread out the earth and what comes from it, who gives breath to the people on it and spirit to those who walk in it. Wesley: description of infinite power seasonably added to give assurance of certain accomplishment of his promises.

Arminian hearing: omnipotence is why the Servant-promises will stand. Do not invent open theism: the God who stretched the heavens is not waiting to discover whether he can keep the Servant. Shared: Creator-speech grounds the calling of vv. 6–7.

Reformed

Thus saith God the LORD. Calvin: a magnificent description of power so faith is confirmed when the outward aspect is contrary. Created heavens, spread earth, gives breath—he will easily perform what he promises concerning the reign of Christ.

Reformed hearing: contrary appearances do not cancel the promise; the Creator will do it. Shared with Wesley: infinite power, certain accomplishment. Match the hub: God rules; the idols of v. 8 cannot stretch the heavens.

Isaiah 42:6

6“I am the LORD; I have called you in righteousness; I will take you by the hand and keep you; I will give you as a covenant for the people, a light for the nations,

Arminian

“I am the LORD; I have called you in righteousness; I will take you by the hand and keep you; I will give you as a covenant for the people, a light for the nations.” Wesley: called thee—to declare my righteousness or faithfulness. With hold—will give thee counsel and strength for the work. Give thee—to be the mediator in whom my covenant of grace is confirmed with mankind. The people—of all people, not only Jews but Gentiles. A light—to enlighten them with true and saving knowledge.

Arminian hearing: the Servant is mediator of the covenant of grace with mankind, a light to enlighten with saving knowledge. Shared christology still: this “you” is Christ. Do not invent unlimited versus limited atonement as the fork—Wesley’s “all people, Jews and Gentiles” is the same wideness Calvin names without exception on this verse. Historic Messiah, not a later dispensation.

Reformed

Called thee in righteousness. Calvin: a lawful, firm calling; or God seeks reason from himself. Hold thy hand: immediate assistance. Covenant of the people: Jews first rank, then Gentiles; the covenant with Abraham had its foundation in Christ (Genesis 22:18); ratified in the seed, that is Christ. Light of the Gentiles: Gentiles plunged in darkness; Christ appointed a light to them. He calls all men to himself without a single exception, and gives Christ to all, that we may be illuminated by him. Let us only open our eyes.

Reformed hearing: the calling is firm; the covenant is in Christ the seed; the light is for Gentiles in darkness. Shared with Wesley: mediator, covenant, light for the nations. Calvin’s “without a single exception” overlaps Wesley’s “all people, not only Jews but Gentiles.” Skip a fork here: both refuse to make the light a Jewish monopoly. “Let us only open our eyes” is the hearer’s duty, not a claim that the gift was never given.

Isaiah 42:7

7to open the eyes that are blind, to bring out the prisoners from the dungeon, from the prison those who sit in darkness.

Arminian

To open the eyes that are blind, to bring out the prisoners from the dungeon, from the prison those who sit in darkness. Wesley has no separate note on this verse; his v. 6 still governs: the Servant is given as mediator of the covenant of grace with mankind, a light to enlighten them with true and saving knowledge. Blind eyes opened and prisoners brought out are that same light doing its work.

Arminian hearing: the light of v. 6 is not a slogan; it opens and leads out. Shared: blindness and dungeon name the condition the Messiah meets. Do not invent a unique Wesley sentence here. Historic redemption by Christ, not a newspaper jailbreak.

Reformed

Open blind eyes, bring prisoners out. Calvin: the condition of men till Christ shines—wretched, empty, surrounded by distresses. He addresses Christ for our sake so we know why the Father sent him.

Reformed hearing: this is why the Father sent the Servant—our wretchedness until he shines. Shared with Wesley’s light of v. 6: saving knowledge, eyes opened. The dungeon is the dark we sit in without Christ, not a later millennial prison.

Isaiah 42:8

8I am the LORD; that is my name; my glory I give to no other, nor my praise to carved idols.

Arminian

I am the LORD; that is my name; my glory I give to no other, nor my praise to carved idols. Wesley: The Lord—Hebrew Jehovah: who have all being in and of myself, and give being to all my creatures. The everlasting, unchangeable, and omnipotent God, who therefore both can and will fulfil all my promises.

Arminian hearing: Jehovah’s name is self-existence and omnipotence; that is why the Servant-promises stand. Shared: glory not to idols. Do not invent open theism: the unchangeable God can and will fulfil what he has said. Match v. 5’s stretched heavens: the same LORD, not a carved rival.

Reformed

I am Jehovah: this is my name, cannot lawfully be given to any other. Calvin: I will not give my glory to another: will not suffer glory to be diminished by being false in promises. The glory of God is chiefly visible in fulfillment of what he promised. Nor my praise to graven images: will not permit wicked men to triumph by oppressing the Church. Exceedingly jealous of his glory (Exodus 20:5).

Reformed hearing: glory kept means promises kept; idols get none of it. Shared with Wesley: Jehovah’s name, omnipotence, no praise to carved gods. Match the hub: God rules; fulfillment is how his glory is seen. Historic idols, not a culture-war on art.

Isaiah 42:9

9Behold, the former things have come to pass, and new things I now declare; before they spring forth I tell you of them.”

Arminian

Behold, the former things have come to pass, and new things I now declare; before they spring forth I tell you of them. Wesley: I tell you—that when they come to pass, you may know that I am God, and that this is my work.

Arminian hearing: prediction then event, so you may know he is God. Do not invent open theism: Wesley’s God tells before they spring, and when they come to pass you know the work is his—not a deity learning the future with us. Shared: former things already happened (Cyrus as the prior instance); new things now declared (the Messiah). Historic Isaiah, not a living politician.

Reformed

Former things came. Calvin: recall former predictions whose fulfillment grounds confidence for the future. New things I declare before they spring forth: this mark distinguishes God from idols—He alone knows and predicts future events. All God has foretold is verified by the event; he rules all things by providence.

Reformed hearing: fulfilled word is why you trust the next word; providence rules. Shared with Wesley: declared before they spring; when they come to pass you know he is God. No fork: both camps refuse idols that cannot tell the future. The Servant-song is among the new things now named.
