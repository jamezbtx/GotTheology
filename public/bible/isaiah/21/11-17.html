Isaiah 21

Verses 11–17 · ESV · Voices

The oracle concerning Dumah. One is calling from Seir, Watchman, what time of the night? Morning comes, and also the night; if you will inquire, inquire; come back again. The oracle concerning Arabia: in the thickets you will lodge, O caravans of Dedanites. To the thirsty bring water; meet the fugitive with bread, O inhabitants of Tema. They have fled from the swords. Within a year, according to the years of a hired worker, all the glory of Kedar will come to an end; the remainder of the archers of Kedar will be few, for the LORD, the God of Israel, has spoken. The unit stays with historic Dumah, Seir, Arabia, Tema, and Kedar in the Assyria age—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper of later states, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 21:11

11The oracle concerning Dumah. One is calling to me from Seir, “Watchman, what time of the night? Watchman, what time of the night?”

Arminian

Wesley: Dumah—of Edom or Idumea. He—the people of Dumah, one of them in the name and by the appointment of the rest. Me—to the watchman: the prophet delivers his prophecy in the form of a dialogue. Seir—out of Edom, which is frequently called Seir. Watchman—the watchman of Edom, whom they had set as people use to do in times of great danger. Night—they come very early to inquire what had happened in the night; which shews a state of great perplexity and fear. The repetition shews the greatness of their solicitude.

Arminian pastoral: Dumah and Seir are historic Edom, not a twenty-first-century night-watch. Do not turn “what time of the night” into a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: the burden of Dumah. It is evident from Genesis 25:14 that this nation was descended from a son of Ishmael. The prophecy is obscure on account of its brevity; yet the Jews needed to be fortified when so many changes took place, that they might not think all things happen by chance. Out of Seir: Mount Seir was a mountain of the Edomites; under this name he includes the whole kingdom. Watchman, what of the night? The Edomites who put the question were solicitous about the danger.

Reformed pastoral: the doubled question is still Seir’s fear. Shared: watchman, what time of the night? Historic Dumah, not a modern state.

Key contrast Both hear Dumah’s call from Seir to a watchman—historic night-fear, not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s Dumah as Edom or Idumea, a dialogue with Edom’s own watchman. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: Dumah as Ishmael’s line from Genesis 25:14, Seir standing for Edom’s kingdom, the brief oracle given so Judah would not think the shaking of the nations was chance.

Isaiah 21:12

12The watchman says: “Morning comes, and also the night. If you will inquire, inquire; come back again.”

Arminian

Wesley: The night—the night is past without any mischief, and the light of the morning is approaching; but tho’ the morning is coming, it will be gone, and the night will return, and your fears with it. Come—if you will inquire, inquire: I perceive your danger is not past, and there will be occasion for farther enquiries. Therefore return, come—come to me the next morning, and so from morning to morning.

Arminian pastoral: morning does not cancel the next night. Historic Edom’s watch, not a millennial decoder. They might yet have inquired while inquiry was still offered.

Reformed

Calvin: the morning cometh, and also the night: if ye will enquire, enquire ye; return, come. The answer does not grant safety; a morning may come, and night with it. The call to enquire again sends them back to the word, not to a new habitation out of reach of danger. Safer to remain with the godly than to flee the Church through fear.

Reformed note: inquire again is still that watchman’s. Shared: morning, and also the night. Historic Seir, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 21:13

13The oracle concerning Arabia. In the thickets in Arabia you will lodge, O caravans of Dedanites.

Arminian

Wesley: Forest—not as you used to do, in the houses or tents of the Arabians: whereby he implies, that that populous country should be a wilderness. Companies—in those parts travelers then did, and still do, go together in companies. Dedanim—these were merchants, who used to trade with Tyre, and their way lay thro’ Arabia. Shared: Dedanite caravans lodging in thickets.

Arminian pastoral: the forest-lodge is historic Arabia’s flight, not a modern trade route. Do not preach Dedan as a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: the burden upon Arabia. He now passes on to the Arabians, and foretells that they too, in their own turn, will be dragged to the judgment-seat of God, so that he does not leave unnoticed any of the nations known to the Jews. They will be seized with such fear that they will leave their houses and flee into the woods; he states the direction of the flight, to Dedanim.

Reformed pastoral: thickets replace tents because the Lord has summoned Arabia. Shared: caravans of Dedanites. Historic merchants, not a millennial map.

Isaiah 21:14

14To the thirsty bring water; meet the fugitive with bread, O inhabitants of the land of Tema.

Arminian

Wesley: Tema—a part of Arabia. Fled—whereby he implies, that those other Arabians, against whom this prophecy is principally directed, should be reduced to great scarcity, and forced to flee for their lives, from a bloody enemy. Shared: water for the thirsty, bread for the fugitive.

Arminian pastoral: Tema’s bread is a real mercy to historic fugitives, not a culture-war speech. Do not aim the verse at a twenty-first-century column.

Reformed

Calvin: to meet the thirsty bring waters. He heightens the trembling: they will not take time even to collect what is necessary for the journey. The Arabians will come into the country of Dedanim empty; he exhorts the inhabitants of Tema to go out and meet them with bread and water, because otherwise they will faint. Some think he mocks at cruel Arabians; that exposition is too constrained. He states the fact more clearly: extreme want.

Reformed note: Tema’s meeting is still that flight’s. Shared: water and bread. Historic Arabia, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 21:15

15For they have fled from the swords, from the drawn sword, from the bent bow, and from the press of battle.

Arminian

Wesley has no separate lemma here; his fled of v. 14 still holds: those Arabians against whom this prophecy is principally directed should be reduced to great scarcity, and forced to flee for their lives, from a bloody enemy. Shared: swords, bow, and battle press them out.

Arminian pastoral: the drawn sword is that age’s, not a later war. Historic flight of Arabia, not a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: for they fled from the swords, from the drawn sword, and from the bent bow, and from the grievousness of war. The reason of the thirst and the thickets is war. They do not roam for trade; they run from the press of battle that the Lord has appointed.

Reformed pastoral: the bent bow is still that judgment’s. Shared: fled from swords. Historic Arabia, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 21:16

16For thus the LORD said to me, “Within a year, according to the years of a hired worker, all the glory of Kedar will come to an end.

Arminian

Wesley: A year—from the time of this prophecy: an exact year. Glory—their power, and riches, and all things wherein they used to glory. This was executed by the Assyrians. Hireling—as in the Moab oracle, hirelings are punctual in observing the time for which they are hired. Shared: a counted year, then Kedar’s glory ends.

Arminian pastoral: the hireling’s year is that age’s clock, not a twenty-first-century date scheme. Historic Kedar, Ishmael’s tents, not a modern people.

Reformed

Calvin: within a year, according to the years of an hireling, all the glory of Kedar shall fail. The time is fixed so that believers may not faint, as with Moab’s three years. The hireling counts exactly; so will this year run. Kedar’s glory—power, wealth, name—fails because the Lord has said it.

Reformed note: the hireling’s clock is the Lord’s. Shared: within a year, Kedar’s glory ended. Historic Arabia, not a newspaper decoder.

Isaiah 21:17

17And the remainder of the archers of the mighty men of the sons of Kedar will be few, for the LORD, the God of Israel, has spoken.”

Arminian

Wesley has no separate lemma here; his glory of v. 16 still names the stroke: power and riches wherein they used to glory, executed by the Assyrians. The remainder few is that glory’s end. Shared: the LORD, the God of Israel, has spoken.

Arminian close: Kedar’s archers made few is still that hireling’s year. Historic sons of Kedar, not a later tribe on a map. The God of Israel has spoken; do not stretch the remainder into a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: and the residue of the number of archers, the mighty men of the children of Kedar, shall be diminished: for the Lord God of Israel hath spoken it. What remains of their strength will be a remnant unlike their former glory. The seal is the speaker: the God of Israel, not Isaiah’s private guess.

Reformed close: the few archers are still Kedar’s. Shared: the LORD has spoken. Historic Arabia in the Assyria age—already enough without a later headline.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Dumah’s watchman from Seir, morning and also the night, Dedanite caravans in the thickets, Tema’s water and bread, and Kedar’s glory ended in a hireling’s year—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 21:11–17.

Arminian Notable voices

“Dumah—Of Edom or Idumea. He—The people of Dumah, one of them in the name and by the appointment of the rest. Me—To the watchman: the prophet delivers his prophecy in the form of a dialogue between the people and the watchman. Seir—Out of Edom, which is frequently called Seir. Watchman—The watchman of Edom, whom they had set as people use to do in times of great danger. Night—The people are supposed to come to him very early in the morning, to inquire what had happened in the night; which shews a state of great perplexity and fear. The night—The night is past without any mischief, and the light of the morning is approaching; but tho’ the morning is coming, it will be gone, and the night will return, and your fears with it. Come—If you will inquire, inquire: I perceive your danger is not past, and there will be occasion for farther enquiries. Forest—Not as you used to do, in the houses or tents of the Arabians: whereby he implies, that that populous country should be a wilderness. Companies—Travelers then did, and still do, go together in companies. Dedanim—These were merchants, who used to trade with Tyre, and their way lay thro’ Arabia. Tema—A part of Arabia. Fled—Those other Arabians, against whom this prophecy is principally directed, should be reduced to great scarcity, and forced to flee for their lives, from a bloody enemy. A year—From the time of this prophecy: an exact year. Glory—Their power, and riches, and all things wherein they used to glory. This was executed by the Assyrians.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 21:11–17)

Reformed Notable voices

“The burden of Dumah. It is evident from Genesis 25:14 that this nation was descended from a son of Ishmael. The cause of their destruction cannot be known with certainty, and this prophecy is obscure on account of its brevity. Yet it was proper that the Jews should be fortified against the dreadful stumbling-blocks which were approaching. Out of Seir: Mount Seir was a mountain of the Edomites; under the name of this mountain he includes the whole kingdom. Watchman, what of the night? It is probable that the Edomites, who put the question, were not at a great distance, and that they were solicitous about the danger. The morning cometh, and also the night: if ye will enquire, enquire ye; return, come. The burden upon Arabia. He now passes on to the Arabians, and foretells that they too will be dragged to the judgment-seat of God. They will be seized with such fear that they will leave their houses and flee into the woods, to Dedanim. To meet the thirsty bring waters: the Arabians will come empty and destitute; he exhorts the inhabitants of Tema to go out and meet them with bread and water. Within a year, according to the years of an hireling, all the glory of Kedar shall fail; the residue of the archers of the children of Kedar shall be diminished: for the Lord God of Israel hath spoken it.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 21:11–17)
