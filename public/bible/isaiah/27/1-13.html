Isaiah 27

Verses 1–13 · ESV · Voices

In that day the LORD with his hard and great and strong sword will punish Leviathan the fleeing serpent, Leviathan the twisting serpent, and he will slay the dragon that is in the sea. In that day, a pleasant vineyard, sing of it! I, the LORD, am its keeper; every moment I water it; I keep it night and day. I have no wrath. Would that I had thorns and briers to battle! Or let them lay hold of my protection, let them make peace with me. In days to come Jacob shall take root, Israel shall blossom and fill the whole world with fruit. Has he struck them as he struck those who struck them? Measure by measure, by exile you contended; he removed them with his fierce breath in the day of the east wind. By this the guilt of Jacob will be atoned for, when he makes altar-stones like chalkstones; no Asherim remain. The fortified city is solitary; the calf grazes; boughs broken; a people without discernment. In that day from the Euphrates to the Brook of Egypt the LORD will thresh; you will be gleaned one by one, O people of Israel. A great trumpet will be blown; those lost in Assyria and driven out to Egypt will come and worship the LORD on the holy mountain at Jerusalem. The unit stays with historic Israel, Assyria, and Egypt, the vineyard kept, Leviathan as the church’s enemy—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 27:1

1In that day the LORD with his hard and great and strong sword will punish Leviathan the fleeing serpent, Leviathan the twisting serpent, and he will slay the dragon that is in the sea.

Arminian

Wesley: Leviathan—by this leviathan, serpent and dragon (for all signify the same thing) he understands some powerful enemy or enemies of God, and of his church or people, which may well be called by these names, partly for their great might, and partly for the great terror and destruction which they cause upon the earth. The piercing—which by its sting pierces deeply into mens bodies. Crooked serpent—winding and turning itself with great variety and dexterity. Whereby he seems to signify the craftiness and activity of this enemy, whose strength makes it more formidable. Shared: the LORD’s sword on the serpent.

Arminian pastoral: historic enemy of the church in that age, not a newspaper dragon. Do not invent a millennial camp fight from Leviathan.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day. Here the Prophet speaks in general of the judgment of God, and thus includes the whole of Satan’s kingdom. Having spoken of vengeance against tyrants who shed innocent blood, he now publishes this proclamation. On leviathan: a large serpent, or whales and sea-fishes, monsters for their size. The description applies to the king of any proud empire that crushed the church, and to the kingdom of Satan standing behind such tyrants. The hard, great, and strong sword is the Lord’s.

Reformed pastoral: the slain dragon is still that enemy’s. Shared: fleeing and twisting serpent. Historic powers around Judah, and the church’s foe—not a millennial decoder.

Key contrast Both hear Leviathan as a powerful enemy of God and the church, slain by the LORD’s sword in that day—not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s piercing and crooked serpent as might and craft of some historic enemy of the people. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: the judgment includes the whole of Satan’s kingdom, sea-monsters figuring tyrants who shed innocent blood.

Isaiah 27:2

2In that day, “A pleasant vineyard, sing of it!

Arminian

Wesley: In that day—when this enemy shall be destroyed. A vineyard—my church and people, of red wine, of the choicest and best wine, which in those parts was red. Shared: sing of the vineyard when the serpent falls.

Arminian pastoral: historic church as vineyard, as in Isaiah 5, now kept. Not a later agribusiness headline.

Reformed

Calvin: sing to the vineyard of redness. He now shews that all this will promote the salvation of the Church. The vineyard of red wine is the choicest. The song answers 26:1: when Leviathan is punished, the vineyard is sung.

Reformed note: the pleasant vineyard is still the church. Shared: in that day, sing. Historic people, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 27:3

3I, the LORD, am its keeper; every moment I water it. Lest anyone punish it, I keep it night and day;

Arminian

Wesley: I keep it—I will protect my church from all her enemies, and supply her with all necessary provisions. Shared: keeper; water every moment; night and day.

Arminian pastoral: the keep is real and constant. Historic vineyard, not open theism: he already names the keeping. Do not invent a gap in care from “lest anyone punish it.”

Reformed

Calvin: I Jehovah keep it. Here the Lord asserts his care and diligence in dressing and guarding this vineyard. I will water it every moment: lest any hurt it, I will keep it night and day. The contrast with the neglected vineyard of chapter 5 is mercy, not a change in his nature.

Reformed pastoral: night-and-day keeping is still his. Shared: I water it every moment. Historic church, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 27:4

4I have no wrath. Would that I had thorns and briers to battle! I would march against them, I would burn them up together.

Arminian

Wesley has no separate lemma here; his or of v. 5 still holds: if at any time fury seem to be in me against my people, let him take hold. Shared: no wrath; thorns and briers would be burned.

Arminian pastoral: “I have no wrath” is toward the vineyard, not a denial that he judges Leviathan. Historic church, not a later slogan. Do not invent a limited God from no wrath.

Reformed

Calvin: fury is not in me. This verse contains excellent consolation; he expresses the incredible forbearance of God toward the Church. Who would set the briers and thorns against me in battle? I would go through them, I would burn them together. Thorns that attack the vineyard are not the vine; he would rather they took hold of peace in v. 5.

Reformed note: no fury is still toward his own. Shared: thorns burned. Historic vineyard, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 27:5

5Or let them lay hold of my protection, let them make peace with me, let them make peace with me.”

Arminian

Wesley: Or—or if at any time fury seem to be in me against my people. Let him—my people. Take hold—which he may by humble prayer not only restrain from doing him hurt, but engage to do him good. Shared: lay hold of my protection; make peace, make peace.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet take hold. Historic people invited to peace, not a culture-war treaty. Humble prayer is the hold.

Reformed

Calvin: will she take hold of my strength? The particle is frequently disjunctive: or let him take hold of my strength, that he may make peace with me; and he shall make peace with me. The doubled peace matches 26:3. Enemies or straying branches may yet seize protection instead of becoming fuel.

Reformed pastoral: doubled peace is still offered. Shared: lay hold; make peace. Historic invitation, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 27:6

6In days to come Jacob shall take root, Israel shall blossom and put forth shoots and fill the whole world with fruit.

Arminian

Wesley: Take root—to be firmly settled in their possessions. Fruit—their posterity shall seek habitations in other countries, and replenish them with people. But this seems to be understood of the spiritual seed of Jacob. Shared: Jacob rooted; Israel filling the world with fruit.

Arminian pastoral: spiritual seed, gospel fruit, not a newspaper migration. This is not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: afterwards shall Jacob put forth roots. He now gives actual proof of that love of the vineyard. Israel shall blossom and bud, and fill the face of the world with fruit. Roots first, then a world-filling fruit: the church’s increase, not a chart of later states.

Reformed note: the world’s fruit is still Jacob’s spiritual increase. Shared: take root, blossom, fill. Historic Israel, gospel reach.

Isaiah 27:7

7Has he struck them as he struck those who struck them? Or have they been slain as their slayers were slain?

Arminian

Wesley: Hath he—he hath not dealt so severely with his people, as he hath dealt with their enemies, whom he hath utterly destroyed. Of them—of those who were slain by God on the behalf of Israel. Shared: not struck as the strikers were struck.

Arminian pastoral: measure for the vine, destruction for Leviathan. Historic distinction, not a later casualty list.

Reformed

Calvin: hath he smitten him, as he smote those that smote him? He confirms the former statement, and shews that even in chastisements God makes a distinction. He does not deal with the Church as with enemies. Those who struck Israel are slain as enemies; Israel is struck as a son.

Reformed pastoral: the unequal stroke is still mercy. Shared: not as their slayers were slain. Historic people, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 27:8

8Measure by measure, by exile you contended with them; he removed them with his fierce breath in the day of the east wind.

Arminian

Wesley: In measure—with moderation. When—when the vine shooteth forth its luxuriant branches, he cuts them off, but so as not to destroy the vine. Contend—God is said to contend with men, when he executes his judgments upon them, Amos vii, 4. Stayeth—he mitigates the severity of the judgment. In the day—in the time when he sends forth his east-wind; which he mentions because that wind in those parts was most violent and most hurtful. Shared: measure; exile; east wind.

Arminian pastoral: historic exile under the east wind—Assyria’s breath—not a twenty-first-century deportation. The vine is cut, not rooted up.

Reformed

Calvin: in measure, when it shooteth forth, thou wilt debate with it: he stayeth his rough wind in the day of the east wind. This is the second proof of the divine compassion toward all the elect. He debates in measure; even the east wind is stayed so the vine is not destroyed. Exile is contention, not annihilation.

Reformed note: the east wind is still that day of removal. Shared: measure by measure. Historic captivity, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 27:9

9Therefore by this the guilt of Jacob will be atoned for, and this will be the full fruit of the removal of his sin: when he makes all the stones of the altars like chalkstones crushed to pieces, no Asherim or incense altars will remain standing.

Arminian

Wesley: By this—by this manner of God’s dealing with them. When—which sin of Jacob’s shall be purged, when he shall truly repent of all his sins, and especially of his idolatry. Altar—their idolatrous altars. Possibly he may say the altar, with respect to that particular altar, which Ahaz had set upon the place of God’s own altar; and this prophecy might be delivered in Ahaz’s time, while that altar stood. Chalk-stones—when he shall break all those goodly altars in pieces. Not stand—shall be thrown down with contempt. Shared: guilt atoned; altar-stones like chalk; Asherim gone.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet repent and smash Ahaz’s altar. Historic idolatry of Judah, not a culture-war shrine. Fruit of removed sin is smashed images, not a millennial decoder.

Reformed

Calvin: therefore in this manner shall the iniquity of Jacob be expiated; and this is all the fruit to take away his sin; when he maketh all the stones of the altar as chalkstones that are beaten in sunder, the groves and images shall not stand up. After speaking of measured strokes, he shews the fruit: idolatry crushed. The altar-stones become chalk; Asherim do not remain. Expiation here is the purging of that guilt by repentance under the rod, the same vineyard kept.

Reformed pastoral: chalkstones are still Jacob’s smashed altars. Shared: guilt atoned by this; no incense altars standing. Historic high places, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 27:10

10For the fortified city is solitary, a habitation deserted and forsaken, like the wilderness; there the calf grazes; there it lies down and strips its branches.

Arminian

Wesley: Yet—yet before this glorious promise be fulfilled, a dreadful and desolating judgment shall come. The city—Jerusalem and the rest of the defensed cities in the land. The habitation—the most inhabited and populous places. The calf—this is put for all sorts of cattle, which may securely feed there, because there shall be no men left to disturb them. Shared: fortified city solitary; calf grazes.

Arminian pastoral: desolation before the rooting of v. 6. Historic Jerusalem and fenced towns, not a later ruin postcard.

Reformed

Calvin: yet the defenced city shall be desolate, and the habitation forsaken, and left like a wilderness: there shall the calf feed, and there shall he lie down, and consume the branches thereof. Before the world-filling fruit, the fenced city lies empty. The calf in the streets is the same sign as Aroer’s flocks in 17:2: men gone, pasture left.

Reformed note: the solitary city is still that judgment’s. Shared: deserted habitation; calf strips branches. Historic fenced Judah, not a millennial map.

Isaiah 27:11

11When its boughs are dry, they are broken; women come and make a fire of them. For this is a people without discernment; therefore he who made them will not have compassion on them; he who formed them will show them no favor.

Arminian

Wesley: Broken—that there may be no hopes of their recovery. Women—he mentions women, because the men would be destroyed. Not understanding—they know not the things which concerns their peace, but they blindly and wilfully go on in sin. Therefore—thus he overthrows their conceit that God would never destroy the work of his own hands. Shared: dry boughs for fire; a people without discernment; maker without compassion on that obstinacy.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have understood. Historic people going on in sin, not a living nation. Formed-them is not a promise that the idolatrous city cannot fall.

Reformed

Calvin: when the boughs thereof are withered, they shall be broken off: the women come, and set them on fire: for it is a people of no understanding: therefore he that made them will not have mercy on them, and he that formed them will shew them no favour. Withered boughs feed the fire. No understanding: they know not their peace. He overthrows the conceit that the Maker will never destroy what he formed, when they refuse the peace of v. 5.

Reformed pastoral: no compassion here is still that obstinate city’s. Shared: without discernment; women burning boughs. Historic Judah’s waste, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 27:12

12In that day from the river Euphrates to the Brook of Egypt the LORD will thresh out the grain, and you will be gleaned one by one, O people of Israel.

Arminian

Wesley: Beat out—it is a metaphor from grain which was beaten out with a rod or staff, and then carefully gathered and laid up. From—from Euphrates to the Nile, which were the two borders of the land of promise. All the Israelites who are left in the land. One by one—which signifies, God’s exact care of them. Shared: thresh from the River to Egypt; gleaned one by one.

Arminian pastoral: exact care of historic Israel between those two streams, not a twenty-first-century border. The rod that beats also gathers.

Reformed

Calvin: and it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall beat off from the channel of the river unto the stream of Egypt, and ye shall be gathered one by one, O ye children of Israel. He confirms the former statement of God’s mercy. The metaphor is grain beaten out and carefully gathered. From the Euphrates to the Nile: the old bounds. One by one: so great will be the care that not one of the remnant is missed.

Reformed note: one-by-one gleaning is still that care’s. Shared: thresh; people of Israel. Historic borders, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 27:13

13And in that day a great trumpet will be blown, and those who were lost in the land of Assyria and those who were driven out to the land of Egypt will come and worship the LORD on the holy mountain at Jerusalem.

Arminian

Wesley: Trumpet—God shall summon them altogether by sound of trumpet, by an eminent call of his providence. He alludes to the custom of calling the Israelites together with trumpets. Shared: a great trumpet; lost in Assyria, driven to Egypt, worship on the holy mountain.

Arminian close: historic scatter to Assyria and Egypt, then worship at Jerusalem. Gospel call, not a newspaper of later states. Do not invent a millennial camp fight from the trumpet. Wesley and Calvin share the gathering to Zion’s mountain.

Reformed

Calvin: and it shall come to pass in that day, that the great trumpet shall be blown, and they shall come which were ready to perish in the land of Assyria, and the outcasts in the land of Egypt, and shall worship the Lord in the holy mount at Jerusalem. This is the explanation of the former verse. He speaks metaphorically of a trumpet, as soldiers are gathered; so great will be the power of God that he will gather his people. Lost in Assyria, driven to Egypt: the same two lands that framed the highway of 19:23 now give back worshipers to Zion.

Reformed close: the trumpet is still that providence’s. Shared: Assyria and Egypt; holy mountain at Jerusalem. Historic exiles, historic Zion—already enough without a later decoder. The vineyard is kept; Leviathan does not get the last word.

Reformed Notable voices

“In that day. Here the Prophet speaks in general of the judgment of God, and thus includes the whole of Satan’s kingdom.
