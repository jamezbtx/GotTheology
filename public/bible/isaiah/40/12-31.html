Passage

Isaiah 40

Verses 12–31 · ESV · Voices

After comfort and the shepherd, Isaiah asks who has measured waters and heavens, who taught the LORD, and to whom God may be likened. Nations are a drop; idols are wood that will not move; He sits above the circle of the earth, calls the starry host by name, and answers Jacob’s complaint: the everlasting God does not faint. Youths fall; those who wait for the LORD renew their strength.

Isaiah 40:12

12Who has measured the waters in the hollow of his hand and marked off the heavens with a span, enclosed the dust of the earth in a measure and weighed the mountains in scales and the hills in a balance?

Arminian

Who has measured waters in the hollow of a hand, marked heavens with a span, weighed mountains? Wesley: who can do this but God? The discourse of infinite power and wisdom is added to give greater assurance that God was able to do the wonderful things He had promised.

Omnipotence serves the comfort of 40:1–11. Do not invent open theism: the God who measures the sea is not waiting to discover whether He can keep a word. Shared: only God does this.

Reformed

Who hath measured? Calvin: he proclaims power so that the promises may be believed; Abraham did not hesitate because he believed God who promised was able to perform. Restore a people beyond belief requires minds raised above the world, not limited to human means.

Reformed: if this conviction were seated in the heart we would not be so alarmed. Shared with Wesley: the measuring God can do what He said. Sovereignty here is why the highway and the shepherd are not wishes.

Isaiah 40:13

13Who has measured the Spirit of the LORD, or what man shows him his counsel?

Arminian

Who has measured the Spirit of the LORD, or what man shows Him counsel? Wesley: who did God either need or take to advise Him in any of His works, creation or government.

God rules; He is not an idol that needs a committee. Shared: no counselor. Romans 11:34 will quote this. Classical Arminians still confess an unsearchable God; they do not smuggle in a deity who learns from us what to do next.

Reformed

Who instructed the Spirit of Jehovah? Calvin: God had no need of a teacher to inform Him about things unknown. Spirit here denotes reason and judgment; he accommodates to our ignorance. Paul quotes this to deter us from judging the unsearchable counsel (Rom 11:34).

Reformed: nothing more foolish than man lifting himself to examine God’s works by his own ability. Shared: no counselor. Match the sovereignty hub: God is not advised; idols are.

Key contrast Arminian readings still confess that no man shows the LORD His counsel (Wesley: God neither needed nor took advice in creation or government). Reformed readings, with Calvin and Paul’s use in Romans 11:34, press the same verse against any mind that would sit as counselor: our understanding is darkness until God speaks. Neither camp reads open theism here.

Isaiah 40:14

14Whom did he consult, and who made him understand? Who taught him the path of justice, and taught him knowledge, and showed him the way of understanding?

Arminian

Whom did He consult? Who taught Him justice, knowledge, understanding? The questions pile so that no creature gets a seat at the table.

Shared: the path of justice is His before anyone showed it to Him. Comfort for captives is that their God does not rent wisdom. No open theism: He is not filling gaps in His understanding from Jacob’s advice.

Reformed

From whom took He counsel? Calvin: nothing more foolish than man venturing into heaven to judge God’s works. Our reason is darkness till enlightened by Christ. All senses captive to obedience.

Reformed match to 40:13 and Rom 11:34. Shared: He was not taught. The nations of v. 15 will look even smaller once this is granted.

Isaiah 40:15

15Behold, the nations are like a drop from a bucket, and are accounted as the dust on the scales; behold, he takes up the coastlands like fine dust.

Arminian

Nations like a drop from a bucket, dust on the scales; He takes up the coastlands like fine dust. Wesley: counted by Him and in comparison of Him. Dust which accidentally cleaves to the balance but makes no alteration in the weight. Isles: those vast countries reached from Judea by sea.

Sovereignty: empires do not tilt God’s scales. Shared: drop, dust, fine dust. This is why Babylon cannot cancel 40:1.

Reformed

Nations as a drop of a bucket, small dust of the balance; He takes up the isles as a very little thing. Calvin: we must not imagine that everything Satan does blocks God’s path. All nations and peoples are nothing compared with God; by breathing He scatters them like small dust.

Reformed: nothing more contrary to reason than to exalt creatures to diminish God’s power. Shared with Wesley: drop and dust. The measuring God of v. 12 now weighs peoples and finds them light.

Isaiah 40:16

16Lebanon would not suffice for fuel, nor are its beasts enough for a burnt offering.

Arminian

Lebanon would not suffice for fuel, nor its beasts for a burnt offering. Wesley: if men were to offer a sacrifice agreeable to His infinite excellency, the whole forest could not afford beasts enough or wood enough.

Worship cannot buy this God. Shared: even the best fuel is too small. He is not an idol you can afford or out-offer.

Reformed

Lebanon would not be sufficient. Calvin: if we must sacrifice according to what He deserves, neither the whole of Lebanon nor the beasts that graze upon it would suffice.

Reformed: he leads readers to deeper reverence; some infer no man entitles himself to God’s favor by sacrifices—true, though the design is confidence that all things are nothing beside Him. Shared: the offering-God of v. 16 is the same as the no-idol God of vv. 18–20.

Isaiah 40:17

17All the nations are as nothing before him, they are accounted by him as less than nothing and emptiness.

Arminian

All the nations are as nothing before Him, less than nothing and emptiness. After drops and Lebanon, the verdict is emptiness.

Shared: not that nations are unreal, but that before Him they cannot found hope or fear as rivals. Match the hub: God rules. Jacob’s complaint in v. 27 will have to answer this scale.

Reformed

All nations as nothing, less than nothing and vanity. Calvin: this is said by comparison; depravity obscures divine majesty and places above it what ought to be subject. We cannot subsist but in God.

Reformed: David’s lighter-than-vanity (Ps 62:9) is the same music. Shared: emptiness before Him. The point is not contempt of people but the end of idol-scale politics.

Isaiah 40:18

18To whom then will you liken God, or what likeness compare with him?

Arminian

To whom then will you liken God, or what likeness compare with Him? Wesley: a proper inference from infinite greatness; from which he shows the folly of mean visible representations of God, and the utter inability of men or idols to oppose Him.

God is not an idol. Shared: the question is the hub. Do not invent an Arminian likeness that makes God a larger man who waits on us. The incomparable One is why comfort stands.

Reformed

To whom then have ye likened God? Calvin: a grievous injury if believers, discouraged by adversity, turn aside to the idols of the Gentiles. He continues to be like Himself; He does not change His nature on account of inventions of men. Impossible to frame from dead matter an image resembling His glory.

Reformed: Paul uses this against idolaters (Acts 17:29). Shared with Wesley: no likeness. The living God of the word, not a figure, is Judah’s shield.

Isaiah 40:19

19An idol! A craftsman casts it, and a goldsmith overlays it with gold and casts for it silver chains.

Arminian

An idol! A craftsman casts it; a goldsmith overlays it with gold and silver chains. Wesley: he melts metal into a mould, afterwards graven to make it more exact.

The parody is sharp: the nations of v. 15 fund a statue that must be chained so it sits. Shared: God measured the heavens; this god is merchandise. Not a culture-war on art; Isaiah’s target is a god you make and then ask to move.

Reformed

The carver prepares a graven image. Calvin: public opinion has great force; he fortifies believers against the error that whatever pleases the multitude passes for law. He attacks the madness of the world laboring to make idols.

Reformed: rich and poor alike (v. 20). Shared: the craftsman is the real agent; the idol does nothing. Match 40:18: liken God to this, and you have already lost the measuring LORD.

Isaiah 40:20

20He who is too impoverished for an offering chooses wood that will not rot; he seeks out a skillful craftsman to set up an idol that will not move.

Arminian

The impoverished chooses wood that will not rot, seeks a skillful craftsman, sets up an idol that will not move. Wesley: he that can hardly procure money for a sacrifice is so mad upon idols that he will find money for the choicest materials—and after all this cost it cannot stir one step to help.

Will not move is the punchline. Shared: God comes with might (v. 10); the idol must be pegged so it does not fall. Omnipotence versus furniture.

Reformed

The poor chooseth wood that will not rot. Calvin: no class is free from the crime; the poor select wood. All carried away by strange madness. Men wish to enjoy God’s presence; this is the beginning of idolatry. God is present by His word and Spirit, not by an idol.

Reformed: sacraments lead upward, not into a statue. Shared with Wesley: it will not move. The sovereignty hub is this contrast: the Holy One of v. 25 versus a well-made stick.

Isaiah 40:21

21Do you not know? Do you not hear? Has it not been told you from the beginning? Have you not understood from the foundations of the earth?

Arminian

Do you not know? Hear? Told from the beginning? Understood from the foundations of the earth? Wesley: known—God to be the only true God, maker and governor of the world.

The questions assume they have already been taught. Shared: creation and old testimony leave them without the excuse that no one mentioned the incomparable God.

Reformed

Do ye not know? Calvin: two methods—hearing of the Word, and the theatre of the world. Addressed to Jews taught by the Law, doubly inexcusable if they imitate Babylon. From the beginning: not a new God, but the God of Abraham and Moses.

Reformed: the world is a mirror of invisible things (Rom 1). Shared: they have heard. Unbelief here is not innocent ignorance of whether God can measure seas.

Isaiah 40:22

22It is he who sits above the circle of the earth, and its inhabitants are like grasshoppers; who stretches out the heavens like a curtain, and spreads them like a tent to dwell in;

Arminian

He sits above the circle of the earth; inhabitants like grasshoppers; stretches heavens like a curtain, a tent to dwell in. Wesley: far above this round earth, in the highest heavens; men appear like grasshoppers. Circle of earth, as elsewhere circle of heaven and of the deep, because the form is circular. Spreadeth for the benefit of earth and mankind.

Shared: He sits; we hop. The tent is for habitation under His sky, not a roof He needs.

Reformed

He sitteth upon the circle of the earth; inhabitants as locusts; heavens as a curtain, a tent. Calvin: not a moment’s creation then abandoned; He governs continually. Sitting denotes government. Earth remains only as upheld by His power. The heavens cover us as our habitation.

Reformed: as easy for God to spread heaven as for a man to spread a curtain. Shared: grasshopper-scale humans. Match omnipotence: He sits; princes (v. 23) will not.

Isaiah 40:23

23who brings princes to nothing, and makes the rulers of the earth as emptiness.

Arminian

He brings princes to nothing, makes rulers of the earth as emptiness. The drop-nations of v. 15 now include their kings.

Shared: God rules. Babylon’s court is emptiness before Him, which is why the comfort of 40:1 can outlast an empire. No extra fight.

Reformed

He bringeth the mighty to nothing; governors of the earth as if they were not. Calvin: God’s government extends; even high rank is not exempt. If the hand of God is so powerful against nobles, will He not drive the crowd wherever He thinks fit?

Reformed: tokens of immediate power, to arouse us. Shared with Wesley’s scale: princes as vanity. Sovereignty is not abstract; it unmakes rulers.

Isaiah 40:24

24Scarcely are they planted, scarcely sown, scarcely has their stem taken root in the earth, when he blows on them, and they wither, and the tempest carries them off like stubble.

Arminian

Scarcely planted, sown, rooted—He blows, they wither, tempest takes them as stubble. Wesley: they shall take no root; they shall not continue and flourish as they imagined, but be rooted up and perish.

The breath of v. 7 now hits princes. Shared: planted is not the same as established. God blows.

Reformed

As if they had not been planted or sown. Calvin: princes totally destroyed, no trace left. He bloweth on them: kings and princes like stubble before God, driven as by a whirlwind. Do not lean on earthly supports.

Reformed: God was not an instantaneous Creator who abandoned the work; nothing is done but by His will. Shared: blow, wither, stubble. Judah in captivity needs this more than a theory of weather.

Isaiah 40:25

25To whom then will you compare me, that I should be like him? says the Holy One.

Arminian

To whom then will you compare me, that I should be like him? says the Holy One. Wesley’s inference from v. 18 now in the first person, with the name Holy One.

Shared: the Holy One will not be ranked with the unmoving idol. Match the hub: God is not an idol. The question is closed by v. 26’s stars.

Reformed

To whom will ye liken me? saith the Holy One. Calvin: He will not suffer Himself to be likened to idols, that the Jews may not detract from His power because captives saw Gentile prosperity. Holy: they would be ungrateful if, set apart by Him, they do not sanctify Him in return. No holiness in the gods of the Gentiles.

Reformed: He is not compared; He is confessed. Shared: Holy One versus likeness. Adversity is not evidence that Bel beat Yahweh.

Isaiah 40:26

26Lift up your eyes on high and see: who created these? He who brings out their host by number, calling them all by name; by the greatness of his might and because he is strong in power, not one is missing.

Arminian

Lift up your eyes: who created these? He brings out their host by number, calls them all by name; by greatness of might not one is missing. Wesley: that at first brought them out of nothing, and from day to day brings them forth, to rise and set in appointed times. Faileth—either to appear when He calls, or to do the work to which He sends them.

Named stars, none missing: eternity and omnipotence in the night sky. Shared: He calls; they come. This is not a God who counts and hopes they show up.

Reformed

Lift up your eyes on high; who created these? Bringing out by number their army; He will call them all by name; by greatness of strength not one wanting. Calvin: stars preach one God; regular order is not by chance. Army: almost infinite number and admirable arrangement. He always has His soldiers in readiness, by number; not one absent. Call by name: utmost submission, as Cyrus will later be called.

Reformed: nothing in heaven or earth that does not depend on His pleasure. Shared with Wesley: named, none missing. Shameful to compare Him to idols.

Isaiah 40:27

27Why do you say, O Jacob, and speak, O Israel, “My way is hidden from the LORD, and my right is disregarded by my God”?

Arminian

Why say, O Jacob, O Israel: my way is hidden from the LORD, my right disregarded? Wesley: why give way to such jealousies concerning thy God, of whose power, wisdom, and goodness there are such demonstrations? The complaint is uttered in the name of the people prophetically supposed in captivity: He takes no notice of my prayers and tears.

The measuring God has not mislaid Jacob. Do not invent open theism: hidden from us is not hidden from Him. Shared: the complaint is wrong. Comfort is that the everlasting God of v. 28 has not passed over their cause.

Reformed

Why wilt thou say, O Jacob? My way is hidden from Jehovah, my judgment passed over. Calvin: they were almost overcome by despair and did not look to the promises. Hidden means disregarded; when God delays we think He has forsaken us. He argues from the less to the greater: since God keeps every part of the world under authority, He cannot forsake His Church.

Reformed: the names Jacob and Israel recall the covenant. Shared: the complaint is unbelief, not information God lacked. Match 40:1’s comfort: the God who measured waters has not lost their file.

Isaiah 40:28

28Have you not known? Have you not heard? The LORD is the everlasting God, the Creator of the ends of the earth. He does not faint or grow weary; his understanding is unsearchable.

Arminian

Have you not known? Heard? The LORD is the everlasting God, Creator of the ends of the earth. He does not faint or grow weary; His understanding is unsearchable. Eternity, creation, unsearchable mind: the hubs of this block in one verse.

Shared: everlasting, not faint, not weary. Classical Arminians wait on this God; they do not reduce Him to a partner who might tire of the plan. Unsearchable is not ignorance on His side.

Reformed

Hast thou not known? Jehovah is the God of eternity, Creator of the ends of the earth; not wearied; no searching of His understanding. Calvin: eternal distinguishes Him from idols that endure for a time. Eternity is never liable to change. Two statements: God is not wearied in doing good; no man can explore His wisdom. His purpose is incomprehensible; do not murmur though He does not at once comply with our wishes.

Reformed: doubly guilty if, taught by benefits and the word, they still distrust. Shared: everlasting, unsearchable. That is why v. 27 was the wrong sentence.

Isaiah 40:29

29He gives power to the faint, and to him who has no might he increases strength.

Arminian

He gives power to the faint, and to him who has no might He increases strength. The everlasting God of v. 28 spends strength on the weak.

Shared: gift to the faint. Wesley’s later wait-and-renew (v. 31) starts here. Comfort is not that Jacob was never faint, but that this God gives power.

Reformed

He giveth power to the faint. Calvin: applies the general doctrine to the present; the Jews were weakened, destitute of strength; on this account it belongs to God to assist the exhausted. Whenever our strength fails, the Lord stretches out His hand to the faint.

Reformed: first feel faintness, that Paul’s word may be fulfilled—power made perfect in weakness (2 Cor 12:9). Shared: He gives. The strength is His, matching the arm of 40:10.

Isaiah 40:30

30Even youths shall faint and be weary, and young men shall fall exhausted;

Arminian

Even youths shall faint and be weary; young men shall fall exhausted. Wesley: the youngest and strongest men, left to themselves.

Shared: creature-strength fails, even at its prime. The contrast is coming in v. 31. No extra fight: both camps know young men fall.

Reformed

The youths are wearied; the young men by falling fall. Calvin: men’s strength easily fails; God’s never. He distinguishes strength that appears born with us from that by which God peculiarly supports His elect. Those proud of gifts sink and faint.

Reformed: the Church marked off from the world by perpetual spiritual grace versus fading earthly vigor. Shared: youths fall. The next verse will name who does not.

Isaiah 40:31

31but they who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles; they shall run and not be weary; they shall walk and not faint.

Arminian

They who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength; mount up with wings like eagles; run and not be weary; walk and not faint. Wesley: wait—that rely upon Him. Renew—shall grow stronger and stronger.

Waiting is reliance, not a God who might not come. Shared: eagle, run, walk. Comfort lands on the faint who wait. Classical Arminians hear a real path of relying that can be taken; they still wait on the everlasting God of v. 28, not on a deity still taking counsel (v. 13).

Reformed

They that wait for Jehovah shall exchange strength; raise wings as eagles; run and not be weary; walk and not faint. Calvin: waiting means patience; violent men dash themselves; the vigor of the godly is refreshed while they wait. We must be convinced of weakness that we may yield to God’s power. The Jews in captivity had great need of this.

Reformed: this is supernatural grace, the elect’s privilege, not the fading strength of v. 30. Shared with Wesley: wait, renew, eagles. The light fork is whether waiting is the reliance of whoever will, growing stronger, or the patience of those God peculiarly supports when human vigor is already nothing.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take waiting as relying upon the LORD, and renewing as growing stronger and stronger—a real path of trust in the everlasting God. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take waiting as patience that yields to God’s power: the unwearied strength is the supernatural grace by which He supports His elect, over against the youths who fall in their own vigor.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the God who measures seas, needs no counselor, will not be likened to an idol, and gives power to the faint who wait—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 40:12–31.

Arminian Notable voices

“Who hath—who can do this but God? And this discourse of God’s infinite power and wisdom is added to give them the greater assurance that God was able to do the wonderful things he had promised. Who—who did God either need or take to advise him in any of his works. To whom—from the foregoing discourse of God’s infinite greatness he takes occasion to shew the folly of those that make mean and visible representations of God. Wait—that rely upon him. Renew—shall grow stronger and stronger.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 40:12–31)

Reformed Notable voices

“Who hath measured? He adorns the power of God that the promises may be believed; Abraham did not hesitate, because he believed that God who had promised was able to perform. Who instructed the Spirit of Jehovah? God had no need of a teacher. To whom then have ye likened God? It is impossible to frame an image which shall have any resemblance to his glory. They that wait for Jehovah: waiting means patience; violent men dash themselves; the vigor of godly persons is refreshed. We must be convinced of our weakness that we may yield to the power of God.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 40:12–13, 18, 31)
