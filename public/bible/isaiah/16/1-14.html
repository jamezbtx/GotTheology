Passage

Isaiah 16

Verses 1–14 · ESV · Voices

Send the lamb to the ruler of the land, from Sela, by way of the desert, to the mount of the daughter of Zion. Like fleeing birds, like a scattered nest, so are the daughters of Moab at the fords of the Arnon. Give counsel; grant justice; make your shade like night at the height of noon; shelter the outcasts; do not reveal the fugitive; let the outcasts of Moab sojourn among you; be a shelter to them from the destroyer. When the oppressor is no more, and destruction has ceased, and he who tramples underfoot has vanished from the land, then a throne will be established in steadfast love, and on it will sit in faithfulness in the tent of David one who judges and seeks justice and is swift to do righteousness. We have heard of the pride of Moab—how proud he is!—of his arrogance, his pride, and his insolence; in his idle boasting he is not right. Therefore let Moab wail for Moab, let everyone wail. Mourn, utterly stricken, for the raisin cakes of Kir-hareseth. For the fields of Heshbon languish, and the vine of Sibmah; the lords of the nations have struck down its branches, which reached to Jazer and strayed to the desert; its shoots spread abroad and passed over the sea. Therefore I weep with the weeping of Jazer for the vine of Sibmah; I drench you with my tears, O Heshbon and Elealeh; for over your summer fruit and your harvest the shout has ceased. And joy and gladness are taken away from the fruitful field, and in the vineyards no songs are sung, no cheers are raised; no treader treads out wine in the presses; I have put an end to the shouting. Therefore my inner parts moan like a lyre for Moab, and my inmost self for Kir-hareseth. And when Moab presents himself, when he wearies himself on the high place, when he comes to his sanctuary to pray, he will not prevail. This is the word that the LORD spoke concerning Moab in the past. But now the LORD has spoken, saying, In three years, like the years of a hired worker, the glory of Moab will be brought into contempt, in spite of all his great multitude, and those who remain will be very few and feeble.

Isaiah 16:1

1Send the lamb to the ruler of the land, from Sela, by way of the desert, to the mount of the daughter of Zion.

Arminian

Wesley: Send—the prophet continues his prophecy against Moab, and gives them counsel what to do, to prevent, if possible, the desolation. Make your peace with God, by sacrifice, for all your injuries done to him, and to his people. Sela—an eminent city of Moab, seated upon a rock. Unto the mount—unto the temple upon mount Zion. Shared: send the lamb toward Zion.

Arminian pastoral: the counsel is a real word to historic Moab. Sela on the rock, the desert road, the mount of the daughter of Zion—not a newspaper. Do not turn the lamb into a modern tribute scheme. Pride is called to send while sending is still possible.

Reformed

Calvin: here the Prophet scoffs at the Moabites for not acknowledging God at the proper time. It is a condemnation of late repentance. Send ye a lamb: view it as spoken ironically; they will send in vain. The Ruler is God himself, not Hezekiah. By a lamb, what was to be offered in sacrifice. From the rock of the desert to the mountain of the daughter of Zion, God’s authorized temple.

Reformed pastoral: late sending does not buy off the stroke. Shared: the lamb is aimed at Zion. Historic Moab, not a twenty-first-century map.

Key contrast Both hear send the lamb from Sela toward Zion—historic Moab continued, not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s counsel: make peace with God by sacrifice, to prevent, if possible, the desolation. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: it is spoken ironically; they will send in vain; the Ruler is God himself, and late repentance does not avail.

Isaiah 16:2

2Like fleeing birds, like a scattered nest, so are the daughters of Moab at the fords of the Arnon.

Arminian

Wesley: Cast out—which knows not whither to go. Arnon—which was the border of the land of Moab, where they were, with design to flee out of their land, tho’ they knew not whither. Like fleeing birds, like a scattered nest, so are the daughters of Moab at the fords of the Arnon. Shared: Moab’s daughters are scattered at the border river.

Arminian pastoral: the fords of the Arnon are a real river of historic Moab, not a modern crossing. Do not preach the scattered nest as a headline. Flight without a place is the fruit of pride, not a puzzle for a map.

Reformed

Calvin: he shows they ought not at that time to think of sending sacrifices, because they will not be able to provide for their safety in any other way than by leaving their native country. By the metaphor of birds he describes the terror with which they shall be struck. He threatens that the Moabites, who had abused their tranquillity, shall have a trembling and wearisome flight.

Reformed pastoral: they fly because they abused peace. Shared: like a scattered nest at the Arnon. Historic Moab, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 16:3

3Give counsel; grant justice; make your shade like night at the height of noon; shelter the outcasts; do not reveal the fugitive;

Arminian

Wesley: Take counsel—consider seriously what course to take. Shadow—or, as the shadow of the night, large and dark. Conceal and protect my people in the time of their distress. The out-casts—those of my people who are driven out of their land. Wandereth—unto their enemies. Shared: shade at noon is shelter, not a slogan.

Arminian pastoral: the call to hide the fugitive is a real moral word. Historic Judah’s outcasts among historic Moab, not a culture-war speech. Do not betray the wanderer; do not aim the verse at a modern state.

Reformed

Calvin: we must set before our minds the dreadful ruin of the Moabites. While everything was in their power they would not listen; now they groan and seek remedies which are nowhere found. Execute judgment: Isaiah ironically advises them to assemble councils and execute judgments which they had formerly overturned. Hide the banished: he means the Jews, whom the Moabites treated cruelly. The same measure is measured to them again.

Reformed pastoral: the shade they refused is the shade they now lack. Shared: shelter the outcasts; do not reveal the fugitive. Historic Moab, not a newspaper.

Key contrast Both hear counsel, justice, and shade at noon for the outcast—not a modern refugee headline. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s charge: conceal and protect the Lord’s people driven out, and do not bewray the wanderer to their enemies. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: the counsel is ironic; Moab abused justice and persecuted the Jews, and now the same measure returns.

Isaiah 16:4

4let the outcasts of Moab sojourn among you; be a shelter to them from the destroyer. When the oppressor is no more, and destruction has ceased, and he who tramples underfoot has vanished from the land,

Arminian

Wesley: Mine out-casts—whom tho’ I have sorely chastened, yet I own for my people. At an end—shall shortly be destroyed, and then thou wilt not lose the fruit of thy kindness. The present tense is put for the future. Shared: when the oppressor is no more, kindness is not wasted.

Arminian pastoral: the chastened remain owned. Historic outcasts, not a twenty-first-century column. The destroyer will end; do not invent a limited God from a sojourn.

Reformed

Calvin: the Prophet addresses the Moabites as if he were humbly beseeching them in the name of the people. God is represented as if he performed the part of a suppliant. It is certain the Moabites did not act in this manner, but joined their efforts with the enemies of the Jews. For the extortioner hath ceased: he now directs his discourse to the Jews; Moab shall perish, but my people shall at length be delivered.

Reformed pastoral: the Lord’s outcasts remain his. Shared: the oppressor vanishes from the land. Historic Moab’s cruelty, not a modern state.

Key contrast Both hear outcasts sheltered and the oppressor gone—Judah’s chastened still owned, not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s kindness: though sorely chastened, they are still his people, and Moab will not lose the fruit of sheltering them when the extortioner is destroyed. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: God takes the suppliant’s part for his banished; Moab did the opposite; the end of the extortioner is the deliverance of the Jews and the ruin of Moab.

Isaiah 16:5

5then a throne will be established in steadfast love, and on it will sit in faithfulness in the tent of David one who judges and seeks justice and is swift to do righteousness.

Arminian

Wesley: In mercy—by my mercy. I am now punishing their sins, yet I will deliver them for my own mercy’s sake. The throne—the kingdom of Judah. He—their king. In truth—firmly and constantly. Tabernacle—in the house, or palace, called a tent with respect to the unsettledness of David’s house. Seeking—searching out the truth of things with care. Hasting—neither denying, nor yet delaying justice. Shared: a throne established in steadfast love; one who judges and seeks justice.

Arminian pastoral: mercy holds the throne while sins are still being punished. Historic David’s house, not a millennial camp fight. Do not invent a pre/post/a-mil war from the tent of David. The king is swift to do righteousness; that is the hope named.

Reformed

Calvin: the Jews explain the whole as referring to Hezekiah, but this is altogether inappropriate. The Prophet speaks of a more important restoration of the Church. It cannot be explained as referring to any other than to Christ, though Hezekiah was a type of Christ. In mercy: this does not take place through the agency of men, but by the kindness of God, who is the builder of this throne. He shall sit in the tabernacle of David: Christ reigns among men. In steadfastness: the kingdom of Christ will be firm and last forever. Who shall judge: one who shall discharge the office of a good governor.

Reformed pastoral: the throne is mercy’s building, not man’s. Shared: one who judges, seeks justice, and is swift to do righteousness. Do not turn the tent of David into a newspaper or a millennial quarrel.

Key contrast Both hear a throne in steadfast love and a faithful judge in David’s tent—shared Davidic hope, not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s kingdom of Judah: their king sits firmly by mercy, though sins are being punished; the house is still like a tabernacle, and he neither denies nor delays justice. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: it cannot be reduced to Hezekiah; Christ is the builder’s king, the type having begun in David’s line, and the throne of mercy will not fall.

Isaiah 16:6

6We have heard of the pride of Moab—how proud he is!—of his arrogance, his pride, and his insolence; in his idle boasting he is not right.

Arminian

Wesley: We—the prophet having spoken to the Moabites, now turns his speech to God’s people. The sense is, I do not expect that my counsels will have any good effect upon Moab; they will still carry themselves insolently and outrageously. His lies—his vain imaginations, and false and crafty counsels, shall not take effect. Shared: the pride of Moab is not right.

Arminian pastoral: arrogance still says no to the offered word. Historic Moab’s idle boasting, not a culture-war speech. Do not baptize pride as strength.

Reformed

Calvin: he added this by way of anticipation. Their pride would not prevent God from overthrowing them. We have heard of the pride of Moab: they idly exalt themselves above God and men. His lies: vain boasting; he will not accomplish what he imagines. Proud men settle everything as if everything were in their power; such arrogance shall be brought down. Pride is highly displeasing to God.

Reformed pastoral: boasts do not bind the Lord’s hand. Shared: judgment on Moab’s arrogance. Historic pride, not a modern state.

Isaiah 16:7

7Therefore let Moab wail for Moab, let everyone wail. Mourn, utterly stricken, for the raisin cakes of Kir-hareseth.

Arminian

Wesley: Moab—one Moabite shall howl or lament to or for another. Kirhareseth—an ancient and eminent city of Moab, which was preserved when their other cities were ruined, and therefore the destruction of it was more lamented. Stricken—or, broken, overthrown or destroyed. Shared: let everyone wail for Kir-hareseth.

Arminian pastoral: wail for raisin cakes is historic Kir-hareseth’s grief, not a market story. Everyone wails; do not make a spectacle of it, and do not aim the howl at a modern people.

Reformed

Calvin: this pride, and the cruelty which springs from it, will be the cause of their destruction. Moab to Moab: a melancholy concert among them. On account of the foundations of Kir-hareseth: he names the foundations rather than the city, because it was to be completely thrown down. You will groan, being only smitten: all who shall be left will be wounded.

Reformed pastoral: pride ends in a howl over foundations. Shared: let everyone wail. Historic Kir-hareseth, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 16:8

8For the fields of Heshbon languish, and the vine of Sibmah; the lords of the nations have struck down its branches, which reached to Jazer and strayed to the desert; its shoots spread abroad and passed over the sea.

Arminian

Wesley: The lords—the Assyrians or Chaldeans, the great rulers of the eastern nations. Plants—the choicest vines. Under which he seems to understand not only all other fruits and goods, but even their choicest people. They—the lords of the heathen are come as far as Jazer. Wandered—the Moabites fled for their lives. The sea—over the Dead-sea, the border of Moab.

Arminian pastoral: Heshbon’s fields and Sibmah’s vine are a real vintage east of the Dead Sea. Do not turn the lords of the nations into a modern coalition. Historic Moab, not a twenty-first-century map.

Reformed

Calvin: he describes allegorically the desolation of the whole country. There is reason to believe it abounded in the choicest vines. The lords of the nations have trodden down his choicest shoots. Which reached even to Jazer: this points out the extent of the devastation. He says they crossed the sea, because cultivation was extended even there.

Reformed pastoral: the vine is cut from Jazer to the sea because the lords are sent. Shared: the vine of Sibmah is struck down. Historic Moab, not a millennial map.

Key contrast Both hear Heshbon languish and the vine of Sibmah struck by the lords of the nations. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s lords as the Assyrians or Chaldeans, the plants as choicest vines and even choicest people, Moabites wandering, and flight over the Dead Sea. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: the vines figure the whole land’s ruin; the reach to Jazer and over the sea marks how far the devastation runs.

Isaiah 16:9

9Therefore I weep with the weeping of Jazer for the vine of Sibmah; I drench you with my tears, O Heshbon and Elealeh; for over your summer fruit and your harvest the shout has ceased.

Arminian

Wesley: Sibmah—I will bewail Sibmah, as I did bewail Jazer, which was destroyed before Sibmah. Fallen—those joyful shouts which were customary in the time of harvest and vintage, shall cease. I drench you with my tears, O Heshbon and Elealeh. The prophet’s tears, not a claim that God does not know what he has decreed.

Arminian pastoral: pity can be honest without open theism. Historic Heshbon, Elealeh, Jazer, and Sibmah—not a modern harvest story. Do not invent a limited God from "I weep."

Reformed

Calvin: the Prophet here takes upon him the character of another person; in the name of the Moabites he laments and groans. Believers always shudder at the judgments of God. Yet he does not describe his own feelings only; his intention is to give additional weight to his instruction. The shouting of enemies bursts forth on the harvest.

Reformed pastoral: the weeping is the prophet’s office and a real shudder—not process-theism, not a limit on the decree. Shared: the harvest shout has ceased. Historic vintage of Moab.

Key contrast Both hear "I weep" and tears over Heshbon—prophet’s compassion and/or the LORD’s pity through that office, not open theism. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s prophet: I will bewail Sibmah as I did Jazer; harvest shouts fall. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: Isaiah assumes the Moabite’s character as on a stage, and believers shudder at the judgments of God even while the spoiler’s shout breaks in on the vintage.

Isaiah 16:10

10And joy and gladness are taken away from the fruitful field, and in the vineyards no songs are sung, no cheers are raised; no treader treads out wine in the presses; I have put an end to the shouting.

Arminian

Wesley: Treaders—in those times they used to squeeze out the juice of their grapes by treading them with their feet. Joy and gladness are taken away from the fruitful field; no treader treads out wine in the presses. Shared: the vintage shout is ended.

Arminian pastoral: the silenced press is historic Moab’s vintage, not a culture-war speech. The Lord has put an end to the shouting; do not restage it as entertainment.

Reformed

Calvin: he confirms that the whole country shall be desolate. When he threatens that God will cause the vine-dressers to cease to sing, he refers to the ancient custom of singing at vintage. When the produce of the harvest shall be taken away, nothing will be left but to lament their poverty. I have made their vintage-shouting to cease.

Reformed pastoral: the Lord himself ends the shout. Shared: no songs in the vineyards. Historic presses, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 16:11

11Therefore my inner parts moan like a lyre for Moab, and my inmost self for Kir-hareseth.

Arminian

Wesley: My bowels—thro’ compassion. In excessive grief, the bowels are sometimes rolled together, so as to make an audible noise. Shared: inner parts moan like a lyre for Moab, and the inmost self for Kir-hareseth.

Arminian pastoral: the moan is compassion, not a limit on knowledge or decree. Do not build open theism from a lyre in the inner parts. Historic Kir-hareseth, not a modern city.

Reformed

Calvin: assuming the character of a Moabite, the Prophet again describes excessive lamentation, proceeding from grief so intense that even the bowels make a distressing noise. By again naming Kir-hareseth, the chief city, he describes the destruction of the whole country. Lively descriptions bring the events before our eyes.

Reformed pastoral: the sounding bowels are the prophet’s assumed grief and a real shudder at judgment—not process-theism. Shared: a lyre-moan for Moab.

Key contrast Both hear inner parts that moan like a lyre—pity, not a gap in the decree. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s compassion: through grief the bowels roll until they sound. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: Isaiah assumes the Moabite’s character so the ruin of Kir-hareseth is set before the eyes, and the inmost parts sound like a harp for the whole land.

Isaiah 16:12

12And when Moab presents himself, when he wearies himself on the high place, when he comes to his sanctuary to pray, he will not prevail.

Arminian

Wesley: When—when it shall appear that all their other devotions are vain. His sanctuary—to the temple of his great god Chemosh. But—his god can neither hear nor help him. Shared: he wearies himself on the high place and does not prevail.

Arminian pastoral: Chemosh cannot hear. Historic high places of Moab, not a modern shrine. Do not copy the weary prayer that will not prevail; do not aim it at a twenty-first-century people.

Reformed

Calvin: idolaters in affliction betake themselves to their idols. If any uncommon calamity befalls them, they go to another temple more sacred than the rest. Their most celebrated temple was dedicated to Chemosh. He shall not profit by it. They are not guided by reason but by the impressions made on their senses. He shuts out the Moabites from all hope of safety: they will find no protection even in their gods.

Reformed pastoral: the sanctuary of Chemosh is a dead end. Shared: he will not prevail. Historic Moab’s high place, not a culture-war speech.

Isaiah 16:13

13This is the word that the LORD spoke concerning Moab in the past.

Arminian

Wesley: Since—since the beginning of God’s revelation to me concerning Moab, hitherto. This is the word that the LORD spoke concerning Moab in the past. Shared: the earlier word still stands.

Arminian pastoral: the Lord has spoken. Historic Moab, not a delayed headline. Do not treat the oracle as a draft, and do not turn it into a modern state.

Reformed

Calvin: this concluding sentence is the ratification of the prophecy. He has pronounced the decree of God himself, and has brought nothing forward that did not proceed from the Lord, and thus, laying aside the person of a man, he introduces God speaking.

Reformed pastoral: it is the Lord’s word, not Isaiah’s private guess. Shared: this is the word concerning Moab. Historic oracle, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 16:14

14But now the LORD has spoken, saying, In three years, like the years of a hired worker, the glory of Moab will be brought into contempt, in spite of all his great multitude, and those who remain will be very few and feeble.

Arminian

Wesley: The Lord—hath made this farther discovery of his mind to me. Three years—this may well be understood of some great blow given to the Moabites, either by Sennacherib, or his son Esarhaddon, from which notwithstanding they recovered and flourished again till Nebuchadnezzar compleated their destruction. Hireling—within three years precisely counted; for hirelings are very punctual in observing the time for which they are hired. The glory—their strength, and wealth, and other things in which they glory, shall be made contemptible.

Arminian pastoral: three years like a hired worker is a counted span in that age, not a twenty-first-century date scheme. Historic Moab’s multitude made few and feeble. Do not invent a modern countdown.

Reformed

Calvin: the time is fixed, not only for the sake of certainty, but that believers may not become faint through longer delay. He alludes to the labors of hirelings, who long for the appointed day. The Lord fixes a day for the Moabites in which they shall not escape the entire destruction of their power. The remnant shall be feeble: they will have no resemblance to their former condition.

Reformed pastoral: the hireling’s clock is the Lord’s appointment. Shared: glory brought into contempt; those who remain very few and feeble. Historic chronology, not a newspaper decoder.

Key contrast Both hear three years like a hired worker—counted time, not a twenty-first-century date scheme. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s chronology: a great blow by Sennacherib or Esarhaddon, then recovery until Nebuchadnezzar completed the destruction, the years counted as hirelings count. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: the Lord fixes the hireling’s day so believers will not faint; Moab will not escape the ruin of his power, and the remnant will be feeble, unlike what he was.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on sending the lamb from Sela toward Zion, the daughters of Moab at the fords of the Arnon, a throne in steadfast love, the pride of Moab, and three years like a hired worker–so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 16:1–14.

Arminian Notable voices

“Send—The prophet continues his prophecy against Moab, and gives them counsel what to do, to prevent, if possible, the desolation. Make your peace with God, by sacrifice, for all your injuries done to him, and to his people. Sela—An eminent city of Moab, seated upon a rock. Unto the mount—Unto the temple upon mount Zion. In mercy—By my mercy. I am now punishing their sins, yet I will deliver them for my own mercy’s sake. The throne—The kingdom of Judah. He—Their king. In truth—Firmly and constantly. Tabernacle—In the house, or palace, which is called a tent, or tabernacle, with respect to the unsettledness of David’s house. Seeking—Searching out the truth of things with care and diligence. Hasting—Neither denying, nor yet delaying justice. We—I do not expect that my counsels will have any good effect upon Moab; they will still carry themselves insolently and outrageously. His lies—His vain imaginations, and false and crafty counsels, shall not take effect. Three years—This may well be understood of some great blow given to the Moabites, either by Sennacherib, or his son Esarhaddon, from which notwithstanding they recovered and flourished again till Nebuchadnezzar compleated their destruction. Hireling—Within three years precisely counted; for hirelings are very punctual in observing the time for which they are hired.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Isaiah 16

Reformed Notable voices

“Here the Prophet scoffs at the Moabites for not acknowledging God at the proper time. It is a condemnation of late repentance. Send ye a lamb: we ought to view it as spoken ironically; they will send in vain. The appellation Ruler must be viewed as referring to God himself. By a lamb, he means what was to be offered in sacrifice. From the rock of the desert to the mountain of the daughter of Zion, that is, to God’s authorized temple. The throne shall be prepared in mercy: the Jews explain this as referring to Hezekiah, but this is altogether inappropriate. It cannot be explained as referring to any other than to Christ, though Hezekiah was a type of Christ. This does not take place through the agency of men, but by the kindness of God, who is the builder of this throne. We have heard of the pride of Moab: their pride would not prevent God from overthrowing them. Proud men settle everything as if everything were in their power; such arrogance shall be brought down. Three years: the time is fixed, not only for the sake of certainty, but that believers may not become faint through longer delay. He alludes to the labors of hirelings. The Lord fixes a day for the Moabites, in which they shall not escape from the entire destruction of their power. The remnant shall be feeble: they will have no resemblance to their former condition.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah 16:1–14
