Passage

Isaiah 22

Verses 1–14 · ESV · Voices

The oracle concerning the valley of vision. What do you mean that you have gone up, all of you, to the housetops, tumultuous city? Your slain are not slain with the sword. Leaders fled; look away, let me weep for the daughter of my people. A day of tumult in the valley of vision; Elam bore the quiver, Kir uncovered the shield; choicest valleys full of chariots. He has taken away the covering of Judah. You looked to the House of the Forest, collected waters, broke down houses to fortify the wall, made a reservoir—but you did not look to him who did it, who planned it long ago. The Lord GOD of hosts called for weeping; behold, joy and gladness, let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die. This iniquity will not be atoned for you until you die. The unit stays with historic Jerusalem, the valley of vision, Elam and Kir, Judah’s walls in the Assyria–Babylon age—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper of later states, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 22:1

1The oracle concerning the valley of vision. What do you mean that you have gone up, all of you, to the housetops,

Arminian

Wesley: The valley—of Judah; and especially of Jerusalem, called a valley, because great part of it stood in a valley; and the valley of vision, because of the many and clear visions or Revelations of God’s mind, in that place. House-tops—as they used to do in times of great consternation, that they might look to and cry to heaven for help. Shared: housetops in the valley of vision.

Arminian pastoral: historic Jerusalem, not a twenty-first-century roof. Do not turn the valley of vision into a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: the burden of the valley of vision. Isaiah prophesies against Judea, which he calls the valley of vision; he includes the whole of Judea in the preface. A valley, surrounded on all sides by mountains. Of vision: the Lord enlightened Judea by his word; they were called seers. He beats down the foolish confidence that visions would shield the disobedient. What aileth thee now, that thou art wholly gone up to the house-tops?

Reformed pastoral: light of the word does not cancel the stroke. Shared: valley of vision; housetops. Historic Judah, not a modern city.

Isaiah 22:2

2you who are full of shoutings, tumultuous city, exultant town? Your slain are not slain with the sword or dead in battle.

Arminian

Wesley: Noises—of joyful shouts. Tumultuous—through revelling and jollity. Battle—but either by famine or pestilence in the siege, or in their flight. Shared: slain, yet not by the sword.

Arminian pastoral: reveling city, then death without battle. Historic Jerusalem’s shout, not a culture-war speech.

Reformed

Calvin: thou that art full of stirs, a tumultuous city, a joyous city. Thy slain men are not slain with the sword, nor dead in battle. The tumult is empty joy; the dead fall by siege-miseries rather than by honorable fight.

Reformed note: the slain are still that town’s. Shared: not dead in battle. Historic city, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:3

3All your leaders have fled together; without the bow they were captured. All of you who were found were captured, though they had fled far away.

Arminian

Wesley: Rulers—Zedekiah and his chief commanders, whose flight he foretells. Found—that remain there with Zedekiah in the siege; for those who had fled to the Chaldeans saved their lives and liberties. Bound—in fetters, Jer. lii, 11. Fled—who fled from Jerusalem, but were pursued and overtaken. Shared: leaders fled and were caught.

Arminian pastoral: Zedekiah’s flight is historic Judah’s, not a later evacuation. Do not hunt another ruler to make this a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: all thy rulers are fled together; they are bound by the archers. I cannot be persuaded to expound this as Sennacherib’s siege, for he speaks of the destruction of Jerusalem; when Sennacherib came, the Lord immediately delivered it, none were taken. These events happened long after the death of the Prophet; sacred history informs us that even the rulers betook themselves to flight, and derived no advantage from it.

Reformed pastoral: flight does not save. Shared: leaders captured. Historic destruction of Jerusalem, not Sennacherib’s spared year.

Key contrast Both hear historic Jerusalem’s rulers fled and bound—not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s Zedekiah and his commanders, fetters as in Jeremiah 52. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: not Sennacherib’s siege, which the Lord reversed, but the later destruction of Jerusalem after the prophet’s death, when flight still failed.

Isaiah 22:4

4Therefore I said: “Look away from me; let me weep bitter tears; do not labor to comfort me concerning the destruction of the daughter of my people.”

Arminian

Wesley: Look away—take off your eyes and thoughts from me, and leave me alone. Shared: bitter tears for the daughter of my people.

Arminian pastoral: pity can be honest without open theism. Historic Judah’s destruction, not a claim that God does not know the decree. Do not invent a limited God from “let me weep.”

Reformed

Calvin: look away from me; I will weep bitterly; labour not to comfort me, because of the spoiling of the daughter of my people. The prophet refuses comfort; he takes the mourner’s part, as the prophets shudder at the judgments of God even against their own people. This is office, not a gap in the decree.

Reformed pastoral: the tears are the seer’s, not process-theism. Shared: do not comfort me. Historic daughter of Judah.

Isaiah 22:5

5For the Lord GOD of hosts has a day of tumult and trampling and confusion in the valley of vision, a battering down of walls and a shouting to the mountains.

Arminian

Wesley: Treading down—in which my people are trodden under foot by their enemies. By the Lord—not only men, but God himself fought against them. Walls—of the strong cities of Judah. Mountains—with such loud and dismal outcries as should reach to the neighbouring mountains. Shared: the LORD’s day in the valley of vision.

Arminian pastoral: God fights against his vision-valley when they will not look to him. Historic walls of Judah, not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: for it is a day of trouble, and of treading down, and of perplexity by the Lord God of hosts in the valley of vision, breaking down the walls, and of crying to the mountains. The day is the Lord’s; vision does not cancel trampling. Walls fall because he fights.

Reformed note: tumult is still that day’s. Shared: shouting to the mountains. Historic Jerusalem, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:6

6And Elam bore the quiver with chariots and horsemen, and Kir uncovered the shield.

Arminian

Wesley: Elam—the Persians, who now, and for a long time after, were subject to the Assyrian and Chaldean emperors. Quiver—being expert bowmen. Horsemen—as some fought on foot, so others fought from chariots and horses. Kir—the Medes, so called from Kir, an eminent city and region of Media. Uncovered—prepared it and themselves for the battle. Shared: Elam’s quiver, Kir’s shield.

Arminian pastoral: historic Persians and Medes in that empire’s host, not a modern coalition.

Reformed

Calvin: and Elam bare the quiver with chariots of men and horsemen, and Kir uncovered the shield. Elam and Kir are named as serving in the assault on the valley of vision. The uncovered shield is readiness for the day of v. 5.

Reformed pastoral: the quiver is still that siege’s. Shared: Elam and Kir. Historic nations around Judah, not a later map.

Isaiah 22:7

7Your choicest valleys were full of chariots, and the horsemen took their stand at the gates.

Arminian

Wesley: Valleys—valleys were the most proper places for the use of chariots. Gate—to assist the footmen while they made their assault, and to prevent those who endeavoured to escape. Shared: chariots in the best valleys; horsemen at the gates.

Arminian pastoral: choicest valleys of historic Judah, not a twenty-first-century plain.

Reformed

Calvin: and it shall come to pass that thy choicest valleys shall be full of chariots, and the horsemen shall set themselves in array at the gate. The best ground is occupied by the enemy; the gate is watched so that flight of v. 3 fails.

Reformed note: horsemen at the gates are still Jerusalem’s. Shared: choicest valleys full. Historic siege, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:8

8He has taken away the covering of Judah. In that day you looked to the weapons of the House of the Forest,

Arminian

Wesley: He—the enemy. Covering—he took those fenced cities, which were a covering or safe-guard both to the people of Judah, and to Jerusalem. The armour—thy trust was placed in the arm of flesh. The forest—more fully called the house of the forest of Lebanon, 1 Kings vii, 2, not because it was built in Lebanon, for it was in Jerusalem; but because it was built of the trees of Lebanon. Shared: covering gone; eyes on stored weapons.

Arminian pastoral: the armory is historic Solomon’s house in Jerusalem. Do not preach the House of the Forest as a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: he discovered the covering of Judah. The Lord, or the enemy as his rod, strips the defenses. In that day thou didst look to the armour of the house of the forest: they turn to stored weapons, not to the Maker named in v. 11.

Reformed pastoral: the look is still to flesh. Shared: covering of Judah taken; House of the Forest. Historic armory, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 22:9

9and you saw that the breaches of the city of David were many. You collected the waters of the lower pool,

Arminian

Wesley: Seen—that is, observed in order to the reparation of them. The waters—that you might both deprive the enemy of water, and supply the city with it. Shared: many breaches; waters collected.

Arminian pastoral: the city of David’s walls are a real count in that siege. Do not turn the lower pool into a modern water story.

Reformed

Calvin: ye have seen also the breaches of the city of David, that they are many; ye gathered together the waters of the lower pool. They notice what is broken and store water—prudent as far as it goes, and still not the look of v. 11.

Reformed note: breaches are still David’s city’s. Shared: lower pool collected. Historic Jerusalem, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:10

10and you counted the houses of Jerusalem, and you broke down the houses to fortify the wall.

Arminian

Wesley: Numbered—that they might exactly know their own strength. The houses—which stood upon or without the walls, and so gave their enemies advantage, and hindered the fortifying the city. Shared: houses counted, then pulled for the wall.

Arminian pastoral: census of houses is historic Jerusalem’s emergency. Do not preach demolished homes as a culture-war speech.

Reformed

Calvin: and ye have numbered the houses of Jerusalem, and the houses have ye broken down to fortify the wall. They sacrifice dwellings to the rampart. Industry without looking to the Maker is still unbelief.

Reformed pastoral: the broken houses are still that wall’s. Shared: counted, then pulled down. Historic city, not a later headline.

Isaiah 22:11

11You made a reservoir between the two walls for the water of the old pool. But you did not look to him who did it, or see him who planned it long ago.

Arminian

Wesley: Thereof—of Jerusalem, expressed in the foregoing verse. Him—God, who made it a city, and the place of his special presence and worship. Long ago—which may be added to aggravate their sin, in distrusting that God, who had now for a long time given proof of his care and kindness in defending this city. Shared: reservoir made; Maker not looked to.

Arminian pastoral: the old pool is Hezekiah’s works in historic Jerusalem. Do not invent a limited God from “you did not look”; he planned it long ago.

Reformed

Calvin: ye made also a ditch between the two walls for the water of the old pool; but ye have not looked unto the maker thereof, neither had respect unto him that fashioned it long ago. All the water-works are named so that the omitted look may sting. He who planned the city long ago is the one they would not see.

Reformed note: the unseen planner is still the LORD of the valley of vision. Shared: reservoir yes; look no. Historic walls, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:12

12In that day the Lord GOD of hosts called for weeping and mourning, for baldness and wearing sackcloth;

Arminian

Wesley: Call—by his providence, and by his precepts requiring these things in such times. Baldness—by plucking or shaving off the hair of their heads, as was usual in great sorrows. Shared: a call to weep, not to feast.

Arminian pastoral: the call is real. Historic sackcloth in Judah, not a later ritual story. They might yet have wept.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day did the Lord God of hosts call to weeping, and to mourning, and to baldness, and to girding with sackcloth. The day of v. 5 had a prescribed response. Providence and precept agree: weep.

Reformed pastoral: the call is still the Lord’s. Shared: weeping and sackcloth. Historic Judah, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 22:13

13and behold, joy and gladness, killing oxen and slaughtering sheep, eating flesh and drinking wine. “Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.”

Arminian

Wesley: Let us, &c.—a most perverse and desperate conclusion. Shared: feast instead of sackcloth.

Arminian pastoral: tomorrow we die is despair, not a joke. Historic oxen and wine in the vision-valley, not a culture-war banquet. Do not copy the sentence.

Reformed

Calvin: and behold joy and gladness, slaying oxen and killing sheep, eating flesh and drinking wine: let us eat and drink, for to-morrow we shall die. They invert the call of v. 12. The proverb of despair is not courage; it is refusal of repentance.

Reformed note: the feast is still that day’s. Shared: eat and drink, tomorrow we die. Historic Jerusalem, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:14

14The LORD of hosts has revealed himself in my ears: “Surely this iniquity will not be atoned for you until you die,” says the Lord GOD of hosts.

Arminian

Wesley: 'Till ye die—you shall feel the sad effects of this, as long as you live. Shared: this iniquity not atoned until death.

Arminian close of this half: the feast of v. 13 is sealed in the prophet’s ears. Historic Judah’s iniquity, not a later people. The next unit will name Shebna and Eliakim in the same city. Do not stretch “until you die” into a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: and it was revealed in mine ears by the Lord of hosts, Surely this iniquity shall not be purged from you till ye die. The Lord himself seals what the prophet heard. The desperate feast will not be atoned by more walls or more wine. Death is the term named.

Reformed close: the iniquity is still that day’s. Shared: revealed in my ears; until you die. Historic valley of vision—already enough without a later decoder.

Reformed Notable voices

“The burden of the valley of vision. Isaiah again prophesies against Judea, which he calls the valley of vision. He gives this appellation to the whole of Judea rather than to Jerusalem; he appropriately calls it a valley, for it was surrounded on all sides by mountains. Of vision: the Lord enlightened the whole of Judea by his word; the prophets were continually employed in it, and that was the reason why they called them seers. He intended to beat down that foolish confidence with which the Jews were puffed up, because God had distinguished them by visions; visions will not prevent God from punishing their ingratitude. What aileth thee now, that thou art wholly gone up to the house-tops? Thou that art full of stirs, a tumultuous city, a joyous city: thy slain men are not slain with the sword, nor dead in battle. All thy rulers are fled together; they are bound. I cannot be persuaded to expound this as the siege of Sennacherib, for he speaks of the destruction of Jerusalem; when it was besieged by Sennacherib the Lord immediately delivered it. These events happened long after the death of the Prophet. Look away from me; I will weep bitterly. For it is a day of trouble, and of treading down, and of perplexity by the Lord God of hosts in the valley of vision. Elam bare the quiver, and Kir uncovered the shield. He discovered the covering of Judah. Ye looked to the armour of the house of the forest; ye made a ditch between the two walls for the water of the old pool; but ye have not looked unto the maker thereof. In that day did the Lord God of hosts call to weeping; and behold joy and gladness, eating flesh and drinking wine: let us eat and drink, for to-morrow we shall die. Surely this iniquity shall not be purged from you till ye die.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 22:1–14)
