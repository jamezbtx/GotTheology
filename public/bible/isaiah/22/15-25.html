Passage

Isaiah 22

Verses 15–25 · ESV · Voices

Thus says the Lord GOD of hosts, Come, go to this steward, to Shebna, who is over the household. What have you to do here, that you have cut out a tomb on the height and carve a dwelling in the rock? The LORD will hurl you away, whirl you like a ball into a wide land; there you shall die, and there your glorious chariots, you shame of your master’s house. I will thrust you from your office. In that day I will call my servant Eliakim the son of Hilkiah; I will clothe him with your robe and sash, and he shall be a father to Jerusalem and Judah. I will place on his shoulder the key of the house of David; he shall open, and none shall shut. I will fasten him like a peg in a secure place. They will hang on him the whole honor of his father’s house, every small vessel. In that day the peg that was fastened will give way, and the load on it will be cut off, for the LORD has spoken. The unit stays with historic Shebna and Eliakim in Hezekiah’s court—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper of later states, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 22:15

15Thus says the Lord GOD of hosts, “Come, go to this steward, to Shebna, who is over the household, and say to him:

Arminian

Wesley has no separate lemma here; his what of v. 16 names the man: what right hast thou to this place and office? Shared: Shebna over the household.

Arminian pastoral: the steward is historic Hezekiah’s officer, not a living politician. Do not turn Shebna into a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: come, go to this treasurer, even unto Shebna, which is over the house. He now comes to a particular person, the steward of the king’s house, whose pride he rebukes. The Lord of hosts sends the prophet to one man in the valley of vision.

Reformed pastoral: the household is still that court’s. Shared: Shebna over the house. Historic Jerusalem, not a modern cabinet.

Isaiah 22:16

16What have you to do here, and whom have you here, that you have cut out here a tomb for yourself, you who cut out a tomb on the height and carve a dwelling for yourself in the rock?

Arminian

Wesley: What—what right hast thou to this place and office? Whom—what kindred or relations? For the Jews say, he was a stranger by birth; which is the more probable, because his pedigree is not mentioned in any of those places of scripture where he is named. On high—in an high and eminent place. An habitation—he erected a stately house to live in, and a stately sepulchre to receive him when he died. A rock—a fit place both for strength and state.

Arminian pastoral: the rock-tomb is historic pride in Jerusalem. Do not preach a later mausoleum as this verse.

Reformed

Calvin: what hast thou here, and whom hast thou here, that thou hast hewed thee out a sepulchre here, as he that heweth him out a sepulchre on high, and that graveth an habitation for himself in a rock? He has no right to nest in Judah as if the place were his forever. The carved tomb is a claim the Lord will undo.

Reformed note: the height is still Shebna’s. Shared: tomb on the height, dwelling in the rock. Historic steward, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:17

17Behold, the LORD will hurl you away violently, O you strong man. He will seize firm hold on you

Arminian

Wesley: Will carry—will cause thee to be carried into captivity by a strong hand. Cover—this may be an allusion to the ancient custom of covering the faces of condemned persons. Shared: hurled, seized.

Arminian pastoral: the strong man is still Shebna. Historic captivity, not a later exile headline.

Reformed

Calvin: behold, the Lord will carry thee away with a mighty captivity, and will surely cover thee. The strong man is seized by a stronger hand. Covering the face is the condemned man’s shame.

Reformed pastoral: the hurl is still the Lord’s. Shared: violently taken. Historic Shebna, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 22:18

18and whirl you around and around, and throw you like a ball into a wide land. There you shall die, and there shall be your glorious chariots, you shame of your master's house.

Arminian

Wesley: Thy chariots—thy glorious chariots where thou didst ride in great state at Jerusalem, shall be turned into shame to thyself, and to thy master. Shared: a ball into a wide land; chariots become shame.

Arminian pastoral: the wide land is captivity’s country, not a twenty-first-century map. Do not restage the chariots as a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: he will violently turn and toss thee like a ball into a large country; there shalt thou die, and there the chariots of thy glory shall be the shame of thy lord’s house. The ball has no say in where it lands. Glory on wheels dies far from the rock-tomb.

Reformed note: the wide land is still that exile’s. Shared: thrown like a ball; shame of the master’s house. Historic court, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:19

19I will thrust you from your office, and you will be pulled down from your station.

Arminian

Wesley: He—the Lord; such sudden changes of persons being very usual in these writings. Shared: thrust from office and station.

Arminian pastoral: the Lord himself deposes. Historic Shebna’s post, not a later cabinet shuffle.

Reformed

Calvin: I will drive thee from thy station, and from thy state shall he pull thee down. The change of persons is the Lord’s own voice. Office is held at his pleasure.

Reformed pastoral: the station is still Shebna’s. Shared: pulled down. Historic household, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 22:20

20In that day I will call my servant Eliakim the son of Hilkiah,

Arminian

Wesley: Call—by my powerful providence. Shared: Eliakim called my servant.

Arminian pastoral: Hilkiah’s son is a named man in Hezekiah’s court (Isaiah 36–37). Do not turn Eliakim into a newspaper. They might yet have received a father in his place.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day I will call my servant Eliakim the son of Hilkiah. The Lord names the successor before Shebna is gone. My servant: office is service, not a tomb in the rock.

Reformed note: the call is still that day’s. Shared: Eliakim son of Hilkiah. Historic court, not a later politician.

Isaiah 22:21

21and I will clothe him with your robe, and will bind your sash on him, and will commit your authority to his hand. And he shall be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem and to the house of Judah.

Arminian

Wesley: Girdle—there was a peculiar sort of robe and girdle which was the badge of his office. A father—he shall govern them with fatherly care and affection. Shared: robe, sash, authority; a father to Jerusalem and Judah.

Arminian pastoral: fatherly government in historic Judah, not a culture-war speech. The badge changes hands because the Lord clothes him.

Reformed

Calvin: I will clothe him with thy robe, and strengthen him with thy girdle, and I will commit thy government into his hand; and he shall be a father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and to the house of Judah. By father he shews the duty of a good magistrate; a good king holds the place of a father. Eliakim will perform that part because he has been endued with the Spirit of God.

Reformed pastoral: the robe is still Shebna’s transferred. Shared: a father to Jerusalem and Judah. Historic household, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:22

22And I will place on his shoulder the key of the house of David. He shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open.

Arminian

Wesley: The key—the government, the power of opening and shutting, of letting men into it, or putting them out of it, whereof a key is a fit emblem. Shoulder—he mentions the shoulder rather than the hand, in which keys are commonly carried, from some ceremony then in use, of carrying a key upon the shoulder of the officer of state. Shared: key of David’s house on the shoulder.

Arminian pastoral: the key is historic Eliakim’s office in David’s court. Wesley does not turn the verse into a millennial chart. Do not hunt another key to make this a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: and the key of the house of David will I lay upon his shoulder. This expression is metaphorical, taken from an ordinary custom: keys are delivered to stewards. By the house of David is meant the royal house, because it had been promised to David that his kingdom would be for ever. Some commentators have viewed this passage as referring to Christ, but improperly; the Prophet draws a comparison between two men, Shebna and Eliakim. Eliakim was not a type of Christ; the Prophet does not here describe any hidden mystery. By the keys he means the government of the king’s house. (Christ later uses key-speech for the office of teaching, Matthew 16:19; that is another text.)

Reformed note: open and shut is still Eliakim’s charge. Shared: key on the shoulder. Historic house of David, not a millennial camp fight.

Key contrast Both keep the key as historic Eliakim’s government of David’s house in Hezekiah’s court, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads the key as the power of opening and shutting, the shoulder as a ceremony of state. Calvin reads the same steward’s custom, then refuses commentators who make Eliakim a type of Christ in this verse: the comparison is between two men; the keys mean the king’s house. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight; they agree the first referent is Eliakim.

Isaiah 22:23

23And I will fasten him like a peg in a secure place, and he will become a throne of honor to his father's house.

Arminian

Wesley: Fasten—I will establish the power in his hands. Sure place—in the strong walls, or solid timber, in the house. He shall be—by his prudent and righteous government he shall procure great glory, to all that have any relation to him. Shared: a peg fastened; a throne of honor.

Arminian pastoral: the secure place is still that household. Historic Eliakim, not a later dynasty headline.

Reformed

Calvin: I will fasten him as a nail in a sure place; and he shall be for a glorious throne to his father’s house. The Lord guards and upholds such a prince. If he permits even tyrants for a time, what shall happen when a prince defends justice and the true worship of God? He will be still more confirmed.

Reformed pastoral: the peg is still Eliakim’s. Shared: fastened in a secure place. Historic court, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 22:24

24And they will hang on him the whole honor of his father's house, the offspring and issue, every small vessel, from the cups to all the flagons.

Arminian

Wesley: The glory—of his own kindred and family, who shall all depend upon him, and receive glory from him; of the house of David, which is called his father’s house, because he was of the blood-royal. The offspring—great and small, the children and grand-children of his father’s house. All vessels—all sorts of vessels, great or small, mean or precious, may be hanged upon him, without any fear of falling. Shared: cups to flagons hang on the peg.

Arminian pastoral: small vessels included. Historic father’s house, not a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: and they shall hang upon him all the glory of his father’s house, the grandchildren and great-grandchildren, all vessels of small quantity. Eliakim would be fully qualified and not indolent; the office of a prince is laborious. Father’s house: Eliakim was of royal blood; he will have charge of all in the king’s house. Smaller vessels: uniform justice, the lowest rank as well as nobles; musical vessels explain little—nothing so small that he would not take charge of it.

Reformed note: cups and flagons are still that house’s. Shared: whole honor hung on him. Historic Eliakim, not a later headline.

Isaiah 22:25

25In that day, declares the LORD of hosts, the peg that was fastened in a secure place will give way, and it will be cut down and fall, and the load that was on it will be cut off, for the LORD has spoken.”

Arminian

Wesley: The nail—Shebna, who seemed to be so. The burden—all those wicked officers that were advanced and supported by his power. Shared: the seeming-sure peg falls; the load is cut off.

Arminian close: Shebna’s sure place was sure only to men. Historic officers hanging on him, not a twenty-first-century cabinet. The LORD has spoken; do not stretch the falling peg into a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day. It might be thought inconsistent with what he had said; but he no longer speaks of Eliakim, for he returns to Shebna, who was about to be cast down from his rank. In a sure place: this must be understood with respect to men; God casts it down with the smallest breath. And the burden that was upon it shall be cut off: when wicked men are ruined, all who relied on their authority must also be ruined.

Reformed close: the falling peg is still Shebna’s. Shared with Wesley: not Eliakim’s sure nail, but the steward who seemed fastened. Historic court of Judah—already enough without a later decoder.
