Passage

Isaiah 2

Verses 1–5 · ESV · Voices

After Zion’s dross and a restored name, Isaiah sees a word concerning Judah and Jerusalem: in the latter days the mountain of the Lord’s house is established above the hills, and nations flow to it. Peoples say, Come, let us go up, that He may teach us His ways. Law goes out from Zion; He judges; swords become plowshares. House of Jacob is told to walk in that light.

Isaiah 2:1

1The word that Isaiah the son of Amoz saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem.

Arminian

The word that Isaiah son of Amoz saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem. Same seer, same city as chapter 1. Wesley: the word is the matter or thing—the prophecy.

Arminian word: a real vision over historic Judah, not a newspaper. The mountain that follows is still this Jerusalem’s hope after dross.

Reformed

Calvin: this vision seals the restoration already promised (1:26–27). Doctrine is given weight by a picture set before the eyes, so that whatever calamities arise, the Church will never perish.

Reformed word: God adds vision to promise. Shared: Isaiah, Judah, Jerusalem. Latter days are v. 2.

Isaiah 2:2

2It shall come to pass in the latter days that the mountain of the house of the Lord shall be established as the highest of the mountains, and shall be lifted up above the hills; and all the nations shall flow to it,

Arminian

In the latter days the mountain of the house of the Lord shall be established as the highest of the mountains, lifted above the hills; all the nations shall flow to it. Wesley: last days—the times of the Messiah; Christ’s institutions continue to the end of the world. The mountain is not the material temple on Moriah but mystically the church; nations did not flow to that house, nor was it fulfilled till the temple was destroyed.

Arminian latter days: Messiah’s time, already begun, running on. Nations really flow—Gentiles coming to the word, not a millennial camp chart. Shared with Calvin: the stream is to the teaching, not to a rebuilt hill hung in the clouds.

Reformed

Calvin: the end of days is the kingdom of Christ. Fullness began at His coming and flows until He appear the second time (Heb 9:28). Zion was a little hill; wicked men scoffed. From it the gospel sounded through the world, and so the hill is higher than Olympus. Nations flow as rivers—the Church collected from every quarter, not a mass migration into Judea.

Reformed latter days: same Messiah, same gospel-height. The when/how is the fork: begun at the first coming, unfinished till the second; full height not looked for as an earthly timetable. Shared: nations stream to the word. Not a dispensational-versus-amillennial fight.

Key contrast Both confess the latter-day mountain is Messiah’s, and that nations stream to the word—not to a newspaper, and not as an Arminian-versus-Reformed millennial camp fight. Wesley hears the times of the Messiah and a mystical church, the flow not to the material temple and not fulfilled till that house fell. Calvin hears the kingdom of Christ begun at the first coming and running until the second; the full extent is not to be looked for on earth.

Isaiah 2:3

3and many peoples shall come, and say: “Come, let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, to the house of the God of Jacob, that he may teach us his ways and that we may walk in his paths.” For out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem.

Arminian

Many peoples come and say, Come, let us go up to the mountain of the Lord, to the house of the God of Jacob, that He may teach us His ways and we may walk in His paths. Out of Zion the law, the word of the Lord from Jerusalem. Wesley: the law is the new law, the doctrine of the gospel, obliging belief and practice as the old law did.

Arminian come: real speech, real going, real walking. They ask to be taught and then walk. The word goes out from historic Zion—Pentecost’s Jerusalem—not a modern-nation chart. Agency under a God who actually teaches.

Reformed

Calvin: many means various nations, formerly strangers, now one agreement about religion. God might bring each by a secret influence, yet He employs the agency of men. They do not sit and command Go up, but walk along as companions. He will teach us of His ways: God alone is the Church’s teacher; pastors may utter nothing but His word. We will walk: the instruction is not speculation; it directs the course of life. Law is all the instruction God has given; it went forth from Zion when the gospel began there.

Reformed come: the going is gathered by the word He sends. Shared: mountain, Jacob’s God, teach, walk, Zion, Jerusalem.

Isaiah 2:4

4He shall judge between the nations, and shall decide disputes for many peoples; and they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruning hooks; nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they learn war anymore.

Arminian

He shall judge between the nations, decide disputes for many peoples; they beat swords into plowshares, spears into pruning hooks; nation shall not lift up sword, nor learn war anymore. Wesley: Christ sets up His authority among all nations, convincing consciences, changing hearts, ordering lives; He rebukes by His word and Spirit, and by judgments on implacable enemies who obstruct the gospel.

Arminian judge: real heart-change now, in Messiah’s days to the end of the world. Plowshares are the fruit of that change, not a culture-war against armies and not a date-chart for a millennial camp. The how is word and Spirit meeting real nations.

Reformed

Calvin: doctrine is a king’s scepter; God rules among all nations in the person of the Son. Rebuke: stubborn flesh must be subdued; Christ begins the gospel by reproving the world of sin (John 16:8). Swords into plowshares: the gospel of reconciliation brings men into peace with each other. Not to be limited to Actium, nor twisted into anarchy. Full extent is not on earth; the kingdom here is only beginning. It is enough to experience the beginning, being reconciled to God and abstaining from harm.

Reformed judge: peace is the fruit of His reign, incomplete till He appears. Shared: He judges, swords, plowshares, no more war as the aim of the word.

Isaiah 2:5

5O house of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord.

Arminian

O house of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the Lord. Wesley: take heed that you do not reject that light which is so clear that even the blind Gentiles will discern it.

Arminian come: Judah is still addressed. Gentiles flowing is not an excuse for Jacob to sit. A real walk in a light already shining.

Reformed

Calvin: a sharp rebuke by the Gentiles’ example. Strangers run to Zion; the first-born of the house sit still. We will walk: the light is before their feet; they shut their eyes. Its brightness draws distant nations.

Reformed come: the same word that gathers nations still summons Jacob. Shared: house of Jacob, walk, light of the Lord.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a mountain raised above the hills and nations that stream to the word—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 2:1–5.

Arminian Notable voices

“In the last days—in the times of the Messiah. For Christ’s institutions were to continue to the end of the world. The mountain—the temple of the Lord upon mount Moriah; not to be understood literally of that material temple, but mystically of the church of God; as appears from the flowing of all nations to it, which was not to that temple, nor indeed was fulfilled till that temple was destroyed. The law—the new law, the doctrine of the gospel. He—Christ shall set up his authority among all nations, not only giving laws to them, but convincing their consciences, changing their hearts, and ordering their lives.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 2:2–4)

Reformed Notable voices

“When he mentions the end or completion of days, he is speaking of the kingdom of Christ. While the fullness of days began at the coming of Christ, it flows on in uninterrupted progress until he appear the second time. The mountain of the Lord’s house: Zion was a little hill; yet from it the gospel sounded through the whole world. All nations shall flow: the Church collected from every quarter. Though this was never fulfilled that the nations left their native country for Judea, yet because the gospel proceeded from Mount Zion, he justly says they will come to it. The fulfillment of this prophecy in its full extent must not be looked for on earth. It is enough if we experience the beginning.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 2:2–4)
