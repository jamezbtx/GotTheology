Passage

Isaiah 2

Verses 6–22 · ESV · Voices

After nations streaming to the word, the house of Jacob is full of eastern rites, Philistine fortune-tellers, silver, horses, and handmade idols. Man is brought low. Hide in the rock from the Lord’s terror. A day of the Lord of hosts against all that is proud—cedars, hills, towers, ships of Tarshish. The Lord alone is exalted; idols pass away; silver gods go to moles and bats. Stop regarding man, in whose nostrils is breath.

Isaiah 2:6

6For you have rejected your people, the house of Jacob, because they are full of things from the east and of fortune-tellers like the Philistines, and they strike hands with the children of foreigners.

Arminian

You have rejected your people, the house of Jacob: full of things from the east, fortune-tellers like the Philistines, striking hands with foreigners’ children. Wesley: the body of the nation; eastern idolatrous manners; Philistines infamous for those practices; leagues, friendships, and marriages.

Arminian rejected: historic Judah filling itself. The east is Syria and Chaldea, not a newspaper. They pleased themselves in strangers; the forsaking answers that filling. Real rites, real hands struck.

Reformed

Calvin: surely thou hast forsaken. Perverse disposition—idolatry, then just rejection. He still addresses them, even when he stands astonished. Full from the east: vices contracted from that quarter. Like the Philistines, though God had separated them. Children of strangers: foreign nations they preferred to God.

Reformed rejected: the forsaking is His just sentence on their filling. Shared: house of Jacob, east, fortune-tellers, foreigners. Silver is v. 7.

Isaiah 2:7

7Their land is filled with silver and gold, and there is no end to their treasures; their land is filled with horses, and there is no end to their chariots.

Arminian

Land filled with silver and gold, no end of treasures; filled with horses, no end of chariots. Wesley: they have heaped up riches and still greedily pursue more.

Arminian filled: real wealth in Judah’s days. The sin is the heap and the trust, not a culture-war on money or cavalry. Horses were a king’s forbidden stockpile (Deut 17).

Reformed

Calvin: not the things themselves but covetousness and sinful confidence. Insatiable eagerness—no end. Horses and chariots: God forbade kings a multitude lest they return to Egypt and trust the stable.

Reformed filled: false confidence in what the land holds. Shared: silver, gold, horses, chariots. Idols are v. 8.

Isaiah 2:8

8Their land is filled with idols; they bow down to the work of their hands, to what their own fingers have made.

Arminian

Land filled with idols; they bow down to the work of their hands, what their fingers made. The filling of v. 7 now has a god attached.

Arminian bow: real fingers, real bowing in Judah. Handmade gods are still chosen. The Holy One of 1:4 is traded for a carving.

Reformed

Calvin: idols are empty things (elilim). A block neglected, then finished by a mortal, is worshipped as Deity. He who bows before an idol professes divine worship. The work of their own hands aggravates the crime.

Reformed bow: they honor what they cannot confer on themselves. Shared: idols, hands, fingers. Humbled is v. 9.

Isaiah 2:9

9So man is humbled, and each one is brought low—do not forgive them!

Arminian

So man is humbled, and each one is brought low—do not forgive them! Wesley: men of all ranks fall down and worship idols.

Arminian humbled: the bow of v. 8 becomes the bringing-low. Do not forgive: a real plea over a nation’s unrepented idolatry, not a denial that snow was offered in 1:18. The body of the nation is in view.

Reformed

Calvin: mean and mighty—no rank free of the stain. Some read it as the coming ruin. Forgive them not: toward such obstinate men God will not be appeased. Tacit reservation of a remnant; he does not speak of every individual. Restoration of a new Church is not to be expected till judgments have destroyed the temple.

Reformed humbled: sentence on the obstinate, remnant not named here. Shared: man brought low. The rock is v. 10.

Isaiah 2:10

10Enter into the rock and hide in the dust from before the terror of the Lord, and from the splendor of his majesty.

Arminian

Enter into the rock and hide in the dust from before the terror of the Lord, and from the splendor of his majesty. Wesley: such calamities that you will be ready to hide in rocks and caves from glorious and terrible judgments.

Arminian enter: a real dread in historic invasion, not a millennial camp. Majesty is the Lord’s; the caves are Judah’s.

Reformed

Calvin: not an exhortation but a denunciation. The judgment is more to be dreaded than a thousand deaths. Dread of the Lord: the scourges; His magnificent glory: He is to be dreaded according to the measure of that glory.

Reformed enter: terror is His appearing as judge. Shared: rock, dust, terror, majesty.

Isaiah 2:11

11The haughty looks of man shall be brought low, and the lofty pride of men shall be humbled, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day.

Arminian

Haughty looks brought low, lofty pride humbled, the Lord alone exalted in that day. The day is His, not a newspaper cycle.

Arminian looks: real pride in real faces. The exaltation of the Lord does not empty the looks of being theirs before they fall.

Reformed

Calvin: wicked men, relying on wealth, regard threatenings with disdain. The time will come when this pride is brought down. When crimes pass unpunished, a cloud hides His glory; vengeance makes it shine. He alone exalted: they thought themselves higher than God.

Reformed looks: He lays low that He alone may be seen. Shared: haughty, humbled, Lord alone, that day.

Isaiah 2:12

12For the Lord of hosts has a day against all that is proud and lofty, against all that is lifted up—and it shall be brought low;

Arminian

The Lord of hosts has a day against all that is proud and lofty, all that is lifted up—it shall be brought low. Wesley: the time of God’s taking vengeance upon sinners.

Arminian day: historic vengeance on Judah’s pride, which also names how God always meets the proud. Not a millennial camp fight, not a rapture chart. Shared: a day that is His.

Reformed

Calvin: God is the enemy of all the proud. He appoints a day, as in judicial trials, on which proud men are summoned. If our minds were convinced of this, who would not abhor pride?

Reformed day: previously appointed, not a surprise to Him. Shared: Lord of hosts, proud, lofty, brought low. Cedars follow.

Isaiah 2:13

13against all the cedars of Lebanon, lofty and lifted up; and against all the oaks of Bashan;

Arminian

Against all the cedars of Lebanon, lofty and lifted up; against all the oaks of Bashan. Wesley: cedars and oaks thrown down by wind, earthquake, or lightning; stately houses built of them destroyed.

Arminian cedars: real trees and the houses they became. Pride used what Lebanon grew. Historic timber, not a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: allegory—mortal wishes never reach the height of mountains and trees, which God throws down as wind scatters leaves. He is not angry with cedars He made; the evil lies in men who trust what is high.

Reformed cedars: a painting of how idle proud confidence is. Shared: Lebanon, Bashan, lofty.

Isaiah 2:14

14against all the lofty mountains, and against all the uplifted hills;

Arminian

Against all the lofty mountains, and against all the uplifted hills. Wesley: hills to which men betake themselves in danger.

Arminian hills: real high ground in Judah’s map, and the trust parked there. The mountain of 2:2 is the Lord’s; these hills are rivals.

Reformed

Calvin: harmless creatures hold out the judgment, to convince the unprincipled that presumption will not pass unpunished.

Reformed hills: creation is not the culprit; trust in height is. Shared: mountains, hills, lifted up.

Isaiah 2:15

15against every high tower, and against every fortified wall;

Arminian

Against every high tower, and every fortified wall. Wesley: that to which you trusted for defence.

Arminian towers: real walls of Judah’s cities. Trust is the sin; masonry is the scene. No culture-war on fortresses.

Reformed

Calvin: towers and walls are not merely figurative. Men who think themselves well defended congratulate themselves that they no longer need God. He will throw down the defenses of false confidence.

Reformed towers: nests from which they look down on heaven. Shared: tower, wall.

Isaiah 2:16

16against all the ships of Tarshish, and against all the beautiful craft.

Arminian

Against all the ships of Tarshish, and against all the beautiful craft. Wesley: ships of the sea by which you fetched riches from remote parts.

Arminian ships: historic trade, not a newspaper on commerce. Remote gold funded the idols of v. 8.

Reformed

Calvin: Tarshish as the sea-trade that fed wealth. Navigation is not condemned in itself; abundance leads to pride, and distant trade to covetousness. Beautiful pictures: luxury that follows riches.

Reformed ships: the haul becomes the trust. Shared: Tarshish, beautiful craft. Haughtiness again in v. 17.

Isaiah 2:17

17And the haughtiness of man shall be humbled, and the lofty pride of men shall be brought low, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day.

Arminian

Haughtiness of man humbled, lofty pride brought low, the Lord alone exalted in that day. The refrain of v. 11 after the catalogue.

Arminian again: the same day, the same pride, still theirs until it falls. The Lord alone—shared confession.

Reformed

Calvin: he had his eye on men when he described loftiness. The Lord will not cease to inflict punishment till haughtiness is brought low, even if reprobate hearts are not changed.

Reformed again: pride is the target; He alone is left standing. Shared: humbled, Lord alone, that day.

Isaiah 2:18

18And the idols shall utterly pass away.

Arminian

And the idols shall utterly pass away. The fillings of vv. 7–8 meet a day that empties them.

Arminian pass: handmade gods do not survive the day. Judah actually has to lose them. Not a fake warning.

Reformed

Calvin: as he joined idolatry with luxury and covetousness in the reproof, he joins them in the punishment. Idols abolished.

Reformed pass: He removes what fingers made. Shared: idols utterly gone. Caves are v. 19.

Isaiah 2:19

19And people shall enter the caves of the rocks and the holes of the ground, from before the terror of the Lord, and from the splendor of his majesty, when he rises to terrify the earth.

Arminian

People enter caves of the rocks and holes of the ground from the terror of the Lord and the splendor of His majesty, when He rises to terrify the earth. Wesley: the idolatrous Israelites.

Arminian enter: the invitation of v. 10 becomes what they will do. Historic hiding in Judah’s limestone, under a terror that is His. Not a millennial camp.

Reformed

Calvin: repetition because it is hard to impress sincere fear. They choose to be swallowed by gulfs rather than come under His eye. Arise: He appears to sleep while He delays; He arises as judge. The Chaldeans and Assyrians promote His glory even as scourges.

Reformed enter: when He rises, the earth is shaken by His hand, including the empires He uses. Shared: caves, terror, majesty, rises.

Isaiah 2:20

20In that day mankind will cast away their idols of silver and their idols of gold, which they made for themselves to worship, to the moles and to the bats,

Arminian

In that day mankind will cast away idols of silver and gold, which they made for themselves to worship, to the moles and to the bats. Wesley: into the meanest and darkest places, where moles and bats abide.

Arminian cast: a real throwing when the terror lands. They made them; they will not keep them. Silver from v. 7 goes underground.

Reformed

Calvin: idolaters are not ashamed till visited by sore distress. The work of their hands is not entitled to adoration: what sort of gods are made by men? Moles’ holes: filthy places for a disgraceful concealment.

Reformed cast: adversity unmasks the cloak. Shared: silver, gold, moles, bats.

Isaiah 2:21

21to enter the caverns of the rocks and the clefts of the cliffs, from before the terror of the Lord, and from the splendor of his majesty, when he rises to terrify the earth.

Arminian

To enter caverns of the rocks and clefts of the cliffs, from before the terror of the Lord and the splendor of His majesty, when He rises to terrify the earth. The same rising as v. 19; the idols are already thrown.

Arminian caverns: still hiding, now without the silver gods. Majesty has not lessened.

Reformed

Calvin: Christ takes this threatening for the Jews: they shall say to the mountains, Cover us (Luke 23:30). The ungodly prefer the cleft to His eye.

Reformed caverns: the same day, the same rise. Shared: rocks, cliffs, terror, majesty.

Isaiah 2:22

22Stop regarding man in whose nostrils is breath, for of what account is he?

Arminian

Stop regarding man in whose nostrils is breath, for of what account is he? Wesley: never admire or place your trust in man; breath quickly stopped; what excellency is in him, considered in himself, and without dependence on God.

Arminian stop: a real command to Judah after the catalogue of pride. Man’s breath is frail; dependence on God is not canceled. They must cease from the prince, the soothsayer, and themselves as saviors—creaturely under a King who alone is exalted. Match the hub: a King who governs, leaving this ceasing as a real second cause.

Reformed

Calvin: cease from man—you are blinded by false hope because you claim too much for yourselves. Breath in the nostrils: life is a breath that vanishes. Begin with ourselves: do not trust our own wisdom or industry. This throws down the pompous applauses of free-will and merits. As soon as men claim the smallest thing, let them perceive they are nothing. We are brought back to God, the Author of every blessing.

Reformed stop: not trusting man includes not trusting our will as a rival to grace. Shared: breath in the nostrils, of what account.

Key contrast Both confess man is breath, and the Lord alone is to be regarded after a day that humbles pride (2:11, 17, 22). Arminians typically hear a real ceasing from creaturely trust—no excellency in man without dependence on God (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear the line as throwing down free-will and merits: begin by not trusting our own wisdom; men are nothing; God alone is the Author of every blessing (Calvin).

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a rejected house, a day against pride, and a man whose breath is in his nostrils—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 2:6–22.

Arminian Notable voices

“Thou—wilt certainly forsake and reject. Because—their land is full of the idolatrous manners of the eastern nations. They please—they delight in their company, making leagues, friendships, and marriages with them. The great man—men of all ranks fall down and worship idols. The day—the time of God’s taking vengeance upon sinners. Cease ye—never admire or place your trust in man. Breath—whose breath is quickly stopped and taken away. Wherein—what excellency is in him, considered in himself, and without dependence on God.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 2:6–22)

Reformed Notable voices

“Surely thou hast forsaken thy people. Why should I waste words on a nation grown desperate, which thou, O Lord, hast justly rejected? Cease therefore from man, whose breath is in his nostrils. Let us begin with ourselves; that is, let us not in any respect trust to our own wisdom or industry. This single expression throws down the pompous applauses of free-will and merits, by which papists extol themselves in opposition to the grace of God. As soon as men begin to make the smallest claim for themselves, they ought to perceive their vanity, that they may acknowledge themselves to be nothing.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 2:6, 22)
