Passage

Isaiah 41

Verses 21–29 · ESV · Voices

The LORD, King of Jacob, summons the idols to court: set forth your case, bring your proofs, tell us the former things and the things to come, that we may know you are gods. They cannot do good or harm; they are nothing, and an abomination is he who chooses them. God stirred one from the north and from the rising of the sun to trample rulers as mortar; none of the images declared it beforehand. Zion is given a herald of good news, and the metal images are empty wind.

Isaiah 41:21

21Set forth your case, says the LORD; bring your proofs, says the King of Jacob.

Arminian

Set forth your case, says the LORD; bring your proofs, says the King of Jacob. Wesley’s note is little more than the summons itself: produce your cause. The King of Jacob calls the idols into court; they must bring proofs, not slogans.

Shared: God is advocate and King. Do not invent a deity still collecting evidence from events. Classical Arminians hear a real court: the images may speak, and they will lose.

Reformed

Plead your cause. Calvin: God as advocate for the nation against mockery. King of Jacob: God will be successful if impartial judges tried the cause. He does not actually submit to men or angels as judges.

Reformed: the plea is demonstration, not a surrender of the throne. Shared with Wesley: the LORD summons; proofs must be brought. Match the hub: God declares former things; idols cannot.

Isaiah 41:22

22Let them bring them, and tell us what is to happen. Tell us the former things, what they are, that we may consider them, that we may know their outcome; or declare to us the things to come.

Arminian

Let them bring them, and tell us what is to happen. Tell us the former things, that we may know their outcome; or declare the things to come. Wesley: them—the idols. Former things—such things as should shortly come to pass. The latter end—whether the events answer to their predictions.

Shared: God declares the future; idols cannot. Do not invent open theism as Arminian. Classical Arminians still confess the God who foretells; they do not smuggle a deity who learns the future from events.

Reformed

Let them bring the gods; let them tell us what is to come. Calvin: true religion is distinguished by a rule that cannot deceive. Almighty power and foreknowledge of all things are peculiar to the Godhead; whoever knows all and can do all is God. Idols know nothing past or future, can do nothing in prosperity or adversity, therefore are not gods. Heathen oracles were obscure and doubtful (Pyrrhus, Croesus); Satan was permitted to deceive. God revealed to the elect people what is profitable.

Reformed: foreknowledge is not a luxury of the Godhead; it is how you tell God from an image. Shared with Wesley: former things and things to come belong to the LORD. No fork: both camps refuse a god who waits on events to find out.

Isaiah 41:23

23Tell us what is to come hereafter, that we may know that you are gods; do good, or do harm, that we may be dismayed and terrified.

Arminian

Tell us what is to come hereafter, that we may know that you are gods; do good, or do harm, that we may be dismayed and terrified. Wesley: do good—protect your worshippers whom I intend to destroy, and destroy my people whom I intend to save. That I and my people may be astonished, and forced to acknowledge your godhead.

Shared: the test of deity is to declare and to act. Do not invent open theism: Wesley’s God already intends to destroy and to save; the idols cannot reverse it. God rules; the images cannot.

Reformed

Do good or do evil. Calvin: not commit injustice; it means inflict punishment and send adversity, which belongs to providence (Amos 3:6; Lamentations 3:38)—wars, pestilence, famine. Tell us hereafter, that we may know you are gods.

Reformed: good and harm are providence, not a dare to commit injustice. Shared with Wesley: if they cannot tell or act, they are not gods. The hub holds: omnipotence and foreknowledge belong to the LORD.

Isaiah 41:24

24Behold, you are nothing, and your work is less than nothing; an abomination is he who chooses you.

Arminian

Behold, you are nothing, and your work is less than nothing; an abomination is he who chooses you. Wesley: your work—your operations are like your beings: no reality in your beings, nor efficacy in your actions.

Shared: idols are nothing. Historic images of Babylon, not a living politician. He who chooses them chooses abomination; God rules, and the workshop cannot.

Reformed

Ye are of nothing. Calvin: quality, not material—gold or stone does not make a god. Paul: an idol is nothing (1 Corinthians 8:4). He hath chosen abomination: men cannot frame idols without framing abomination. Worship that is the result of choice is at variance with true godliness.

Reformed: nothingness is the idol’s nature, not its metal. Shared with Wesley: no reality, no efficacy. No fork: both camps empty the image.

Isaiah 41:25

25I stirred up one from the north, and he has come, from the rising of the sun, and he shall call upon my name; he shall trample on rulers as on mortar, as the potter treads clay.

Arminian

I stirred up one from the north, and he has come, from the rising of the sun, and he shall call upon my name; he shall trample on rulers as on mortar, as the potter treads clay. Wesley: raised—Cyrus, might be said to come from the north, because he was a Mede by his mother, as he was a Persian by his father; or because a great part of his army was gathered out of Media, which was northward in reference to Judea, and because Darius the Mede was joined with him. Proclaim—this Cyrus did in express, emphatical terms, Ezra 1:1,2. As on mortar—treading them down as easily as a man treads down mortar.

Historic Cyrus, the Persian king—not a modern newspaper proxy. Shared: God stirred him; potter treads clay. Omnipotence here is why the idols’ silence in v. 26 matters.

Reformed

I have raised him from the north. Calvin: some explain Cyrus, others Christ; but he takes two things—from the north the Babylonians, from the east the Medes and Persians. Two changes: God raises the Babylonian empire, then Persians become their masters. Captivity and deliverance are His work. Kings are clay. He shall call on my name: Cyrus’s intention was not to serve Israel’s God, but God led him in secret; the event showed it (Ezra). Potter treads clay: Babylon’s power no obstacle.

Reformed: two historic empires, both stirred by God. Shared with Wesley: the LORD raised them; clay cannot resist the potter. Neither camp reads a living politician here.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take the one from the north and the rising of the sun as one man, Cyrus the Persian: Mede by his mother, Persian by his father; a great part of his army gathered out of Media, northward from Judea; Darius the Mede joined with him; he called upon the LORD’s name in express terms (Ezra 1:1–2). Reformed readings, with Calvin, take two historic movements: from the north the Babylonians raised as scourge, from the east the Medes and Persians who become their masters (he notes some say Cyrus or Christ, but he takes the two empires). Shared: God stirred them; kings are clay the potter treads; the idols did not declare it. Both stay in Babylon, Media, and Persia.

Isaiah 41:26

26Who declared it from the beginning, that we might know, and beforehand, that we might say, “He is right”? There was none who declared it, none who proclaimed, none who heard your words.

Arminian

Who declared it from the beginning, that we might know, and beforehand, that we might say, “He is right”? There was none who declared it, none who proclaimed, none who heard your words. Wesley: who—which of your idols could foretel such things from the beginning of the world unto this day? Before-time—before the things come to pass. Righteous—his cause is good: he is a God indeed. Heareth—because you are dumb and cannot speak.

Shared: God declares beforehand; idols are dumb. Do not invent open theism as Arminian. Wesley’s God foretold from the beginning; the images never spoke.

Reformed

Who hath declared from the beginning? Calvin: God claims this for himself; idols leave worshippers in suspense. We will say he is righteous—approved.

Reformed: the rightness of God is known because He spoke first. Shared with Wesley: none declared, none proclaimed, none heard the idols. No fork: both camps put foreknowledge on the LORD, not on events teaching Him.

Isaiah 41:27

27I was the first to say to Zion, “Behold, here they are!” and I give to Jerusalem a herald of good news.

Arminian

I was the first to say to Zion, “Behold, here they are!” and I give to Jerusalem a herald of good news. Wesley: the first—I who am the first, do and will foretel to my people things to come. Them—I also represent future things as if they were present. One—messengers who shall foretel the good tidings of their deliverance from captivity.

Shared: Zion hears good news of deliverance from captivity. God is first to speak; the herald does not invent the news. Historic return from Babylon, not a millennial chart.

Reformed

The first to Zion. Calvin: comfort to desolate Zion. Messenger (mebashsher): Jerome “Evangelist”; prophets who bring the cheering message of deliverance. Commendation of preaching. Behold they come.

Reformed: preaching is God’s gift to Jerusalem. Shared with Wesley: messengers, good tidings of deliverance. They agree enough: prophets and heralds of the return, not a fork between camps.

Isaiah 41:28

28But when I look, there is no one; among these there is no counselor who, when I ask, gives an answer.

Arminian

But when I look, there is no one; among these there is no counselor who, when I ask, gives an answer. Wesley: I looked to see if I could find any man that could foretel future events. No man—not any of the idols (man sometimes used of lifeless things). No counsellor—idols often consulted, none able to give solid certain advice concerning future things.

Shared: no counselor among the images. Historic consultation of Babylon’s gods, not a living cabinet. God asks; they cannot answer.

Reformed

I looked and there was none. Calvin: no counselor among the idols. Comparison: God speaks by prophets and works; idols cannot.

Reformed: the silence of the workshop versus the God who speaks. Shared with Wesley: none answers. Idol nothingness, not an Arminian/Reformed split.

Isaiah 41:29

29Behold, they are all a delusion; their works are nothing; their metal images are empty wind.

Arminian

Behold, they are all a delusion; their works are nothing; their metal images are empty wind. Wesley: conclusion of the dispute; under these he comprehends all images. Wind—empty and unsatisfying. Confusion—confused and useless, like the rude heap at the beginning of creation; this very word in Gen 1:2.

Shared: empty wind, tohu. God declared; the images are chaos. Historic metal images of the nations, never a newspaper.

Reformed

All vanity. Calvin: worshippers like the idols (Psalm 115:8). Works a failure. Wind and chaos (tohu, Genesis 1:2). Images quench true knowledge.

Reformed: the images empty their makers too. Shared with Wesley: wind, tohu, the rude heap of Gen 1:2. Conclusion of the dispute: the LORD spoke; they did not.
