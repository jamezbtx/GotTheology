Passage

Isaiah 44

Verses 21–28 · ESV · Voices

After 44:9–20—fashioners of idols are nothing; craftsmen only human; the same wood for fire and for a god; they pray Deliver me to a block of wood; eyes shut so they cannot see; a deluded heart cannot say, Is there not a lie in my right hand?—the word turns: Remember these things, O Jacob, and Israel, for you are my servant. I formed you; you will not be forgotten; transgressions blotted like a cloud; return, for I have redeemed you. Heavens, earth, mountains, and forest sing: the LORD has redeemed Jacob. Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, who formed you from the womb: I am the LORD who made all things, who frustrates the signs of liars, who confirms the word of his servant, who says of Jerusalem, She shall be inhabited, who says to the deep, Be dry, who says of Cyrus, He is my shepherd, and he shall fulfill all my purpose; Jerusalem built; the temple’s foundation laid.

Isaiah 44:21

21Remember these things, O Jacob, and Israel, for you are my servant; I formed you; you are my servant; O Israel, you will not be forgotten by me.

Arminian

Remember these things, O Jacob, and Israel, for you are my servant; I formed you; you are my servant; O Israel, you will not be forgotten by me. Wesley: These—These things, the deep ignorance and stupidity of idolaters. Forgotten—I will not forget nor forsake thee; therefore thou shalt have no need of idols.

Arminian hearing: remember the idolaters’ stupidity; Forgotten is the LORD’s not-forgetting, so Israel has no need of idols. Shared: Remember these things, O Jacob; my servant; I formed you; you will not be forgotten. Historic Jacob after 44:9–20’s satire, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “These” and “Forgotten” are his lemmas for “Remember these things” and “you will not be forgotten by me.” No open theism: he formed them; they will not be forgotten. Match v. 22: the one who will not forget is the one who blots out transgressions. Match 44:9–20: these things are the fashioners’ nothing and the lie in the right hand.

Reformed

Remember these things, O Jacob. Calvin: apply the idol satire so they are not corrupted in Babylon. Thou art my servant; I have formed thee: regenerated and sanctified, heir of eternal life. Do not thou forget me: forgetfulness of God is the cause of apostasy; error never prevails so long as remembrance of God is rooted.

Reformed hearing: formed is regeneration of the servant-heir; do not forget me—forgetfulness is the cause of apostasy. Shared with Wesley: Remember these things, O Jacob; my servant; I formed you. Historic Israel in Babylon’s shadow, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Do not thou forget me” is his lemma for “you will not be forgotten by me,” turning the verb toward Israel’s remembrance, not a different verse. No open theism: he formed the servant; remembrance is rooted. Match v. 2: Maker as regeneration of the elect continues here. Match v. 24: Redeemer who formed from the womb.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “these things” as the deep ignorance and stupidity of idolaters, and “Forgotten” as I will not forget nor forsake thee—therefore thou shalt have no need of idols. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take “I have formed thee” as regenerated and sanctified, servant and heir of eternal life, and “Do not thou forget me” as forgetfulness of God being the cause of apostasy—error never prevails so long as remembrance of God is rooted. Shared: Remember these things, O Jacob; you are my servant; I formed you; Israel will not be forgotten. Historic Jacob after the idol satire, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 44:22

22I have blotted out your transgressions like a cloud and your sins like mist; return to me, for I have redeemed you.

Arminian

I have blotted out your transgressions like a cloud and your sins like mist; return to me, for I have redeemed you. Wesley: As a cloud—So that there is no remnant of it left.

Arminian hearing: blotting leaves no remnant, as a cloud is gone; return, for I have redeemed you. Shared: transgressions blotted like a cloud; sins like mist; return; I have redeemed you. Historic Jacob redeemed, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “As a cloud” is his lemma for “like a cloud…like mist.” Prefer no keyContrast: Wesley’s no-remnant cloud and Calvin’s guilt-and-punishment-both-remitted overlap. No open theism: he has blotted; he has redeemed. Match v. 21: not forgotten, now blotted. Match v. 23: the LORD has redeemed Jacob, so the heavens sing.

Reformed

I have blotted out as a cloud thy iniquities. Calvin: future deliverance; hearts cannot be raised unless God is perceived reconciled. Guilt remitted, therefore punishment remitted (overthrows those who grant guilt remitted but deny punishment remitted). Return thou to me; For I have redeemed thee: confirmation that they will return though banishment makes them doubt.

Reformed hearing: blotting is reconciliation perceived, so hearts rise; guilt remitted means punishment remitted; return is confirmed though exile makes them doubt. Shared with Wesley: blotted like a cloud; no remnant; return, for I have redeemed you. Historic exile and return, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “thy iniquities” is his lemma for “your transgressions…your sins.” Prefer no keyContrast: the no-remnant cloud and both-remitted overlap. No open theism: he has blotted; he has redeemed. Match v. 21: the servant not forgotten. Match v. 23: redeemed Jacob, heavens sing.

Isaiah 44:23

23Sing, O heavens, for the LORD has done it; shout, O depths of the earth; break forth into singing, O mountains, O forest, and every tree in it! For the LORD has redeemed Jacob, and will be glorified in Israel.

Arminian

Sing, O heavens, for the LORD has done it; shout, O depths of the earth; break forth into singing, O mountains, O forest, and every tree in it! For the LORD has redeemed Jacob, and will be glorified in Israel. Wesley: Sing—By such invitations to the senseless creatures, he signifies the transcendent greatness of this mercy, sufficient to make even the stones, if it were possible, to break forth into God’s praises.

Arminian hearing: even stones would praise if they could; the mercy is that great. Shared: heavens, depths, mountains, forest, every tree; the LORD has redeemed Jacob; he will be glorified in Israel. Historic redemption of Jacob, not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “stones” names the same senseless creatures as heavens, earth, mountains, and forest. Prefer no keyContrast: Wesley’s stones-would-praise and Calvin’s Church-from-the-world overlap as the greatness of this redemption; do not slide into a millennial/mission chart. Match v. 22: I have redeemed you. Match v. 15: the same forest whose trees were fire and a god now sings.

Reformed

Praise, O ye heavens. Calvin: dictate a song as if already delivered. Heaven and earth and mountains and forest. For Jehovah hath redeemed Jacob: not only return to native country, but the end included—God collect under one head a Church taken out of the whole world.

Reformed hearing: a song as if already delivered; return to the land, and the end included—a Church gathered under one head from the whole world. Shared with Wesley: heavens and earth praise; the LORD has redeemed Jacob. Historic return plus the Church gathered, not a newspaper, not a dispensational chart. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Praise, O ye heavens” and “Jehovah hath redeemed Jacob” are his lemmas for “Sing, O heavens” and “the LORD has redeemed Jacob.” Prefer no keyContrast on 23: both camps hear the greatness of this redemption; do not invent a millennial fight. Match v. 22: redeemed. Match v. 28: Cyrus the shepherd for that return.

Isaiah 44:24

24Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, who formed you from the womb: “I am the LORD, who made all things, who alone stretched out the heavens, who spread out the earth by myself,

Arminian

Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, who formed you from the womb: I am the LORD, who made all things, who alone stretched out the heavens, who spread out the earth by myself. Wesley has no extra lemma. Paraphrase from v. 21 honestly: I formed you; you are my servant; Forgotten—I will not forget nor forsake thee; therefore thou shalt have no need of idols. So here: Redeemer who formed you from the womb; I am the LORD who made all things, who alone stretched out the heavens. Do not invent unique Wesley sentences.

Arminian hearing: the same former of the servant now names himself Redeemer from the womb, maker of all things, heavens stretched by himself. Shared: Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer; formed from the womb; made all things; stretched out the heavens; spread out the earth by myself. Historic Redeemer of Israel, not a newspaper. Prefer no keyContrast: skip rather than invent Wesley prose against Calvin’s regeneration-from-the-womb. No open theism: he made all things; he alone stretched the heavens. Match v. 21: I formed you. Match v. 2: formed from the womb. Match v. 25: the same LORD frustrates liars.

Reformed

Thus saith Jehovah thy Redeemer and Maker from the womb. Calvin: not first creation only but regeneration of those adopted. Past redemption (Egypt) to expect continual advancement. Who alone stretcheth out the heavens: government of the whole world; power united to his word.

Reformed hearing: Redeemer and Maker from the womb is regeneration of the adopted, not first creation only; Egypt’s past redemption grounds continual advancement; stretching the heavens is government of the world, power united to his word. Shared with Wesley: Redeemer; formed from the womb; made all things; heavens stretched alone. Historic Redeemer of Israel and Egypt, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Maker from the womb” is his lemma for “who formed you from the womb.” Prefer no keyContrast: Wesley has no extra lemma here; Calvin’s regeneration-from-the-womb already forked at 44:2 and v. 21. Match v. 21: formed as regenerated servant. Match v. 25–28: the same word frustrates liars, confirms his servant, names Cyrus.

Isaiah 44:25

25who frustrates the signs of liars and makes fools of diviners, who turns wise men back and makes their knowledge foolish,

Arminian

who frustrates the signs of liars and makes fools of diviners, who turns wise men back and makes their knowledge foolish. Wesley: Liars—Of the magicians, and astrologers, who were numerous, and greatly esteemed in Babylon, and who had foretold the long continuance and prosperity of the Chaldean empire. Mad—With grief for the disappointment of their predictions, and their disgrace which followed it. Turneth—Stopping their way, and blasting their designs.

Arminian hearing: Babylon’s magicians and astrologers, esteemed, had foretold Chaldean continuance; he makes them mad with grief when the signs fail; he stops their way. Shared: signs of liars frustrated; diviners made fools; wise men turned back; their knowledge foolish. Historic Babylon magicians, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Liars,” “Mad,” and “Turneth” are his lemmas for “liars,” “makes fools of diviners,” and “turns wise men back.” Prefer no keyContrast: both camps hear historic Babylon astrologers. No open theism: he frustrates what they signed; events will not agree with them. Match v. 24: the LORD who made all things. Match v. 26: he confirms his servant’s word against those unfounded signs.

Reformed

Frustrating the signs of liars. Calvin: Babylon surpassed in arms AND in pretended sagacity—astrologers/Chaldeans, judicial astrology. Makes diviners mad. Condemns using stars to predict governments and events; God remains judge of the world.

Reformed hearing: Chaldean astrologers’ pretended sagacity; diviners made mad; stars do not judge governments; God remains judge of the world. Shared with Wesley: Babylon magicians and astrologers; their signs frustrated; diviners mad. Historic Babylon magicians, not a newspaper, not a living politician. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Frustrating the signs of liars” and “Makes diviners mad” are his lemmas for “who frustrates the signs of liars and makes fools of diviners.” Prefer no keyContrast: 25 is shared historic satire of Babylon’s diviners. No millennial fight. Match v. 9–20: the same lie in the right hand. Match v. 26: prophets versus Chaldean predictions.

Isaiah 44:26

26who confirms the word of his servant and fulfills the counsel of his messengers, who says of Jerusalem, ‘She shall be inhabited,’ and of the cities of Judah, ‘They shall be built, and I will raise up their ruins’;

Arminian

who confirms the word of his servant and fulfills the counsel of his messengers, who says of Jerusalem, She shall be inhabited, and of the cities of Judah, They shall be built, and I will raise up their ruins. Wesley has no extra lemma. Paraphrase from v. 25 and v. 28 honestly: magicians and astrologers of Babylon had foretold the long continuance of the Chaldean empire; Cyrus is named two hundred years before he was born as undeniable evidence of the exactness of God’s fore-knowledge, and a convincing argument to conclude this dispute between God and idols. So here: he confirms the word of his servant against those signs; Jerusalem shall be inhabited; Judah built. Do not invent unique Wesley sentences.

Arminian hearing: the servant’s word stands; the magicians’ Chaldean continuance does not; Jerusalem inhabited, cities of Judah built, ruins raised. Shared: confirms the word of his servant; counsel of his messengers; Jerusalem inhabited; Judah built; ruins raised. Historic Jerusalem and Judah, not a newspaper. Prefer no keyContrast: skip rather than invent Wesley prose. No open theism: he confirms his servant’s word; he says she shall be inhabited. Match v. 25: liars frustrated. Match v. 28: Cyrus the shepherd; temple foundation laid.

Reformed

Confirming the word of his servant. Calvin: prophets versus Chaldean unfounded predictions. Promise: Babylon shall fall, my people restored. Jerusalem shall be inhabited; cities of Judah built. Church will be raised when it appears ruinous.

Reformed hearing: the servant is the prophet against Chaldean predictions; Babylon falls, the people restored; Jerusalem inhabited; the Church raised when it looks ruinous. Shared with Wesley: confirms his servant’s word; Jerusalem inhabited; Judah built. Historic Jerusalem and Judah after Babylon, not a newspaper, not a millennial timetable. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Confirming the word of his servant” is his lemma for “who confirms the word of his servant.” Prefer no keyContrast: both hear the prophets’ word against the diviners, and Jerusalem rebuilt. Match v. 25: liars frustrated. Match v. 28: Cyrus, Jerusalem built, temple founded.

Isaiah 44:27

27who says to the deep, ‘Be dry; I will dry up your rivers’;

Arminian

who says to the deep, Be dry; I will dry up your rivers. Wesley: That saith—That with a word can dry up the sea and rivers, and remove all impediments.

Arminian hearing: a word dries the sea and rivers and removes all impediments. Shared: says to the deep, Be dry; rivers dried. Historic deep and rivers, not a newspaper canal. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “That saith” is his lemma for “who says to the deep”; “sea and rivers” names the same deep. Prefer no keyContrast: Wesley’s dry-the-sea impediments and Calvin’s Red Sea / unexpected change overlap. No open theism: he says Be dry; the rivers will be dry. Match v. 26: ruins raised, now impediments dried. Match v. 28: the way opened for Cyrus the shepherd.

Reformed

Saying to the deep, Be dry. Calvin: not limited only to Babylon as allegory; unexpected change, people drawn as from the depths. Alludes to the Red Sea (Exod 14)—I did this for your fathers.

Reformed hearing: not Babylon-only allegory; unexpected change; people drawn from the depths; the Red Sea for the fathers is the type of this drying. Shared with Wesley: a word dries the deep and rivers; impediments removed. Historic Exodus and the coming drying, not a newspaper canal. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Saying to the deep, Be dry” is his lemma for “who says to the deep, ‘Be dry.’” Prefer no keyContrast: Red Sea and dry rivers overlap as the same LORD removing impediments. Match v. 24: he who stretched the heavens. Match v. 28: Cyrus comes on that dried way.

Isaiah 44:28

28who says of Cyrus, ‘He is my shepherd, and he shall fulfill all my purpose’; saying of Jerusalem, ‘She shall be built,’ and of the temple, ‘Your foundation shall be laid.’”

Arminian

who says of Cyrus, He is my shepherd, and he shall fulfill all my purpose; saying of Jerusalem, She shall be built, and of the temple, Your foundation shall be laid. Wesley: Cyrus—Whom God here mentions by his proper name, two hundred years before he was born, that this might be an undeniable evidence of the exactness of God’s fore-knowledge, and a convincing argument to conclude this dispute between God and idols. Shepherd—Then will I set up to be the shepherd of my people, to rescue them from wolves or tyrants, to gather them together, to rule them gently, and to provide comfortably for them.

Arminian hearing: Cyrus named two hundred years before birth proves exact fore-knowledge against idols; shepherd means he will rescue, gather, rule gently, and provide. Shared loudly: Cyrus named before birth; he is my shepherd; he shall fulfill all my purpose; Jerusalem built; the temple’s foundation laid. Historic Persian Cyrus, not a living politician, not a newspaper, not the Christ-Servant of 42:1–9. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “two hundred years before he was born” is his dating of this naming, not a different verse. No open theism: he named Cyrus; events will agree. Prefer no keyContrast: both camps name historic Cyrus before birth as proof against idols. Match v. 25–26: liars frustrated; the servant’s word confirmed. Match v. 21: Remember these things—the dispute with idols ends here.

Reformed

Saying to Cyrus. Calvin: named long before he was born (more than a century after Isaiah/Manasseh). Who would have conjectured he should come from the most distant mountains of Persia to Babylon? Shepherd of my people to bring them back. Jerusalem built; temple founded. Later “living temples” (2 Cor 6:16) is application; the verse’s referent is historic Cyrus and the Jerusalem temple.

Reformed hearing: Cyrus named long before birth, from Persia’s mountains to Babylon; shepherd to bring the people back; Jerusalem built; this temple’s foundation laid. Shared loudly with Wesley: historic Cyrus named before birth; my shepherd; all my purpose; Jerusalem built; temple founded. Historic Persian king, not a living politician, not a newspaper, not the Christ-Servant of 42:1–9, not a millennial fork on the temple. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “more than a century after Isaiah/Manasseh” is his dating; Wesley’s “two hundred years” is his; both mean named before birth. No open theism: he named Cyrus; he shall fulfill all my purpose. Prefer no keyContrast on 28: both camps share this as proof of foreknowledge against idols. Match v. 26: Jerusalem inhabited. Match v. 23: the LORD has redeemed Jacob.

Voices from both camps

Reformed Notable voices
