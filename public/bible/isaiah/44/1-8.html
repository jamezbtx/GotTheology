Passage

Isaiah 44

Verses 1–8 · ESV · Voices

After 43:22–28—Jacob weary of the LORD, empty cult, sins that wearied him, blotting out for his own sake, an empty courtroom, first father and mediators, princes of the sanctuary profaned and Jacob delivered to destruction—the word turns: But now hear, O Jacob my servant, Israel whom I have chosen. The LORD who made them and formed them from the womb says Fear not to Jacob and Jeshurun; he will pour water on the thirsty land and his Spirit upon their offspring, and they shall spring up like willows by streams. One will say, I am the LORD’s; another will name himself by Israel. Then the King of Israel and his Redeemer: I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god; you are my witnesses; there is no Rock.

Isaiah 44:1

1“But now hear, O Jacob my servant, Israel whom I have chosen!

Arminian

But now hear, O Jacob my servant, Israel whom I have chosen. Wesley has no extra lemma on this verse. Paraphrase from 43:22’s turn and v. 2 honestly: after 43’s wearying and “Therefore—I have exposed them to contempt and destruction,” the word turns—But now hear, O Jacob my servant, Israel whom I have chosen—the same people he has, in v. 2, been forming from the time he first took them to be his people. Do not invent unique Wesley sentences here.

Arminian hearing: the “But now” is a turn from exposure to the chosen servant, not a new Wesley paragraph. Shared: Jacob my servant; Israel whom I have chosen. Historic Jacob and Israel, not a newspaper. Do not invent open theism: the chosen servant is already named; he is not discovering whom to choose. Prefer no keyContrast: Calvin’s remnant-after-severity is thicker than Wesley’s thin turn; skip the fork rather than invent Wesley prose. Match v. 2: formed and Jeshurun name the same people now told to hear.

Reformed

Yet now hear. Calvin: mitigates the severity of ch. 43. Though grievous afflictions overtake thee, hear what I will do. God is never so angry with his Church as not to leave some room for mercy. Servant and Elect are synonymous, yet election comes first; David was God’s servant before he was born (Ps 22:10).

Reformed hearing: Yet now is mercy after 43’s severity; servant and elect name one people, election first; David servant before birth. Shared with Wesley: Jacob my servant; Israel chosen; hear now. Historic Church of Israel, not a newspaper. No millennial fight. Prefer no keyContrast with Wesley’s thin “But now hear”—do not invent Wesley prose to thicken the fork. Match v. 2: Maker as regeneration of the elect and formed from the womb continue this elect-servant line.

Isaiah 44:2

2Thus says the LORD who made you, who formed you from the womb and will help you: Fear not, O Jacob my servant, Jeshurun whom I have chosen.

Arminian

Thus says the LORD who made you, who formed you from the womb and will help you: Fear not, O Jacob my servant, Jeshurun whom I have chosen. Wesley: Formed thee—from the time that I first took thee to be my people, I have been forming and fashioning thee. Jesurun—another name of Jacob or Israel, given to him, Deut 32:15.

Arminian hearing: forming is national fashioning from the time he first took them as his people; Jesurun is Jacob’s other name (Deut 32:15). Shared: Fear not, O Jacob my servant; Jeshurun chosen; the LORD made and will help. Historic Jacob/Israel/Jeshurun, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Jesurun” is his spelling of the ESV’s “Jeshurun”; “Formed thee” from first taking them as people is his lemma for “formed you from the womb,” not a different verse. Match v. 1: the servant now told to hear is the people he has been fashioning.

Reformed

Thus saith the LORD that made thee. Calvin: Maker is not the first creation by which we are born human, but regeneration belonging to the elect, that they may obtain a place in the Church. Formed thee from the womb: hereditary covenant; God separated them before they were born (Rom 9:11); cuts off all merits. Will help thee. Jeshurun: Beloved (Deut 32:15), not merely Upright. Fear not: Luke 12:32; the Father’s good will.

Reformed hearing: Maker is regeneration of the elect; womb is hereditary covenant before birth, merits cut off; Jeshurun is Beloved; Fear not rests on the Father’s good will. Shared with Wesley: Jacob/Jeshurun chosen; Deut 32:15; Fear not. Historic Israel, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Beloved, not merely Upright” is his reading of Jeshurun, not a different English Bible. Match v. 1: election first in servant and elect continues here in womb and Rom 9:11.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “formed thee” as national fashioning from the time he first took them to be his people, and Jesurun as another name of Jacob or Israel given him in Deut 32:15. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take Maker not as the first creation by which we are born human but as regeneration belonging to the elect, that they may obtain a place in the Church; formed from the womb is the hereditary covenant—God separated them before they were born (Rom 9:11)—cutting off all merits; Jeshurun is Beloved, not merely Upright. Shared: Fear not, O Jacob my servant; Jeshurun whom I have chosen; the LORD made them and will help. Historic Israel and Deut 32:15, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 44:3

3For I will pour water on the thirsty land, and streams on the dry ground; I will pour my Spirit upon your offspring, and my blessing on your descendants.

Arminian

For I will pour water on the thirsty land, and streams on the dry ground; I will pour my Spirit upon your offspring, and my blessing on your descendants. Wesley: Water—upon him that is destitute of it.

Arminian hearing: the pour is water on the destitute; Spirit on offspring and blessing on descendants name the same gift. Shared: water on thirsty land; Spirit poured; blessing on descendants. Historic promise to Jacob’s offspring, not a newspaper revival or a dispensational chart. Do not invent a Pentecost-versus-millennium fork; Wesley’s lemma is water on the destitute, not a calendar. No open theism: he will pour what he has named. Match v. 4: the destitute who drink are the ones who spring like willows.

Reformed

I will pour waters. Calvin: people like a dry thirsty land (Ps 143:6). I will pour my Spirit: waters = Spirit. Not merely regeneration; alludes to universal grace that revives the face of the earth (Ps 104:30). Secret moisture of his power quickens souls. Blessing.

Reformed hearing: dry people drink; waters are the Spirit; secret moisture quickens souls; Ps 104:30 is the earth revived, not a newspaper revival. Shared with Wesley: water on the destitute / Spirit reviving a dry people. Historic promise to offspring, not a dispensational chart. Do not invent a Pentecost-versus-millennium fork; Calvin’s “not merely regeneration” is Spirit as water and earth revived, not a millennial timetable. Match v. 4: when the Spirit is sent, they spring up.

Isaiah 44:4

4They shall spring up among the grass like willows by flowing streams.

Arminian

They shall spring up among the grass like willows by flowing streams. Wesley has no extra lemma. Paraphrase from v. 3 honestly: water upon him that is destitute of it; Spirit poured on offspring and blessing on descendants is why they spring up among the grass like willows by flowing streams. Do not invent a unique Wesley sentence here.

Arminian hearing: destitute land drinks, then willows rise. Shared: they spring up; willows by streams. Historic offspring of Jacob, not a newspaper revival. Match v. 3: the water and Spirit poured there are why they spring here. Do not invent a millennial camp fight on grass and willows.

Reformed

They shall spring up as among grass, willows by the water courses. Calvin: when the Spirit is sent, the earth is renewed. Restoration of the Church; chief part is the new creature restoring God’s image in the elect. First explain the Prophet’s design (restoration), then the doctrine of regeneration may be drawn more copiously.

Reformed hearing: Spirit sent, earth renewed; restoration of the Church first, then regeneration drawn more copiously; chief part is the new creature in the elect. Shared with Wesley: they spring up like willows by water. Historic restoration of Israel’s Church, not a newspaper or a dispensational chart. No keyContrast: Calvin’s restoration-then-regeneration is method, not a fight with Wesley’s thin paraphrase from v. 3. Match v. 5: those who spring up will say, I am the LORD’s.

Isaiah 44:5

5This one will say, ‘I am the LORD’s,’ another will call on the name of Jacob, and another will write on his hand, ‘The LORD’s,’ and name himself by the name of Israel.”

Arminian

This one will say, I am the LORD’s; another will call on the name of Jacob, and another will write on his hand, The LORD’s, and name himself by the name of Israel. Wesley: Israel—the blessing of God upon the Jews shall be so remarkable, that the Gentiles shall join them, and accept the Lord for their God.

Arminian hearing: blessing on the Jews is so remarkable Gentiles join them and accept the Lord. Shared: one says I am the LORD’s; others take Jacob’s name; Gentiles come in. Historic Jews and Gentiles, not a newspaper. Prefer no keyContrast rather than a millennial fight: Wesley’s Gentiles joining the Jews and Calvin’s Church gathered from all nations overlap; shared christology and mission without a fork. Match v. 3–4: Spirit and willows are why names are written on the hand.

Reformed

One shall say, I am the LORD’s. Calvin: out of all nations the Lord will gather his people; strangers into the Church (Ps 87:4). Small number returned from captivity, but others from every quarter. Change that surpasses nature: out of Adam’s accursed race a spiritual Israel. True faith breaks forth into confession. None are lawful citizens of the Church but those who submit to the government of God.

Reformed hearing: gathered from all nations; strangers enrolled; spiritual Israel out of Adam’s race; confession is true faith; lawful citizens submit to God’s government. Shared with Wesley: Gentiles come in; they say I am the LORD’s. Historic return and nations, not a newspaper, not a millennial timetable. Prefer no keyContrast on 5: both camps hear Gentiles coming in; do not invent a millennial fight between Jews-joined and spiritual Israel. Match v. 6: the King of Israel is the one they name themselves by.

Isaiah 44:6

6Thus says the LORD, the King of Israel and his Redeemer, the LORD of hosts: “I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god.

Arminian

Thus says the LORD, the King of Israel and his Redeemer, the LORD of hosts: I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god. Wesley has no extra lemma on first and last. Hear the verse; do not invent Wesley prose. Shared sovereignty: the King of Israel and his Redeemer is first and last; besides him there is no god.

Arminian hearing: one LORD, no other god; first and last already is. Shared loudly with Calvin: sovereignty; no god besides. Historic King of Israel, not a newspaper. No open theism: he is first and last; he is not becoming god as events unfold. Match v. 7–8: heathen gods cannot declare; he told from of old; Israel is witness. No millennial fight.

Reformed

Thus saith Jehovah, the King of Israel and his Redeemer. Calvin: not enough to perceive power if we are not convinced of good-will. I am the first and the last: not merely asserting eternity, but that He is always like himself, so they may hope he will be to them in future what they have found in the past. We sometimes ascribe less to him than to the creature.

Reformed hearing: King and Redeemer name power with good-will; first and last means always like himself, hope from past to future. Shared with Wesley: I am the first and the last; besides me there is no god. Historic Redeemer of Israel, not a newspaper. No open theism: always like himself; not learning whether he will be their God. No keyContrast: 44:6–8 are shared sovereignty. Match v. 8: no Rock besides; they are witnesses.

Isaiah 44:7

7Who is like me? Let him proclaim it. Let him declare and set it before me, since I appointed an ancient people. Let them declare what is to come, and what will happen.

Arminian

Who is like me? Let him proclaim it. Let him declare and set it before me, since I appointed an ancient people. Let them declare what is to come, and what will happen. Wesley: Who—which of all the Heathen gods. Declare—shall by his powerful word cause it to be, and by his fore-knowledge declare that it shall be. Set in order—orderly relate all future events as they shall happen. Since—since I first made man upon earth. The things—near at hand, and to come hereafter.

Arminian hearing: heathen gods cannot cause or declare; he both makes it be and by fore-knowledge declares it shall be; all future events related as they shall happen. Shared: who is like me; he declares what is to come. Historic ancient people, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Set in order” is his lemma for “set it before me”; “Since—since I first made man upon earth” is his reading of “since I appointed an ancient people,” not a different verse; “The things” names near and hereafter. No open theism: fore-knowledge declares what shall be; events will agree. Match v. 8: witnesses of predictions and their agreeableness.

Reformed

Who as I shall call. Calvin: God versus idols. Call may be foreknowledge or action; Calvin prefers action—raise up, announce, appoint deliverers. Ancient people: his own people the Church, preferred to all others; antiquity estimated from election not years; all men appointed to be of service to the Church.

Reformed hearing: idols cannot call; he raises and appoints deliverers; ancient people is the Church, antiquity from election; all men of service to that Church. Shared with Wesley: God versus heathen gods; he declares what is to come. Historic Church of Israel, not a newspaper. No open theism: he appointed an ancient people and declares what is to come; he is not waiting to learn the future. No keyContrast: 44:6–8 are shared sovereignty; Calvin preferring action to mere foreknowledge is not a fight with Wesley’s cause-and-declare. Match v. 6: the first and last is the one who calls.

Isaiah 44:8

8Fear not, nor be afraid; have I not told you from of old and declared it? And you are my witnesses! Is there a God besides me? There is no Rock; I know not any.”

Arminian

Fear not, nor be afraid; have I not told you from of old and declared it? And you are my witnesses! Is there a God besides me? There is no Rock; I know not any. Wesley: Ye—thee, O Israel, whom he bids not to fear. Told thee—even from the first ages of the world. Declared—have published it to the world in my sacred records. Witnesses—both of my predictions, and of the exact agreeableness of events to them.

Arminian hearing: Israel is told not to fear; from the first ages he told; sacred records published it; they witness predictions and events that agree with them. Shared: Fear not; told from of old; you are my witnesses; no God besides; no Rock. Historic Israel as witnesses, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Ye” is thee, O Israel; “Told thee—even from the first ages of the world” is his lemma for “from of old,” not a different verse. No open theism: he told from of old; events agree with predictions. Match v. 6–7: first and last, no other god, heathen gods cannot declare.

Reformed

Fear not. Calvin: conclusion from his power. Have I not from then made thee hear: long period or fit season; he kept their faith alive by many predictions. Ye are my witnesses. No strong God besides me: not enough to acknowledge eternal essence if we do not also ascribe strength.

Reformed hearing: Fear not follows his power; many predictions kept faith alive; witnesses of those words; not enough to grant eternal essence without ascribing strength. Shared with Wesley: Fear not; told from of old; you are my witnesses; no God besides. Historic witnesses of Israel, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “No strong God besides me” is his lemma for “Is there a God besides me? There is no Rock,” not a different English Bible. No open theism: he declared it; they are witnesses. No keyContrast: 44:6–8 shared sovereignty. Match v. 2: Fear not, O Jacob, now concluded from this power.
