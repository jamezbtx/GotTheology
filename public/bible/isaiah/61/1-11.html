Passage

Isaiah 61

Verses 1–11 · ESV · Voices

The anointed Servant brings good news, liberty, and the year of the Lord’s favor with a day of vengeance, to comfort Zion’s mourners. Ruins rise; strangers tend flocks; the people are named priests under an everlasting covenant. Garments of salvation clothe the church. Jesus reads 61:1–2 at Nazareth (Luke 4:16–21).

Isaiah 61:1

1The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me, because the Lord has anointed me to bring good news to the poor; he has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to those who are bound;

Arminian

Wesley: though the prophet may speak of himself, yet it is principally to be understood of Christ. Anointed—gifts, commission, and a power to make all effectual. Liberty belongs to Christ’s kingly office: he proclaims liberty from the dominion of sin, and from the fear of hell.

Arminian offer: Luke 4:16–21 is the key. Jesus reads this as himself. The King actually proclaims liberty—sincerely held out to captives. Poor, brokenhearted, and bound may still come. Do not invent a millennial fight; both camps take the Speaker as Christ.

Reformed

Calvin: Christ explains this of himself (Luke 4:18). The Head of the prophets holds the chief place. Anointing is the Spirit’s power and gifts, not an empty title. The word is a powerful medicine for wounded consciences, not for the hard-hearted. We are captives till set free through the grace of Christ.

Reformed effect: the Spirit upon the Messiah is not a mere announcement. Gospel liberty is Christ’s actual release of the humbled elect. Shared christology with Wesley: this is the anointed Lord at Nazareth. The fork is how the liberty lands, not who speaks.

Key contrast Both take 61:1 as Christ (Luke 4:16–21)—not a later millennial timetable. Arminians typically stress the King’s sincere proclamation of liberty to captives still able to hear it. Reformed readers typically stress the Spirit’s effectual anointing of the Head, so the word is medicine that actually frees the brokenhearted elect. The Speaker is not in dispute.

Isaiah 61:2

2to proclaim the year of the Lord’s favor, and the day of vengeance of our God; to comfort all who mourn;

Arminian

Wesley: vengeance is necessary where God delivers his people—principally on the church’s enemies, and the spiritual ones chiefly: Satan, sin, and death. Favor and vengeance are gospel and judgment, not a chart.

Arminian year: the acceptable year is sincerely proclaimed. Comfort is held out to all who mourn. Delay is unbelief, not a locked timetable. Historic return and Christ’s Nazareth reading belong together.

Reformed

Calvin: the year of good-pleasure is God’s chosen time, with a Jubilee hint (Lev 25:10). Day of vengeance meets persecutors when the Church is delivered. Salvation lies in his gracious will, not our merits. If we miss the benefit, it is unbelief that refuses God, who freely offers himself.

Reformed time: Paul calls it the fullness (Gal 4:4; 2 Cor 6:2). The door is open in the gospel. Comfort for mourners is real; vengeance is the just Judge, not a millennial newspaper.

Isaiah 61:3

3to grant to those who mourn in Zion—to give them a beautiful headdress instead of ashes, the oil of gladness instead of mourning, the garment of praise instead of a faint spirit; that they may be called oaks of righteousness, the planting of the Lord, that he may be glorified.

Arminian

Wesley: ashes for mourning, oil of joy, a festival garment of praise. Trees of righteousness—firm, well rooted, by faith engrafted into Christ, and bringing forth fruit suitable to the soil.

Arminian planting: mourners in Zion can still be granted beauty. Engrafting is by faith. Oaks are a people who actually praise, the planting of the Lord that he may be glorified—not a later forest of politics.

Reformed

Calvin: he gives beauty for ashes by an elegant inversion. Trees of righteousness are planted by the Lord. We are by nature a dry stock. First planting is election (Eph 1:4); then calling, ingrafted through faith by the gospel. God alone gives the increase (1 Cor 3:7).

Reformed oaks: true righteousness must reign in us. End: that he may be glorified. Same planting as 60:21. Not a millennial arboretum.

Isaiah 61:4

4They shall build up the ancient ruins; they shall raise up the former devastations; they shall repair the ruined cities, the devastations of many generations.

Arminian

Wesley does not pause here; the restoration of 58:12 still governs. Ancient ruins rebuilt are the same mercy: historic Zion first, then the gospel people raised.

Arminian repair: devastations of many generations do not cancel the invitation to build. Cities ruined by unbelief can still be repaired under the Servant’s year of favor. Not a later temple timetable.

Reformed

Calvin: long ruins shall not prevent a new rising. Deserts of the age, wildernesses of many ages—Athens may stay down; Jerusalem will not. It is God’s peculiar office to raise what was devoted to eternal rottenness.

Reformed rebuilding: first the return from exile, then the Church under the cross, till the last day. Do not read this as a newspaper reconstruction of stone only.

Isaiah 61:5

5Strangers shall stand and tend your flocks; foreigners shall be your plowmen and vinedressers;

Arminian

Wesley: strangers are Gentiles, not of the Jews’ natural race—converts, or at most an outward profession. They stand ready. Flocks are churches fed with the word; plowmen manage God’s spiritual husbandry.

Arminian service: nations still may tend and plow. This is gospel help, as in 60:10, not a millennial labor map. The flocks remain the Lord’s people.

Reformed

Calvin: foreigners stand ready to assist. Sheep, fields, and vines are figures of Christ’s spiritual kingdom, that we may understand its happiness from things we know.

Reformed strangers: we are truly happy when Christ exercises his dominion. Advantages the children of Adam justly lose are restored beyond expectation under his rule.

Isaiah 61:6

6but you shall be called the priests of the Lord; they shall speak of you as the ministers of our God; you shall eat the wealth of the nations, and in their glory you shall boast.

Arminian

Wesley: the whole body shall now be as near to God as the priests were formerly—a royal priesthood. This is most certainly true of all the faithful under the gospel.

Arminian priesthood: the church is a spiritual priesthood now, not a later temple timetable. Wealth of nations is consecration, as in 60:5–11. Ministers of our God is the gospel people, Jew and Gentile.

Reformed

Calvin: not a license to seize the nations’ gold. All things come under Christ’s dominion, and so are called ours because he has nothing separate from his Church. All the saints are consecrated; Peter’s royal priesthood (1 Pet 2:9). Papists abuse this to re-sacrifice Christ.

Reformed priests: offer yourselves (Rom 12:1). Ministers slay men with the word that they may obey the Lord (Rom 15:16). Earthly beasts are done. This is the gospel church, not a newspaper temple.

Isaiah 61:7

7Instead of your shame there shall be a double portion; instead of dishonor they shall rejoice in their lot; therefore in their land they shall possess a double portion; they shall have everlasting joy.

Arminian

Wesley: double means honour; the portion is the honour God will give. Shame is not the last word over the people he comforts.

Arminian joy: everlasting joy is held out to the restored. Historic Zion’s disgrace turns; the church may still rejoice in her lot. Double is mercy’s measure, not a millennial payroll.

Reformed

Calvin: the Church’s prosperity shall far exceed her present afflictions—an eternal weight of glory against a momentary lightness (2 Cor 4:17). Wicked men vaunt now; the earth is not finally theirs.

Reformed portion: everlasting joy is the Spirit’s, the peace that surpasses understanding (Phil 4:7). It differs from the wicked’s brief glee, which turns to gnashing of teeth.

Isaiah 61:8

8For I the Lord love justice; I hate robbery and wrong; I will faithfully give them their recompense, and I will make an everlasting covenant with them.

Arminian

Wesley: I will do them right, for I love justice in myself and in them that practise it. Robbery even for sacrifice is hated. He will lead them to sincerity. Though you have broken covenant, he renews the ancient one, confirmed with the blood of Christ, never to be abrogated.

Arminian covenant: the everlasting covenant is sincerely made. Justice is to be practised, not only credited. Robbery dressed as worship still stinks. The blood of Christ confirms a covenant people may still keep in truth.

Reformed

Calvin: he reasons from God’s nature. Judgment is true equity, not masks. Robbery in the burnt-offering is hypocritical worship—lies mixed with sacrifice. He demands mercy rather than sacrifice (Hos 6:6; Matt 9:13). He will establish their work in truth and make an everlasting covenant, freely making himself their debtor.

Reformed covenant: prosperity is his blessing, not fortune. The covenant is everlasting because he is the guide of the journey, owing us nothing, giving all.

Isaiah 61:9

9Their offspring shall be known among the nations, and their descendants in the midst of the peoples; all who see them shall acknowledge them, that they are an offspring the Lord has blessed.

Arminian

Wesley: a promise of the church’s increase. The church shall have a seed of the Gentiles; confined to one corner no more, it shall fill the nations. There shall be visible characters of God’s love to them, and of his grace in them.

Arminian seed: the blessed offspring is a people known by grace, not a racial timetable. Nations still may acknowledge them. Increase is missionary, already under the gospel.

Reformed

Calvin: the Church, once a narrow corner and then diminished, shall be visible among all nations. The return sparked it; full brightness shone in Christ, when foreigners were ingrafted by faith. Jews go first by mercy, not merit (Rom 3:2).

Reformed known: all who see them shall acknowledge a seed the Lord has blessed. This is fulfilled every day as nations join the same confession.

Isaiah 61:10

10I will greatly rejoice in the Lord; my soul shall exult in my God, for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation; he has covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decks himself like a priest with a beautiful headdress, and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels.

Arminian

Wesley: spoken in the person of the church. Clothed with salvation as with a garment, and with righteousness as with a robe. Bridegroom and bride language is the people’s joy, not a private mystic.

Arminian dress: the church actually wears what she is given—salvation and righteousness put on as holiness, a people decked for praise (v. 3). Rejoicing is sincere. The robe is not postponed to a later wedding only.

Reformed

Calvin: righteousness and salvation bestowed; from him alone we seek them. The Church lay in filth and rags; received into favor with her husband, she shines. Accomplished at Christ’s coming, bestowed daily, completed at his last coming.

Reformed robe: the covering is justifying righteousness and salvation given, not earned jewelry. Faith sees what sense cannot, while the church still wears the cross’s revolting dress in the world’s eyes.

Isaiah 61:11

11For as the earth brings forth its sprouts, and as a garden causes what is sown in it to sprout up, so the Lord God will cause righteousness and praise to sprout up before all the nations.

Arminian

Wesley: righteousness is his great work of salvation breaking out and appearing. Praise is the natural product and fruit of it.

Arminian spring: as a garden sprouts what is sown, so the Lord causes righteousness and praise before the nations. The fruit is real holiness and thanks, already, not a millennial crop report. Nations still see it.

Reformed

Calvin: the earth every year puts forth her bud. Will he who gave that power fail to make the elect seed bud? Righteousness is his, chiefly seen when Christ was manifested. Praise must accompany the benefit; it is base to be dumb.

Reformed harvest: he will cause it before all the nations. The Church’s bounds are no longer a corner. Firstfruits now; fullness when the last praise is public.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the Spirit-anointed Servant, liberty for captives, and garments of salvation—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 61:1–11.

Arminian Notable voices

“Upon me—Though the prophet may speak of himself, yet it is principally to be understood of Christ. Anointed—Set me apart, both capacitating him with gifts, and commissioning him with authority; and yet more, as it is applied to Christ, a power to make all effectual. Liberty—This appertains to Christ’s kingly office, whereby he proclaims liberty from the dominion of sin, and from the fear of hell. The priests—The whole body of them shall now be as near to God as the priests were formerly, and shall be a royal priesthood. This is most certainly true of all the faithful under the gospel. Garments—With salvation as with a garment, and with righteousness as with a robe.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 61:1, 6, 10)

Reformed Notable voices

“As Christ explains this passage with reference to himself (Luke 4:18)… Christ, who is the Head of the prophets, holds the chief place, and alone makes all those revelations. We are prisoners and captives, therefore, till we are set free (John 8:36) through the grace of Christ; and when Christ wishes to break asunder our chains, let us not refuse the grace that is offered to us. The preaching of the word is not an empty sound, but a powerful medicine, the effect of which is felt… by wounded consciences. All the saints have been consecrated to Christ… a royal priesthood (1 Pet 2:9). Righteousness and salvation had been bestowed upon them. From him alone we should seek and expect them.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 61:1–2, 6, 10)
