Isaiah 47

Verses 1–15 · ESV · Voices

After 46:1–13—Bel bows down and Nebo stoops, idols borne as burdens on weary beasts; Listen, O house of Jacob, borne from before birth; even to old age I will carry you; declaring the end from the beginning, my counsel shall stand; calling a bird of prey from the east; I will put salvation in Zion for Israel my glory—the word turns: Come down and sit in the dust, O virgin daughter of Babylon; sit on the ground without a throne, O daughter of the Chaldeans.

Isaiah 47:1

1Come down and sit in the dust, O virgin daughter of Babylon; sit on the ground without a throne, O daughter of the Chaldeans! For you shall no more be called tender and delicate.

Arminian

Come down and sit in the dust, O virgin daughter of Babylon; sit on the ground without a throne, O daughter of the Chaldeans! For you shall no more be called tender and delicate. Wesley: Down—From thy throne. Virgin—So called, because she was tender and delicate. No throne—For thee. The empire is taken from thee, and translated to the Persians. Called—Be so.

Arminian hearing: she comes down from the throne; virgin names her tender and delicate, not chaste; the empire is taken and translated to the Persians; she shall no more be called tender and delicate. Shared: Come down, O virgin daughter of Babylon; sit without a throne, O daughter of the Chaldeans; throne gone to the Persians. Historic Babylon, not a newspaper, not a living city or state, not a millennial camp fight. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Down—From thy throne” and “Called—Be so” are his lemmas for “Come down” and “you shall no more be called.” Prefer no keyContrast on 1: both camps take historic Babylon coming down; skip Calvin’s Venice illustration rather than invent a newspaper fight. Match 46:1: Bel and Nebo already bowing. Match v. 5: mistress of kingdoms no more.

Reformed

Come down and sit in the dust, O virgin daughter of Babylon. Calvin: explains the counsel of God—overthrow of Babylon and deliverance of the people connected; Babylon’s rank was a grave for the Jews. Imperative Come down more forceful, as from the judgment-seat (Jer 1:10). Virgin daughter: not modest or chaste, but brought up softly, never forced by enemies (as formerly of Sidon). His Venice illustration is illustration only—historic Babylon, not a newspaper.

Reformed hearing: Come down is the judgment-seat’s sentence; virgin is soft upbringing, not chastity; overthrow of Babylon and Israel’s deliverance are one counsel. Shared with Wesley: historic Babylon; throne gone; tender and delicate no more. Historic Babylon, not a newspaper, not a living city or state. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Come down” and “virgin daughter” name the ESV lemmas. Prefer no keyContrast on 1: both historic Babylon; Venice is illustration, not the verse’s referent. Match 46:1: Bel and Nebo stoop. Match v. 2: millstones after the throne is gone.

Isaiah 47:2

2Take the millstones and grind flour, put off your veil, strip off your robe, uncover your legs, pass through the rivers.

Arminian

Take the millstones and grind flour, put off your veil, strip off your robe, uncover your legs, pass through the rivers. Wesley: Millstones—Thou shalt be brought to the basest slavery, which grinding at the mill was esteemed. For this work was not performed by horses, as now it is, but by the labour of slaves and captives. Grind—Grind bread-corn into meal for thy master’s use. Uncover—Take off the ornaments wherewith such women as were of good quality used to cover and dress their heads. These are predictions of what they should be forced to do or suffer. Thigh—Gird up thy garments close and short about thee, that thou mayest be fit for travelling on foot, and for passing over those rivers, through which thou wilt be constrained to wade, in the way to the land of thy captivity.

Arminian hearing: mill-slavery, not horse-mills; grind meal for a master; ornaments off; garments girt short to wade rivers toward captivity. Shared: millstones; grind flour; veil and robe off; legs uncovered; pass through the rivers. Historic Babylon’s fall into slavery, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Thigh” is his lemma for ESV “uncover your legs”; “Grind bread-corn” for “grind flour.” Prefer no keyContrast: both hear highest honor to mill-slavery. Match v. 1: throne gone, then the mill. Match v. 3: nakedness uncovered.

Reformed

Take the millstones and grind flour. Calvin: great change—highest honor to lowest disgrace. Millstones: marks of degrading slavery; meanest slaves shut up in a mill. Unbind curled locks; uncover limbs to cross rivers.

Reformed hearing: from throne to mill; curled locks unbound; limbs uncovered to wade. Shared with Wesley: mill-slavery; ornaments off; rivers crossed on the way down. Historic Babylon, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “unbind curled locks” and “uncover limbs to cross rivers” name ESV “put off your veil” and “uncover your legs, pass through the rivers.” Prefer no keyContrast. Match v. 1: Come down from the throne. Match v. 3: disgrace seen.

Isaiah 47:3

Arminian

Your nakedness shall be uncovered, and your disgrace shall be seen. I will take vengeance, and I will spare no one. Wesley: Uncovered—Either for want of raiment to cover it; or rather, by thine enemies in way of scorn and contumely. Quote-honesty: ESV “I will spare no one” is KJV “I will not meet thee as a man”; Wesley comments the KJV lemma “As a man,” not a different verse. Prefer no keyContrast on 3: lexical as-a-man versus spare-no-one is one no-mercy; do not fork. No open theism: he will take vengeance; he will spare no one. Match v. 2: legs uncovered, then nakedness. Match v. 4: Our Redeemer is the one who takes vengeance.

Calvin prefers the former as smoother; either way the Lord will destroy the Babylonians and there will be no room for mercy.

Reformed hearing: veils of wealth fall; vengeance meets no man; no room for mercy. Shared with Wesley: nakedness uncovered; vengeance; no mercy. Historic Babylon, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: ESV “I will spare no one” is KJV “I will not meet thee as a man”; Calvin comments that KJV lemma, not a different English Bible. Prefer no keyContrast on 3: both no mercy; as-a-man versus spare-no-one is lexical, not a camp fork. No open theism: I will take vengeance; I will spare no one. Match v. 2: mill and uncovered legs. Match v. 4: the Redeemer who takes this vengeance.

Isaiah 47:6

The ancient—Who besides their common calamity were afflicted with the miseries of old age, and therefore did require both pity and reverence.

Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Polluted” is his lemma for ESV “I profaned my heritage”; “The ancient” for “the aged.” No open theism: I was angry; I gave them into your hand. Match v. 3: I will take vengeance. Match v. 7: these things—the cruel usages—not laid to heart.

How could they go beyond the limit God assigned? Not in their power actually to go beyond the limit, but he looked at their cruelty—they endeavored utterly to ruin those who had surrendered (Zech 1:15; James 2:13). On the old man: they did not spare even the aged.

Reformed hearing: scourge then heal; executioner of a child; not actually beyond the assigned limit, but cruelty of intent counted; covenant not cast away; elect do not perish. Shared with Wesley: I was angry with my people; I gave them into your hand; you showed them no mercy; heavy yoke on the aged. Historic Babylon and historic Israel, not a millennial camp fight. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “I profaned my heritage” names the ESV; “the old man” is his lemma for “the aged.” No open theism: he who struck will heal; anger is not a change of purpose. Match v. 4: still the Holy One of Israel. Match v. 7: she did not remember their end.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “No mercy” as Thou hast exceeded the bounds of thy commission; Into thine hand—To punish them; the ancient required pity and reverence. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take the Babylonians as seizing the occasion for cruelty like an executioner of a child a father had put into their hands; not in their power actually to go beyond the limit God assigned, but he looked at their cruelty of intent (Zech 1:15); he who struck will heal; elect do not perish; anger is not a change of purpose so as to cast away the covenant. Shared: I was angry with my people; I gave them into your hand; you showed them no mercy; heavy yoke on the aged. Historic Babylon and historic Israel, not a millennial camp fight.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Come down, O virgin daughter of Babylon, millstones and grinding, nakedness uncovered, Our Redeemer the Holy One of Israel, mistress of kingdoms no more, no mercy on the aged, I am and there is no one besides me, loss of children and widowhood, sorceries that cannot charm away disaster, stargazers wearied, stubble in the flame, and none to save—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 47:1–15.

Arminian Notable voices

“Down—From thy throne. Virgin—So called, because she was tender and delicate. No throne—For thee. The empire is taken from thee, and translated to the Persians. Called—Be so. Millstones—Thou shalt be brought to the basest slavery, which grinding at the mill was esteemed. For this work was not performed by horses, as now it is, but by the labour of slaves and captives. Grind—Grind bread-corn into meal for thy master’s use. Uncover—Take off the ornaments wherewith such women as were of good quality used to cover and dress their heads. These are predictions of what they should be forced to do or suffer. Thigh—Gird up thy garments close and short about thee, that thou mayest be fit for travelling on foot, and for passing over those rivers, through which thou wilt be constrained to wade, in the way to the land of thy captivity. Uncovered—Either for want of raiment to cover it; or rather, by thine enemies in way of scorn and contumely. As a man—With moderation and gentleness, as those men who have not quite put off humanity use to do. Silent—Thro’ grief and shame, as mourners use to do. The lady—The chief and glory of all kingdoms. Polluted—I cast them away as an unclean thing. Into thine hand—To punish them. No mercy—Thou hast exceeded the bounds of thy commission. The ancient—Who besides their common calamity were afflicted with the miseries of old age, and therefore did require both pity and reverence. These things—Thy cruel usages of my people, and the heavy judgments which thou hadst reason to expect for them. Nor remember—Thou didst not consider what might and was likely to befal thee afterward. I am—Independent, and self-sufficient. None—Which is not either subject to me, or far inferior to me in power and glory. Shall not sit—I shall never want either a king or people to defend me. Perfection—In the highest degree. Trusted—Confidently expecting to preserve thyself by these and other wicked arts. None seeth—My counsels are so deeply laid. Perverted—Hath misled thee into the way of perdition. None seeth—Which is repeated, to denote their intolerable self-confidence. Therefore—This agrees with the history. Babylon being surprized by Cyrus, when they were in deep security. Stand—Persist in these practices. Laboured—From the beginning of thy kingdom. For the Chaldeans in all ages were famous for the practice of these arts. Wearied—Thou hast spent thy time and strength in going from one to another, and all to no purpose. Thus—Such comfortless and helpless creatures. They—Merchants who came from several countries to trade with Babylon. And the verse may be thus rendered; Thus (vain and unprofitable) shall they (thy sorcerers) with whom thou hast laboured be unto thee: also thy merchants shall wander every one to his own quarter.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 47:1–15)

Reformed Notable voices

“Explains the counsel of God: overthrow of Babylon and deliverance of the people connected; Babylon’s rank was a grave for the Jews. Imperative Come down more forceful—as from the judgment-seat (Jer 1:10). Virgin daughter: not modest or chaste, but brought up softly, never forced by enemies (as formerly of Sidon). Venice is illustration only; historic Babylon, not a newspaper. Great change: highest honor to lowest disgrace. Millstones: marks of degrading slavery; meanest slaves shut up in a mill. Unbind curled locks; uncover limbs to cross rivers. Baseness discovered when wealth’s veils are removed (Demosthenes on cities—illustration). I will take vengeance and will not meet a man: some supply “as a man”—you do not deal with man whose attack you can resist; others “I will not allow a man to meet me” as intercessor. Calvin prefers the former as smoother; either way the Lord will destroy the Babylonians and there will be no room for mercy. Our Redeemer: punishment of Babylon is for the salvation of his people (Isa 45:4). Holy One of Israel: not in vain he chose this people. Jehovah of Hosts. Sit silent: shame; contrast with her former loud commands over the East. Lady of kingdoms no more; Babylonian monarchy very widely extended. I was angry with my people: captivity is God’s scourge; he who struck will heal. Babylonians seized the occasion for cruelty, as if one became the executioner of a child a father had put into his hands to be chastised. I profaned my heritage: not a change of purpose so as to cast away the covenant; still his people; elect do not perish. Not in their power actually to go beyond the limit God assigned, but he looked at their cruelty (Zech 1:15). They did not spare even the aged. Thou saidst I shall for ever be a mistress: haughtiness of perpetual dominion; children of this world intoxicated by prosperity. Didst not remember her end: the end of Jerusalem. Delicate woman. I am, and there is none besides me: only God can truly say I AM (Ex 3:14); Babylon by this made war with God. Two things suddenly: widowhood and childlessness in perfection. For or in the multitude of divinations: either cause or notwithstanding; they trusted such delusions and dreaded nothing. Trustedst in thy malice: fraud and oppression by which they enlarged the kingdom; great kingdoms are great robberies. Blasphemies repeated. Sudden destruction when they say Peace (1 Thess 5:3); Daniel 5:30—Babylon taken in a night. Stand now amidst thy divinations: do as thou art wont; in the end the event will instruct. Alludes to augurs remaining unmoved till a sign is seen. Wearied in the multitude of counsels. Binders of the heavens, astrologers, monthly prognosticators. Judicial astrology versus prophets; fatal necessity of stars would extinguish divine predictions. Historic Chaldean arts. They shall be as stubble; fire consumes; not wood for heat but useless stubble. Traffickers and dealers, chiefly the diviners, from thy youth. Every one to his quarter: flight, with allusion to quarters of the heavens they divide. None shall save thee.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 47:1–15)
