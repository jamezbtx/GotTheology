Isaiah 37

Verses 21–38 · ESV · Voices

Then Isaiah sent to Hezekiah: virgin daughter of Zion; whom have you mocked; I know your sitting down; remnant; sign of eating this year; I will defend this city; the angel of the LORD struck 185,000; Sennacherib returned to Nineveh and was killed by his sons in the house of Nisroch. The unit stays with that historic 8th-century word, remnant, angel, and death in his own land—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 37:21

21Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, “Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: Because you have prayed to me concerning Sennacherib king of Assyria,

Arminian

Wesley: Isaiah's prophecy, ver. 21-35. Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent unto Hezekiah: whereas thou hast prayed to me against Sennacherib king of Assyria—still that word after the prayer of vv. 14–20. The history related here is, almost in the same words contained 2 Kings 19.

Arminian reading: they might yet have heard Isaiah sent because that prayer had been made. Historic sending after Hezekiah’s prayer, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another dispatch to make “because you have prayed” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: then Isaiah sent to Hezekiah. This shews the result of the prayer; as soon as matters have come to an extremity, God suddenly holds out his hand to assist the pious king by the Prophet Isaiah. Not that he immediately stretches out his arm to drive away the enemies, but he promises deliverance by the mouth of the Prophet, and thus calls even now into exercise the faith of his servant. Since thou hast prayed to me: God grants it as an answer to the prayers of Hezekiah.

Reformed note: the sending is still that historic word after the prayer of vv. 14–20. Calvin will not let Isaiah’s message become a newspaper of later briefs.

Isaiah 37:22

22this is the word that the LORD has spoken concerning him: “‘She despises you, she scorns you—the virgin daughter of Zion; she wags her head behind you—the daughter of Jerusalem.

Arminian

Wesley: virgin—so he calls Zion, or Jerusalem; because she was pure in good measure from that gross idolatry wherewith other people were defiled, which is called spiritual whoredom: and to signify, that God would defend her from the rape which Sennacherib intended to commit upon her with no less care than parents do their virgin daughters from those who seek to force and deflower them.

Arminian reading: they might yet have named Zion a virgin for that purity and that defense, not a later chart. Historic daughter of Zion under Sennacherib, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another maiden to make “virgin daughter of Zion” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: the virgin daughter of Zion. There is greater emphasis in this address to the whole Church than if he had said the same thing to Hezekiah as a private individual; this circumstance heightens the baseness of treating with scorn the defencelessness of a wretched people. He intended to express the weakness of the city of Jerusalem, because she was like an orphan and destitute virgin, who was insulted by this base ruffian; God, as the father to whom this insult is offered, declares that he knows well what are the schemes of that wicked man.

Reformed note: the scorn stays that historic word concerning Sennacherib. Calvin will not let the virgin daughter become a newspaper of later cities.

Key contrast Both keep the virgin daughter of Zion as that historic Jerusalem under Sennacherib, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads virgin as Zion pure in good measure from that gross idolatry which is called spiritual whoredom, and as God’s care to defend her from the rape Sennacherib intended, with no less care than parents do their virgin daughters. Calvin reads the same title as the weakness of the city: an orphan and destitute virgin insulted by a base ruffian; the address is to the whole Church, and the insult is offered to God as father. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. Neither makes the daughter a later headline.

Isaiah 37:23

23“‘Whom have you mocked and reviled? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes to the heights? Against the Holy One of Israel!

Arminian

Wesley’s prophecy of vv. 21–35 still holds: whom hast thou reproached and blasphemed? and against whom hast thou exalted thy voice, and lifted up thine eyes on high? even against the Holy One of Israel—still that word after the virgin daughter of v. 22. The history is, almost in the same words contained 2 Kings 19.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have heard the mock named as against the Holy One, not a later chart. Historic blasphemy of Sennacherib, 8th century, is already named. The church is not to stretch “whom have you mocked” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: whom hast thou reproached? In the former verse he describes the fact; now he raises their minds higher, by shewing that this tyrant insults not only Jerusalem but God himself. Let this passage be brought to our remembrance, whenever we see ourselves exposed to the taunts and insolence of wicked men; they who fight against us wage war with the living God. The less strength that is left to us, let us be more fully convinced that the power of God is close at hand.

Reformed accent: “against the Holy One of Israel” is still that historic taunt. Calvin will not let the mock become a newspaper of later boasts.

Isaiah 37:24

24By your servants you have mocked the Lord, and you have said, With my many chariots I have gone up the heights of the mountains, to the far recesses of Lebanon, to cut down its tallest cedars, its choicest cypresses, to come to its remotest height, its most fruitful forest.

Arminian

Wesley: mountains—I have brought up my very chariots to those mountains which were thought inaccessible by my army. Lebanon—an high hill, famous for cedars and fir-trees. Cut down—I will cut down the trees that hinder my march, and plane the way for my numerous army and chariots. Lodgings—those cities (which he calls lodgings in way of contempt) which are in his utmost borders. I am come into the land of Canaan at one border, Lebanon, and I resolve to march on to the other border.

Arminian reading: they might yet have heard the chariots and Lebanon as that boast, not a later map. Historic mock by the servants, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another forest to make “far recesses of Lebanon” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: by the hand of thy servants. This also heightens the baseness and cruelty of the insult, for it is harder to bear reproaches from a servant than from his master. Sennacherib had not only vomited it out of his own mouth, but had employed his servant Rabshakeh to utter scornful language against the holy name of God. Perceiving almost the whole of Judea subjected to his power, he swells with insolence as if he had gained a complete victory; thus do tyrants, though they acknowledge that war is doubtful, still dream of having in their power the successful results of battles.

Reformed note: the chariots stay that historic boast against Lebanon. Calvin will not let the servants become a newspaper of later armies.

Isaiah 37:25

25I dug wells and drank waters, to dry up with the sole of my foot all the streams of Egypt.

Arminian

Wesley: strange waters—such as were never discovered by others. Dried up—and as I can furnish my army with water digged out of the earth; so I can deprive my enemies of their water, and can dry up their rivers, and that with the sole of my feet; with the march of my vast and numerous army, who will easily do this, either by marching through them, and each carrying away part with them: or by making new channels, and driving the waters of the river into them.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have heard the sole of the foot as that army’s boast, not a later drought. Historic streams of Egypt in Sennacherib’s mouth, 8th century, are already named. The church is not to stretch “I dug wells” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: I shall dig and drink water. The tyrant still goes on to boast of his strength, and threatens that he will bring so powerful an army that by means of their numbers he will dry up all the fountains and rivers. This narrative of the Prophet is intended to lead us to consider the stupendous judgment of God against that tyrant.

Reformed accent: the wells stay that historic swagger. Calvin will not let “the sole of my foot” become a newspaper of later campaigns.

Isaiah 37:26

26“‘Have you not heard that I determined it long ago? I planned from days of old what now I bring to pass, that you should make fortified cities crash into heaps of ruins,

Arminian

Wesley: hast thou not—hast thou not long since learned, that which some of thy philosophers could teach thee; that there is a supreme and powerful God, by whose decree and providence all these wars and calamities were sent, and ordered; whose mere instrument thou art, so that thou hast no cause for these vain boastings? This work is mine, not thine. I have so disposed of things by my providence, that thou shouldest be a great and victorious prince, and that thou shouldest be so successful as thou hast hitherto been, first against the kingdom of Israel, and now against Judah.

Arminian reading: they might yet have heard the long-ago plan as that ordering of the Assyrian rod, not a later chart. Historic decree over those fortified cities, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another ruin to make “I determined it long ago” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: hast thou not heard? The greater part of commentators explain this verse as if the Lord declared that nothing was now done, or had formerly been done by this tyrant, which he had not foretold. But I explain it in a different manner, which is, that Jerusalem will be preserved by the assistance of God, because he is the protector of it. For the sake of giving greater emphasis, he suppresses the name of the city. I have founded the Church, and therefore the salvation of the Church shall always be my care; because I will not leave unfinished the work which I have begun. Shall I now bring it to be desolation, like other cities that have been destroyed and razed?

Reformed note: Calvin will not let “I determined it long ago” be read only as the tyrant’s commission. He will not let it become a newspaper of later falls, nor an open-theist gap in God’s plan.

Key contrast Both keep “I determined it long ago” as that historic word against Sennacherib, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads it with the common sense: Sennacherib is a mere instrument; by decree and providence all these wars were sent and ordered; this work is mine, not thine; God disposed that he should be victorious first against Israel and now against Judah. Calvin expressly explains it in a different manner: “it” is Jerusalem, founded from ancient days, the Church whose salvation shall always be God’s care; shall I now bring it to be desolation like the other cities? Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. Neither makes the long-ago plan an open-theist briefing of a God who had not determined it.

Isaiah 37:27

27while their inhabitants, shorn of strength, are dismayed and confounded, and have become like plants of the field and like tender grass, like grass on the housetops, blighted before it is grown.

Arminian

Wesley: therefore—because I had armed thee with my commission and strength, and taken away their spirit and courage. The inhabitants like grass of the field and grass on the housetops are still that word after the long-ago plan of v. 26, as in the Kings parallel.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have named those inhabitants blighted because the commission was God’s, not a later chart. Historic dismay under Assyria, 8th century, is already named. The church is not to stretch “grass on the housetops” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: for their inhabitants were maimed. We ought not to judge of the condition of the Church from the stability of this world; although fortified cities are taken, yet the Church shall stand and flourish, because it does not rest on its own strength, and has its foundation not on the earth but in heaven. All fortresses are of no avail, if the hand of the Lord do not assist. Like the grass of the field: they flourish for a time, but quickly wither. The grass of the housetops: the more elevated its position, it is the nearer to the heat, and withers more quickly.

Reformed accent: the blight stays those historic inhabitants under the rod. Calvin will not let the grass become a newspaper of later roofs.

Isaiah 37:28

28“‘I know your sitting down and your going out and coming in, and your raging against me.

Arminian

Wesley: I know—though thou dost not know me, yet I throughly know thee, and all thy designs and actions, all thy secret contrivances in the place of thy abode, in thy own kingdom and court; and the execution of thy designs abroad, what thou intendest in thy going out, and with what farther thoughts thou comest in, or returnest to thy own land.

Arminian reading: they might yet have heard that thorough knowing of the sitting down, not a later watch. Historic knowledge of Sennacherib’s rage, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another seat to make “I know your sitting down” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: I know thy sitting down and thy rising up. By sitting down, and rising up, are here denoted deliberations, plans, and schemes. The providence of God restrains them, and drives them hither and thither, so that frequently, contrary to their intention, they are conducted to a very good end, as God thinks fit. He warns Sennacherib that he is well aware of his rage, and declares that, while wicked men storm on the earth, he preserves calm silence, and laughs at their madness.

Reformed note: “I know” is still that historic knowing, not an open question in God. Calvin will not let the sitting down become a newspaper of later courts, nor an open-theist gap in God’s knowledge.

Isaiah 37:29

29Because you have raged against me and your complacency has come to my ears, I will put my hook in your nose and my bit in your mouth, and I will turn you back on the way by which you came.’

Arminian

Wesley: my hook—what a comfort is it, that God has a hook in the nose and a bridle in the jaws of all his and our enemies? The turn-back on the way he came is still that word after “I know” in v. 28, as in the Kings parallel.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have heard the hook and the bit as that turning of this king, not a later chart. Historic rage of Sennacherib, 8th century, is already named. The church is not to stretch “hook in your nose” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: because thou wast angry against me. The more furiously wicked men rise up against God, so much the more is he wont eventually to set himself in opposition to them. Since thou canst not be tamed, I will curb thee like a savage beast. Sennacherib was compelled to return by the way by which he came, because, while he was revolving the project of subduing every part of Judea and Egypt, he hastily, without having accomplished anything, took the speediest method of returning, which he would not have done of his own accord, if God had not drawn him back by unseen methods.

Reformed accent: the hook stays that historic curb of this tyrant. Calvin will not let the bit become a newspaper of later noses.

Isaiah 37:30

30“And this shall be the sign for you: this year you shall eat what grows of itself, and in the second year what springs from that. Then in the third year sow and reap, and plant vineyards, and eat their fruit.

Arminian

Wesley: a sign—of the certain accomplishment of the promises here made: that God will not only preserve the city from his present fury, but also bless his people with a durable prosperity. The third year—this was an excellent sign; especially, considering the waste and havock which the Assyrians had made in the land; and that the Jews had been forced to retire into their strong hold, and consequently to neglect their tilling, and sowing, and reaping; and yet this year they should have sufficient provision from those fruits of the earth which the Assyrians left; and the second year, which was the year of release, in which they might neither sow, nor reap, from such fruits as the earth brought forth of its own accord; and so in the third year. And eat—you shall not sow, and another reap, as lately you did; but you shall enjoy the fruit of your own labours.

Arminian reading: they might yet have eaten what grows of itself in that year, and named the second year a year of release. Historic sign to Hezekiah, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another harvest to make “eat what grows of itself” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: and this shall be a sign to thee. He now directs his discourse to Hezekiah and the whole nation. There are two kinds of signs. Some go before the event, and lead us to it as by the hand; while others follow for the purpose of confirming the event. It is a sign of this nature which Isaiah here describes. Amidst so great scarcity as seemed likely to ensue after the wasting and pillaging of the fields, the Lord promises that there will be no lack of food. They who were besieged or fugitives had it not in their power to attend to agricultural labors; he promises that they shall have food without sowing till they sow on the third year.

Reformed note: the sign stays that historic eating after the siege. Calvin will not let the third year become a newspaper of later farms.

Key contrast Both keep the sign of eating this year as that historic word to Hezekiah after the Assyrian waste, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads the second year as the year of release, in which they might neither sow nor reap, from fruits the earth brought forth of its own accord; and eat: you shall not sow and another reap, as lately you did. Calvin reads the same sign as a following confirmation after war: wasting and pillaging, the besieged and fugitives unable to till; food without sowing till they sow on the third year. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. Neither makes the sign a later harvest chart.

Isaiah 37:31

31And the surviving remnant of the house of Judah shall again take root downward and bear fruit upward.

Arminian

Wesley: the remnant—they shall be well fixt and provided for themselves, and then do good to others. Root downward and fruit upward are still that remnant after the sign of v. 30, as in the Kings parallel.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have taken root as that house of Judah left. Historic surviving remnant, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to stretch “take root downward” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: and that which shall be preserved of the house of Judah. The Lord will deliver Jerusalem so as not to cast away his care of her afterwards. The defense of Jerusalem belonged to God, because he had chosen it to be his sanctuary, and because the Messiah would proceed from it. Although the Church does not make professions of towering greatness, yet the Lord imparts a secret vigor which causes it to spring up and grow beyond human expectation. Let us not be terrified, therefore, when no roots are seen.

Reformed accent: the remnant stays that historic house of Judah after the siege. Calvin will not let the root become a newspaper of later trees.

Isaiah 37:32

32For out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant, and out of Mount Zion a band of survivors. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will do this.

Arminian

Wesley: go forth—that handful of Jews who were now gathered together, and shut up in Jerusalem, shall go out of their several habitations, and by my singular blessing increase exceedingly. The zeal—although when you reflect upon yourselves, and consider either your present fewness, and weakness, or your great unworthiness, this may seem too great a blessing for you to expect; yet God will do it from the zeal which he hath, both for his own name, and for the good of his undeserving people.

Arminian reading: they might yet have gone forth as that handful shut up in Jerusalem. Historic remnant out of Zion, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another band to make “out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: for out of Jerusalem shall go forth a remnant. When not only was Judea again covered by a multitude of men, but from the remnant there sprung vast multitudes who were spread over the various countries of the world, this could not have been done, unless out of that small number the Lord had created not merely a single nation, but many nations. The zeal of Jehovah of hosts will do this. Not only does he contrast the zeal of God with the purposes of men; God regards his Church with such fervent love, that he does not hesitate to work in an extraordinary manner for promoting her salvation.

Reformed note: the zeal stays that historic doing for Jerusalem. Calvin will not let the remnant become a newspaper of later camps.

Key contrast Both keep the remnant out of Jerusalem as that historic leftover under Sennacherib, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads it as that handful of Jews shut up in Jerusalem, who shall go out and by singular blessing increase exceedingly; the zeal is both for his own name and for the good of his undeserving people. Calvin reads the same remnant as the Church’s increase from a small number into many nations, and the zeal as God’s fervent love for that Church, sufficient for every purpose. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. Neither makes the remnant a later leftover chart.

Isaiah 37:33

33“Therefore thus says the LORD concerning the king of Assyria: He shall not come into this city or shoot an arrow there or come before it with a shield or cast up a siege mound against it.

Arminian

Wesley: he shall not—the army sent with Rabshaketh did not form a close siege against it, but only disposed themselves so as to block it up at some distance; possibly waiting till the king of Assyria had taken Libnah and Lachish (which they presumed he would speedily do). No arrow, no shield, no mound is still that word concerning this king after the remnant of v. 32.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have heard that this city would not see the arrow. Historic word against the king of Assyria, 8th century, is already named. The church is not to stretch “he shall not come into this city” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: therefore thus saith Jehovah. He now returns to the deliverance of which he had formerly spoken. He shall not enter into the city. God threatens that he will be as a fortress, to hinder him from entering into the city, and that he will even meet him, so as to hinder him from coming nearer or fighting against it; for he says that he shall not cast an arrow nor a balister.

Reformed accent: the refusal stays that historic bar at Jerusalem. Calvin will not let the siege mound become a newspaper of later walls.

Isaiah 37:34

34By the way that he came, by the same he shall return, and he shall not come into this city, declares the LORD.

Arminian

Wesley’s prophecy of vv. 21–35 still holds: by the way that he came, by the same shall he return, and shall not come into this city, saith the LORD—still that word after “he shall not come” in v. 33. The history is, almost in the same words contained 2 Kings 19.

Arminian reading: they might yet have seen him go back by that same way. Historic return-route of Sennacherib, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another road to make “by the way that he came” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: by the way that he came shall he return. To return by the same way is to depart without having accomplished anything. To confirm this, he adds that thus hath the Lord spoken; for as soon as he who cannot lie hath spoken, we ought to embrace and kiss his word, as if the result were rendered certain by the removal of every obstruction.

Reformed note: the same way stays that historic empty return. Calvin will not let the declaration become a newspaper of later marches.

Isaiah 37:35

35For I will defend this city to save it, for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David.”

Arminian

Wesley: Isaiah's prophecy, ver. 21-35. For I will defend this city to save it for mine own sake, and for my servant David's sake—still that close of the prophecy after the return-route of v. 34. The history is, almost in the same words contained 2 Kings 19.

Arminian close of the prophecy: they might yet have heard this city defended for the LORD’s sake and David’s, not a later chart. Historic defense of Jerusalem, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another city to make “I will defend this city” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: and I will be a protector. For my own sake: he calls on Hezekiah and all believers to remember his gracious covenant. Not because he finds any cause in them, but rather because he looks to himself first, that he may adhere firmly to his purpose, not to cast away the posterity of Abraham which he adopted. And for my servant David's sake: not as a private individual, but as a holy king whose throne was established by the hand of God; David is here represented as the type of the only Mediator. Though we most highly deserve a thousand deaths, yet it is enough for God to look to his goodness and faithfulness, that he may fulfill what he hath promised.

Reformed close of the prophecy: “for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David” stays that historic defense. Calvin will not let David become a newspaper of later courts, nor a God who shares the throne.

Isaiah 37:36

36And the angel of the LORD went out and struck down 185,000 in the camp of the Assyrians. And when people arose early in the morning, behold, these were all dead bodies.

Arminian

Wesley: an angel slays the Assyrians, ver. 36. Angel—such an angel as destroyed the first-born of Egypt. Arose—the few that were left alive: all their companions were dead.

Arminian reading: they might yet have risen to find those dead bodies after that angel went out. Historic 185,000 in the Assyrian camp, 8th century, are already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another stroke to make “the angel of the LORD” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: and the angel of Jehovah went out. It is expressly added that so great a multitude was slain by the hand of the angel. The Lord makes use of the ministrations of angels to promote the safety of believers; the angels may be regarded as his hand. We must beware of falling into the superstition of the Papists, who by their absurd worship of angels ascribe to them that power which belongs to God. Yet it is more probable that a single angel was commissioned to execute this judgment, as in the ancient redemption an angel passed through the whole of Egypt to slay the firstborn. Although God sometimes executes his vengeance by means of evil angels, yet he chose one of his willing servants. It is enough that the Lord, having determined to save Jerusalem from the hand of the Assyrian, cut off his army by a sudden death, without any agency of man.

Reformed note: the 185,000 stay that historic camp. Calvin will not let the angel become a newspaper of later plagues.

Isaiah 37:37

37Then Sennacherib king of Assyria departed and returned home and lived at Nineveh.

Arminian

Wesley: Sennacherib is slain at Nineveh by his own sons, ver. 37, 38. So Sennacherib—the manner of the expression intimates the great disorder and distraction of mind he was in. Departed, went, returned, dwelt at Nineveh is still that going home after the angel of v. 36.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have seen him live at Nineveh after that night. Historic return to his own land, 8th century, is already named. The church is not to stretch “lived at Nineveh” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: then Sennacherib, king of Assyria, went away and returned. He now shows how disgraceful was the retreat of this haughty tyrant. By employing a variety of words to express his departure, the Prophet indirectly censures the shameful flight; the repetition is not superfluous: he set out, he went away, he returned. And dwelt in Nineveh: he would not willingly have remained at rest, if despair had not held him like a chain. This means that he was satisfied with his ancient domains, of which Nineveh was the chief city and royal residence.

Reformed accent: Nineveh stays that historic dwelling after the empty return. Calvin will not let the departure become a newspaper of later thrones.

Isaiah 37:38

38And as he was worshiping in the house of Nisroch his god, Adrammelech and Sharezer, his sons, struck him down with the sword. And after they escaped into the land of Ararat, Esarhaddon his son reigned in his place.

Arminian

Wesley: was worshipping—the God of Israel had done enough to convince him, that he was the only true God. Yet he persists in his idolatry. Justly then is his blood mingled with his sacrifices, who will not be convinced by so dear-bought a demonstration, of his folly in worshipping idols. Sennacherib is slain at Nineveh by his own sons, ver. 37, 38.

Arminian close of the unit: they might yet have seen him fall in that house of Nisroch, in his own land. Historic death by his sons, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another temple to make “the house of Nisroch” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: while he was worshipping. The Jews allow themselves that liberty of conjecture in which they are always accustomed to indulge; it is unnecessary to spend time in such conjectures. Here it is highly important to behold, as in a picture, the unhappy death of tyrants, whom the Lord destroys without the agency of men. He is slain, not by a foreign enemy, not by a people in a state of sedition, and in a word, not by servants, but by his own sons, that the murder may be more disgraceful. This is more shocking than if they had been put to death by strangers. Insatiable gluttons who freely wallow in the blood of others are slain by their own followers.

Reformed close: death in the house of Nisroch stays that historic end in his own land. Calvin will not let the sons become a newspaper of later courts.

Key contrast Both keep Sennacherib’s death in the house of Nisroch as that historic end in his own land, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads the worship as persistent idolatry after enough to convince him: justly then is his blood mingled with his sacrifices, who will not be convinced by so dear-bought a demonstration of his folly in worshipping idols. Calvin refuses Jewish fables about the oracle and the intended sacrifice of a son, and reads the same death as the picture of tyrants destroyed without the agency of men, slain by their own sons, more shocking than death by strangers. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. Neither makes the house of Nisroch a later headline.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Isaiah sent to Hezekiah, the virgin daughter of Zion, whom have you mocked, I know your sitting down, the remnant, the sign of eating this year, I will defend this city, the angel of the LORD who struck 185,000, and Sennacherib killed by his sons in the house of Nisroch—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 37:21–38.

Arminian Notable voices

“Isaiah's prophecy, ver. 21-35. An angel slays the Assyrians, ver. 36. Sennacherib is slain at Nineveh by his own sons, ver. 37, 38. Virgin—So he calls Zion, or Jerusalem; because she was pure in good measure from that gross idolatry wherewith other people were defiled, which is called spiritual whoredom: and to signify, that God would defend her from the rape which Sennacherib intended to commit upon her with no less care than parents do their virgin daughters from those who seek to force and deflower them. Hast thou not—Hast thou not long since learned, that there is a supreme and powerful God, by whose decree and providence all these wars and calamities were sent and ordered; whose mere instrument thou art, so that thou hast no cause for these vain boastings? This work is mine, not thine. I know—Though thou dost not know me, yet I throughly know thee, and all thy designs and actions. My hook—What a comfort is it, that God has a hook in the nose and a bridle in the jaws of all his and our enemies? A sign—Of the certain accomplishment of the promises here made: that God will not only preserve the city from his present fury, but also bless his people with a durable prosperity. The remnant—They shall be well fixt and provided for themselves, and then do good to others. Go forth—That handful of Jews who were now gathered together, and shut up in Jerusalem, shall go out of their several habitations, and by my singular blessing increase exceedingly. The zeal—God will do it from the zeal which he hath, both for his own name, and for the good of his undeserving people. Angel—Such an angel as destroyed the first-born of Egypt. Was worshipping—The God of Israel had done enough to convince him, that he was the only true God. Yet he persists in his idolatry. Justly then is his blood mingled with his sacrifices.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 37:21–38; 2 Kgs 19:20–37)

Reformed Notable voices

“Then Isaiah sent to Hezekiah. This shews the result of the prayer; as soon as matters have come to an extremity, God suddenly holds out his hand to assist the pious king by the Prophet Isaiah. Not that he immediately stretches out his arm to drive away the enemies, but he promises deliverance by the mouth of the Prophet, and thus calls even now into exercise the faith of his servant. The virgin daughter of Zion: there is greater emphasis in this address to the whole Church than if he had said the same thing to Hezekiah as a private individual; he intended to express the weakness of the city of Jerusalem, because she was like an orphan and destitute virgin, who was insulted by this base ruffian; God, as the father to whom this insult is offered, declares that he knows well the schemes of that wicked man. Whom hast thou reproached? this tyrant insults not only Jerusalem but God himself; they who fight against us wage war with the living God. Hast thou not heard? The greater part of commentators explain this verse as if the Lord declared that nothing was now done by this tyrant which he had not foretold; but I explain it in a different manner, which is, that Jerusalem will be preserved by the assistance of God, because he is the protector of it; I have founded the Church, and therefore the salvation of the Church shall always be my care. I know thy sitting down: the providence of God restrains them and drives them hither and thither. I will put my hook in thy nose: since thou canst not be tamed, I will curb thee like a savage beast. This shall be a sign: God will not only once rescue them, but largely and bountifully bestows his kindness. Out of Jerusalem shall go forth a remnant: the zeal of Jehovah of hosts will do this; he regards his Church with such fervent love that he does not hesitate to work in an extraordinary manner. I will be a protector: for my own sake, and for my servant David's sake—David is here represented as the type of the only Mediator. The angel of Jehovah went out and slew in the camp of the Assyrians a hundred and eighty-five thousand; more probable that a single angel was commissioned, as in the ancient redemption an angel passed through Egypt. Sennacherib went away and returned and dwelt in Nineveh; while he was worshipping in the temple of Nisroch, his own sons slew him.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 37:21–38)
