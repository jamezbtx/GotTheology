Passage

Isaiah 37

Verses 14–20 · ESV · Voices

Hezekiah received the letter and spread it before the LORD; prayer: you are God, you made heaven and earth; the kings of Assyria have laid waste the nations; their gods were wood and stone; save us that all kingdoms may know you alone are the LORD. The unit stays with that historic 8th-century prayer in the house of the LORD—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 37:14

14Hezekiah received the letter from the hand of the messengers, and read it; and Hezekiah went up to the house of the LORD, and spread it before the LORD.

Arminian

Wesley: his prayer, ver. 14-20. The history related here is, almost in the same words contained 2 Kings 19. Hezekiah received the letter from the messengers, went up unto the house of the LORD, and spread it before the LORD—still that prayer after the blasphemous letter of vv. 8–13.

Arminian reading: they might yet have taken that letter into the house. Historic spreading before the LORD, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another dispatch to make “spread it before the LORD” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: Hezekiah took the letters. He immediately went into the Temple, to lament before the Lord the calamity which he could not remove, and to cast upon him his grief and his anxieties. Nor was this a blind or confused lamentation, but the pious king wished to move God by his tears and complaints to render assistance. In spreading the letters before the Lord, he does not do this as if the Lord did not know what was contained in the letters; God knows our wants and necessities before we ask anything from him. The reason and design was this, that he might excite his own earnestness, and inflame his own ardor, in prayer.

Reformed note: the spreading is still that historic going into the house of the LORD. Calvin will not let the letter become a newspaper of later briefs, nor an open-theist notice to a God who had not read it.

Isaiah 37:15

15And Hezekiah prayed to the LORD:

Arminian

Wesley’s heading still holds: his prayer, ver. 14-20. And Hezekiah prayed unto the LORD after spreading the letter in v. 14, as in the Kings parallel.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have prayed in that house after the letter was spread. Historic prayer of Hezekiah, 8th century, is already named. The church is not to stretch “Hezekiah prayed” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: because Sennacherib was the agent employed by Satan to shake the faith of Hezekiah, he defends himself by this rampart, that God possesses infinite power; by bestowing on God those lofty praises, he undoubtedly encourages himself to confidence in supplication. Before forming any prayer, he overturns the delusions by which Satan had endeavored to shake his courage. That our prayers may not be unsuccessful, we ought always to hold it as certain that God is the rewarder of all who seek him.

Reformed accent: the praying is still that historic rampart after the letter of v. 14. Calvin will not let “Hezekiah prayed” become a newspaper of later courts.

Isaiah 37:16

16“O LORD of hosts, God of Israel, enthroned above the cherubim, you are the God, you alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth; you have made the heavens and the earth.

Arminian

Wesley: he calls him the God of Israel, because Israel was his peculiar people; but yet the God of the whole earth, not as Sennacherib fancied, the God of Israel only. Let them say what they will, thou art sovereign Lord, the God of gods, even thou alone: universal Lord of all the kingdoms of the earth; and rightful Lord; for thou hast made heaven and earth. Being creator of all, by an incontestable title thou art owner and ruler of all.

Arminian reading: they might yet have named him God of Israel and still God of the whole earth, not a later chart. Historic titles in that house, 8th century, are already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another throne to make “enthroned above the cherubim” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: thou alone art God over all the kingdoms of the earth: not only does he assert God’s almighty power, but likewise maintains the authority which he exercises over the whole world; these statements strengthen the faith he entertained about the providence of God, by which he governs the world and every part of it. It is impossible that God, who is the Creator of heaven and earth, should forsake his work; on the contrary, he governs by his providence the human race. Sitting between the cherubim is on account of the form of the ark; it was a symbol of the presence of God, though his power was not confined to it; Hezekiah intended to express his firm belief that God was present with him. God accommodates himself to our weakness that the sacraments may serve to us the purpose of ladders.

Reformed note: the titles stay that historic prayer in the house. Calvin will not let “you have made the heavens and the earth” become a newspaper of later worlds.

Key contrast Both keep Hezekiah’s titles as that historic prayer in the house of the LORD, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads God of Israel as peculiar people, yet God of the whole earth, not as Sennacherib fancied the God of Israel only: sovereign Lord, the God of gods, even thou alone; being creator of all, by an incontestable title thou art owner and ruler of all. Calvin reads the same titles as providence and presence: it is impossible that God, who is the Creator of heaven and earth, should forsake his work; he governs by his providence; sitting between the cherubim is on account of the form of the ark, a symbol of the presence of God, though his power was not confined to it; Word and sacraments serve as ladders. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. Neither makes the prayer an open-theist briefing of a God who did not know the letter.

Isaiah 37:17

17Incline your ear, O LORD, and hear; open your eyes, O LORD, and see; and hear all the words of Sennacherib, which he has sent to mock the living God.

Arminian

Wesley: him—Rabshakeh: he would not do him the honour to name him. “Incline your ear… hear all the words of Sennacherib” is still that prayer after the titles of v. 16, as in the Kings parallel.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have asked the LORD to hear the mock without naming the envoy as a peer. Historic words of Sennacherib, 8th century, are already named. The church is not to stretch “mock the living God” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: from these words we conclude how great was the perplexity of Hezekiah; the earnestness that pervades the prayer breathes an amazing power of anguish. Does he think that he is asleep or does not hear? By no means; but in a matter of such difficulty we frequently speak as if we thought that God was absent. Open thine eyes: it is as if Hezekiah had prayed that the assistance of God, which he had long kept in his heart committed to the guardianship of hope, would be actually and publicly manifested. Although he was deeply affected by anxiety about his kingdom and people, yet he set a higher value on the glory of God than on all other sources of uneasiness.

Reformed accent: “incline your ear” is still that historic plea, not an open question in God. Calvin will not let the mock become a newspaper of later boasts.

Key contrast Both keep “incline your ear” as that historic prayer after the letter, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads “him” as Rabshakeh: he would not do him the honour to name him. Calvin reads the same petition as not implying that God is asleep or does not hear: Hezekiah wants the assistance he had kept in hope to be actually and publicly manifested; he set a higher value on the glory of God than on all other sources of uneasiness. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. Neither makes “incline your ear” an open-theist gap in God’s knowledge.

Isaiah 37:18

18Truly, O LORD, the kings of Assyria have laid waste all the nations and their lands,

Arminian

Wesley’s prayer of vv. 14–20 still holds: of a truth the kings of Assyria have laid waste all the nations and their countries—still that prayer after the mock of v. 17. The history is, almost in the same words contained 2 Kings 19.

Arminian reading: they might yet have granted the waste of the nations without weighing Jerusalem with their gods. Historic kings of Assyria, 8th century, are already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another ruin to make “laid waste all the nations” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: here Hezekiah begins to distinguish between the false gods and the true God. It is not enough that we believe in something which heathens imagine to be a deity; we must believe in God in such a manner as to distinguish him from pretended gods. The beginning of true piety is, that from the whole multitude of false gods we shall wisely distinguish that one God to whom we ought to be entirely devoted. The waste of the nations is still that historic confession after v. 17.

Reformed note: the nations laid waste stay those historic Assyrian falls. Calvin will not let “all the nations” become a newspaper of later maps.

Isaiah 37:19

19and have cast their gods into the fire. For they were no gods, but the work of men's hands, wood and stone. Therefore they were destroyed.

Arminian

Wesley’s prayer still holds: his prayer, ver. 14-20. They were no gods, but the work of men’s hands, wood and stone—still that prayer after the waste of v. 18, as in the Kings parallel.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have named wood and stone and not called the LORD one more name. Historic gods cast into the fire, 8th century, are already named. The church is not to stretch “wood and stone” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: by two arguments Hezekiah shews that they were not gods; first, because they consisted of matter, and secondly, because they were formed by the hands of men. Nothing can be more absurd than for a man to assume the right to create a god. It is in the highest degree absurd to frame some deity out of matter which is corruptible and devoid of feeling, as if wood or stone, whenever it received a shape, began to be a god.

Reformed accent: wood and stone stay those historic no-gods under Assyria. Calvin will not let the fire become a newspaper of later idols.

Isaiah 37:20

20So now, O LORD our God, save us from his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that you alone are the LORD.”

Arminian

Wesley: his prayer, ver. 14-20. Now therefore, O LORD our God, save us from his hand, that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that thou art the LORD, even thou only—still that close of the prayer after wood and stone in v. 19.

Arminian close of the unit: they might yet have asked to be saved that the kingdoms would know the LORD alone, not a later chart. Historic save-us in the house, 8th century, is already named. This is not a millennial camp fight. The church is not to hunt another kingdom to make “you alone are the LORD” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: at the conclusion of his prayer, the pious king now rises above that fear with which he had struggled. Although God does not always deliver his people from temporal evils, yet as he had promised that he would be the protector of the city, Hezekiah could firmly believe that all the efforts of that wicked tyrant would be fruitless. Nothing is more desirable than to make his name glorious; this is even the chief design of our salvation. Those men are unworthy of his assistance who, satisfied with their own salvation, disregard why God chooses to preserve them. Hezekiah does not only desire that the God of Israel shall hold a certain rank, but that all idols shall be abolished, and that he shall reign alone; he does not admit companions.

Reformed close: “save us… that all the kingdoms of the earth may know” stays that historic plea in the house. Calvin will not let the save-us pass as a newspaper of later camps, nor as a God who shares the throne.
