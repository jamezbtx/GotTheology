Passage

Isaiah 48

Verses 12–22 · ESV · Voices

After 48:1–11—Hear this, O house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel but not in truth or right; obstinate neck of iron sinew and forehead brass; former things declared of old and done; new and hidden things of deliverance by Cyrus; rebel from the womb; for my name’s sake I defer my anger; refined in the furnace of affliction, not as silver; for my own sake I do it, and my glory I will not give to another—the word turns: Listen to me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I called; I am he; I am the first, and I am the last.

Isaiah 48:12

12“Listen to me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I called! I am he; I am the first, and I am the last.

Arminian

Listen to me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I called! I am he; I am the first, and I am the last. Wesley: O Israel—Whom I have called out of the world to be my peculiar people.

Arminian hearing: Israel called out of the world as his peculiar people; I am he, first and last, already is. Shared loudly with Calvin: I am the first and I am the last; Israel whom I called. Historic Jacob and Israel, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “O Israel—Whom I have called” is his lemma for ESV “Israel, whom I called.” No open theism: he is first and last; he is not becoming himself as events unfold. Prefer no keyContrast on 12: shared first-and-last sovereignty; do not fork. Match 48:1: Hear this, O house of Jacob, called by the name but not in truth; now Listen, O Jacob, whom I called. Match 44:6: I am the first and I am the last. Match v. 13: the same hand that founded earth.

Reformed

Listen to me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I called. Calvin: Hearken—he declares his eternity so we may know he is always like himself, not measured by our capacity. Israel my called: contrasts the reprobation and false name at the start of the chapter; a father may call a son bastard in rebuke yet still acknowledge him; calling no ingratitude can set aside. I am the first and the last: always like himself, does not alter his counsel (Rom 3:3-4; 11:29); not merely eternal essence but that he will be to us what he has always been; distinguish him from idols.

Reformed hearing: eternity is always-like-himself, counsel unaltered; Israel my called still names the son after 48:1’s false name; first and last is what he will be to us, not mere essence. Shared with Wesley: I am the first and I am the last; Israel whom I called. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Hearken” and “Israel my called” name ESV “Listen to me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I called.” No open theism: always like himself; does not alter his counsel (Rom 3:3-4; 11:29). Prefer no keyContrast on 12: shared sovereignty; do not fork. Match 48:1: name-not-truth now answered by Israel my called. Match 44:6: first and last. Match v. 13: hand that founded earth.

Isaiah 48:13

13My hand laid the foundation of the earth, and my right hand spread out the heavens; when I call to them, they stand forth together.

Arminian

My hand laid the foundation of the earth, and my right hand spread out the heavens; when I call to them, they stand forth together. Wesley: Stand up—They are still continually in readiness to execute my commands.

Arminian hearing: heaven and earth stand up, still continually in readiness to execute his commands. Shared: my hand laid the foundation; when I call they stand forth together. Historic creation, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Stand up” is his lemma for ESV “they stand forth together.” No open theism: his hand already laid the foundation; they stand when he calls. Prefer no keyContrast on 13: Wesley’s continually-in-readiness overlaps Calvin’s continual government; skip the fork. Match v. 12: the first and last is this founder. Match v. 14: the same caller now assembles Jacob to hear of Cyrus.

Reformed

My hand laid the foundation of the earth, and my right hand spread out the heavens. Calvin: Hand founded the earth; right hand measured and upheld the heavens. When I call, they stand up together: either first creation (he spake and they were done, Ps 33:9) or continual government—heaven and earth yield to his voice, infinite power.

Reformed hearing: founding and measuring are infinite power; they stand at his voice, creation or government. Shared with Wesley: when I call they stand forth; continually ready / yield to his voice. Historic creation and government, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “they stand up together” names ESV “they stand forth together”; Ps 33:9 is his sense of first creation, not a different verse. No open theism: he spake and they were done. Prefer no keyContrast on 13: overlap with Wesley’s readiness; skip the fork. Match v. 12: first and last. Match v. 14: who among the idols declared these things.

Isaiah 48:14

14“Assemble, all of you, and listen! Who among them has declared these things? The LORD loves him; he shall perform his purpose on Babylon, and his arm shall be against the Chaldeans.

Arminian

Assemble, all of you, and listen! Who among them has declared these things? The LORD loves him; he shall perform his purpose on Babylon, and his arm shall be against the Chaldeans. Wesley: Which—Which of the gods whom any of you serve. Him—Cyrus.

Arminian hearing: which of the gods you serve has declared these things? Him is Cyrus; the LORD loves him; he shall perform his purpose on Babylon. Shared loudly with Calvin: Him = historic Cyrus against Babylon and the Chaldeans, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: ESV “The LORD loves him”; Wesley “The LORD hath loved him” (Cyrus) is the KJV lemma, not a different verse; “Which” for “Who among them.” Prefer no keyContrast on 14: both camps take historic Cyrus; do not fork electing vs common love as a millennial fight. No open theism: the LORD loves him; he shall perform his purpose. Match 45:1: the LORD’s anointed, Cyrus. Match v. 15: I have spoken and called him.

Reformed

Assemble, all of you, and listen! Who among them has declared these things? The LORD loves him. Calvin: Assemble and hear. Who among them (idols/diviners) foretelleth those things? Jehovah hath loved him: Cyrus, not named, chosen to take Babylon. Loved not in the absolute electing sense; limited to the successful expedition (Saul likewise dear for a time). He executes God’s counsel; Cyrus not intentionally obedient; God makes even the ignorant go where he pleases.

Reformed hearing: idols cannot foretell; him is Cyrus, loved for the expedition, not as elect believer; God makes the ignorant go where he pleases. Shared with Wesley: Him = Cyrus; the LORD loves him; purpose on Babylon. Historic Cyrus, historic Babylon, not a newspaper, not a living politician. Quote-honesty: ESV “The LORD loves him”; Calvin “Jehovah hath loved him” is the KJV lemma, not a different English Bible. Prefer no keyContrast on 14: Calvin’s not-absolute-electing-love is about Cyrus’s person, still historic; do not millennial-fight Wesley’s Him = Cyrus. No open theism: he shall perform his purpose. Match 45:1: anointed Cyrus. Match v. 15: I have spoken and called him.

Isaiah 48:15

15I, even I, have spoken and called him; I have brought him, and he will prosper in his way.

Arminian

I, even I, have spoken and called him; I have brought him, and he will prosper in his way. Wesley has no extra lemma. Paraphrase from v. 14 honestly: Him—Cyrus. So here: I, even I, have spoken and called him—that same Cyrus—I have brought him, and he will prosper in his way. Do not invent unique Wesley sentences.

Arminian hearing: the speaker is the one who called Cyrus in v. 14; no new Wesley paragraph. Shared: I have spoken and called him; I have brought him; he will prosper. Historic Cyrus, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: ESV “called him” names the same him Wesley called Cyrus at v. 14. No open theism: I have spoken; I have brought him; he will prosper. Prefer no keyContrast on 15: shared historic Cyrus; do not fork. Match v. 14: The LORD loves him. Match v. 16: not spoken in secret; I have been there.

Reformed

I, even I, have spoken and called him; I have brought him, and he will prosper in his way. Calvin: It is I who have spoken; therefore he shall prosper. Twofold: none but Israel’s God spoke hidden events; he is faithful so they shall happen. Called and conducted Cyrus: not the electing call of believers; external blessing; Cyrus had vices and a lamentable end. Agency for delivering the Church.

Reformed hearing: I have spoken, therefore he prospers; the call is not the electing call of believers but external blessing and agency to deliver the Church; Cyrus had vices and a lamentable end. Shared with Wesley: I have spoken and called him—Cyrus; he will prosper. Historic Cyrus, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “It is I who have spoken; therefore he shall prosper” names ESV “I, even I, have spoken—he will prosper in his way.” Prefer no keyContrast on 15: skip not-electing-call rather than invent a Wesley fight; both historic Cyrus. No open theism: I have spoken; they shall happen. Match v. 14: loved him for the expedition. Match v. 16: from the time it came to be I have been there.

Isaiah 48:16

16Draw near to me, hear this: from the beginning I have not spoken in secret, from the time it came to be I have been there.” And now the Lord GOD has sent me, and his Spirit.

Arminian

Draw near to me, hear this: from the beginning I have not spoken in secret, from the time it came to be I have been there. And now the Lord GOD has sent me, and his Spirit. Wesley: In secret—I have openly revealed my mind to you. The beginning—Either from the first time that I began to prophecy until this time: or from the beginning of my taking you to be my people, and of revealing my mind unto you. From the time—From the time that I first spoke of it, I am or was there, to effect what I had foretold. The Lord—God by his Spirit. Me—The prophet Isaiah; who was a type of Christ, and so this may have a respect to him also.

Arminian hearing: not in secret is openly revealed; beginning is first prophecy or first taking them as people; from the time he spoke it he was there to effect it; Me is Isaiah, type of Christ, and so this may respect him also; The Lord is God by his Spirit. Shared: not spoken in secret; from the time it came to be he was there; now sent. Historic Isaiah’s commission, not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight. Quote-honesty: ESV “the Lord GOD has sent me, and his Spirit”; KJV “the Lord GOD, and his Spirit, hath sent me”—Wesley’s Me and The Lord follow that KJV lemma, not a different verse. No open theism: I am or was there, to effect what I had foretold. Match v. 15: I have spoken and called him. Match v. 17: Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer.

Reformed

Draw near to me, hear this: from the beginning I have not spoken in secret, from the time it came to be I have been there. And now the Lord GOD has sent me, and his Spirit. Calvin: Draw near—God speaking first; not from the beginning in secret; ignorance is their depravity. From the time it was done, I was there: what he uttered was executed by his power. Then Isaiah begins to speak of himself: that God who spoke from the beginning now speaks by him. And his Spirit: not something different from God; in one essence Three Persons; the Spirit is the only teacher and director of all the prophets (1 Cor 12:3, 6); proof of the Spirit’s divinity, since sending prophets belongs to God alone. Reject those who boast mission without the Spirit’s gifts—illustration only, not this verse’s referent.

Reformed hearing: God speaks first, not in secret; he was there executing what he uttered; then Isaiah testifies that this God now sends him; And his Spirit is not another god but Three Persons in one essence; the Spirit is God, teacher of the prophets. Shared with Wesley: not spoken in secret; from the time it came to be he was there; now sent. Historic Isaiah’s commission, not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight. Quote-honesty: ESV “the Lord GOD has sent me, and his Spirit”; Calvin’s “And his Spirit” comments the KJV lemma “the Lord GOD, and his Spirit, hath sent me,” not a different English Bible. No open theism: what he uttered was executed by his power. Match v. 15: I have spoken. Match v. 17: Thus says the LORD.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “Me” as the prophet Isaiah, who was a type of Christ, and so this may have a respect to him also; “The Lord”—God by his Spirit; from the time he first spoke of it, he is or was there, to effect what he had foretold. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take God speaking first—not in secret from the beginning; he was there when the event occurred; then Isaiah testifies that this God now sends him; “And his Spirit”: Three Persons in one essence; the Spirit is the only teacher of the prophets and is God, because sending prophets belongs to God alone. Shared: not spoken in secret; from the time it came to be he was there; now sent. Historic Isaiah’s commission and the God who effects what he foretold, not a millennial camp fight, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 48:17

17Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: “I am the LORD your God, who teaches you to profit, who leads you in the way you should go.

Arminian

Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: I am the LORD your God, who teaches you to profit, who leads you in the way you should go. Wesley: Teacheth—Who from time to time have made known to thee, all necessary doctrines; which, if observed by thee, would have been infinitely profitable to thee, both for this life and that to come. So that it is not my fault, but thine own, if thou dost not profit. Leadeth—Who acquainteth thee with thy duty in all the concerns of thy life; so that thou canst not pretend ignorance.

Arminian hearing: teaches is all necessary doctrines, profitable for this life and that to come, so the fault if they do not profit is theirs; leads is acquaintance with duty, no pretended ignorance. Shared: I am the LORD your God who teaches you to profit and leads you in the way. Historic Redeemer of Israel, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: ESV “teaches you to profit”; Wesley “teacheth thee to profit” is the KJV lemma, not a different verse; “Leadeth” for “leads you.” Prefer no keyContrast on 17: both teaching-for-profit and leading-in-the-way; skip rather than invent a Law-versus-gospel fight. Match v. 16: now sent. Match v. 18: Oh that you had paid attention to my commandments.

Reformed

Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel. Calvin: Thus saith Jehovah—four following verses same subject: promises deliverance but first shows it was their fault they were in slavery. Teaching thee profitably: God’s teaching would keep them safe if they rested on it; he teaches for our salvation. Leading thee: way of salvation if we hearken; Law as rule of a holy life (Deut 30). If they had not defrauded themselves, nothing profitable would have been hidden.

Reformed hearing: deliverance promised, but slavery was their fault; teaching would have kept them safe; leading is the way of salvation if they hearken; Law as holy-life rule (Deut 30). Shared with Wesley: teaches to profit; leads in the way; the fault is theirs if they do not profit. Historic Redeemer, historic slavery, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Teaching thee profitably” names ESV “teaches you to profit.” Prefer no keyContrast on 17: both not-my-fault-but-thine; Calvin’s Deut 30 thickens the same leading. Match v. 16: God who spoke now says. Match v. 18: O that thou hadst hearkened.

Isaiah 48:18

18Oh that you had paid attention to my commandments! Then your peace would have been like a river, and your righteousness like the waves of the sea;

Arminian

Oh that you had paid attention to my commandments! Then your peace would have been like a river, and your righteousness like the waves of the sea. Wesley: As the waves—Infinite and continual.

Arminian hearing: waves are infinite and continual; peace like a river and righteousness like those waves would have followed attention to the commandments. Shared: Oh that you had paid attention; peace like a river; righteousness like the waves. Historic Jacob’s missed peace, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: ESV “Oh that you had paid attention”; KJV “O that thou hadst hearkened”—Wesley has no extra lemma on hearkened, only “As the waves—Infinite and continual.” Prefer no keyContrast on 18: Wesley’s lemma is only waves; Calvin forbids penetrating why the Spirit was not added; do not invent an Arminian-versus-Reformed fight on the Spirit. Match v. 17: teaches to profit, leads in the way. Match v. 19: offspring like the sand.

Reformed

Oh that you had paid attention to my commandments! Then your peace would have been like a river, and your righteousness like the waves of the sea. Calvin: O if thou hadst hearkened—meets murmurs about captivity; they did not submit to the doctrine of salvation. Alludes to Deut 32:29. God as a father deplores the wretchedness of children; figurative appropriation of human affections; he takes no pleasure in our distresses. Ready to bestow blessings if we did not drive him away. Foolish to attempt to penetrate his secret counsel and inquire why he did not add the efficacy of the Spirit to the external word; nothing is said here about his power, only a reproof of hard-heartedness, that they may be inexcusable. Peace as a river: all prosperous events, never dried up. Righteousness as waves: a well-regulated commonwealth, not (here) spiritual righteousness or forgiveness—those commentators wander from the meaning.

Reformed hearing: fatherly lament, not a window into secret counsel; do not ask why the Spirit’s efficacy was not added to the external word; peace is prosperous events; righteousness is a well-regulated commonwealth. Shared with Wesley: peace like a river; waves infinite and continual. Historic captivity’s missed peace, not a millennial or atonement fight. Quote-honesty: ESV “Oh that you had paid attention”; Calvin “O if thou hadst hearkened” is the KJV lemma, not a different verse. Prefer no keyContrast on 18: Calvin himself forbids penetrating why the Spirit was not added; Wesley has only waves; skip the fork. Match v. 17: teaching they would not rest on. Match v. 19: seed as the sand.

Isaiah 48:19

19your offspring would have been like the sand, and your descendants like its grains; their name would never be cut off or destroyed from before me.”

Arminian

your offspring would have been like the sand, and your descendants like its grains; their name would never be cut off or destroyed from before me. Wesley has no extra lemma. Paraphrase from v. 18 honestly: As the waves—Infinite and continual. So here: offspring like the sand and descendants like its grains would have been that same infinite and continual fruit, their name never cut off from before him. Do not invent unique Wesley sentences.

Arminian hearing: the waves’ infinite-and-continual of v. 18 is why the sand-seed is named here; no new Wesley paragraph. Shared: offspring like the sand; name never cut off from before me. Historic promise to Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: ESV “your offspring would have been like the sand” alludes with Calvin to Gen 22:17; Wesley has no extra lemma, so do not invent one. Prefer no keyContrast on 19: skip rather than invent Wesley prose. Match v. 18: peace like a river, waves of the sea. Match v. 20: the LORD has redeemed his servant Jacob.

Reformed

your offspring would have been like the sand, and your descendants like its grains; their name would never be cut off or destroyed from before me. Calvin: Seed as the sand—alludes to Gen 22:17. They prevented God from causing them to enjoy the fruit of that promise. Name cut off: captivity as cut off from the land and Temple presence.

Reformed hearing: sand-seed is Gen 22:17; they blocked the fruit of that promise; name cut off is captivity from land and Temple. Shared with Wesley: offspring like the sand; name never cut off—here the missed fruit of v. 18’s waves. Historic captivity, historic Temple, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Seed as the sand” names ESV “your offspring would have been like the sand”; Gen 22:17 is allusion, not a different verse. Prefer no keyContrast on 19: Wesley has no extra lemma. Match v. 18: hearkened not. Match v. 20: yet Go out from Babylon.

Isaiah 48:20

20Go out from Babylon, flee from Chaldea, declare this with a shout of joy, proclaim it, send it out to the end of the earth; say, “The LORD has redeemed his servant Jacob!”

Arminian

Go out from Babylon, flee from Chaldea, declare this with a shout of joy, proclaim it, send it out to the end of the earth; say, The LORD has redeemed his servant Jacob! Wesley: Singing—With joy and songs of praise. Declare—Publish God’s wonderful works.

Arminian hearing: shout of joy is singing with songs of praise; declare is publish God’s wonderful works; the LORD has redeemed his servant Jacob. Shared loudly with Calvin: Go out from Babylon; historic Jacob redeemed from historic Chaldea, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Singing” is his lemma for ESV “with a shout of joy”; “Declare—Publish” for “declare this—proclaim it.” Prefer no keyContrast on 20: both historic Babylon and Jacob. No open theism: the LORD has redeemed. Match v. 19: name not cut off. Match v. 21: they did not thirst in the deserts. Match 47:1: virgin daughter of Babylon in the dust.

Reformed

Go out from Babylon, flee from Chaldea, declare this with a shout of joy. Calvin: Go out of Babylon—second clause: he will still be Redeemer though they were unworthy. Unwearied forbearance. Voice of rejoicing: future as if arrived. Historic Babylon, historic Jacob.

Reformed hearing: still Redeemer though unworthy; forbearance unwearied; the shout is future spoken as arrived. Shared with Wesley: Go out from Babylon; joy and praise; the LORD has redeemed his servant Jacob. Historic Babylon, historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Go out of Babylon” and “voice of rejoicing” name ESV “Go out from Babylon” and “shout of joy.” Prefer no keyContrast on 20: shared historic redemption. Match 47:1: Babylon already told to sit in the dust. Match v. 21: water from the rock on that road. Match v. 22: no peace for the wicked.

Isaiah 48:21

21They did not thirst when he led them through the deserts; he made water flow for them from the rock; he split the rock and the water gushed out.

Arminian

They did not thirst when he led them through the deserts; he made water flow for them from the rock; he split the rock and the water gushed out. Wesley: They thirsted not—They shall not thirst. He speaks of things to come, as if they were already past.

Arminian hearing: they thirsted not means they shall not thirst; things to come spoken as already past; water from the rock is that coming supply. Shared with Calvin: Exodus typology for the historic return from Babylon, not a millennial chart. Historic deserts and historic rock, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “They thirsted not—They shall not thirst” is his lemma for ESV “They did not thirst.” Prefer no keyContrast on 21: both future-as-past and Exodus 16–17 / Num 20 for the return. Match v. 20: go out from Babylon. Match v. 22: yet no peace for the wicked.

Reformed

They did not thirst when he led them through the deserts; he made water flow for them from the rock. Calvin: They thirsted not—recall Exodus 16–17, Num 20; God will easily surmount wildernesses on the return. Things to come as if past.

Reformed hearing: the Exodus rock is the type; wildernesses on the Babylon-return will be as easily surmounted. Shared with Wesley: they shall not thirst; things to come as if already past; historic return, not a millennial chart. Historic Exodus and historic return, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “They thirsted not” names ESV “They did not thirst”; Exodus 16–17 and Num 20 are the recalled type, not a different verse. Prefer no keyContrast on 21: shared Exodus typology for the historic return. Match v. 20: flee from Chaldea. Match v. 22: no peace for the wicked.

Isaiah 48:22

22“There is no peace,” says the LORD, “for the wicked.”

Arminian

There is no peace, says the LORD, for the wicked. Wesley: No peace—God having foretold that peace which he would give to his servant Jacob, adds an explication; and declares, that wicked men should not enjoy the benefit of this mercy.

Arminian hearing: Jacob’s peace of v. 18 and v. 20 is explained: wicked men should not enjoy that mercy. Shared loudly with Calvin: no peace for the wicked; promises are not for hypocrites. Historic Jacob’s mercy, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “No peace” is his lemma for ESV “There is no peace—for the wicked.” Prefer no keyContrast on 22: both camps take wicked men shut out of Jacob’s peace; do not fork. Match v. 18: peace like a river, missed by the inattentive. Match v. 20: the LORD has redeemed his servant Jacob.

Reformed

There is no peace, says the LORD, for the wicked. Calvin: No peace to the wicked—the Lord denies to wicked men that peace of which they are unworthy; promises do not belong to hypocrites, lest they cherish false confidence. Also: weak consciences should not be troubled when the multitude despise the doctrine.

Reformed hearing: wicked men are denied the peace they are unworthy of; hypocrites must not claim the promises; weak consciences need not be troubled by the multitude’s contempt. Shared with Wesley: wicked men should not enjoy Jacob’s mercy; no peace for the wicked. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “No peace to the wicked” names ESV “There is no peace—for the wicked.” Prefer no keyContrast on 22: shared no-peace-to-the-wicked; skip Calvin’s weak-consciences thickening rather than invent a fork. Match v. 18: peace like a river. Match v. 20: redeemed Jacob. Match 48:1: called by the name but not in truth.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Foolish to attempt to penetrate his secret counsel and inquire why he did not add the efficacy of the Spirit to the external word; nothing is said here about his power, only a reproof of hard-heartedness, that they may be inexcusable. Peace as a river: all prosperous events, never dried up. Righteousness as waves: a well-regulated commonwealth, not (here) spiritual righteousness or forgiveness—those commentators wander from the meaning. Seed as the sand (Gen 22:17); they prevented God from causing them to enjoy the fruit of that promise. Name cut off: captivity as cut off from the land and Temple presence. Go out of Babylon: he will still be Redeemer though they were unworthy; unwearied forbearance; voice of rejoicing, future as if arrived. They thirsted not: recall Exodus 16–17, Num 20; God will easily surmount wildernesses on the return. No peace to the wicked: the Lord denies to wicked men that peace of which they are unworthy; promises do not belong to hypocrites, lest they cherish false confidence; weak consciences should not be troubled when the multitude despise the doctrine.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 48:12–22)
