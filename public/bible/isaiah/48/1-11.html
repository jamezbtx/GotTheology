Isaiah 48

Verses 1–11 · ESV · Voices

After 47:1–15—Come down and sit in the dust, O virgin daughter of Babylon; sit on the ground without a throne, O daughter of the Chaldeans; millstones and grinding, nakedness uncovered, Our Redeemer the Holy One of Israel, mistress of kingdoms no more, no mercy on the aged, I am and there is no one besides me, sorceries that cannot charm, stargazers wearied, none to save—the word turns: Hear this, O house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel, and who came from the waters of Judah, who swear by the name of the LORD and confess the God of Israel, but not in truth or right.

Isaiah 48:1

1Hear this, O house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel, and who came from the waters of Judah, who swear by the name of the LORD and confess the God of Israel, but not in truth or right.

Arminian

Hear this, O house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel, and who came from the waters of Judah, who swear by the name of the LORD and confess the God of Israel, but not in truth or right. Wesley: Called—Who are Israelites in name, but not in truth. Are come—From the lineage of your progenitor, Judah, as waters flow from a fountain. Swear—Who profess the true religion; (one act of religion being put for all) and call themselves by his name.

Arminian hearing: Israelites in name, not in truth; waters of Judah are lineage from the progenitor as waters from a fountain; swear is one act standing for the whole profession. Shared: Hear this, O house of Jacob; called by the name of Israel; not in truth. Historic house of Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: ESV “not in truth or right”; Wesley “not in truth, nor in righteousness” is the KJV lemma, not a different verse. Prefer no keyContrast on 1: both camps take name-not-truth hypocrites; skip rather than invent a fork. Match 47:1: after the virgin daughter of Babylon in the dust, now Hear, O house of Jacob. Match v. 2: holy city and vain confidence.

Reformed

Hear this, O house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel. Calvin: addresses the Jews; spurious Israel with nothing more than the name; honorable name used to shame false boasting. Waters of Judah: fountain from which the Jews proceeded; degenerate children of holy fathers. Swear by Jehovah: one department of worship for the whole; hypocrites loudly boast his name yet in their hearts opposed. Not in truth nor in righteousness: mere pretense.

Reformed hearing: the name Israel shames false boasting; waters of Judah are the fountain of holy fathers, now degenerate; swear is one department for the whole; not in truth nor in righteousness is mere pretense. Shared with Wesley: Hear this, O house of Jacob; called by the name, not in truth; hypocrites. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: ESV “not in truth or right”; Calvin “not in truth nor in righteousness” is the KJV lemma, not a different English Bible; “Swear by Jehovah” is his lemma for “swear by the name of the LORD.” Prefer no keyContrast on 1: both name-not-truth. Match 47:1: Babylon in the dust; the word now turns to Jacob. Match v. 2: the holy city they defile.

Isaiah 48:2

2For they call themselves after the holy city, and stay themselves on the God of Israel; the LORD of hosts is his name.

Arminian

For they call themselves after the holy city, and stay themselves on the God of Israel; the LORD of hosts is his name. Wesley: Though—They glory that they are citizens of Jerusalem, a city sanctified by God, to be the place of his true worship, and gracious presence. And stay—Not by a true faith, but a vain confidence.

Arminian hearing: they glory as citizens of sanctified Jerusalem; stay is vain confidence, not true faith. Shared: they call themselves after the holy city; stay themselves on the God of Israel; the LORD of hosts is his name. Historic Jerusalem, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Though” and “stay—vain confidence” are his lemmas for “they call themselves after the holy city, and stay themselves on the God of Israel.” Prefer no keyContrast on 2: both empty/vain confidence, not a millennial camp fight. Match v. 1: name-not-truth. Match v. 8: the mask of the name Israel.

Reformed

For they call themselves after the holy city, and stay themselves on the God of Israel; the LORD of hosts is his name. Calvin: falsely boasted they were citizens of the holy city which they defiled. Rely on the God of Israel: empty confidence, not sincere belief; intoxicated by unfounded belief. Jehovah of hosts added as threatening.

Reformed hearing: the holy city they name they defile; rely is empty confidence; Jehovah of hosts threatens. Shared with Wesley: they call themselves after the holy city; stay/rely is vain or empty confidence; the LORD of hosts is his name. Historic Jerusalem, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Jehovah of hosts” is his lemma for ESV “the LORD of hosts”; “Rely” for “stay themselves.” Prefer no keyContrast on 2: both vain/empty confidence. Match v. 1: spurious Israel. Match v. 11: his name will not be profaned.

Isaiah 48:3

3“The former things I declared of old; they went out from my mouth, and I announced them; then suddenly I did them, and they came to pass.

Arminian

The former things I declared of old; they went out from my mouth, and I announced them; then suddenly I did them, and they came to pass. Wesley has no extra lemma. Paraphrase from v. 4 honestly: I knew—Therefore I gave thee clearer demonstrations of my nature and providence, because I knew thou wast an unbelieving nation. So here: the former things declared of old, gone out from his mouth, done suddenly, and come to pass are those clearer demonstrations. Do not invent unique Wesley sentences.

Arminian hearing: he declared of old and did what he announced; the unbelieving nation of v. 4 got clearer demonstrations, not a new Wesley paragraph. Shared: The former things I declared of old; I did them; they came to pass. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. No open theism: he declared, he did them, they came to pass; he is not discovering the former things as they unfold. Prefer no keyContrast on 3: shared he-declared-and-did; skip rather than invent Wesley prose. Match v. 4: obstinate neck is why the demonstrations. Match v. 5: lest the idol get the credit.

Reformed

The former things I declared of old; they went out from my mouth, and I announced them; then suddenly I did them, and they came to pass. Calvin: long ago I declared the former things—accuses of ingratitude; he promised nothing he did not perform. Include Abraham’s prediction of captivity and restoration (Gen 15:13) and later predictions. Event did not happen by chance. They still would not believe they would return; some preferred Babylon; some suspected Cyrus’s craft.

Reformed hearing: former things declared and performed, not chance; Abraham’s captivity-and-restoration word (Gen 15:13) and later predictions; unbelief still preferred Babylon or suspected Cyrus. Shared with Wesley: he declared, he did them, they came to pass. Historic Jacob, Abraham, Babylon, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Long ago I declared the former things” names the ESV lemma. No open theism: he promised nothing he did not perform; the event did not happen by chance. Prefer no keyContrast on 3: shared he-declared-and-did. Match v. 4: obstinate. Match v. 6: now new things.

Isaiah 48:4

4Because I know that you are obstinate, and your neck is an iron sinew and your forehead brass,

Because I know that you are obstinate, and your neck is an iron sinew and your forehead brass. Wesley: I knew—Therefore I gave thee clearer demonstrations of my nature and providence, because I knew thou wast an unbelieving nation. Thy neck—Will not bow down to receive my yoke. Thy brow—Thou wast impudent.

Arminian hearing: obstinacy is why the clearer demonstrations of v. 3; iron neck will not take the yoke; brass brow is impudence. Shared: I know that you are obstinate; neck an iron sinew; forehead brass. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “I knew” is his lemma for ESV “I know”; “Thy brow” for “your forehead brass.” Prefer no keyContrast on 4: both refractory and impudent. No open theism: I know; the demonstrations were given because he knew. Match v. 3: former things declared. Match v. 8: called a rebel.

Reformed

Because I know that you are obstinate, and your neck is an iron sinew and your forehead brass. Calvin: I knew thou art obstinate—iron sinew neck, brazen forehead—refractory and impudent. If depravity had not been incurable he would not have needed so many predictions. Two proofs of abandoned wickedness: not submissive, not ashamed to repent.

Reformed hearing: iron neck and brass brow are refractory impudence; many predictions mark incurable depravity; two proofs—not submissive, not ashamed to repent. Shared with Wesley: obstinate; iron sinew neck; brass/brazen forehead; impudent. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “I knew thou art obstinate” names ESV “I know that you are obstinate.” Prefer no keyContrast on 4: both refractory and impudent; skip Calvin’s thicker incurable-depravity rather than invent a Wesley fight. No open theism: I know. Match v. 3: so many predictions. Match v. 8: rebel from the womb.

Isaiah 48:5

5I declared them to you from of old, before they came to pass I announced them to you, lest you should say, ‘My idol did them, my carved image and my metal image commanded them.’

Arminian

I declared them to you from of old, before they came to pass I announced them to you, lest you should say, ‘My idol did them, my carved image and my metal image commanded them.’ Wesley has no extra lemma. Paraphrase from v. 4 honestly: I knew—Therefore I gave thee clearer demonstrations of my nature and providence, because I knew thou wast an unbelieving nation. So here: he declared them from of old, before they came to pass, lest that unbelieving nation say, My idol did them. Do not invent unique Wesley sentences.

Arminian hearing: the clearer demonstrations of v. 4 are so the idol cannot take the credit. Shared: I declared them from of old; lest you should say, My idol did them. Historic Jacob and historic idols, not a newspaper. No open theism: he announced before they came to pass; he is not learning whether the idol did them. Prefer no keyContrast on 5: both lest idols get the credit. Match v. 3: former things declared and done. Match v. 7: lest you should say, Behold, I knew them.

Reformed

I declared them to you from of old, before they came to pass I announced them to you, lest you should say, ‘My idol did them, my carved image and my metal image commanded them.’ Calvin: foretold lest thou shouldest say Mine idol hath done them. Jews corrupted by Gentiles (Ezek 8). Foreknowledge connected with power: he not only foresaw but accomplished. Lamp of doctrine must shine.

Reformed hearing: declared so the idol cannot claim the work; foreknowledge is joined to power—he foresaw and accomplished; the lamp of doctrine must shine. Shared with Wesley: lest idols get the credit; I declared them from of old, before they came to pass. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Mine idol hath done them” is his lemma for ESV “My idol did them.” No open theism: he not only foresaw but accomplished. Prefer no keyContrast on 5: shared lest-idols-get-the-credit. Match v. 3: he did them, they came to pass. Match v. 11: glory not to another.

Isaiah 48:6

6“You have heard; now see all this; and will you not declare it? From this time forth I announce to you new things, hidden things that you have not known.

Arminian

You have heard; now see all this; and will you not declare it? From this time forth I announce to you new things, hidden things that you have not known. Wesley: See—As thou hast heard all these things, from time to time, seriously consider them. Declare—I call you to witness: must you not be forced to acknowledge the truth of what I say? Shewed—And I have now given thee new predictions of secret things, such as till this time were wholly unknown to thee, concerning thy deliverance out of Babylon by Cyrus.

Arminian hearing: hear, then see, then witness; the new and hidden things are secret predictions of deliverance out of Babylon by Cyrus. Shared: You have heard; now see; will you not declare it? new things, hidden things. Historic Babylon and Cyrus, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Shewed” is his lemma for ESV “I announce to you new things”; Cyrus names that historic deliverance. Prefer no keyContrast on 6: Wesley’s Cyrus and Calvin’s redemption after captivity are one historic Babylon, not a millennial camp fight. Match v. 3: former things already done. Match v. 7: created now, not long ago.

Reformed

You have heard; now see all this; and will you not declare it? From this time forth I announce to you new things, hidden things that you have not known. Calvin: Thou hast heard, see all this—consider his word. Will ye not declare it: be heralds. New and hidden things: future captivity and redemption; human conjecture could not foresee.

Reformed hearing: consider the word already heard; be heralds of it; the new and hidden things are captivity and redemption no conjecture could foresee. Shared with Wesley: hear, see, declare; new and hidden things of historic Babylon’s furnace and deliverance, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “future captivity and redemption” is his lemma for “new things, hidden things”; Wesley specifies Cyrus—same historic deliverance, not a different verse. Prefer no keyContrast on 6: both historic Babylon, not a millennial timetable. No open theism: I announce; they have not known because he had not said. Match v. 7: created now. Match v. 3: former things already declared.

Isaiah 48:7

7They are created now, not long ago; before today you have never heard of them, lest you should say, ‘Behold, I knew them.’

Arminian

They are created now, not long ago; before today you have never heard of them, lest you should say, ‘Behold, I knew them.’ Wesley: Created—Revealed to thee by me; brought to light, as things are by creation. Not—not from these ancient times when other things were revealed to thee. I knew—Either by thine own sagacity: or by the help of thine idols.

Arminian hearing: created is revealed, brought to light as by creation; not from the ancient times of the former things; lest they claim sagacity or the idols’ help. Shared: created now, not long ago; never heard of them; lest you should say, Behold, I knew them. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Created—Revealed” is his lemma for ESV “They are created now”; “I knew” for “Behold, I knew them.” No open theism: he creates them now by revealing them; they did not know them beforehand. Prefer no keyContrast on 7: both not-by-sagacity. Match v. 5: lest My idol did them. Match v. 6: new and hidden things.

Reformed

They are created now, not long ago; before today you have never heard of them, lest you should say, ‘Behold, I knew them.’ Calvin: now first created—not known by sagacity, not fortune; so they cannot defraud the Spirit of praise.

Reformed hearing: created now so sagacity and fortune cannot steal the Spirit’s praise. Shared with Wesley: created now, not long ago; not by sagacity; lest you should say, Behold, I knew them. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “now first created” names ESV “They are created now, not long ago.” No open theism: they are created now; they cannot defraud the Spirit of praise. Prefer no keyContrast on 7: both not-by-sagacity. Match v. 6: hidden things. Match v. 8: ear not opened.

Isaiah 48:8

8You have never heard, you have never known, from of old your ear has not been opened. For I knew that you would surely deal treacherously, and that from before birth you were called a rebel.

Arminian

You have never heard, you have never known, from of old your ear has not been opened. For I knew that you would surely deal treacherously, and that from before birth you were called a rebel. Wesley: Yea—The same thing is repeated, because this was so illustrious a proof of the infinite power and providence of God. Thine ear—Thou didst not hear, I did not reveal these things unto thee: for so this phrase of opening the ear is understood, 1Sam 9:15. I knew—I knew all these cautions were necessary to cure thine infidelity. Called—Justly, thou wast indeed such.

Arminian hearing: never heard because he did not open the ear (1Sam 9:15); the cautions of v. 5–7 were to cure infidelity; called a rebel—justly, they were indeed such. Shared: never heard, never known; ear not opened; I knew you would deal treacherously; called a rebel from before birth. Historic Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Thine ear” is his lemma for ESV “your ear has not been opened”; 1Sam 9:15 is his sense of opening the ear, not a different verse. No open theism: I knew; called a rebel from before birth. Prefer no keyContrast on 8: both treacherous from of old; do not millennial-fight Calvin’s Church—historic Israel from the womb. Match v. 1: name-not-truth. Match v. 4: obstinate.

Reformed

You have never heard, you have never known, from of old your ear has not been opened. For I knew that you would surely deal treacherously, and that from before birth you were called a rebel. Calvin: I knew that by transgressing thou wouldest transgress. Rebel from the womb: nativity of the Church at the Exodus (Ex 12:51); they did not cease to act treacherously. Mask of the name Israel torn off.

Reformed hearing: he knew they would transgress; rebel from the womb is the Church’s nativity at the Exodus (Ex 12:51), historic Israel, not a millennial camp fight; they did not cease to act treacherously; the name Israel’s mask is torn off. Shared with Wesley: never heard; I knew; called a rebel; treacherous. Historic Jacob from the womb/Exodus, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “by transgressing thou wouldest transgress” is his lemma for “you would surely deal treacherously”; “from the womb” for “from before birth.” No open theism: I knew. Prefer no keyContrast on 8: skip Church-as-Exodus rather than invent a millennial fork. Match v. 1: spurious Israel. Match v. 9: yet for my name’s sake I defer anger.

Isaiah 48:9

For my name’s sake I defer my anger; for the sake of my praise I restrain it for you, that I may not cut you off. Wesley: For my sake—I will spare thee, and deliver thee out of captivity, not for thy sake, but merely for my own sake, and for the vindication of my name, that I may be praised for my power, faithfulness, and goodness.

Arminian hearing: spare and deliver out of captivity not for their sake but merely for his own, to vindicate his name—power, faithfulness, goodness praised. Shared loudly with Calvin: not for thy sake but my name; I defer anger; I may not cut you off. Historic Israel out of Babylon, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “For my sake” is his lemma for ESV “For my name’s sake.” Prefer no keyContrast on 9: do not fork; both not-for-thy-sake-but-my-name. No open theism: I defer; I restrain; I may not cut you off. Match v. 8: called a rebel, yet not cut off. Match v. 11: for my own sake, I do it.

Reformed

For my name’s sake I defer my anger; for the sake of my praise I restrain it for you, that I may not cut you off. Calvin: For my name’s sake—owing to his mercy they survive; they deserved to perish a thousand times. Not owing to their merit that he brings them out of the grave of Babylon. Remission by free grace for God’s name’s sake. Sophists’ distinction on deserved satisfaction falls.

Reformed hearing: they survive by mercy, not merit; out of Babylon’s grave by free grace for his name’s sake; they deserved to perish a thousand times. Shared loudly with Wesley: not for thy sake but my name; I defer anger that I may not cut you off. Historic Israel out of Babylon, not a newspaper. Prefer no keyContrast on 9: do not fork; Sophists’ deserved-satisfaction is Calvin thickening the same free grace, not a fight with Wesley’s not-for-thy-sake. No open theism: I defer; I restrain. Isaiah 48:10

10Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have tried you in the furnace of affliction.

Arminian

Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have tried you in the furnace of affliction. Wesley: Behold—Although I will not cut thee off, yet I will put thee into the furnace. Silver—Which is kept in the furnace so long till all the dross be purged away, I will not deal so rigorously with thee; for then I should wholly consume thee. I will chuse—God had in a manner rejected Israel, and therefore it was necessary he should chuse and try this people a second time.

Arminian hearing: not cut off, yet into the furnace; not as silver—not kept till all dross is gone, or they would be consumed; chuse is a second choosing after a manner of rejection, to try this people again. Shared: Behold, I have refined you in the furnace of affliction; I will not cut you off. Historic Israel in Babylon’s furnace, not a millennial camp fight. Quote-honesty: ESV “I have tried you in the furnace of affliction”; Wesley “I will chuse” comments the KJV lemma “chosen” (ESV footnote: Or I have chosen), not a different verse. Match v. 9: not cut off. Match v. 11: for my own sake I do it.

Reformed

Behold, I have refined you, but not as silver; I have tried you in the furnace of affliction. Calvin: I have tried thee—moderation in chastising; stripes serviceable; we prove that which we do not wish to be lost. Not like silver: we contain nothing pure and would be consumed like chaff; he considers what we can endure; by secret influence of his Spirit makes punishments profitable. I have chosen thee in the furnace: choose means distinguish/preserve; wide difference between punishment of good men and of the wicked; even when he appears to abandon, he is still gracious.

Reformed hearing: tried is prove-not-lose; not as silver because nothing in us is pure and we would be ashes; he tries what we can endure; the Spirit makes the stripes profitable; choose is distinguish/preserve; even apparent abandonment is still gracious. Shared with Wesley: Behold, I have refined you in the furnace of affliction; I will not cut you off. Historic Israel in Babylon’s furnace, not a millennial camp fight. Quote-honesty: ESV “tried you”; Calvin “I have chosen thee in the furnace” comments the KJV lemma “chosen” (ESV footnote: Or I have chosen), not a different English Bible. Match v. 9: I may not cut you off. Match v. 11: my glory I will not give to another.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “Silver” as I will not deal so rigorously as to keep thee till all the dross is purged, for then I should wholly consume thee; “I will chuse”—God had in a manner rejected Israel, and therefore it was necessary he should chuse and try this people a second time. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take “not as silver” as in us nothing is pure and we would be reduced to ashes; he tries what we can endure and by his Spirit makes the punishments profitable; “choose” means distinguish/preserve; even when he appears to abandon his people he is still gracious. Shared: Behold, I have refined you in the furnace of affliction; I will not cut you off. Historic Israel in Babylon’s furnace, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 48:11

11For my own sake, for my own sake, I do it, for how should my name be profaned? My glory I will not give to another.

Arminian

For my own sake, for my own sake, I do it, for how should my name be profaned? My glory I will not give to another. Wesley: It—This great work of delivering my people out of Babylon. Name—If I should not deliver my people, my name would be profaned and blasphemed. Glory—I will not give any colour to idolaters, to ascribe the divine nature and properties, to idols, as they would do if I did not rescue my people out of their hands in spite of their idols.

Arminian hearing: it is deliverance out of Babylon; if he did not deliver, his name would be profaned; glory withheld from idolaters who would ascribe the divine to idols. Shared loudly with Calvin: deliver out of Babylon so idols cannot claim the glory; for my own sake I do it; my glory I will not give to another. Historic Jacob out of Babylon, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “It—delivering my people out of Babylon” is his lemma for “I do it”; “colour” is his spelling. Prefer no keyContrast on 11: do not fork; both glory-not-to-idols. No open theism: he will do it; he will not give his glory away. Match v. 5: lest My idol did them. Match v. 9: for my name’s sake. Match v. 10: the furnace, not cut off.

Reformed

For my own sake, for my own sake, I do it, for how should my name be profaned? My glory I will not give to another. Calvin: For my own sake—how should my name be profaned? I will not give my glory to another. Men do all they can to profane it; he preserves it. Connects our salvation with his glory. Would have been mocked “Where is their God?” (Ps 79:10; Deut 32:27).

Reformed hearing: he does it for his own sake; men profane, he preserves; salvation is bound to his glory; else the mock “Where is their God?” Shared loudly with Wesley: deliver out of Babylon so idols cannot claim the glory; for my own sake I do it; my glory I will not give to another. If depravity had not been incurable he would not have needed so many predictions. Two proofs of abandoned wickedness: not submissive, not ashamed to repent. Foretold lest thou shouldest say Mine idol hath done them. Jews corrupted by Gentiles (Ezek 8). Foreknowledge connected with power: he not only foresaw but accomplished. Lamp of doctrine must shine. Thou hast heard, see all this: consider his word. Will ye not declare it: be heralds. New and hidden things: future captivity and redemption; human conjecture could not foresee. Now first created: not known by sagacity, not fortune; so they cannot defraud the Spirit of praise. I knew that by transgressing thou wouldest transgress. Rebel from the womb: nativity of the Church at the Exodus (Ex 12:51); they did not cease to act treacherously. Mask of the name Israel torn off. For my name’s sake: owing to his mercy they survive; they deserved to perish a thousand times. Not owing to their merit that he brings them out of the grave of Babylon. Remission by free grace for God’s name’s sake. Sophists’ distinction on deserved satisfaction falls. I have tried thee: moderation in chastising; stripes serviceable; we prove that which we do not wish to be lost. Not like silver: we contain nothing pure and would be consumed like chaff; he considers what we can endure; by secret influence of his Spirit makes punishments profitable. I have chosen thee in the furnace: choose means distinguish/preserve; wide difference between punishment of good men and of the wicked; even when he appears to abandon, he is still gracious. For my own sake: how should my name be profaned? I will not give my glory to another. Men do all they can to profane it; he preserves it. Connects our salvation with his glory. Would have been mocked “Where is their God?” (Ps 79:10; Deut 32:27).”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 48:1–11)
