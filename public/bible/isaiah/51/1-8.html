Passage

Isaiah 51

Verses 1–8 · ESV · Voices

Those who pursue righteousness are told to look to Abraham and Sarah—the rock and the quarry—because the LORD who called one man can still comfort Zion’s wastes like Eden. A law goes out as light to the peoples; his righteousness and salvation outlast heavens and earth, so the people in whose heart is his law need not fear the reproach of men.

Isaiah 51:1

1Listen to me, you who pursue righteousness, you who seek the LORD: look to the rock from which you were hewn, and to the quarry from which you were dug.

Arminian

Listen to me, you who pursue righteousness, you who seek the LORD: look to the rock from which you were hewn, and to the quarry from which you were dug. Wesley: Look—consider the state of Abraham and Sarah, from whom all of you sprang. The chapter-head still governs: Abraham, our pattern to trust in Christ, in his promises, and righteous salvation.

Arminian hearing: the rock and the quarry are real Abraham and real Sarah, the stock from which Judah sprang—not a later-age chart. Shared: hewn from the rock; dug from the quarry; those who pursue righteousness must look back. Historic then gospel: the comfort that reaches the church in Christ starts here, at the one man God called. No millennial fight; the point is the LORD who can still multiply from one.

Reformed

Hearken to me, ye that follow righteousness. Calvin: the Prophet now exhorts the Jews not to despair because they are few in number; they had been cut down and diminished to such a degree that they appeared to be on the eve of being reduced to nothing. Look to the rock of your hewing: some take Abraham as a Rock because he was strong in faith, others because he was worn out by age and Sarah a Pit because barren; but both, in my opinion, are in the wrong; it is a simple metaphor, taken from quarries. He does not speak to all, but to those only who could rely on the promise, that is, to those whom he calls followers of righteousness; although they boasted that they were the children of Abraham, yet all were not capable of receiving this doctrine. Following righteousness consists in seeking the Lord.

Reformed hearing: quarry, not a faith-rock or a barren-pit allegory. Shared: Abraham and Sarah are the stone and the pit; God can multiply a remnant. The address lands on those who seek Jehovah, not on every boast of Abraham’s name. Carry the Servant-block: the remnant that looks to the quarry is the same people the Servant of 49 is light for, not a second Israel of a later scheme.

Key contrast Both look to Abraham and Sarah as the quarry. Wesley takes “all of you sprang” from that stock, Abraham as pattern to trust in Christ. Calvin refuses to speak to all who boast they are Abraham’s children: only those who can rely on the promise—followers of righteousness who also seek the Lord.

Isaiah 51:2

2Look to Abraham your father and to Sarah who bore you; for he was but one when I called him, that I might bless him and multiply him.

Arminian

Look to Abraham your father and to Sarah who bore you; for he was but one when I called him, that I might bless him and multiply him. Wesley: Him alone—to follow me to an unknown land: him only of all his kindred. Increased—into a vast multitude, when his condition was desperate in the eye of reason. And therefore God can as easily raise his church when they are in the most forlorn condition.

Arminian call: the call of one man was real, to an unknown land; the increase was against reason’s eye. Shared: one when called; blessed; multiplied. Historic Abraham, then the church raised from forlorn estate. Those who despair of Zion’s smallness do not despair because the LORD cannot multiply, but because they will not look to the one he called. No culture war about ancestry; the point is the God who called.

Reformed

For I called him alone. Calvin: not only because he had none along with him when called out of his country, but because the Lord suffered him to dwell in Canaan without children up to a worn out old age; yet the Lord increased and enriched him with a great number of children. To us also, amidst this distracted condition of the Church, it is highly necessary that we may not be discouraged because our number is small, and that we may hope that God will increase his Church by unexpected methods. That promise relates not to the Jews only, but to other nations; on this account he was no longer called Abram, but Abraham.

Reformed call: childless to old age, then a people. Shared: one called; blessed; multiplied. The gospel reach is already in Abraham’s new name, not a dispensational chart. Calvin’s unexpected methods are the same LORD Wesley hears raising his church from the forlorn.

Isaiah 51:3

3For the LORD comforts Zion; he comforts all her waste places and makes her wilderness like Eden, her desert like the garden of the LORD; joy and gladness will be found in her, thanksgiving and the voice of song.

Arminian

For the LORD comforts Zion; he comforts all her waste places and makes her wilderness like Eden, her desert like the garden of the LORD; joy and gladness will be found in her, thanksgiving and the voice of song. Wesley: Therefore—for the sake of Abraham, and of that covenant which I made with him. Garden—flourishing as the garden of Eden.

Arminian comfort: Zion’s wastes are real; the ground of comfort is the covenant with Abraham, not a later millennial garden. Shared: wilderness like Eden; joy and thanksgiving. Historic Zion first, then the church that inherits the same comfort in Christ. The Servant who is light for the nations in 49:6 does not cancel this Zion; he is how the comfort travels.

Reformed

Surely Jehovah will comfort Zion. Calvin: in the person of Abraham there was exhibited an example which applies to all ages; as the Lord suddenly produced from one man so numerous an offspring, so he will also people his Church by wonderful and unknown methods, and not once only, whenever she shall be thought childless and solitary. He will comfort his Church not only when flourishing, but when desolate and reduced to solitude. The desert like a place of delights alludes to Eden, from which man was driven by his own fault; we who have been deprived of that blessing are exiles throughout the whole world. Restored, she shall rejoice and render thanksgiving to God.

Reformed comfort: Abraham is the pattern of how God peoples a desolate Church. Shared: Zion comforted; wastes made Eden; song. No newspaper about gardens; the point is the LORD who comforts. Calvin’s Church-from-solitude is the gospel already reaching those gathered in the Head, not a second Zion.

Isaiah 51:4

4Give attention to me, my people, and give ear to me, my nation; for a law will go out from me, and I will set my justice for a light to the peoples.

Arminian

Give attention to me, my people, and give ear to me, my nation; for a law will go out from me, and I will set my justice for a light to the peoples. Wesley: My people—ye Jews, whom I chose to be my peculiar people. A law—a new law, even the doctrine of the gospel. Judgment—judgment is here the same thing with law, the word of God, or the evangelical doctrine, of which he saith that he will make it to rest, that is settle and establish it. The people—people of all nations.

Arminian light: the gospel is a real new law going out, settled for the nations, while “my people” remains the Jews he chose. Shared: a law from him; justice as light to the peoples. Carry 49:6: the Servant is light for the nations; this verse is that light as teaching, not a forced Isa 53 atonement fork. Those who stay in the dark do not stay because no law went out, but because they will not give ear.

Reformed

Attend to me, my people. Calvin: the Lord so frequently demands that he shall be heard because we are slow to hear him, especially in adversity. The law shall go forth from me: the Lord will again reign, and will arouse his Church to call on his name. Law is equivalent to the edict which God shall order to be proclaimed when he shall be pleased to gather his Church; he describes his manner of reigning by his Law and by his doctrine. Wherever doctrine is rejected, God’s government is not found. By judgment he means the order and administration of government by which he shall restore his kingdom. For a light of the peoples: when God begins to reign, miserable men are rescued from darkness and enlightened by the doctrine of the word.

Reformed light: the nations’ light is the Word by which he reigns. Shared: law going out; light to the peoples. The Servant’s light from 49:6 is here the doctrine that actually enlightens. No millennial chart of later laws; the fork is how the going-out is named, not whether the peoples are in view.

Key contrast Both hear a law going out as light to the peoples. Wesley takes the law as a new law, even the doctrine of the gospel, settled for people of all nations, while “my people” remains the Jews he chose as peculiar. Calvin takes the Law as the edict by which the Lord will again reign when he gathers his Church: wherever doctrine is rejected, God’s government is not found; miserable men are enlightened by the word.

Isaiah 51:5

5My righteousness draws near, my salvation has gone out, and my arms will judge the peoples; the coastlands hope for me, and for my arm they wait.

Arminian

My righteousness draws near, my salvation has gone out, and my arms will judge the peoples; the coastlands hope for me, and for my arm they wait. Wesley: My righteousness—my salvation, the redemption of all my people, Jews and Gentiles, which is the effect of his righteousness, his justice, faithfulness, or mercy. Is gone—shall shortly go forth. Judge—shall subdue the Gentiles to my authority, and rule them by my word and spirit. Isles—the remote countries shall expect this salvation from me, and from me only.

Arminian salvation: redemption of Jews and Gentiles is what “my righteousness” means here—sincerely gone forth, not a feint. Shared: righteousness near; salvation gone out; coastlands wait. Historic then gospel: real isles waiting on the same arm that called Abraham. Carry the Servant-block: the nations’ hope is the light of 49:6, not a forced 53-fork. Those who perish do not perish because this salvation never went out to the remote countries, but because they will not trust the arm.

Reformed

My righteousness is near. Calvin: the righteousness of the Lord has relation to men who know by experience that he is righteous. By the word righteous the Prophet does not mean that he renders to every one a righteous reward, but that he yields the best protection, and dispenses the largest kindness to his people, that he faithfully performs his promises to all believers, when he delivers them and does not suffer them to be finally overwhelmed. My salvation hath gone forth: the righteousness of God shone brightly in the deliverance of the people. The captivity in Babylon was a kind of death, in consequence of which that deliverance is here called Salvation. My arms shall judge the peoples: by arms he means the wide exercise of his power; he will spread his kingdom far and wide.

Reformed salvation: Babylon first as death and deliverance; then the arms that spread the kingdom. Shared: righteousness near; salvation gone out; coastlands hope. Calvin does not deny the peoples; he will not read “righteous” as a wage paid to every one. The kingdom spreading is the same gospel light, not a later-age chart.

Key contrast Both hear righteousness near and salvation gone forth, coastlands waiting. Wesley takes “my righteousness” as the redemption of all my people, Jews and Gentiles, the effect of justice, faithfulness, or mercy, and the arms as subduing the Gentiles to his word and Spirit. Calvin denies that “righteous” here means he renders to every one a righteous reward: it is the best protection and largest kindness to his people; Babylon was a kind of death, and that deliverance is called Salvation; the arms then spread his kingdom far and wide.

Isaiah 51:6

6Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look at the earth beneath; for the heavens vanish like smoke, the earth will wear out like a garment, and they who dwell in it will die in like manner; but my salvation will be forever, and my righteousness will never be dismayed.

Arminian

Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look at the earth beneath; for the heavens vanish like smoke, the earth will wear out like a garment, and they who dwell in it will die in like manner; but my salvation will be forever, and my righteousness will never be dismayed. Wesley: The heavens—the heavens and earth shall pass away, in regard of their present state, and properties, and use, as smoak is said to vanish, tho’ the substance of it be not destroyed.

Arminian forever: men are transitory; the righteous salvation is constant—the chapter-head again. Shared: heavens like smoke; earth like a garment; salvation forever; righteousness not dismayed. No newspaper about skies; the point is the salvation that outlasts the present use of heaven and earth. Trust in Christ, Wesley said, in his promises: that is the forever, not a millennial timetable.

Reformed

Lift up your eyes toward heaven. Calvin: when we see so great changes in the world, we are apt to think that the Church comes within the influence of the same violent motion; we need to have our minds elevated above the ordinary course of nature, otherwise the salvation of the Church will appear to hang on a thread. Though heaven and earth hasten to decay, it is impossible that the Church shall fail, the stability of which is founded on God. Everything contained in heaven and earth is frail and fading; but God’s salvation, by which he guards the Church, is eternal. The grace he displays in the preservation of his Church surpasses all his other works.

Reformed forever: the Church does not ride the same thread as the garment-earth. Shared: look up and down; salvation forever; righteousness not abolished. Calvin’s Church-guarded-eternally is the same forever Wesley names; the fork is not invented here as Arminian versus Reformed. No dispensational chart of vanishing heavens.

Isaiah 51:7

7Listen to me, you who know righteousness, the people in whose heart is my law; fear not the reproach of man, nor be dismayed at their revilings.

Arminian

Listen to me, you who know righteousness, the people in whose heart is my law; fear not the reproach of man, nor be dismayed at their revilings. Wesley: Know—that love and practise it.

Arminian heart: knowing righteousness is loving and practising it, not a party badge. Shared: listen; law in the heart; fear not man’s reproach. Historic then gospel: the same people told to look to Abraham are now told not to flinch at reviling. Carry 50:6–7: the Servant set his face like flint; those who know righteousness need not be dismayed. No culture war about who is reviled; the point is the law already in the heart.

Reformed

Hearken to me, ye that know righteousness. Calvin: wicked men, when they enjoy prosperity, laugh at our faith; the Prophet exhorts believers to patience, that they may not dread their reproaches. He does not address unbelievers, but those who know righteousness; though they do not intentionally reject the word, they often shut the door against his righteousness under adversity, shut their ears and almost despair. A people in whose heart is my law: without the word of the Lord there can be no righteousness; no laws of men, however well framed, will lead us to true righteousness. The seat of the law is not in the brain, but in the heart, that being imbued with heavenly doctrine we may be altogether renewed.

Reformed heart: Word in the heart, not human codes in the brain. Shared: know righteousness; law in the heart; fear not revilings. Calvin’s warning that knowers still shut the door under adversity is not a sneer at Wesley’s love-and-practise; both send the fearful to hear again. The Servant’s voice in 50:10 is still the voice to obey.

Isaiah 51:8

8For the moth will eat them up like a garment, and the worm will eat them like wool; but my righteousness will be forever, and my salvation to all generations.

Arminian

For the moth will eat them up like a garment, and the worm will eat them like wool; but my righteousness will be forever, and my salvation to all generations. Wesley: Like wool—like a woollen garment, which is sooner corrupted by moths or such creatures, than linen.

Arminian moth: revilers wear out faster than wool; the LORD’s righteousness does not. Shared: moth; worm; righteousness forever; salvation to all generations. Carry 50:9: accusers of the Servant are moth-eaten; here the same moth eats those who reproach the people in whose heart is the law. Do not force Isa 53 atonement onto this verse; Wesley does not go there. Men are transitory; the righteous salvation is constant, and the church may trust it in Christ.

Reformed

For the moth shall eat them up like a garment. Calvin: believing servants of God must endure many reproaches from enemies of the word; true valor springs from this, that although the world reject us as filth and offscourings, God holds us in estimation. The death of wicked men would yield small consolation if we were not saved: we shall never be left destitute of God’s righteousness and salvation. Salvation is in God, that by it he may preserve, not himself, but us; righteousness is in God, that he may display it for our defense and preservation. From the eternity of God’s salvation and righteousness we ought to infer that the servants of God cannot possibly perish; the children of God do not subsist in themselves, but in God, and have the foundation of their salvation in him.

Reformed moth: revilers are food for worms; the servants subsist in God. Shared: moth-eaten garments; righteousness forever; salvation to all generations. The same moth as 50:9; the same forever as v. 6. No millennial fight about generations; the point is that those held in God’s estimation cannot perish because their salvation is in him.
