Passage

Isaiah 54

Verses 11–17 · ESV · Voices

The barren of 54:1–10 is now the storm-tossed city: O afflicted one, not comforted, I will set your stones in antimony and lay your foundations with sapphires. Children taught by the LORD; established in righteousness, far from oppression. If anyone stirs up strife, it is not from me; the smith and the ravager are his creatures. No weapon fashioned against you shall succeed; this is the heritage of the servants of the LORD, their vindication from him. Historic Zion rebuilt after exile, then the gospel church.

Isaiah 54:11

11"O afflicted one, storm-tossed and not comforted, behold, I will set your stones in antimony, and lay your foundations with sapphires.

Arminian

O afflicted one, storm-tossed and not comforted, behold, I will set your stones in antimony, and lay your foundations with sapphires. Wesley: O thou—Who hast been, in a most afflicted and comfortless condition. With sapphires—I will make thee exceeding beautiful and glorious, by a plentiful effusion of excellent gifts, and graces.

Arminian stones: historic comfortless Zion, then gifts and graces that make her beautiful. Shared: afflicted; storm-tossed; not comforted; stones set; foundations with sapphires. Carry 54:1: the barren now built. Carry 51:21: the afflicted who were drunk not with wine. Historic city first, then the church's gifts. No millennial floor-plan of later sapphires; Wesley's beauty is effusion of grace, not a newspaper skyline.

Reformed

Thou wretched. Calvin: he continues the same subject, and promises not only that the Church shall be restored to her ancient splendor, but that God will cause her to be adorned with attire of greater magnificence, as if it had been wholly composed of precious stones. All this was expressed by Haggai in a single word, when he said, The glory of the latter temple shall be greater than the glory of the former (Haggai 2:9). This earnest address is exceedingly well fitted for soothing the grief of believers; it represents the Church, which was ready to be drowned, as being now rescued by him from shipwreck. Whenever therefore we shall see her violently shaken by tempests, and weighed down by a load of distresses, and deprived of all consolation, let us remember that these are the very circumstances which induce God to give assistance.

Reformed stones: drowned Church rescued; latter glory greater. Shared: afflicted; not comforted; rebuilt. Carry 54:8: the same momentary wrath. Historic then gospel. No dispensational chart of later temples. No open theism: the architect already speaks the stones.

Isaiah 54:12

12I will make your pinnacles of agate, your gates of carbuncles, and all your wall of precious stones.

Arminian

I will make your pinnacles of agate, your gates of carbuncles, and all your wall of precious stones. Wesley: Agates—The proper signification of the Hebrew names of precious stones is unknown to the Jews themselves. It may suffice us to know that this was some very clear and transparent, and precious stone. Thy borders—The utmost parts or walls.

Arminian walls: clear stones, utmost borders made precious; Wesley will not pretend a jeweler's lexicon. Shared: pinnacles; gates; wall of precious stones. Carry v. 11's sapphires. Historic Zion's walls first, then this beauty. No culture war about later masonry.

Reformed

And I will lay thy windows with pearls. Calvin: by these metaphors he shows that the condition of the Church will be far better than at any former period. The Church is compared to a building, which is customary in every part of Scripture. For this reason he now draws a picture of a costly and magnificent structure. But it ought to be remarked that the Prophet represents God as the architect of this building; for this work ought to be entirely ascribed to him alone. Whether these jewels mean doctrine, as Paul uses gold, silver, precious stones (1 Corinthians 3:10), will appear from the following verse.

Reformed walls: God the only architect; the next verse will say the stones are people taught by the LORD. Shared: gates; precious wall. Carry 1 Corinthians 3 as Calvin flags it. Historic then gospel. No millennial fight about later carbuncles.

Isaiah 54:13

13All your children shall be taught by the LORD, and great shall be the peace of your children.

Arminian

All your children shall be taught by the LORD, and great shall be the peace of your children. Wesley: Taught—Not only outwardly by his word, but inwardly by his spirit. The peace—1. Inward peace arising from the clear discoveries of God's love and reconciliation to us, and wrought by the spirit of adoption, which is more abundantly given to believers under the gospel, than under the law. 2. Outward peace, safety, and happiness.

Arminian taught: word and Spirit; inward peace of adoption more under the gospel; outward safety too. Shared: all your children taught by the LORD; great peace. Carry John 6:45 as Christ quotes this. Historic Zion's children first, then gospel disciples. No millennial school of later children; Wesley's peace is adoption and safety, not a newspaper truce.

Reformed

For all thy children. Calvin: I consider that Isaiah spoke not of doctrine, but of men, of which the spiritual building of the Church is reared. It is by doctrine, indeed, that the Church is built; but the building of it is effected by assembling men together, and reducing them to a state of obedience to God. Taught by Jehovah: all that belongs to the ornament of the Church proceeds from no other source than from the grace of God; if we are carbuncles and sapphires in consequence of our being taught by the Lord, it follows that this does not proceed from nature. There are two ways in which the Lord teaches us: by external preaching, and by the secret revelation of the Holy Spirit. Christ quotes this passage (John 6:45). If this passage were to be understood as relating to external preaching only, the conclusion which Christ draws from it would not be well founded. The Gospel is preached indiscriminately to the elect and the reprobate; but the elect alone come to Christ, because they have been taught by God. Fanatics who abuse this passage for overturning the preaching of the word cannot be the children of the Church. And great peace: by the word peace he denotes happiness; the true happiness of men is when God enlightens our understandings, so that we embrace the salvation which has been revealed to us in Christ.

Reformed taught: stones are people; preaching plus Spirit; elect come; fanatics who scrap the pulpit are not her children. Shared with Wesley: taught by the LORD, word and Spirit, great peace. Carry John 6:45. Do not invent a millennial camp fight; Calvin's peace is happiness in Christ.

Key contrast Both hear Zion's children taught by the LORD, and great peace. Wesley takes taught as not only outwardly by his word but inwardly by his Spirit, and peace as inward discoveries of love and adoption more abundant under the gospel, plus outward safety. Calvin takes the jewels of vv. 11–12 as men assembled into the building, two ways of teaching (external preaching and secret Spirit), Christ in John 6:45 proving that only those taught by the Father come, the elect alone sincerely teachable, against fanatics who overturn the ministry; peace as the happiness of embracing salvation in Christ. Shared historic and christological referent: the same rebuilt Zion, the same LORD who teaches.

Isaiah 54:14

14In righteousness you shall be established; you shall be far from oppression, for you shall not fear; and from terror, for it shall not come near you.

Arminian

In righteousness you shall be established; you shall be far from oppression, for you shall not fear; and from terror, for it shall not come near you. Wesley: Established—Thine affairs shall be managed with righteousness, which is the glory of any society. Oppression—Either by thine own governors, or by foreign powers.

Arminian establishment: righteous management; far from home tyrants and foreign powers. Shared: established in righteousness; far from oppression; shall not fear; terror shall not come near. Carry v. 13's peace. Historic Zion first, then the church so ordered. No newspaper about later terrors.

Reformed

In righteousness. Calvin: he means that God will be the maker and architect of his Church. Some think that righteousness means good works, and that exposition has some plausibility arising from the Prophet having spoken about doctrine. But the Prophet's meaning was different, namely, that the Church shall be restored under God's guidance, who wishes to be its guardian and defender. He contrasts righteousness with the violence and oppression by which the Church has been thrown down; or, at least, he expresses stability, as if he had said that it shall not be a frail building. Thus, although men are leagued in every way for the destruction of the Church, they will gain nothing; for the Lord guards her by his righteousness.

Reformed establishment: God's righteousness as architect and guard, not first our good works as the mortar. Shared: established; far from oppression; no terror. Carry v. 10's unmoved covenant. Historic then gospel. No millennial timetable of later establishments.

Isaiah 54:15

15If anyone stirs up strife, it is not from me; whoever stirs up strife with you shall fall because of you.

Arminian

If anyone stirs up strife, it is not from me; whoever stirs up strife with you shall fall because of you. Wesley: Behold—It is true some will combine and make an attempt against thee. But—Without any such commission from me, as Sennacherib and Nebuchadnezzar had.

Arminian strife: some will combine, but not with the old commission of exile's tyrants; they fall. Shared: if anyone stirs up strife, it is not from me; they shall fall. Carry v. 14: far from oppression. Historic Sennacherib and Nebuchadnezzar named as the contrast. No culture war about later leagues; the point is whose commission the gathering is.

Reformed

Behold, he who assembleth shall assemble. Calvin: although many rise up for the purpose of overwhelming the Church, yet all their efforts and attacks shall fail. He appears to promise God's assistance not only against external foes, but against foes that are domestic and internal. Many assemble in the bosom of the Church, as if they wished to join themselves to her, but afterwards carry on internal war. Without me signifies in vain or unsuccessfully; they rush forward with blind fury, but it is the Lord's hand alone that gives prosperity. He alludes to what was formerly said, that the Babylonians should come under God's guidance to destroy Judea. At that time the people were ruined, because they had God for their enemy; but now, because without God, that is, without his guidance, the enemies shall attack her, they shall be ashamed and turn back. He who shall assemble in thee shall fall against thee: all that he shall attempt against thee shall fall back on his own head.

Reformed strife: Babylon came with him; these gather without him and fall, even from inside. Shared: not from me; they fall. Carry 41:25 as Calvin carries the contrast. Historic then gospel. No dispensational chart of later assemblies.

Isaiah 54:16

16Behold, I have created the smith who blows the fire of coals and produces a weapon for its purpose. I have also created the ravager to destroy;

Arminian

Behold, I have created the smith who blows the fire of coals and produces a weapon for its purpose. I have also created the ravager to destroy. Wesley: The smith—Both the smith that makes warlike instruments, and the soldier that uses them, are my creatures, and totally at my command, and therefore they cannot hurt you without my leave. The waster—To destroy only whom and when I please.

Arminian smith: maker and soldier both his; no hurt without leave; the waster destroys only whom and when he please. Shared: I created the smith; weapon for its purpose; I created the ravager to destroy. Carry v. 15: strife not from me. Do not invent open theism: the creatures are already at his command. Historic weapons of exile first. No millennial fight about later smiths.

Reformed

Behold, I have created the workmen. Calvin: the Lord shows how easily he delivers his Church from the base attacks of wicked men; for they can do nothing but so far as the Lord permits them, though he makes use of them as instruments for chastising his people. This may be viewed as referring both to the Babylonians and to other foes. He formerly said that wicked men attack the Church without the Lord, and now he says that they fight under God as their leader. The contrast: the Lord had raised up the Babylonians to destroy the Church, the metaphor of the deluge; but he solemnly declares that henceforth he is resolved to restrain his anger, so as never to permit the Church to be destroyed by her enemies, though he chastise her by his own hand. I have created the waster: this does not refer merely to the nature with which men are born, but to the very act of wasting. We must not, on that account, lay blame on God, as if he were the author of the unjust cruelty which dwells in men alone; God does not give assent to their wicked inclinations, but regulates their efforts by his secret providence, and employs them as the instruments of his anger.

Reformed smith: permitted instruments, not authors of their cruelty; Babylon the one deluge, not a standing commission. Shared with Wesley: created; cannot hurt without him. Carry 54:9: the Noah oath. No newspaper about later ravagers.

Isaiah 54:17

17no weapon that is fashioned against you shall succeed, and you shall refute every tongue that rises against you in judgment. This is the heritage of the servants of the LORD and their vindication from me, declares the LORD."

Arminian

no weapon that is fashioned against you shall succeed, and you shall refute every tongue that rises against you in judgment. This is the heritage of the servants of the LORD and their vindication from me, declares the LORD. Wesley: Condemn—And I will deliver thee not only from the fury of war, but also from the strife of tongues. This—This blessed condition, is the portion allotted them by me. Righteousness—The reward of their righteousness. Of me—I give it, and I will continue it to them.

Arminian heritage: war and tongues both answered; the portion allotted; the reward given and continued by him, not seized. Shared: no weapon shall succeed; every tongue refuted; heritage of the servants of the LORD; vindication from me. Carry 50:8: he who vindicates is near. Historic Zion first, then the servants. The invitation of 55:1–13 is the next unit, kept whole; do not split Come, everyone who thirsts, onto this heritage. No millennial camp fight about later weapons.

Reformed

Every weapon. Calvin: wicked men, even though they exert themselves to the utmost, will gain nothing; their attacks are guided and restrained by the secret purpose of God. Heaven permits them, indeed, to a great extent, in order to try the patience of believers; but, when God thinks proper, he strips them of their strength and armor. And every tongue: after having spoken of the weapons, he expressly mentions the tongue, because no other weapon is so deadly and destructive. Not only do they revile and slander and defame the servants of God, but, as far as lies in their power, they extinguish the truth of God. The Papists, when they call us heretics, plead against us as it were in judgment, and wish to be regarded as the defenders of catholic truth, though they maintain falsehood and idolatry. This is the inheritance: the Lord has granted this to believers, as it were, by a right of inheritance, so that they shall never be deprived of it. And their righteousness from me: whenever we are attacked and injured by men, let us learn to betake ourselves forthwith to the Lord; when we seek other assistance, the consequence is that we are deprived of his guardianship.

Reformed heritage: weapons and tongues fail; inheritance sure; righteousness from him, so run to him, not to other help. Shared: no weapon prospers; tongues answered; heritage of servants. Carry 54:10: the same unmoved covenant. Historic then gospel. No dispensational chart of later tongues. Come, everyone who thirsts, waits in 55, kept whole.
