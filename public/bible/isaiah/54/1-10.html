Passage

Isaiah 54

Verses 1–10 · ESV · Voices

After the Servant's guilt offering, Zion is told to sing, O barren one: historic Jerusalem, desolate in exile, shall have more children than the married. Enlarge the tent; offspring will possess the nations and people desolate cities. Fear not: your Maker is your husband, the Holy One of Israel your Redeemer. For a brief moment he deserted her; with everlasting love he will have compassion. This is like the days of Noah: mountains may depart, but his covenant of peace shall not be removed.

Isaiah 54:1

1"Sing, O barren one, who did not bear; break forth into singing and cry aloud, you who have not been in labor! For the children of the desolate one will be more than the children of her who is married," says the LORD.

Arminian

Sing, O barren one, who did not bear; break forth into singing and cry aloud, you who have not been in labor! For the children of the desolate one will be more than the children of her who is married, says the LORD. Wesley: Sing—The prophet having largely discoursed of the sufferings of Christ, and of the blessed fruits thereof, and here foreseeing that glorious state of the church, he breaks forth into this song of triumph. And as the foregoing chapter literally speaks of Christ, so doth this of the church of Christ. This church, consisting at first of the Jews, and afterwards of the Gentiles, had been barren, till the coming of Christ. The desolate—The church of the Gentiles, which in the times of the Old Testament was desolate, does now bring forth to God a more numerous posterity than that of the Jews.

Arminian singing: after 53, the church of Christ sings; historic Jews first, then more numerous Gentiles. Shared: sing; barren; desolate has more children than the married. Shared christology: Wesley's foregoing chapter is Christ; this is his church, not a newspaper city. Carry Galatians 4:27 as Paul carries this barren. Historic Zion's exile first, then this gospel fruit. Do not invent a millennial census as the Arminian/Reformed fork. No living politician as the husband of v. 5.

Reformed

Shout. Calvin: after having spoken of the death of Christ, he passes on with good reason to the Church; we cannot behold it in Christ if he be viewed by himself; we must come to his body, because Christ suffered for the Church, and not for himself. Thou barren, that didst not bear: he calls the Church barren, because no offspring could be expected from her so long as she groaned under wretched bondage; and even apart from her external wretchedness, everything was corrupted and defiled by superstitions. He calls the Church not merely barren but a widow. I prefer the more simple view: this extraordinary fertility of the Church will be at variance with what usually takes place, so that men may not judge of her condition by the ordinary course of nature; and yet I acknowledge that she was at that time in widowhood; God had actually divorced that nation by driving it into banishment. This punishment will be temporary.

Reformed singing: from the Servant's death to the body; exile as widowhood, then a fertility that breaks nature's odds. Shared: barren sings; more children. Carry 53:10's seed: the same offspring. Historic Judah first, then the Church. No dispensational chart of later brides.

Isaiah 54:2

2"Enlarge the place of your tent, and let the curtains of your habitations be stretched out; do not hold back; lengthen your cords and strengthen your stakes.

Arminian

Enlarge the place of your tent, and let the curtains of your habitations be stretched out; do not hold back; lengthen your cords and strengthen your stakes. Wesley: Enlarge—That it may be capable of the Gentiles, who shall flock to thee in great numbers. Strengthen—That they may be able to support that great weight which the tents thus enlarged, shall be upon them.

Arminian tent: stretched for Gentiles who actually flock; stakes strong enough for the weight. Shared: enlarge; stretch curtains; lengthen cords; strengthen stakes. Carry v. 1: the desolate's children need room. Historic Zion's tent first, then the nations. No millennial floor-plan of later curtains.

Reformed

Widen the place of thy tabernacles. Calvin: the Lord will not only restore his Church, but will bestow upon her a condition far more excellent. This prophecy began to be fulfilled under Cyrus, who gave the people liberty to return, and afterwards extended to Christ, in whom it has its full accomplishment. The Church conceived when the people returned to their native country; the conception was concealed, as it were, in the mother's womb; afterwards the people were increased, and after the birth the Church grew from infancy to manhood, till the Gospel was preached. The metaphor is borrowed from tabernacles; the Church has no solid building in the world; it appears to be wandering and unsettled. On the other hand, it resembles tents because earthly wealth, forces, and strength are not its support; its solid firmness relies on the invincible power of God.

Reformed tent: Cyrus first, then Christ the full birth; tents because no earthly citadel. Shared: enlarge; cords; stakes. Historic then gospel, both required. Carry Haggai 2:9 as Calvin carries the latter house. No newspaper about later canvas.

Isaiah 54:3

3For you will spread abroad to the right and to the left, and your offspring will possess the nations and will people the desolate cities.

Arminian

For you will spread abroad to the right and to the left, and your offspring will possess the nations and will people the desolate cities. Wesley: For—Thou shalt bring forth a multitude of children; for this word is commonly used of any extraordinary propagation of living creatures. On the left—On every side, in all the parts of the world. Thy seed—Thy spiritual seed, the church of the New Testament, which is accounted Abraham's seed, or children.

Arminian seed: spiritual seed, Abraham's children, filling the world, populating what exile emptied. Shared: spread right and left; offspring possess the nations; desolate cities inhabited. Carry Genesis 28:14. Historic desolate cities of Judah first, then the nations. Shared christology still: this seed is the fruit of 53, not a later politician's map. No millennial fight about later possessions.

Reformed

Because thou shalt be multiplied. Calvin: now follows the reason why he commanded the cords to be lengthened; a moderate space would not contain a numerous people, whom the Lord will gather into one from every quarter. Now, because Judea was hideous on account of its ruins and desolation, he says that the forsaken cities shall be inhabited.

Reformed seed: gathered from every quarter; Judea's ruins inhabited. Shared: spread; nations; desolate cities. Carry v. 2's Cyrus-then-Christ. Historic ruins first, then the gathering. No dispensational chart of later cities.

Isaiah 54:4

4"Fear not, for you will not be ashamed; be not confounded, for you will not be disgraced; for you will forget the shame of your youth, and the reproach of your widowhood you will remember no more.

Arminian

Fear not, for you will not be ashamed; be not confounded, for you will not be disgraced; for you will forget the shame of your youth, and the reproach of your widowhood you will remember no more. Wesley: Ashamed—For the barrenness and widowhood, which once was the matter of thy grief and shame. Forget—Thou shalt not be upbraided with thy former barrenness in thy youthful state.

Arminian fear-not: the shame of barren exile is actually forgotten, not a feint. Shared: fear not; not ashamed; not disgraced; forget the shame of youth; widowhood remembered no more. Carry 52:2: the captive daughter now unashamed. Historic widowhood of Zion first. No newspaper about later shames.

Reformed

Fear not, for thou shalt not be ashamed. Calvin: he strengthens the hearts of believers and addresses the whole Church; the calamity was universal, and the Church appeared to be totally ruined. He bids her be of good cheer; the issue of her troubles will be such that she shall not be ashamed. Thou shalt forget the shame of thy youth: the calamities which befell the Church while she was still young, the remembrance of which will be wholly obliterated by the prosperity which she shall afterwards enjoy. Widowhood is a term used because God had forsaken, and, so to speak, had divorced her.

Reformed fear-not: universal ruin, then the issue that wipes youth's shame. Shared: not ashamed; widowhood forgotten. Carry 50:1: the missing bill is not a cancelled covenant. Historic divorce-appearance first, then this forgetting. No millennial timetable of later widowhoods.

Isaiah 54:5

5For your Maker is your husband, the LORD of hosts is his name; and the Holy One of Israel is your Redeemer, the God of the whole earth he is called.

Arminian

For your Maker is your husband, the LORD of hosts is his name; and the Holy One of Israel is your Redeemer, the God of the whole earth he is called. Wesley: Thy maker—Will own thee for his spouse. The Lord—Who hath the sovereign command of all men and creatures, and therefore can subdue the Gentiles to thee, and can make thee to increase and multiply in so prodigious a measure, even in thine old age, notwithstanding thy barrenness in the days of thy youth. The God—The God and father of all nations, whereas formerly he was called only the God of Israel.

Arminian husband: Maker owns her; LORD of hosts can multiply her in old age; God of the whole earth, not Israel only. Shared: Maker is husband; LORD of hosts; Holy One of Israel your Redeemer; God of the whole earth. Shared christology: the Redeemer of 53 is this husband. Historic Zion first, then the nations. Do not invent open theism: the sovereign command actually subdues. No living politician as husband.

Reformed

For thy Maker is thy husband. Calvin: captivity might be said to be a kind of divorce; he now says, He who created thee shall be thy husband. He calls himself the Maker of his Church, not only because he created the Church as he created other men, but because he condescended to adopt her as his heritage; this privilege may be regarded as a new life. Whose name is Jehovah of hosts: this refers to his power, that we may be permitted to glory in it, seeing that we are his children. Thy Redeemer: he calls himself the Redeemer, in order that he may more fully confirm the people in that hope; that, although the former deliverance appeared to be cancelled, because the people were again led into captivity, yet they shall be restored. Shall be called the God of the whole earth: the name of God had formerly been, in some respects, confined to Judea, but by the preaching of the Gospel the Gentiles have been called to the same hope of salvation. The Lord is the God of the Gentiles as well as of the Jews.

Reformed husband: Maker by adoption; Redeemer after a deliverance that looked cancelled; God of the whole earth by the gospel. Shared with Wesley: husband; hosts; Redeemer; whole earth. Carry Romans 3:29. Historic captivity first, then Gentile hope. No dispensational monopoly of the Name.

Isaiah 54:6

6For the LORD has called you like a wife deserted and grieved in spirit, like a wife of youth when she is cast off, says your God.

Arminian

For the LORD has called you like a wife deserted and grieved in spirit, like a wife of youth when she is cast off, says your God. Wesley: Called thee—To return to him. As forsaken—When thou wast like a woman forsaken. And grieved—For the loss of her husband's favour. Of youth—As affectionately as an husband recalls his wife which he married in his youth.

Arminian call: a real summons to return; the affection of youth's husband, not a feint. Shared: called; wife deserted; grieved in spirit; wife of youth cast off. Carry v. 4's widowhood. Historic Zion's grief first. Those who stay deserted do not stay because no call was spoken. No millennial fight about later wives.

Reformed

For as a woman forsaken. Calvin: he meets a doubt which might arise in the minds of believers amidst so distressing a calamity. It seemed as if the Lord had rejected them, so that they had nothing to look for but destruction. The Prophet therefore reminds them that they ought not to despair, because they have been thus forsaken; for God, according to his mercy, is ready to be reconciled, and is even willing to raise them from the dead. And a wife of youth: the hearts of young husbands are more easily reconciled than the hearts of older husbands, being attracted by youthful age and tender love. In like manner, he shows that God will be easily reconciled. True, thou wast divorced; but the divorce shall not be of long duration. The Lord will show himself ready to be reconciled, and will even, of his own accord, be the first to invite thee to reconciliation.

Reformed call: he invites first; the divorce is not forever. Shared: forsaken wife; youth's wife recalled. Carry 50:1: the Husband did not cancel the bond. No open theism: mercy is ready, not undecided. Historic exile first, then this invitation.

Isaiah 54:7

7For a brief moment I deserted you, but with great compassion I will gather you.

Arminian

For a brief moment I deserted you, but with great compassion I will gather you. Wesley: For a moment—In comparison of God's everlasting kindness. Gather thee—From all the places where thou art dispersed, from all parts of the world.

Arminian moment: exile is brief beside everlasting kindness; the gathering is from all the world, sincere. Shared: brief moment deserted; great compassion; I will gather you. Carry v. 1: the barren now gathered. Historic dispersion first, then this compassion. No newspaper about later desertions. No open theism: the gatherer already speaks compassion.

Reformed

For a little moment I forsook thee. Calvin: the Prophet shows what will be the nature of this divorce, namely, that she shall be speedily restored to her former condition. He magnifies the mercy of God, and extenuates the sorrow; this divorce shall last but for a moment, but his mercy shall be everlasting. When he says that he forsook his people, it is a sort of admission of the fact. We are adopted by God in such a manner that we cannot be rejected by him on account of the treachery of men; for he is faithful. What the Prophet says in this passage must therefore refer to our feelings and to outward appearance, because we seem to be rejected by God when we do not perceive his presence and protection. And it is necessary that we should thus feel God's wrath, that we may know that we are justly chastised. But we must also perceive his mercy; and because it is infinite and eternal, we shall find that all afflictions in comparison of it are light and momentary.

Reformed moment: forsaken to our feeling and in just chastisement; not cast off as to adoption; mercy infinite. Shared: a little moment; great mercies gather. Carry the remnant he actually restores. No millennial chart of later moments.

Isaiah 54:8

8In overflowing anger for a moment I hid my face from you, but with everlasting love I will have compassion on you," says the LORD, your Redeemer.

Arminian

In overflowing anger for a moment I hid my face from you, but with everlasting love I will have compassion on you, says the LORD, your Redeemer. Wesley: With everlasting kindness—With kindness to thee and thy seed through all succeeding generations.

Arminian kindness: the hid face is a moment; the kindness runs through generations. Shared: overflowing anger for a moment; hid face; everlasting love; compassion; the LORD your Redeemer. Carry v. 5: the same Redeemer. Historic anger of exile first, then everlasting love. Those who still see a hid face do not see it because no compassion was spoken. No dispensational timetable of later hidings.

Reformed

In a moment of wrath. Calvin: he again repeats and enforces this statement, that they may not be at all discouraged by adversity. Amidst that frightful darkness, it was not easy for the captives to behold God's smiling face. We quickly lose courage and faint, if the Lord be not nigh. The afflictions of the Church are always momentary, when we raise our eyes to its eternal happiness. We ought to remember what Paul has taught us (2 Corinthians 4:17), that all the afflictions of believers are light and easy to be endured, and are justly considered to be momentary, while they look at the eternal weight of glory. There would be no propriety in comparing the seventy years of the captivity of the Jews to a moment, if it were not contrasted with the uninterrupted progress of the grace of God.

Reformed wrath: seventy years are a moment only beside uninterrupted grace. Shared: anger a moment; everlasting compassion; Redeemer. Carry 2 Corinthians 4:17. Historic Babylon first, then the Church's momentary stripes. No millennial fight as Arminian versus Reformed; both hear everlasting love.

Key contrast Both hear a moment of anger, a hid face, then everlasting compassion from the Redeemer. Wesley takes the moment as in comparison of God's everlasting kindness, and everlasting kindness as to thee and thy seed through all succeeding generations. Calvin takes the same moment as referring to our feelings and outward appearance when we do not perceive his presence, adoption not cancelled by men's treachery, seventy years a moment only when set against the uninterrupted progress of grace and the eternal weight of glory.

Isaiah 54:9

9"This is like the days of Noah to me: as I swore that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth, so I have sworn that I will not be angry with you, and will not rebuke you.

Arminian

This is like the days of Noah to me: as I swore that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth, so I have sworn that I will not be angry with you, and will not rebuke you. Wesley: This—This covenant of grace and peace made with thee shall be as certain and perpetual as that which I made with Noah, that there should never be another flood to drown the world. Wrath—So as to forsake thee utterly.

Arminian oath: as certain as Noah's; he will not forsake utterly. Shared: like Noah's days; swore; waters no more; not angry; not rebuke. Historic flood as the token, Zion's exile as the analog, then the gospel covenant. Carry Genesis 9:11. No open theism: the oath already stands. No newspaper about later floods.

Reformed

For the waters of Noah. Calvin: he compares that captivity to a deluge, which destroyed the face of the earth; for it appeared as if the Church was utterly ruined. This calamity will resemble the deluge; so that, as he was satisfied with a single deluge and would never again send another, so he is satisfied with this one destruction, so to speak, of the Church, and will never again permit the face of it to be destroyed. That I will not be wroth with thee must not be taken in an absolute, but in a comparative sense: he will never chastise his people so severely as not to mitigate the severity of the punishment. Although therefore tyrants indulge in wanton rage, and Satan employ his utmost efforts, yet he will never suffer the Church to be ruined.

Reformed oath: Babylon as the Church's one deluge; not wrathless in every stripe, but never ruined again. Shared: Noah's waters; sworn; not that wrath. Carry 53:10: the same will of the LORD. Historic captivity first, then this limit. No millennial chart of later deluges.

Isaiah 54:10

10For the mountains may depart and the hills be removed, but my steadfast love shall not depart from you, and my covenant of peace shall not be removed," says the LORD, who has compassion on you.

Arminian

For the mountains may depart and the hills be removed, but my steadfast love shall not depart from you, and my covenant of peace shall not be removed, says the LORD, who has compassion on you. Wesley: The mountains—Shall sooner depart from their places than any kindness shall depart from thee. The covenant—That covenant whereby I have made peace and friendship with thee, and have promised to thee all manner of happiness. God will not cast off his Christian church, as he cast off the church of the Jews, the New covenant is established upon better and surer promises than the Old. The Lord—Who doth this not for thine own merits, but merely for his own grace and mercy.

Arminian covenant: mountains go first; kindness stays; New covenant surer than the Old, by grace not merits. Shared: mountains may depart; steadfast love shall not; covenant of peace shall not be removed; the LORD who has compassion. Carry v. 8's everlasting love. Historic Zion first, then the Christian church Wesley names. Do not invent a millennial camp fight as Arminian versus Reformed; both hear the covenant unmoved. The storm-tossed stones of 54:11–17 are the next unit, kept whole.

Reformed

For the mountains shall indeed be moved. Calvin: he confirms the former statement, and declares that sooner shall the whole world be turned upside down than his mercy shall fail. The comparison is drawn from those things which appear to be strongest and most deeply rooted, in order to show that the foundation of the Church is far more durable. My mercy: it ought to be remarked what is the nature of the foundation of the covenant; for we can have no friendship with God unless he have mercy upon us, and receive us by free grace. The covenant of my peace: he calls it the covenant of peace, because the Lord offers to us all that belongs to perfect happiness; under the word peace the Hebrew writers include all prosperity. Since therefore this covenant contains solid and perfect happiness, it follows that all who are excluded from it are miserable. Saith Jehovah, who hath compassion on thee: he will be reconciled in no other way, and for no other reason, than because he is compassionate and ready to pardon.

Reformed covenant: Church's foundation more durable than mountains; mercy the base; peace as complete happiness; compassion the only reason. Shared with Wesley: mountains move, kindness does not; free grace, not merits. Carry Psalms 102:26 as Calvin carries the unshaken Church. After the Head of 53, the body is promised this peace. No dispensational chart of later hills.

Reformed Notable voices

“Shout. After having spoken of the death of Christ, he passes on with good reason to the Church; we must come to his body, which is the Church, because Christ suffered for the Church, and not for himself. Thou barren, that didst not bear: he calls the Church barren, because no offspring could be expected from her so long as she groaned under wretched bondage; he calls her not merely barren but a widow. I prefer the more simple view: this extraordinary fertility of the Church will be at variance with what usually takes place. And yet I acknowledge that she was at that time in widowhood; God had sent a bill of divorcement. Widen the place of thy tabernacles: the Lord will not only restore his Church, but will bestow upon her a condition far more excellent. This prophecy began to be fulfilled under Cyrus, who gave the people liberty to return, and afterwards extended to Christ, in whom it has its full accomplishment. The Church conceived when the people returned to their native country. The metaphor is borrowed from tabernacles; the Church has no solid building in the world. Because thou shalt be multiplied: a moderate space would not contain a numerous people. Fear not, for thou shalt not be ashamed: the issue of her troubles will be such that she shall not be ashamed; thou shalt forget the shame of thy youth. For thy Maker is thy husband: captivity might be said to be a kind of divorce; he who created thee shall be thy husband. Whose name is Jehovah of hosts: the greater the power of God, so much the greater is our boasting, so long as we are his children. Thy Redeemer: they shall be restored in such a manner as to know that the grace of God is not without effect. Shall be called the God of the whole earth: the name of God had formerly been, in some respects, confined to Judea, but by the preaching of the Gospel the Gentiles have been called. For as a woman forsaken: they ought not to despair, because they have been thus forsaken; God, according to his mercy, is ready to be reconciled. For a little moment I forsook thee: this divorce shall last but for a moment, but his mercy shall be everlasting. What the Prophet says must refer to our feelings and to outward appearance. In a moment of wrath: the afflictions of the Church are always momentary, when we raise our eyes to its eternal happiness (2 Corinthians 4:17). For the waters of Noah: he compares that captivity to a deluge; he is satisfied with this one destruction of the Church, and will never again permit the face of it to be destroyed. For the mountains shall indeed be moved: sooner shall the whole world be turned upside down than his mercy shall fail. My mercy: we can have no friendship with God unless he have mercy upon us and receive us by free grace. The covenant of my peace: the Lord offers to us all that belongs to perfect happiness.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 54:1–10)
