Passage

Isaiah 25

Verses 1–12 · ESV · Voices

O LORD, you are my God; I will exalt you; I will praise your name, for you have done wonderful things, plans formed of old, faithful and sure. You have made the city a heap, the fortified city a ruin; the foreigners’ palace a city no more. Strong peoples will glorify you; you have been a stronghold to the poor, a shelter from the storm. You subdue the noise of the foreigners. On this mountain the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a feast of rich food and well-aged wine. He will swallow up the covering and the veil; he will swallow up death forever; the Lord GOD will wipe away tears; the reproach of his people he will take away. Behold, this is our God; we have waited for him. The hand of the LORD will rest on this mountain, and Moab shall be trampled down as straw in a dunghill; he will lay low his pompous pride; the high fortifications he will bring down to the dust. The unit stays with historic Zion, Moab, and the nations around Judah—as Wesley and Calvin actually read, death swallowed in Christ—not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 25:1

1O LORD, you are my God; I will exalt you; I will praise your name, for you have done wonderful things, plans formed of old, faithful and sure.

Arminian

Wesley: O Lord—the prophet reflecting upon those great and glorious prophecies which he had delivered, interrupts the course of his prophecies, and breaks forth into a solemn celebration of God’s wonderful works. Thy counsels—from which all thy works proceed, and which thou hast from time to time revealed to thy prophets and people, which were of old, being conceived from all eternity, are true and firm, and shall certainly be accomplished. Shared: plans formed of old, faithful and sure.

Arminian pastoral: old counsels are not open theism; they were conceived from all eternity. Historic praise after the nations’ oracles, not a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: O Lord, thou art my God. Hitherto Isaiah has prophesied about judgments threatening almost the whole world. Godly persons tremble at the slightest token of his anger; the Prophet, having the calamities almost before his eyes, betakes himself to the Lord. He takes courage from the belief that in the midst of these tempests the Lord still determines to promote the advantage of his Church. Wonderful things; counsels of old are faithfulness and truth.

Reformed pastoral: the praise is still the seer’s after ch. 13–24. Shared: plans of old, sure. Historic church, not a millennial decoder. Not process-theism.

Isaiah 25:2

2For you have made the city a heap, the fortified city a ruin; the foreigners' palace is a city no more; it will never be rebuilt.

Arminian

Wesley: A city—which is put for cities: or of enemies of God and his people. And under the name cities he comprehends their countries and kingdoms. Strangers—the royal cities, in which were the palaces of strangers, of Gentiles. No city—their cities and palaces have been or shall be utterly and irrecoverably destroyed. Shared: fortified city a ruin, never rebuilt.

Arminian pastoral: historic enemy-cities around Judah, not a later capital. Do not hunt another heap.

Reformed

Calvin: for thou hast made of a city a heap. Some refer this to Jerusalem; but I think that the Prophet means cities of enemies, or, indefinitely, cities hostile to the people of God. The foreigners’ palace is a city no more; it shall never be built. The heap is the LORD’s work against those who crushed the poor of v. 4.

Reformed note: the ruin is still the enemy’s. Shared: never rebuilt. Historic nations, not a newspaper.

Key contrast Both hear a city made a heap—historic enemies, not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s cities of God’s enemies, Gentile palaces. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: not Jerusalem, but hostile cities; some would make it Zion, which he refuses.

Isaiah 25:3

3Therefore strong peoples will glorify you; cities of ruthless nations will fear you.

Arminian

Wesley: Shall fear—thy stoutest enemies observing thy wonderful works, shall be converted, or at least forced to tremble before thee. Shared: strong peoples glorify; ruthless cities fear.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet glorify. Historic ruthless nations of the oracles, not a culture-war speech.

Reformed

Calvin: therefore shall the strong people glorify thee; the city of the terrible nations shall fear thee. This is the end of the heap: conversion or trembling. Strong peoples learn whose hand made the ruin.

Reformed pastoral: the fear is still that lesson’s. Shared: ruthless nations will fear you. Historic cities, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 25:4

4For you have been a stronghold to the poor, a stronghold to the needy in his distress, a shelter from the storm and a shade from the heat; for the breath of the ruthless is like a storm against a wall,

Arminian

Wesley: For—for thou hast defended thy poor and helpless people. As a storm—makes a great noise, but without any effect. Shared: stronghold, shelter, shade; ruthless breath like a storm.

Arminian pastoral: the poor of historic Judah, not a later class headline. The wall holds because the LORD is the stronghold.

Reformed

Calvin: for thou hast been a strength to the poor, a strength to the needy in his distress, a refuge from the storm, a shadow from the heat, when the blast of the terrible ones is as a storm against the wall. Hence we see the fruit of conversion: they glorify because he sheltered the needy when the ruthless blew.

Reformed note: the storm against the wall is still that blast’s. Shared: stronghold to the poor. Historic remnant, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 25:5

5like heat in a dry place. You subdue the noise of the foreigners; as heat by the shade of a cloud, so the song of the ruthless is put down.

Arminian

Wesley: The noise—the tumultuous noise, as the word properly signifies; the rage and furious attempts of those Heathen nations that fought against God’s people. As the heat—with as much ease as thou dost allay the heat of a dry place, by the shadow of thy clouds. The branch—the arm or power, as a branch is the arm of a tree. Shared: foreigners’ noise subdued; ruthless song put down.

Arminian pastoral: historic heathen rage against Judah, not a modern anthem.

Reformed

Calvin: as the heat in a dry place. If the Lord did not aid when violent men rush upon us, our life would be destroyed. Thou shalt bring down the noise of strangers; as the heat by the shadow of a cloud, the branch of the terrible ones shall be brought low. The song of the ruthless is silenced as easily as heat under a cloud.

Reformed pastoral: the put-down song is still the ruthless’. Shared: noise of foreigners subdued. Historic nations, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 25:6

6On this mountain the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a feast of rich food, a feast of well-aged wine, of rich food full of marrow, of aged wine well refined.

Arminian

Wesley: And—in mount Zion, in God’s church. All people—both Jews and Gentiles. A feast—a feast made up of the most delicate provisions, which is manifestly meant of the ordinances, graces, and comforts given by God in his church. Of wines—which have continued upon the lees a competent time, whereby they gain strength, and are afterwards drawn off, and refined. Shared: a feast on this mountain for all peoples.

Arminian pastoral: Zion’s feast is gospel ordinance, not a millennial banquet chart. Historic mountain, Jews and Gentiles, not a newspaper catering story.

Reformed

Calvin: and the Lord of hosts shall make unto all people a feast of fat things in this mountain, a feast of wines on the lees. This passage has received various interpretations. This mountain is Zion. All people: the feast is not locked to one nation. Fat things and wines well refined: the richness of God’s grace in the Church, after the covering of v. 7 is destroyed.

Reformed note: the feast is still Zion’s. Shared: all peoples; rich food and refined wine. Historic mountain, gospel table—not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 25:7

7And he will swallow up on this mountain the covering that is cast over all peoples, the veil that is spread over all nations.

Arminian

Wesley: The face—the covering of the face. The veil—the ignorance of God, and of the true religion, which then was upon the Gentiles, and now is upon the Jews. Shared: covering and veil swallowed on this mountain.

Arminian pastoral: ignorance lifted in Christ, shared with Calvin. Historic nations’ veil, not a later headline. Do not invent a millennial chart from the covering.

Reformed

Calvin: and he will destroy in this mountain the face of the covering cast over all people, and the vail that is spread over all nations. Commentators differ; by the covering he means what hides God from the nations, destroyed on Zion when the feast is made. The same mountain that hosts the feast takes away the veil.

Reformed pastoral: the swallowed veil is still that mountain’s work. Shared: all peoples, all nations. Gospel knowledge, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 25:8

8He will swallow up death forever; and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from all faces, and the reproach of his people he will take away from all the earth, for the LORD has spoken.

Arminian

Wesley: He—Christ will by his death destroy the power of death, take away the sting of the first death, and prevent the second. In victory—Hebrew. unto victory; so as to overcome it perfectly; which complete victory Christ hath already purchased for, and will in due time actually confer upon his people. Rebuke—the reproach and contempt cast upon his faithful people by the ungodly world. Shared: death swallowed; tears wiped; reproach taken away.

Arminian pastoral: shared christology with Calvin and with 1 Corinthians 15. Historic people of God, not a millennial camp fight. The LORD has spoken; do not invent open theism from tears.

Reformed

Calvin: he hath destroyed death eternally. The Prophet continues his subject; in general he shews that God will put an end to all the distresses of his people. The Lord God will wipe away tears from off all faces, and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the Lord hath spoken it. Death swallowed is the same victory the Apostle later names; the feast of v. 6 is not complete while death remains.

Reformed close of this promise: death’s swallowing is still Christ’s. Shared with Wesley: forever; tears gone; reproach lifted. Historic church’s hope, not a newspaper, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 25:9

9It will be said on that day, “Behold, this is our God; we have waited for him, that he might save us. This is the LORD; we have waited for him; let us be glad and rejoice in his salvation.”

Arminian

Wesley: Our God—our Messiah, long since promised, and for whom we have waited long, is come into the world, bringing salvation with him. Shared: behold our God; we waited; we rejoice in his salvation.

Arminian pastoral: waiting is faith, not a gap in the decree. Historic Messiah come, shared christology. Do not stretch “that day” into a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: and it shall be said in that day, Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us. This is the Lord; we have waited for him; we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation. The verb is indefinite; the Church speaks. Waiting is the remnant’s office until the salvation of v. 8 is seen.

Reformed pastoral: our God is still the awaited LORD. Shared: we have waited; his salvation. Historic Zion’s confession, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 25:10

10For the hand of the LORD will rest on this mountain, and Moab shall be trampled down in his place, as straw is trampled down in a dunghill.

Arminian

Wesley: Rest—the powerful and gracious presence of God shall have its constant and settled abode. Moab—the Moabites are put for all the enemies of God’s church. Shared: hand rests on this mountain; Moab trampled as straw.

Arminian pastoral: historic Moab from ch. 15–16, then as type of the church’s enemies. Not a living nation on a map. Do not turn the dunghill into a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: for in this mountain shall the hand of the Lord rest, and Moab shall be trodden down under him, even as straw is trodden down for the dunghill. The design of this beginning is to confirm the feast and the swallowed death: the hand stays on Zion. Moab, named from the oracles already given, stands for the proud enemy still in his place, now straw.

Reformed note: the trampling is still Moab’s. Shared: hand on this mountain. Historic Moab and Zion, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 25:11

11And he will spread out his hands in the midst of it as a swimmer spreads his hands out to swim, but the LORD will lay low his pompous pride together with the skill of his hands.

Arminian

Wesley: He—the Lord, whose power they shall be no more able to resist, than the waters can resist a man that swims. Spread—to smite and destroy them. The spoils—with all their wealth which they have gained by rapine, and spoiling of God’s people. Shared: swimmer’s hands; pride laid low.

Arminian pastoral: Moab’s skill cannot match the swimmer. Historic pride, not a culture-war boast.

Reformed

Calvin: and he shall spread out his hands in the midst of them, as he that swimmeth spreadeth forth his hands to swim; and he shall bring down their pride together with the spoils of their hands. The Prophet now explains the former statement. Moab’s own swimming cannot save him; the LORD’s spread hands lay pride low, spoils and all.

Reformed pastoral: the skill of his hands is still Moab’s. Shared: pompous pride laid low. Historic enemy, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 25:12

12And the high fortifications of his walls he will bring down, lay low, and cast to the ground, to the dust.

Arminian

Wesley: And—all thy fortifications, in which thou trustest. Shared: high walls to the dust.

Arminian close: Moab’s forts join the heap of v. 2. Historic walls east of Judah, not a twenty-first-century bunker. The feast on Zion remains; do not stretch the dust into a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: and the fortress of the high fort of thy walls shall he bring down, lay low, and bring to the ground, even to the dust. He now directs his discourse to the country of Moab. The high fort matches the lofty city later named in 26:5. Dust is the end of pride that would not come to the mountain’s feast.

Reformed close: walls to dust are still Moab’s. Shared: high fortifications brought down. Historic Moab in the Assyria age—already enough without a later decoder.
