Passage

Isaiah 18

Verses 1–7 · ESV · Voices

Ah, land of whirring wings that is beyond the rivers of Cush, which sends ambassadors by the sea in vessels of papyrus. Go, you swift messengers, to a nation tall and smooth, feared near and far, whose land the rivers divide. All inhabitants of the world, when a signal is raised, look; when a trumpet is blown, hear. The LORD quietly looks from his dwelling like clear heat and a cloud of dew in harvest; before the harvest he cuts off the shoots. Birds of prey summer on them, beasts winter on them. At that time tribute is brought to the LORD of hosts from that people to Mount Zion. The unit stays with historic Cush beyond the rivers of Ethiopia in the Assyria age—ambassadors, papyrus boats, a present to Zion—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper of later states, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 18:1

1Ah, land of whirring wings that is beyond the rivers of Cush,

Arminian

Wesley: The Lord—either Ethiopia beyond Egypt; or of Egypt. Wings—the title of wings is given to battlements, to an army, and to the sails of a ship, as this word is here commonly understood. Shadowing with wings is nothing else but overspread or filled with them, in regard of the great numbers either of their armies, or of their ships. Rivers—called rivers in the plural, either for its greatness, or for the many rivulets that run into it. Shared: a land beyond the rivers of Cush.

Arminian pastoral: Cush beyond Egypt is a real neighbor in the Assyria age. Do not turn whirring wings into a newspaper. Historic Ethiopia, not a twenty-first-century map.

Reformed

Calvin: woe to the land. I cannot determine with certainty what is the nation of which Isaiah speaks, though he shews plainly that it bordered on Ethiopia. Some consider it to refer to the whole of Egypt; but this is a mistake, for in the next chapter he treats of Egypt separately. It is not far from Egypt and Ethiopia; some may view it as that part of Ethiopia which lay on the sea-coast, for the Assyrians were at war with the king of the Ethiopians (Isaiah 37:9). Shadowing with wings: its sea was well supplied with harbours, so that it had many vessels and was wealthy.

Reformed pastoral: the land is still that Cushite neighbor, not a later state. Shared: whirring wings beyond the rivers. Historic Cush, not a millennial decoder.

Key contrast Both hear a land beyond the rivers of Cush in the Assyria age, not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s either: Ethiopia beyond Egypt, or Egypt itself, wings as armies or sails. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: not the whole of Egypt, which has its own chapter; a distinct people bordering Ethiopia, perhaps the seacoast of Cush at war with Assyria, though he will not fix the name with certainty.

Isaiah 18:2

2which sends ambassadors by the sea, in vessels of papyrus on the waters! Go, you swift messengers, to a nation tall and smooth, to a people feared near and far, a nation mighty and conquering, whose land the rivers divide.

Arminian

Wesley: Sendeth—that at this time are sending ambassadors, to strengthen themselves with alliances. Bulrushes—both the Egyptians and Ethiopians used boats of rushes or reeds, cheaper, swifter, and lighter. These seem to be the words of the prophet, who gives a commission from God to these messengers. Scattered—not by banishment but in their habitations, which agrees well to the Ethiopians. Peeled—having their hair plucked off; used in scripture for some great calamity, and this title may be given prophetically. Terrible—such were the Egyptians and Ethiopians. Meted—meted out as it were with lines to destruction. The rivers—which may be understood of the Assyrians or Babylonians breaking in upon them like a river.

Arminian pastoral: papyrus boats and swift messengers are historic Nile craft, not a modern embassy. Do not preach the tall nation as a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: sending ambassadors by the sea. This relates strictly to the state of those times. It would appear that this nation solicited the Egyptians or Syrians to harass the Jews, or that the Assyrians employed them, or that they had formed an alliance with the Egyptians against the Assyrians; nothing more than conjectures can be offered. In ships of reeds: the Egyptians used such because of the Nile’s cataracts. Go, ye swift messengers: if they were enemies of the Jews, the design was consolation to believers; if the Jews were led into an unlawful league, the exhortation is ironical. A nation scattered and peeled, terrible from their beginning, whose land the rivers have spoiled.

Reformed note: the ambassadors are still that age’s. Shared: vessels of papyrus; a feared people whose land the rivers divide. Historic Cush, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 18:3

3All you inhabitants of the world, you who dwell on the earth, when a signal is raised on the mountains, look! When a trumpet is blown, hear!

Arminian

Wesley: When—when God shall gather together the nations, as it were by the lifting up of an ensign, or by the sound of a trumpet, to execute his judgments upon this people. Shared: look when the signal is raised.

Arminian pastoral: the world is called to watch a real stroke on historic Cush. Do not stretch the ensign into a later headline. The trumpet is the Lord’s, not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: all ye inhabitants of the world. He shews that this work of God will be so manifestly displayed that the whole world will be constrained to behold it. When he lifteth up an ensign on the mountains: God will erect a banner, as it were, that all may know that the work is his. When he bloweth a trumpet, hear ye: none will be able to plead ignorance.

Reformed pastoral: the signal is still that judgment’s. Shared: look; hear. Historic nations around Judah, not a newspaper of later mountains.

Isaiah 18:4

4For thus the LORD said to me: “I will quietly look from my dwelling like clear heat in sunshine, like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest.”

Arminian

Wesley: Rest—I will not bestir myself, to help this people. God is said in scripture to rest, or sit still, when he doth not work on the behalf of a person or people. Dwelling-place—in heaven, the place where God dwells. Harvest—the sense is, that God would look upon them with as uncomfortable an influence as the sun with a clear heat upon the herbs, which are scorched and killed by it; and as a cloud of the dew, which brings dew or rain, in the heat of harvest, when it is unwelcome and hurtful.

Arminian pastoral: quiet looking is not open theism; the LORD has already said what he will do. Historic Cush waiting on a harvest that will not be gathered. Do not invent a limited God from “I will quietly look.”

Reformed

Calvin: but thus said Jehovah unto me. After having threatened a slaughter of that people, he adds that God will be at rest, and will quietly look on from heaven, till they are ripe for destruction. Like a clear heat upon herbs, and like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest: the resting of Jehovah, hovering over the enemy till they are ripe for destruction, compared to the gloom before a hot day and the sheet of dew in harvest. He is not idle; he waits his time.

Reformed note: rest is decree, not absence. Shared: quiet look from the dwelling. Historic harvest over Cush, not a newspaper, and not process-theism.

Isaiah 18:5

5For before the harvest, when the blossom is over, and the flower becomes a ripening grape, he cuts off the shoots with pruning hooks, and the spreading branches he lops off and clears away.

Arminian

Wesley: For—before they receive the end of their hopes. When—when the bud or flower is turned into a grape, which gives hopes of good vintage. He—the Lord. The branches—instead of gathering the grapes, shall cut down the tree, and throw it into the fire. Shared: cut before the vintage is gathered.

Arminian pastoral: hopes of a good vintage do not bind the pruner. Historic Cush’s ripening, not a modern vineyard. Do not preach the pruning-hook as a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: for when the harvest shall be at hand. Literally it is, in presence of the harvest. When they think that they are just about to gather the fruits of their labor, the Lord will disappoint them. He shall cut off the sprigs with pruning-hooks, and take away and cut down the branches: their flourishing leaders, devoted by Jehovah to destruction, shall be cut off. The people here spoken of are, as some take it, the Assyrians under Sennacherib; in any case it is that age’s harvest, not a later one.

Reformed pastoral: the lopping is still before that harvest. Shared: shoots cut with pruning hooks. Historic nations around Judah, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 18:6

6They shall all of them be left to the birds of prey of the mountains and to the beasts of the earth. And the birds of prey will summer on them, and all the beasts of the earth will winter on them.

Arminian

Wesley: Thy—the branches being cut down and thrown upon the ground, with the unripe grapes upon them. Left—they shall lie upon the earth, so that either birds or beasts may shelter themselves with them, or feed on them, both summer and winter. Shared: summer birds, winter beasts.

Arminian pastoral: unburied shoots become a year’s food for scavengers. Historic field after the pruning of v. 5, not a newspaper of later carcasses.

Reformed

Calvin: they shall be left together. He means that they will be cast aside as a thing of no value, and will be exposed as a prey to birds and beasts. The fowls shall summer upon them, and the beasts of the earth shall winter upon them: the slaughter will be so great that the wild animals will have food for a whole year.

Reformed note: the year’s feeding is still that battlefield’s. Shared: birds summer, beasts winter. Historic Cush, not a later headline.

Isaiah 18:7

7At that time tribute will be brought to the LORD of hosts from a people tall and smooth, from a people feared near and far, a nation mighty and conquering, whose land the rivers divide, to Mount Zion, the place of the name of the LORD of hosts.

Arminian

Wesley: In that time—at or after that time, when the judgment shall be compleatly executed. A people—the people of whom I am speaking shall present themselves, and their sacrifices, to the true God. Shared: tribute to Zion from that same people.

Arminian close: Cush may yet bring a present to the LORD of hosts. Historic Mount Zion, not a millennial camp fight. Do not turn the tribute into a newspaper of later states. Wesley and Calvin share the homage as to the true God at Zion.

Reformed

Calvin: in that time. The Prophet again shews why he threatened the destruction of a heathen nation: that the Church may know that God watches over her. The present shall be brought unto the Lord of hosts of a people scattered and peeled, to the place of the name of the Lord of hosts, the mount Zion. Foreigners shall do homage to God; the Church shall see even distant nations acknowledging his name. This is not a newspaper of later empires; it is that people’s homage after the pruning, brought to historic Zion.

Reformed close: the present is still Cush’s, to the place of the Name. Shared: tribute to Mount Zion. Historic Cush and Zion in the Assyria age, already enough without a later decoder.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the land of whirring wings beyond Cush, papyrus ambassadors, a tall and smooth nation, the LORD’s quiet look from his dwelling, shoots cut before harvest, and tribute brought to Mount Zion—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 18:1–7.

Arminian Notable voices

“The Lord—Either Ethiopia beyond Egypt; or of Egypt. Wings—The title of wings is given, in scripture, to divers things which have some kind of resemblance to wings, as to the battlements of an house or temple, to an army, and to the sails of a ship, as this word is here commonly understood. And shadowing with wings is nothing else but overspread or filled with them. Which title may be given either to Ethiopia or Egypt, in regard of the great numbers either of their armies, or of their ships or vessels sailing upon the sea or rivers. Rivers—Called rivers, in the plural number, either for its greatness, or for the many rivulets that run into it. Sendeth—That at this time are sending ambassadors, to strengthen themselves with alliances. Bulrushes—Both the Egyptians and Ethiopians used boats of rushes or reeds. Messengers—Whom I have appointed for this work. Scattered—Not by banishment but in their habitations. Peeled—Having their hair plucked off; this title may be given to them prophetically, to signify their approaching destruction. Terrible—Such were the Egyptians, and Ethiopians. Meted—Meted out as it were with lines to destruction. Rest—I will not bestir myself, to help this people. Harvest—God would look upon them with as uncomfortable an influence as the sun with a clear heat upon the herbs. For—Before they receive the end of their hopes. He—The Lord. The branches—Instead of gathering the grapes, shall cut down the tree. In that time—When the judgment shall be compleatly executed. A people—The people of whom I am speaking shall present themselves, and their sacrifices, to the true God.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 18:1–7)

Reformed Notable voices

“Woe to the land. I cannot determine with certainty what is the nation of which Isaiah speaks, though he shews plainly that it bordered on Ethiopia. Some consider it to refer to the whole of Egypt; but this is a mistake, for in the next chapter he treats of Egypt separately. Still it is uncertain whether they leagued against the Jews or joined with the Egyptians in driving out the Assyrians. If they were avowed enemies to the Jews, Isaiah threatens punishment; but if they deceived them by false promises, he shews that nothing is to be expected from them. When he says that that land shadows with wings, we learn from it that its sea was well supplied with harbours. Sending ambassadors by the sea: this relates strictly to the state of those times; in ships of reeds, such as the Egyptians used because of the Nile’s cataracts. All ye inhabitants of the world: this work of God will be so manifestly displayed that the whole world will be constrained to behold it. But thus said Jehovah unto me: after having threatened a slaughter of the Ethiopians or of that people, he adds that God will be at rest, and will quietly look on from heaven, like a clear heat and a cloud of dew in harvest, till they are ripe for destruction. For when the harvest shall be at hand, he shall cut off the sprigs with pruning-hooks. They shall be left together unto the fowls. In that time shall the present be brought unto the Lord of hosts, to the place of the name of the Lord of hosts, the mount Zion: the Prophet shews why he threatened the destruction of a heathen nation—that the Church may know that God watches over her, and that foreigners shall do homage to God on Zion.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 18:1–7)
