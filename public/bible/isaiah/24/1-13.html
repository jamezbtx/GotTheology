Passage

Isaiah 24

Verses 1–13 · ESV · Voices

Behold, the LORD will empty the earth and make it desolate, twist its surface and scatter its inhabitants. As with the people, so with the priest; buyer and seller, lender and borrower. The earth shall be utterly empty, for the LORD has spoken. The earth mourns; the highest people languish. The earth lies defiled; they have transgressed the laws, broken the everlasting covenant. A curse devours the earth; few men are left. The wine mourns, tambourines stilled, wine bitter. The wasted city is broken down; outcry for lack of wine; desolation left in the city; gates battered. In the midst of the earth among the nations, as when an olive tree is beaten. The unit stays with historic Judah and the nations named from chapter 13 onward in the Assyria–Babylon age—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper of later states, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 24:1

1Behold, the LORD will empty the earth and make it desolate, and he will twist its surface and scatter its inhabitants.

Arminian

Wesley: The land—of Canaan. Waste—he will shortly make it waste, first by the Assyrians, and then by the Chaldeans. Turneth—brings it into great disorder and confusion. Shared: the LORD empties and scatters.

Arminian pastoral: historic Canaan under Assyria then Babylon, not a twenty-first-century globe. Do not turn “the earth” into a newspaper. This is not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: behold, Jehovah maketh the earth empty. This prophecy is the conclusion of all from the thirteenth chapter downwards: Jews and Israel, Moabites, Assyrians, Egyptians, and other nations near the Jews. Some refer it only to Israel or the Jews; as he mentions the world, it is a comprehensive statement of all he formerly said. Others think he refers to the last day, which I consider to be an excessively forced interpretation; after threatenings he adds consolation. The earth is covered when inhabited, laid bare when stripped.

Reformed pastoral: emptying is still that survey’s. Shared: the LORD scatters inhabitants. Historic nations around Judah, not a last-day decoder pasted onto the verse.

Key contrast Both hear the LORD emptying a historic land in the Assyria–Babylon age, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s land of Canaan, wasted first by the Assyrians and then by the Chaldeans. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: a summary of chapters 13–23 on all the near nations, Jews especially in view; to force the last day here is excessive.

Isaiah 24:2

2And it shall be, as with the people, so with the priest; as with the slave, so with his master; as with the maid, so with her mistress; as with the buyer, so with the seller; as with the lender, so with the borrower; as with the creditor, so with the debtor.

Arminian

Wesley: It shall be—the approaching calamity shall be universal, without any distinction of persons or ranks; the priests themselves having been partakers of the peoples sins, shall also partake with them in their plagues. The seller—the purchaser of lands shall have no more left than he that hath sold all his patrimony; all persons shall be made equal in beggary and slavery. Shared: priest with people; buyer with seller.

Arminian pastoral: ranks collapse in historic Judah. Do not preach priest and slave as a culture-war speech.

Reformed

Calvin: and it shall be as with the people, so with the priest. Utmost desolation, in which there will be no longer any distinction of ranks or any appearance of a commonwealth. So long as government remains, some distinction continues; this emptying levels it. The priest is named because Judah holds first rank in the summary.

Reformed note: equal ruin is still that emptying’s. Shared: as with the people, so with the priest. Historic land, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 24:3

3The earth shall be utterly empty and utterly plundered; for the LORD has spoken this word.

Arminian

Wesley has no separate lemma here; his land of Canaan emptied in v. 1 still holds, first by Assyrians then Chaldeans. Shared: utterly empty; the LORD has spoken.

Arminian pastoral: the seal is the speaker. Historic plunder, not a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: by emptying shall the earth be emptied. He confirms what he had already said, and shews that it is not a slight calamity. For the Lord hath spoken this word: the certainty rests on the speaker, not on Isaiah’s guess.

Reformed pastoral: the word is still that decree’s. Shared: utterly plundered; the LORD has spoken. Historic summary, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 24:4

4The earth mourns and withers; the world languishes and withers; the highest people of the earth languish.

Arminian

Wesley: The world—the land of Judea. The majesty—not only common people, but the high and lofty ones. Shared: earth and highest people languish.

Arminian pastoral: Judea’s lofty ones fall with the ground. Historic withering, not a modern climate story.

Reformed

Calvin: the earth hath lamented and faded away; the world languisheth; the haughty people of the earth do languish. He proceeds with the subject; all this tends to explain the emptying. Height of rank does not exempt.

Reformed note: the highest languish is still that land’s. Shared: world withers. Historic peoples of ch. 13–23, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 24:5

5The earth lies defiled under its inhabitants; for they have transgressed the laws, violated the statutes, broken the everlasting covenant.

Arminian

Wesley: Defiled—by the wickedness of its people. The laws—the laws of God. Ordinance—God’s ordinances concerning his worship and service; the singular being put for the plural. Covenant—the covenant made between God and Abraham, and all his posterity, which was everlasting, both on God’s part, who engaged to be a God to them and to their seed forever, and on Israel’s part, who were obliged to constant and perpetual obedience thro’ all generations. Shared: defiled earth; everlasting covenant broken.

Arminian pastoral: Abraham’s covenant is historic Israel’s, not a later treaty. They might yet have kept the ordinance.

Reformed

Calvin: and the earth was deceitful under the inhabitants thereof, because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant. Others render defiled or polluted. The cause of the emptying is guilt under a known word. The everlasting covenant is God’s settled order, broken by those who had the law.

Reformed pastoral: the defilement is still that inhabitant’s. Shared: laws, statutes, covenant. Historic Judah first, then the nations in the summary.

Key contrast Both hear a defiled land because inhabitants broke God’s law and covenant—historic, not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s Abrahamic covenant, everlasting on God’s part and Israel’s duty. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: the earth deceitful or defiled because they changed the ordinance; the covenant is God’s settled order, with Judah first in the survey.

Isaiah 24:6

6Therefore a curse devours the earth, and its inhabitants suffer for their guilt; therefore the inhabitants of the earth are scorched, and few men are left.

Arminian

Wesley: The curse—the curse of God threatened to transgressors. Burned—are consumed by the wrath of God, which is commonly compared to fire. Shared: curse, guilt, few left.

Arminian pastoral: few left prepares the gleaning of v. 13. Historic scorched inhabitants, not a later census.

Reformed

Calvin: therefore hath the curse devoured the earth. Some render it perjury; as (ālāh) also denotes an oath, the curse of the covenant-breakers is in view. Inhabitants suffer for their guilt; few men are left. The remnant is already in the sentence.

Reformed note: the devouring curse is still that guilt’s. Shared: few men left. Historic land, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 24:7

7The wine mourns, the vine languishes, all the merry-hearted sigh.

Arminian

Wesley: Mourneth—because there are none to drink it. Grief is ascribed to senseless creatures by a figure usual in all authors. Languisheth—because there are no people left to dress it, or gather its grapes. The merry-hearted—that made their hearts merry with wine. Shared: vine and merry-hearted fail together.

Arminian pastoral: historic vintage of Judah, not a culture-war cellar.

Reformed

Calvin: the wine hath failed, the vine languisheth, all the merry-hearted do sigh. He threatens chiefly those who lived in pleasure. When the vintage fails, the sigh replaces the song.

Reformed pastoral: the mourning wine is still that land’s. Shared: merry-hearted sigh. Historic vine, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 24:8

8The mirth of the tambourines is stilled, the noise of the jubilant has ceased, the mirth of the lyre is stilled.

Arminian

Wesley: Tabrets—which they used in their feasts. The noise—the word properly signifies a confused clamour, such as drunken men make. Shared: tambourine, shout, and lyre stop.

Arminian pastoral: stilled feasts in historic Judah, not a later concert.

Reformed

Calvin’s wine of v. 7 still holds the feast: they shall not drink wine with a song. Tambourine and harp belong to that silenced mirth. The emptying reaches the instruments.

Reformed note: stilled lyres are still that city’s. Shared: jubilant noise ceased. Historic feasts, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 24:9

9No more do they drink wine with singing; strong drink is bitter to those who drink it.

Arminian

Wesley: Bitter—because of the fears and miseries wherewith it is mixed. Shared: wine without song; drink made bitter.

Arminian pastoral: fear spoils the cup. Historic siege-miseries, not a modern tasting.

Reformed

Calvin: they shall not drink wine with a song. To drink wine is not in itself evil, because God created it; he condemns the abuse in feasts when judgment stands at the door. Strong drink shall be bitter to them that drink it.

Reformed pastoral: bitterness is still that fear’s. Shared: no more wine with singing. Historic Judah, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 24:10

10The wasted city is broken down; every house is shut up so that none can enter.

Arminian

Wesley: The city—Jerusalem, and other cities; for the singular word may be here taken collectively. A city of confusion or disorder, breaking all the laws and orders which God had established among them. Shut up—because the inhabitants are either dead, or gone into captivity. Shared: wasted city; houses shut.

Arminian pastoral: historic Jerusalem and her sister towns, not a twenty-first-century ruin.

Reformed

Calvin: the city of vanity is broken down; every house is shut up, that no man may come in. I do not object to viewing this as relating especially to Jerusalem, yet in this summary it may take in the ruined cities of the nations already named. Vanity: tohu, confusion, the order gone.

Reformed note: shut houses are still that emptying’s. Shared: wasted city broken down. Historic towns, not a millennial map.

Isaiah 24:11

11There is an outcry in the streets for lack of wine; all joy has grown dark; the gladness of the earth is banished.

Arminian

Wesley: A crying—such was their gross sensuality and sottishness, that instead of crying for their sins, they did only howl for their corn, and wine, and oil, Hosea vii, 14. Shared: outcry for wine; joy darkened.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have cried for sin. Historic streets, not a culture-war howl.

Reformed

Calvin: there is a cry about wine in the streets; all joy is darkened, the mirth of the land is gone. He means scarcity of wine; where they howled for drink they would not howl for guilt. Joy grows dark because the vintage of v. 7 is gone.

Reformed pastoral: the outcry is still that street’s. Shared: gladness banished. Historic land, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 24:12

12Desolation is left in the city; the gates are battered into ruins.

Arminian

Wesley has no separate lemma here; his city of v. 10 still holds: Jerusalem and other cities, inhabitants dead or captive. Shared: desolation left; gates ruined.

Arminian pastoral: battered gates of historic towns, not a later fortress headline.

Reformed

Calvin: in the city is left desolation, and the gate is smitten with destruction. By an elegant expression he describes desolation remaining as the only inhabitant. Gates, the city’s strength, are smashed.

Reformed note: the leftover is still emptiness. Shared: gates battered. Historic city in the summary, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 24:13

13For thus it shall be in the midst of the earth among the nations, as when an olive tree is beaten, as at the gleaning when the grape harvest is done.

Arminian

Wesley: When—when this judgment shall be executed, there shall be left a remnant; as there are some few olives or grapes left after the vintage is over. Shared: beaten olive, post-vintage gleaning, remnant among the nations.

Arminian close of this half: the remnant prepares the song of vv. 14–23. Historic nations around Judah, not a newspaper. Do not stretch the olive into a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: for it shall be in the midst of the land among the nations, as the shaking of an olive tree, as the gleaning grapes when the vintage is done. As this statement is inserted between emptying and the remnant’s song, he shews both the scarcity and the leftover berries. The small number of the godly shall be left out of an abundant vintage.

Reformed close: the beaten olive is still that survey’s remnant. Shared: gleaning among the nations. Historic Assyria–Babylon age—already enough without a later headline.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the LORD emptying the earth, priest with people, a defiled land and a broken covenant, a curse, stilled lyres, a wasted city, and olive-gleanings among the nations—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 24:1–13.

Arminian Notable voices

“The land—Of Canaan. Waste—He will shortly make it waste, first by the Assyrians, and then by the Chaldeans. Turneth—Brings it into great disorder and confusion. It shall be—The approaching calamity shall be universal, without any distinction of persons or ranks; the priests themselves having been partakers of the peoples sins, shall also partake with them in their plagues. The seller—The purchaser of lands shall have no more left than he that hath sold all his patrimony; and all persons shall be made equal in beggary and slavery. The world—The land of Judea. The majesty—Not only common people, but the high and lofty ones. Defiled—By the wickedness of its people. The laws—The laws of God. Ordinance—God’s ordinances concerning his worship and service. Covenant—The covenant made between God and Abraham, and all his posterity, which was everlasting, both on God’s part and on Israel’s part. The curse—The curse of God threatened to transgressors. Burned—Are consumed by the wrath of God, which is commonly compared to fire. Mourneth—Because there are none to drink it. The merry-hearted—That made their hearts merry with wine. Tabrets—Which they used in their feasts. Bitter—Because of the fears and miseries wherewith it is mixed. The city—Jerusalem, and other cities; for the singular word may be here taken collectively. A city of confusion or disorder, breaking all the laws and orders which God had established among them. Shut up—Because the inhabitants are either dead, or gone into captivity. A crying—Instead of crying for their sins, they did only howl for their corn, and wine, and oil. When—When this judgment shall be executed, there shall be left a remnant; as there are some few olives or grapes left after the vintage is over.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 24:1–13)

Reformed Notable voices

“Behold, Jehovah maketh the earth empty. This prophecy, so far as I can judge, is the conclusion of all the descriptions that have been given from the thirteenth chapter downwards, in which Isaiah foretold destruction not only to the Jews and to Israel, but to the Moabites, Assyrians, Egyptians, and other nations. Having, as it were, surveyed all the countries which were near the Jews and known to them, he gives a brief summary of the whole. Some view this as referring to Israel, and others to the Jews; as he mentions the world, I can view it in no other light than as a comprehensive statement of all that he formerly said about each of them. Nor is this contradicted by the mention of the priest, for the Jews always hold the highest rank. Others suppose that he means the whole world, but think that he refers to the last day, which I consider to be an excessively forced interpretation; for after having threatened the Jews and other nations, the Prophet afterwards adds a consolation. And it shall be as with the people, so with the priest: utmost desolation, no distinction of ranks. The earth hath lamented. The earth was deceitful under its inhabitants, because they have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant. Therefore hath the curse devoured the earth. The wine hath failed; they shall not drink wine with a song. The city of vanity is broken down. There is a cry about wine. In the city is left desolation. For it shall be in the midst of the land among the nations, as the shaking of an olive tree.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 24:1–13)
