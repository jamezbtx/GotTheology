Passage

Isaiah 24

Verses 14–23 · ESV · Voices

They lift up their voices, they sing for joy; over the majesty of the LORD they shout from the west. In the east give glory to the LORD; in the coastlands of the sea, give glory to the name of the LORD, the God of Israel. From the ends of the earth songs of praise, glory to the Righteous One. But I waste away: the traitors have betrayed. Terror and the pit and the snare upon the inhabitant of the earth; windows of heaven opened, foundations tremble; the earth utterly broken, staggers like a drunken man, falls and will not rise. On that day the LORD will punish the host of heaven and the kings of the earth; they will be gathered as prisoners in a pit, and after many days punished. Then the moon will be confounded and the sun ashamed, for the LORD of hosts reigns on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, and his glory will be before his elders. The unit stays with historic remnant and Zion in the Assyria–Babylon age, the LORD of hosts reigning—as Wesley and Calvin actually read in Christ—not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 24:14

14They lift up their voices, they sing for joy; over the majesty of the LORD they shout from the west.

Arminian

Wesley: They—the remnant shall sing for the glorious power and goodness of God, manifested in their deliverance. The sea—from the isles of the sea; from those parts beyond the sea into which they were carried captive. Shared: remnant songs from the west.

Arminian pastoral: historic captives shouting the LORD’s majesty, not a newspaper choir. The gleaning of v. 13 now sings.

Reformed

Calvin: they shall lift up their voice. The small number of the godly left out of an abundant vintage will nevertheless rejoice so loud that it will be heard in the most distant countries. This was done by the preaching of the gospel. The remnant’s shout is still that vintage’s leftover, not a later headline.

Reformed pastoral: west-shouting is still the remnant’s. Shared: sing for joy over the LORD’s majesty. Historic deliverance, then gospel reach.

Isaiah 24:15

15Therefore in the east give glory to the LORD; in the coastlands of the sea, give glory to the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.

Arminian

Wesley: In distress—when you are in the furnace of affliction. In the isles—in remote countries, beyond the sea, which in scripture are commonly called isles. Shared: east and coastlands glorify the God of Israel.

Arminian pastoral: furnace-glory is still that remnant’s. Historic isles of captivity, not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: wherefore glorify Jehovah in the valleys; in the isles of the sea glorify the name of the Lord God of Israel. God’s benefits ought to excite us to thanksgiving even in distant places. The God of Israel is named so the remnant will not forget whose name they bear.

Reformed note: coastland glory is still that name’s. Shared: give glory to the LORD. Historic remnant, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 24:16

16From the ends of the earth we hear songs of praise, of glory to the Righteous One. But I say, “I waste away, I waste away. Woe is me! For the traitors have betrayed, with betrayal the traitors have betrayed.”

Arminian

Wesley: From—from all the parts of the earth in which the Jews are or shall be. Songs—of joy and praise. Glory—or, glory be to the righteous. The Lord, the righteous one. But—in the midst of these joyful tidings, I discern something which gives me cause of lamentation. My leanness—I faint and pine away for grief. Treacherously—the Jews, who have been frequently guilty of great perfidiousness towards God, are now acting the same part. Even the Hebrew doctors expound this place of the perfidiousness of some Jews in the times of the Messiah. Shared: songs to the Righteous One; then the prophet’s woe over traitors.

Arminian pastoral: pity at treachery is not open theism. Historic Judah’s perfidy, and, as Wesley notes, some Jews in Messiah’s times—still not a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: from the uttermost part of the earth have we heard songs, even glory to the righteous. This verse contains two statements: distant praise, then the prophet’s grief. My leanness, my leanness, woe unto me: the treacherous dealers have dealt treacherously. In the midst of consolation he sees betrayal that will still waste the remnant’s peace. The Righteous One is the Lord; the traitors are those who break faith in that age.

Reformed pastoral: the double waste-away is still the seer’s. Shared: glory to the Righteous One; traitors betray. Historic remnant and historic treachery, not a newspaper.

Key contrast Both hear remnant songs to the Righteous One and a prophet’s woe over treachery—not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s Hebrew doctors: some Jews’ perfidy in the times of the Messiah, alongside Judah’s old faithlessness. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s two statements: distant gospel praise, then the seer’s leanness at treacherous dealers in that same history.

Isaiah 24:17

17Terror and the pit and the snare are upon you, O inhabitant of the earth!

Arminian

Wesley: The snare—great and various judgments, some actually inflicted, and others justly feared. Shared: terror, pit, snare on the inhabitant.

Arminian pastoral: the wordplay is historic judgment, not a later trap headline.

Reformed

Calvin: fear, and the pit, and the snare, are upon thee, O inhabitant of the earth. The three catch the fleeing man of v. 18. Inhabitant of the earth: those named in the summary of ch. 13–23, Judah first.

Reformed note: the snare is still that emptying’s. Shared: upon you, O inhabitant. Historic land, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 24:18

18He who flees at the sound of the terror shall fall into the pit, and he who climbs out of the pit shall be caught in the snare. For the windows of heaven are opened, and the foundations of the earth tremble.

Arminian

Wesley: Fleeth—upon the report of some terrible evil. The foundations—both heaven and earth conspire against him. He alludes to the deluge of waters which God poured down from heaven, and to the earthquakes which he often causes below. Shared: flight fails; windows and foundations.

Arminian pastoral: deluge-allusion is language for historic calamity, as Wesley said of ch. 34’s sky. Do not invent a millennial chart from opened windows.

Reformed

Calvin: he who fleeth from the noise of the fear shall fall into the pit; he that cometh up out of the midst of the pit shall be taken in the snare. The windows from on high are open, and the foundations of the earth do shake. Some think he alludes to the deluge; in my opinion the meaning is simpler: the wrath of God will be revealed above and below, heaven and earth armed to execute vengeance, that wherever they turn they behold destruction.

Reformed pastoral: no escape is still that day’s. Shared: terror, pit, snare. Historic judgment, not a newspaper.

Key contrast Both keep opened windows as historic wrath, not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s deluge and earthquake allusion: heaven and earth conspire. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s simpler sense: God arms height and depth so there is no escape; the deluge allusion is not required.

Isaiah 24:19

19The earth is utterly broken, the earth is split apart, the earth is violently shaken.

Arminian

Wesley: The earth—this is repeated again, to shew the dreadfulness and, certainty of these judgments, and to awaken the stupid Israelites. Shared: broken, split, shaken.

Arminian pastoral: repetition to wake historic Israel, not a later globe-quake story.

Reformed

Calvin: by breaking down is the earth broken down. He heightens punishments by various expressions. Careless men must be aroused; the style is adorned so sluggish minds may be stung. This doctrine ought to be limited to the wicked; the godly are not exempted from the shaking but are not destroyed with the reprobate.

Reformed note: the triple breaking is still that land’s. Shared: violently shaken. Historic summary, not a millennial map.

Isaiah 24:20

20The earth staggers like a drunken man; it sways like a hut; its transgression lies heavy upon it, and it falls, and will not rise again.

Arminian

Wesley: A tent—which is easily and commonly carried from place to place. Shared: stagger, hut, heavy transgression, fall without rising.

Arminian pastoral: the weight is guilt of v. 5. Historic fall, not a newspaper of later states. Will not rise: that order of pride does not recover.

Reformed

Calvin: the earth shall reel to and fro like a drunkard, and shall be removed like a tent; the transgression thereof shall be heavy upon it; and it shall fall, and not rise again. This does not mean that any change will take place in the situation of the earth; he means that the shaking is incurable for the wicked order he has described. Transgression is the weight.

Reformed pastoral: the hut-sway is still that guilt’s. Shared: falls and will not rise. Historic earth of the oracle, not a last-day decoder forced onto the verse.

Isaiah 24:21

21On that day the LORD will punish the host of heaven, in heaven, and the kings of the earth, on the earth.

Arminian

Wesley has no separate lemma here; his gathered of v. 22 still names the stroke: by God’s special providence, in order to their punishment; unbelieving Jews gathered at Jerusalem when Titus besieged them. Shared: the LORD punishes high ones and kings.

Arminian pastoral: host of heaven and kings are still that day’s powers, not a millennial camp fight. Historic rulers around Judah, then, as Wesley adds, later gathering under Titus—still not a newspaper of living politicians.

Reformed

Calvin: and it shall come to pass in that day, that the Lord shall punish the host of the high ones that are on high, and the kings of the earth upon the earth. This passage has tortured many commentators. Host of the high place: whether stars, angels, or earthly princes viewed as high, the Lord punishes what is high with the kings of the earth. The day is still the visitation in this summary, not a forced last-day scheme.

Reformed note: high ones and kings fall together. Shared: the LORD will punish. Historic powers, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 24:22

22They will be gathered together as prisoners in a pit; they will be shut up in a prison, and after many days they will be punished.

Arminian

Wesley: Gathered—by God’s special providence, in order to their punishment. And thus the unbelieving Jews were generally gathered together at Jerusalem, to their solemn feast, when Titus came and besieged, and destroyed them. Shut up—as malefactors, which are taken in several places, are usually brought to one common prison. After—after the apostate Jews shall have been shut up in unbelief, and in great tribulations for many ages together, they shall be convinced of their sin in crucifying the Messiah, and brought home to God and Christ by true repentance. Shared: pit, prison, after many days.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley’s after-many-days is repentance toward Christ, shared christology, not a millennial camp fight. Historic gathering and later unbelief, not a living headline.

Reformed

Calvin: they shall be gathered together as prisoners are gathered in the pit, and shall be shut up in the prison, and after many days shall they be visited. Afterwards they shall be visited is not simply a promise; it includes a threatening: God will punish without making haste. Visit also means to look upon, and thus the Prophet softens the threatening for the godly. After many days: intended to try the faith of the godly; we must wait patiently. This does not refer to all; God had determined to save but a small remnant.

Reformed pastoral: the pit is still that gathering’s. Shared: shut up; after many days. Historic kings, then consolation for the remnant—not a newspaper.

Key contrast Both hear prisoners in a pit and a looking after many days—historic judgment, not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s gathering of unbelieving Jews under Titus, then after ages of unbelief a bringing home to Christ. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s kings shut up, visitation delayed to subdue obstinacy and to try the godly remnant’s patience; visit can also be a father’s look.

Isaiah 24:23

23Then the moon will be confounded and the sun ashamed, for the LORD of hosts reigns on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, and his glory will be before his elders.

Arminian

Wesley: The sun—all earthly powers and glories shall be obscured with the far greater splendour of Christ, the king of kings, at whose feet even the kings of the earth shall fall down and worship. The Lord—the Messiah, who, tho’ man, yet is also God, and the Lord of hosts. Shall reign—shall come in the flesh, and set up his kingdom, first in Jerusalem, and afterward in all other nations. Before—before his ministers, who are in some sort the courtiers of the King of Glory. But the ancients are here put for the whole church, in whose name and for whose service they act. Shared: sun and moon dimmed; the LORD of hosts reigns on Zion; glory before elders.

Arminian close: shared christology with Calvin—Messiah, God and man, reigns. Historic Zion first, then the nations. Do not invent a pre/post/a-mil war from the elders. This is not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: the moon shall be confounded. Many commentators think the Prophet waxes still more wroth against the Jews, so that sun and moon are ashamed of their unbelief; this appears far from his design. I have no doubt that he continues the consolation: when the Lord shall visit his people and cleanse the Church, he will establish a kingdom so illustrious that it will darken the sun and stars by its brightness. When the Lord of hosts shall reign in Mount Zion: the complex phrase always denotes mercy and salvation. He speaks of the restoration of the Church, and hence it follows that it is only in Christ that those things are fulfilled. Before his elders glory: the chief part of the Church for the whole; he reigns so that we feel he is present. The glory of Christ alone must hold a high place.

Reformed close: sun ashamed is still Zion’s greater brightness. Shared with Wesley: the LORD of hosts is Christ; elders stand for the church. Historic Mount Zion, gospel kingdom—already enough without a millennial chart or a newspaper.

Key contrast Both hear the LORD of hosts reigning on historic Zion, sun and moon outshone, glory before the elders—shared christology, not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s Messiah come in the flesh, kingdom first in Jerusalem then in all nations, ancients as the whole church. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s consolation: restoration of the Church fulfilled only in Christ; he refuses the reading that sun and moon merely blush at Jewish unbelief.
