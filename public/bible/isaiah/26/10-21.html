Passage

Isaiah 26

Verses 10–21 · ESV · Voices

If favor is shown to the wicked, he does not learn righteousness; in the land of uprightness he deals corruptly and does not see the majesty of the LORD. Your hand is lifted up, but they do not see it; let the fire for your adversaries consume them. O LORD, you will ordain peace for us, for you have done for us all our works. Other lords besides you have ruled over us, but your name alone we remember. They are dead, they will not live; you have wiped out all remembrance of them. You have increased the nation; you have enlarged all the borders of the land. In distress they sought you. Like a pregnant woman we writhed; we have given birth to wind; we have accomplished no deliverance. Your dead shall live; their bodies shall rise; you who dwell in the dust, awake and sing. Come, my people, enter your chambers; hide until the fury has passed by. Behold, the LORD is coming out from his place to punish the inhabitants of the earth; the earth will disclose the blood shed on it. The unit stays with historic Judah under other lords, resurrection-hope, and a little hiding until fury passes—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 26:10

10If favor is shown to the wicked, he does not learn righteousness; in the land of uprightness he deals corruptly and does not see the majesty of the LORD.

Arminian

Wesley: Will not learn—this is the carriage of thy people; but the course of wicked men is directly contrary in all conditions: for if thou dost spare them, they will not accept of that gracious invitation to repentance. In the land—even in God’s church, and among his people, where righteousness is taught and practiced. Will not behold—tho’ God gives such plain discoveries of his majesty and glory, not only in his word, but also in works, and especially in this glorious work of his patience and mercy to wicked men, yet they will not acknowledge it. Shared: favor shown; righteousness not learned.

Arminian pastoral: the invitation to repent is real. Historic wicked in the land of uprightness, not a later headline. They might yet have seen the majesty.

Reformed

Calvin: the wicked man will obtain favour, yet will he not learn righteousness. Isaiah contrasts this with the remnant who learn by judgments (v. 9). In the land of uprightness he will deal unjustly, and will not behold the majesty of the Lord. Spare him, and he still corrupts the land where righteousness is taught.

Reformed pastoral: unseen majesty is still that refusal’s. Shared: favor; no learning. Historic land of Judah, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 26:11

11O LORD, your hand is lifted up, but they do not see it. Let them see your zeal for your people, and be ashamed. Let the fire for your adversaries consume them.

Arminian

Wesley: Will not see—and they are guilty of the same obstinate blindness when thou dost smite and punish them, which is commonly signified by lifting up the hand. They shall see—they shall know that by sad experience, which they would not learn by easier ways. These—such fire or wrath as thou usest to pour forth upon thine implacable enemies. Shared: hand lifted; they do not see; fire for adversaries.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet see by the harder way. Historic adversaries of Judah, not a living politician. Do not invent open theism from “they do not see.”

Reformed

Calvin: O Jehovah, though thy hand is lifted up, they will not see. This explains the former statement: the same blindness under blows as under favor. But they shall see, and be ashamed for their envy at the people; the fire of thine enemies shall devour them. Seeing comes too late when fire is the lesson.

Reformed note: the lifted hand is still the Lord’s. Shared: they do not see; let fire consume. Historic zeal for his people, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 26:12

12O LORD, you will ordain peace for us, for you have indeed done for us all our works.

Arminian

Wesley: Our works—all the good works done by us, are the effects of thy grace. Shared: peace ordained; all our works are his.

Arminian pastoral: grace does the works; they might yet walk in them. Historic remnant peace, not a later treaty headline. Wesley and Calvin share that good works are God’s doing.

Reformed

Calvin: O Jehovah, thou wilt ordain peace for us: for thou also hast wrought all our works in us. This statement tends to the consolation of the godly. Peace is his appointment; even what we call our works he has wrought. The remnant does not boast a self-made city.

Reformed pastoral: ordained peace is still his. Shared: all our works done by thee. Historic Judah, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 26:13

13O LORD our God, other lords besides you have ruled over us, but your name alone we bring to remembrance.

Arminian

Wesley: Other lords—others besides thee, and besides those governors who have been set up by thee, even foreign and heathen lords. By thee—by thy favour and help. Will we—celebrate thy praise. Shared: other lords ruled; your name alone we remember.

Arminian pastoral: historic foreign lords over Judah—Assyria, then Babylon—not a modern occupation story. The name remembered is the LORD’s.

Reformed

Calvin: O Lord our God, other lords besides thee have had dominion over us: but by thee only will we make mention of thy name. This verse contains a complaint of the saints, that they were oppressed by tyrants. Other lords are those not appointed as fathers, foreign dominion. Yet they will mention his name alone, which v. 8 desired.

Reformed note: the other lords are still that bondage’s. Shared: your name alone. Historic Judah, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 26:14

14They are dead, they will not live; they are shades, they will not arise; to that end you have visited them with destruction and wiped out all remembrance of them.

Arminian

Wesley: Rise—those tyrants are destroyed; they shall never live or rise again to molest us. Shared: dead lords will not rise; remembrance wiped out.

Arminian pastoral: v. 19 will contrast the church’s dead. Historic tyrants, not a later dictator. Do not stretch “will not arise” into a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: the dead shall not live; the deceased shall not rise. The Prophet again speaks of the unhappy end of the tyrants. Therefore hast thou visited and destroyed them, and made all their memory to perish. They are not the dead of v. 19. Memory perishes because the Lord visited them.

Reformed pastoral: shades that will not arise are still those lords’. Shared: visited with destruction. Historic oppressors, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 26:15

15But you have increased the nation, O LORD, you have increased the nation; you are glorified; you have enlarged all the borders of the land.

Arminian

Wesley: The nation—this nation seems to be the people of Israel. Removed—thou hast removed thy people out of their own land, and suffered them to be carried captive to the ends of the earth. Shared: nation added; borders spoken.

Arminian pastoral: increase and removal can stand together in historic Israel’s story. Do not turn enlarged borders into a twenty-first-century map.

Reformed

Calvin: thou hast added to the nation, O Lord; thou hast added to the nation; thou art glorified. This verse is explained in various ways. Some think it is a complaint of scattering; the doubling of added to the nation may praise increase after exile, or confess that he multiplied them even in removing them far to the ends of the earth. In either reading the glory is his, not the lofty city’s.

Reformed note: the nation is still Israel’s. Shared: you are glorified. Historic people, not a millennial decoder.

Key contrast Both hear the LORD dealing with historic Israel’s nation and borders, not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s removal: carried captive to the ends of the earth. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: the verse is read variously as increase after exile or as multiplying even in scattering; either way the Lord is glorified.

Isaiah 26:16

16O LORD, in distress they sought you; they poured out a whispered prayer when your discipline was upon them.

Arminian

Wesley: They—thy people. Visited—come into thy presence, with their prayers and supplications. Shared: distress; whispered prayer; discipline upon them.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet seek in the furnace. Historic chastening of Judah, not a later therapy headline. Do not invent open theism from a whisper; he already ordained peace.

Reformed

Calvin: O Jehovah, in tribulation they have visited thee; they poured out a prayer when thy chastening was upon them. This might be explained as relating to hypocrites; I take it of the godly, whose whispered prayer is poured when the rod is on them. Chastening drives the remnant to the name they remember.

Reformed pastoral: the whisper is still that rod’s. Shared: sought you in distress. Historic people, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 26:17

17Like a pregnant woman who writhes and cries out in her pangs when she is near to giving birth, so were we because of you, O LORD;

Arminian

Wesley: Like—such was our anguish and danger. Shared: pangs because of you, O LORD.

Arminian pastoral: the simile is historic Judah’s anguish, not a claim that God does not know the end of the labor. Do not build open theism from writhing.

Reformed

Calvin: like as a woman with child, that draweth near the time of her delivery, is in pain, and crieth out in her pangs; so have we been in thy sight, O Lord. Two things ought chiefly to be remarked. First, the Church’s anguish is real before him. The pangs are because of you: his judgments, not chance.

Reformed note: the writhing is still that sight’s. Shared: pangs like childbirth. Historic remnant, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 26:18

18we were pregnant, we writhed, but we have given birth to wind. We have accomplished no deliverance in the earth, and the inhabitants of the world have not fallen.

Arminian

Wesley: We—we have had the torment of a woman in child-bearing, but not the comfort of a living child, for we have brought forth nothing but wind; all our labours and hopes were unsuccessful. The world—the Assyrians, or our other enemies. Shared: birth to wind; no deliverance we accomplished.

Arminian pastoral: their labor failed; his dead shall live in v. 19. Historic Assyria not fallen by their hand, not a modern war story.

Reformed

Calvin: we have as it were brought forth wind. The second thing to be remarked: they had the pain without the fruit. We have not wrought any deliverance in the earth; neither have the inhabitants of the world fallen. What they could not bring forth, he will in v. 19.

Reformed pastoral: wind-birth is still their failed works. Shared: no deliverance in the earth. Historic enemies standing, until he acts.

Isaiah 26:19

19Your dead shall live; their bodies shall rise. You who dwell in the dust, awake and sing for joy! For your dew is a dew of light, and the earth will give birth to the dead.

Arminian

Wesley: Thy—the prophet here turns his speech to God’s people, and gives them a cordial in their distress. Thy dead men are not like those, ver. 14, for they shall not live; but thine shall live. You shall be delivered from all your fears and dangers. My dead body—as I myself, who am one of these dead men, shall live again; you shall be delivered together with me. Awake—out of your sleep, even the sleep of death, you that are dead and buried in the dust. Thy dew—the favour and blessing of God upon thee. The dew—which makes them grow and flourish. Shared: your dead shall live; dust-dwellers awake; dew of light.

Arminian pastoral: contrast with v. 14’s tyrants. Deliverance and resurrection-speech for historic Judah, not a millennial camp fight. Wesley’s “I myself shall live again” is hope, not open theism.

Reformed

Calvin: thy dead men shall live, together with my dead body shall they arise. Isaiah continues the same consolation. Awake and sing, ye that dwell in dust: for thy dew is as the dew of herbs, and the earth shall cast out the dead. The church’s dead are not the shades of v. 14. Dust-sleepers are called to sing; dew from God raises what wind-birth could not.

Reformed pastoral: rising bodies are still the remnant’s hope. Shared: awake and sing. Historic people under other lords, resurrection-comfort in the same God who swallowed death in 25:8—not a newspaper, not a millennial chart.

Isaiah 26:20

20Come, my people, enter your chambers, and shut your doors behind you; hide yourselves for a little while until the fury has passed by.

Arminian

Wesley: Shut thy doors—withdraw thyself from the world, and pour out thy prayers to God in thy closet. Indignation—the dreadful effects of God’s anger, mentioned in the following verse. Shared: chambers; shut doors; a little while; fury passing.

Arminian pastoral: hide is prayer, not despair. Historic people waiting out indignation, not a twenty-first-century bunker. A little while: counted by him, not open theism.

Reformed

Calvin: come, my people, enter thou into thy chambers, and shut thy doors about thee: hide thyself as it were for a little moment, until the indignation be overpast. He exhorts the children of God to exercise patience. Chambers: withdraw and pray; the fury is short for them though heavy on the earth of v. 21.

Reformed note: the little while is still that indignation’s. Shared: shut doors; wait. Historic remnant, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 26:21

21For behold, the LORD is coming out from his place to punish the inhabitants of the earth for their iniquity, and the earth will disclose the blood shed on it, and will no more cover its slain.

Arminian

Wesley: Cometh—cometh down from heaven. To punish—all the enemies of God, and of his people. Her slain—the innocent blood which hath been spilled upon the earth shall be brought to light, and severely revenged upon the murderers. Shared: the LORD comes out; earth discloses blood; slain uncovered.

Arminian close: historic blood of the remnant, not a later crime blotter. The coming-out is the same Lord who ordained peace. Do not stretch “inhabitants of the earth” into a newspaper of living states.

Reformed

Calvin: for, behold, Jehovah cometh out of his place to punish the inhabitants of the earth for their iniquity: the earth also shall disclose her blood, and shall no more cover her slain. It is a grievous temptation when God seems to rest; he comes out to punish. Blood hidden in the land of the oracles will not stay covered. The chambers of v. 20 are enough for a little while.

Reformed close: disclosed blood is still that earth’s. Shared: punish iniquity; slain uncovered. Historic Assyria–Babylon age and the remnant’s hiding—already enough without a later headline.
