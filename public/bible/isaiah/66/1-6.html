Passage

Isaiah 66

Verses 1–6 · ESV · Voices

Heaven is his throne and earth his footstool; the house they would build cannot contain him. He looks to the humble, contrite, and those who tremble at his word. Formal sacrifice without that heart is like killing a man or blessing an idol. He called and no one answered. Those who tremble are hated and cast out by brothers who say, Let the Lord be glorified; a voice from the temple renders recompense. Historic Judah’s temple first, then the gospel church Stephen reads here (Acts 7:49)—not a newspaper and not a millennial shrine fight.

Isaiah 66:1

1Thus says the Lord: "Heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool; what is the house that you would build for me, and what is the place of my rest?

Arminian

Wesley: the highest heaven is the place where I shew myself in my majesty (Ps 11:4; 103:19; Matt 5:34). Hence we are taught to pray, Our Father which art in heaven. The earth is my footstool (Matt 5:35). Can there be an house built that will contain me? Where is the place wherein I can be said to rest in a proper sense?

Arminian throne: the Father in heaven is not boxed in the temple. Historic house first; Stephen (Acts 7:49) then the gospel church. Shared christology with Calvin: heaven his throne; he is a Spirit. Not a millennial temple chart.

Reformed

Calvin: they flattered themselves in sins under confidence in sacrifices and the temple. This confidence is diabolical; they frame an idol when they shut him up in the temple. Heaven is his throne so we may raise our minds above the world; yet he protests he is with us. Worship must be spiritual to correspond to his nature (John 4:24). Stephen (Acts 7:49) and Paul (Acts 17:24) apply it: God dwells not in temples made with hands.

Reformed throne: he is not confined; ceremonies without the heart are a mockery. Same question, Where is the house?; the rest named in Psalm 132 is not a prison for his majesty.

Isaiah 66:2

2All these things my hand has made, and so all these things came to be, declares the Lord. But this is the one to whom I will look: he who is humble and contrite in spirit and trembles at my word.

Arminian

Wesley: they were not only made by God, but kept in being by him. Look—yet God will look with a favourable eye to him that hath a broken and contrite spirit, whose heart is subdued to the will of God, and who is poor and low in his own eyes. Trembleth—who trembleth when he hears God’s threatening words, and hears every revelation of his will with reverence.

Arminian look: the contrite may still be looked on; trembling at the word is real reverence, not terror without hope. Historic Judah; the gospel still looks to that man. Shared ground: humble, contrite, trembles at my word.

Reformed

Calvin: ceremonies are empty when men think they satisfy God by them. He made all these things; he has no need of external worship (Ps 50:10). He looks to the humble, contrasted with the splendor of ceremonies. Trembling at the word is the obedience of those who reverence him, not the terror of those who flee. All reverence we owe to God must be paid to his word. Though wretched in abasement, we are happy because the Lord looks upon us.

Reformed look: inward purity and the word, not the array of the house. Same contrite spirit; faith yields obedience and lays aside lofty claims.

Isaiah 66:3

3"He who slaughters an ox is like one who kills a man; he who sacrifices a lamb, like one who breaks a dog's neck; he who presents a grain offering, like one who offers pig's blood; he who makes a memorial offering of frankincense, like one who blesses an idol. These have chosen their own ways, and their soul delights in their abominations;

Arminian

Wesley: Solomon, Prov. 15:8, comments: the sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the Lord. The prophet is not reflecting upon idolatrous worship, but formal worship: those who in a formality worshipped the true God by acts which he had appointed. These services were no more acceptable than murder, idolatry, or the most horrid profanation of his name. Own ways—they live as they lust. Delight—they take pleasure in their sins.

Arminian form: appointed acts, wicked hearts. Historic altar; the church still refuses that delight. Shared ground with Calvin: they have chosen their own ways.

Reformed

Calvin: all the sacrifices of the nation are of no value when they think God is satisfied with a deceitful appearance. He does not absolutely condemn sacrifices, but their blemishes. No sacrifices are acceptable but those which proceed from a pure heart. They mixed Gentile ceremonies with the Law. Streams from a muddy fountain cannot be clean. Choice and desire reveal obstinacy: they devoted their heart to what opposed his commandments.

Reformed form: the thing appointed, the heart refused. Same ox like a slain man; intention does not make invented or hollow worship clean.

Isaiah 66:4

4I also will choose harsh treatment for them and bring their fears upon them, because when I called, no one answered, when I spoke, they did not listen; but they did what was evil in my eyes and chose that in which I did not delight."

Arminian

Wesley: they have chosen to mock and delude me; I will chuse to suffer them to delude themselves; they have chosen to work wickedness, I will chuse the effect. Their fears—the things which they feared. Did not hear—God accounts that those do not hear who do not obey his will.

Arminian call: they could have answered; he called, they did not listen. Harsh treatment matches their choice. Not open theism: he is not surprised they refused. Historic Judah; the church still hears when I called. Shared ground: they chose that in which I did not delight.

Reformed

Calvin: I will choose their delusions—I will scatter the clouds they spread, so that their delusions shall be visible. I will bring upon them their terror. Because I called, and ye did not answer: they sinned through deliberate malice rather than ignorance; those to whom the word is proclaimed are condemned more severely. To hear is to obey. Before mine eyes they sinned as if to provoke him. He condemns foolish devotions, not only gross crimes.

Reformed call: the word was near; they shut the door. Same unanswered calling as 65:12; what they choose, he abhors.

Key contrast Both refuse open theism: he called, they did not answer. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s permission: I will suffer them to delude themselves, and choose the effect of the wickedness they chose. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s judicial exposing of delusions and bringing their terror, because they despised the word on purpose. Shared ground: when I spoke, they did not listen; they chose what he did not delight in.

Isaiah 66:5

5Hear the word of the Lord, you who tremble at his word: "Your brothers who hate you and cast you out for my name's sake have said, 'Let the Lord be glorified, that we may see your joy'; but it is they who shall be put to shame.

Arminian

Wesley: you—that truly fear God. Your brethren—by nation. Cast you out—of their synagogues, of their city, and some of you out of the world. For my name’s sake—for your adherence to my law. Glorified—thinking they did God good service (John 16:2).

Arminian tremble: the small company that fears the word, hated by national brothers. Historic synagogue first; gospel church next. Shared ground: they shall be put to shame. Not a newspaper faction.

Reformed

Calvin: he addresses the true worshippers, a small number, as formerly “bind the testimony among my disciples” (Isa 8:16). The mark is trembling at the word. He calls the mockers brethren by concession; this internal war with hypocrites is not new. Casting you out is tyranny of the many. Let Jehovah be glorified is their sneer or false confidence; he will be seen to your joy, and they shall blush.

Reformed tremble: armed against domestic foes. Same cast-out remnant; God appears for believers’ advantage and the destruction of those who laughed.

Isaiah 66:6

6"The sound of an uproar from the city! A sound from the temple! The sound of the Lord, rendering recompense to his enemies!

Arminian

Wesley: the expression of a prophetical ecstasy, as if he said, I hear a sad and affrighting noise; it comes not from the city only, but from the temple, wherein these formalists have so much gloried. There is a noise of soldiers slaying, and of the poor people shrieking. Of the Lord—not in thunder, but that rendereth recompence to his enemies. Thus he seems to express the destruction of the Jews by the Roman armies, as a thing at that time doing.

Arminian voice: historic temple, later Rome’s stroke, then any house that gloried in form. Shared ground with Calvin: recompense from the temple itself. Not a millennial battle map.

Reformed

Calvin: God hath not threatened in vain. It may relate to Babylon, whose destruction was the Church’s deliverance, but he is more favorable to domestic foes nourished in the Church’s bosom. From the temple, their lurking-place of false confidence, his voice shall come forth. A mitigation: the same terror may not discourage the believing servants of God.

Reformed voice: an armed word where the mild word was scorned. Same uproar from the city; hypocrites shall hear a tumultuous voice, and believers receive the fruit of patience.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on heaven his throne, the contrite to whom he looks, and a voice from the temple—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 66:1–6.

Arminian Notable voices

“The heaven is the place where I shew myself in my majesty. Hence we are taught to pray, Our Father which art in heaven. Can there be an house built that will contain me? Yet God will look with a favourable eye to him that hath a broken and contrite spirit, who trembleth when he hears God’s threatening words, and hears every revelation of his will with reverence. Solomon, Prov. xv. 8, gives a full commentary: The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the Lord. The prophet is not reflecting upon idolatrous worship, but formal worship: upon those who in a formality worshipped the true God by acts which he had appointed. They have chosen to mock and delude me; I will chuse to suffer them to delude themselves. You—that truly fear God. Your brethren—by nation—that cast you out of their synagogues, for my name’s sake, thinking they did God good service (John 16:2). A voice from the temple: thus he seems to express the destruction of the Jews by the Roman armies.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 66:1–6)

Reformed Notable voices

“He shews that they grossly mock God who endeavor to serve him by outward ceremonies. Heaven is my throne: his majesty fills all things; he is so far from being shut up in the temple that he is not confined within any place. Stephen quotes this (Acts 7:49). I look to him who is humble and contrite in spirit and trembleth at my word. True godliness consists in having our senses brought into obedience; all the reverence we owe to God must be paid to his word. He that killeth an ox as if he slew a man: he does not absolutely condemn sacrifices, but the blemishes of them, when men think God is satisfied with a deceitful appearance. I also will choose their delusions, because when I called they did not answer. Hear, ye who tremble: he arms believers against domestic foes who cast them out. A voice from the temple: they shall not escape his hand, and even from the temple, their lurking-place of false confidence, his voice shall come forth.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 66:1–6)
