Passage

Isaiah 66

Verses 15–24 · ESV · Voices

The Lord comes in fire; by fire and sword he judges all flesh. Garden-purifiers who eat the abomination end together. He gathers nations and tongues to see his glory; survivors go to Tarshish, Pul, Lud, Tubal, Javan, and the coastlands; brothers come as an offering to the holy mountain; some he takes for priests. As the new heavens remain, so shall offspring and name; all flesh worships from sabbath to sabbath; they look on the rebel dead, worm and fire unquenched. Historic Judah and the nations first, then the gospel church and the last judgment—not a newspaper, and not an Arminian-versus-Reformed millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 66:15

15"For behold, the Lord will come in fire, and his chariots like the whirlwind, to render his anger in fury, and his rebuke with flames of fire.

Arminian

Wesley: with terrible judgments, or with fire in a proper sense, understanding it of the fire with which enemies consume places brought under their power. Whirlwind—with a sudden sweeping judgment. Fury—with fervour; for fury properly taken is not in God. Rebukes—they had contemned the rebukes of the law; now God will rebuke them with fire and sword.

Arminian fire: historic judgment first, then any day he comes. Not open theism: fury is fervour, not a passion he just acquired. Shared ground with Calvin: he comes in fire. Not a millennial battle chart.

Reformed

Calvin: that believers may not turn aside when the wicked laugh and grow insolent. He comforts the good that they are under God’s protection. He includes the last judgment along with temporal punishments that were forerunners of eternal destruction. This began in the Babylonian carrying away, then in vengeance on Gentile foes, and lastly he will come in fire at the last day. Metaphors help us comprehend what we otherwise could not; do not dispute the refined nature of that fire.

Reformed fire: temporal strokes and the last day in one coming. Same whirlwind chariots; hypocrites are especially in view.

Isaiah 66:16

16For by fire will the Lord enter into judgment, and by his sword, with all flesh; and those slain by the Lord shall be many.

Arminian

Wesley: God at first pleads with sinners by words, but if he cannot so prevail, he will plead with them in a way by which he will overcome; by fire, pestilence and blood. All flesh—thus he threatens to do with all the wicked Jews. The slain—those whom God should cause to be slain.

Arminian plea: words first, then fire. They could have been prevailed with. Historic wicked Judah; all flesh next. Shared ground: the slain of the Lord shall be many.

Reformed

Calvin: he confirms the former statement; this judgment will be dreadful. Fire and sword convey the same meaning. Many shall be the slain of Jehovah—a prodigious mass of corpses. Ungodliness reigned; believers were tempted by the prosperity of the wicked. The more ungodliness abounds, the more the wrath is kindled; multitude and conspiracy shall not hinder him.

Reformed sword: numbers do not restrain his hand. Same many slain; do not envy the prosperous wicked.

Isaiah 66:17

17"Those who sanctify and purify themselves to go into the gardens, following one in the midst, eating pig's flesh and the abomination and mice, shall come to an end together, declares the Lord.

Arminian

Wesley: gardens—in which they worshipped idols. In the midst—behind one of the trees, or one by one behind the trees. The abominations—all those beasts forbidden the Jews for meat. God will not only destroy gross idolaters, but all those who make no conscience of yielding obedience to the law of God in things that seemed to them of a minute nature.

Arminian gardens: historic idol groves and unclean meat (65:3–4). Shared ground with Calvin: they end together. Not a newspaper cult.

Reformed

Calvin: he describes the enemies whose anger will be kindled—false and degenerate Jews. First hypocrites who assumed disguises of holiness; then those who openly despised God. Behind one in the midst: some think a laver; others that every one chose a God and a tree. The statement is general: both open wickedness and covered.

Reformed gardens: hypocrites and the openly profane in one end. Same pig’s flesh; pretended purity in the grove is still revolt.

Isaiah 66:18

18"For I know their works and their thoughts, and the time is coming to gather all nations and tongues. And they shall come and shall see my glory,

Arminian

Wesley: it shall come to pass that I will cast them off, and then I will gather all nations. My glory—my oracles, my ordinances, which hitherto have been locked up in the church of the Jews, and been their glory, shall be published to the Gentiles.

Arminian gather: after casting off, the nations see what Judah locked up. Historic then gospel (Rom 10). Not open theism: he already knows works and thoughts. Shared ground: they shall come and see my glory.

Reformed

Calvin: he confirms that punishment shall be executed on all the ungodly; he sees and observes their works; one day none will be concealed. The time is come to assemble all nations, that he may cast off hypocrites and adopt a people from among the nations. The Jews despised other nations as unholy; he will adopt those nations. God is not confined to any people. They shall see my glory—the Gentiles shall behold the grace formerly the Jews’ special privilege.

Reformed gather: empty possession left for the nations (Rom 10:19). Same coming to see his glory; unbelief does not bind him to the old tenants.

Isaiah 66:19

19and I will set a sign among them. And from them I will send survivors to the nations, to Tarshish, Pul, and Lud, who draw the bow, to Tubal and Javan, to the coastlands far away, that have not heard my fame or seen my glory. And they shall declare my glory among the nations.

Arminian

Wesley: a sign—by this may be understood Christ (Luke 2:34), or the ministry of the word attended with miracles; these were set up among the Jews first, then among the Gentiles. Afar off—to all the quarters of the world. This was eminently made good after the destruction of Jerusalem, when the believers among the Jews, as well as the apostles, went about publishing the gospel to all people, which was declaring the Lord’s glory.

Arminian sign: Christ and the preached word, survivors as missionaries after 70. Shared christology: they declare my glory among the nations. Not a millennial navy to Tarshish.

Reformed

Calvin: I will place in them a sign—God seals his own people that they may be safe, as at the departure from Egypt (Ex 12:13; Rev 7:3). None can escape except the elect on whom he has impressed his mark. From that reserved band some shall be his heralds to celebrate his name among the Gentiles. Tarshish, Pul, Lud, Tubal, Javan, and the islands are far countries plunged in ignorance; the knowledge of God shall be spread throughout the whole world. Nations is emphatic: he was known to not more than one people, but now to all.

Reformed sign: sealed remnant as heralds. Same coastlands that had not heard; the doctrine of salvation spread by a few.

Key contrast Both refuse a millennial map of Tarshish. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s sign as Christ (Luke 2:34) or the word with miracles, and Jewish believers plus apostles declaring glory after Jerusalem’s fall. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s sealing mark on the elect, like Passover, from whom reserved heralds are sent. Shared ground: survivors declare my glory among nations that had not heard my fame.

Isaiah 66:20

20And they shall bring all your brothers from all the nations as an offering to the Lord, on horses and in chariots and in litters and on mules and on dromedaries, to my holy mountain Jerusalem, says the Lord, just as the Israelites bring their grain offering in a clean vessel to the house of the Lord.

Arminian

Wesley: your brethren—those who are the children of Abraham, not considered as the father of the Jewish nation only, but as the father of many nations, and as the father of the faithful, and so are your brethren, shall be brought out of all nations for an offering to the Lord. Holy mountain—they shall be brought into the church, which began at Jerusalem. They shall come with as much joy and sincerity as the godly Jews bringing offerings in clean vessels.

Arminian offering: Abraham’s wider family, the church from all nations. Historic mountain first; gospel house next. Shared ground: brothers from all nations as an offering.

Reformed

Calvin: priests whom he appointed shall not labor in vain. Brethren—those who formerly were strangers; the new relation arises from faith. Foreign nations were ingrafted into Abraham’s family; not all flesh-descendants are children (Rom 9:7). The time he became father of many nations was when God adopted the Gentiles. Holy mountain is accommodation to that period; now the temple is everywhere (1 Tim 2:8). The apostles slew men by the gospel that they might be a living sacrifice (Rom 15:16). Horses and chariots: nothing shall hinder God from gathering his Church.

Reformed offering: living sacrifices, worldwide house. Same brothers on every conveyance; the Lord has all means.

Isaiah 66:21

21And some of them also I will take for priests and for Levites, says the Lord.

Arminian

Wesley: God will find among the converted Gentiles those who though they are not of the tribe of Levi, yet shall do the true work of priests and Levites.

Arminian priests: Gentile ministers doing Levi’s true work. Historic priesthood first; gospel royal priesthood next (1 Pet 2:9). Shared ground with Calvin: some from the nations taken for priests. Not a millennial temple staff.

Reformed

Calvin: the Gentiles shall not only be adopted but elevated to the highest honor. The priesthood under Christ is very different: under the Law one tribe; now all are admitted without distinction. He describes ministers and teachers chosen from the Gentiles—Luke, Timothy, and others—who offered spiritual sacrifices by the Gospel. Every person sacrifices when he devotes himself to God (Rom 12:1).

Reformed priests: gospel ministry and the whole consecrated people. Same taken from them; not a Levitical bloodline restored as a chart.

Isaiah 66:22

22"For as the new heavens and the new earth that I make shall remain before me, says the Lord, so shall your offspring and your name remain.

Arminian

Wesley: the new heavens—the new state of the church to be raised up under the Messiah. Remain—as I intend that shall abide, so there shall be a daily succession of true believers for the upholding of it.

Arminian remain: Messiah’s church, a succession of true believers. Historic then gospel; the same new heavens as 65:17, not a millennial camp fight. Shared christology: what he makes remains before him.

Reformed

Calvin: the restoration of the Church shall last for ever, lest any fear a second ruin. Two benefits: restoration and eternity. New heavens look to the reign of Christ, by whom all things have been renewed (Heb 8:13). Your seed and name remain: as God will establish the world that it may never perish, so the succession of the Church shall be perpetual. This must be referred to the inward renewal of man, not absolutely only to the last judgment; we begin with the deliverance by which Christ regenerates his people (2 Cor 5:17).

Reformed remain: Church perpetual under Christ’s reign, perfect at the last day. Same offspring and name; do not think he has changed his mind.

Isaiah 66:23

23From new moon to new moon, and from Sabbath to Sabbath, all flesh shall come to worship before me, declares the Lord.

Arminian

Wesley: in the gospel-church there shall be as constant and settled a course of worship (though of another nature) as ever was in the Jewish church. Christians are not bound to keep the Jewish sabbaths or new-moons. But New Testament worship is expressed by Old Testament phrases. The Jews were obliged to appear three times a year at Jerusalem; the gospel-church shall worship God from one sabbath to another.

Arminian worship: all flesh, continual, gospel not old moons. Historic feasts as language; church as fulfillment. Shared ground: all flesh shall come to worship. Not a millennial calendar.

Reformed

Calvin: he points out the difference between spiritual worship under Christ and carnal worship under the Law. Under Christ a constant solemnity; no stated days on which we must go to Jerusalem; our oblations are continued from day to day. Pure sacrifices daily (1 Pet 2:5), spiritual, that we may adore with a pure worship (John 4:24). Contrast between Sabbaths under the Law and the perpetual Sabbath we have (Heb 4:9).

Reformed worship: unceasing spiritual offering, not a travel feast. Same all flesh before him; the phrases are borrowed, the thing is new.

Isaiah 66:24

24"And they shall go out and look on the dead bodies of the men who have rebelled against me. For their worm shall not die, their fire shall not be quenched, and they shall be an abhorrence to all flesh."

Arminian

Wesley’s verse notes end at v. 23; the chapter heading names this verse the eternal punishment of the wicked. They go out and look on rebels; worm and fire are not quenched; they are an abhorrence to all flesh.

Arminian close: historic slain first (v. 16), then the last unquenched end of those who rebelled. The church that worships (v. 23) still sees that warning. Shared ground with Calvin: rebels do not outlast his judgment. Not a millennial camp fight, and not a living politician’s grave.

Reformed

Calvin: we must not attempt subtle interpretations; he simply informs the adopted that they shall see around them the dreadful vengeance of God. Not that the slaughter is in the assembly of believers; he threatens the reprobate that the godly may keep themselves in the fear of God. Fire is metaphorical, as is the worm: the wicked shall have a bad conscience as an executioner to torment them without end, a torment greater than all others, and thus consumed they do not die. They shall be an abhorrence to all flesh—those who despised his glory shall be loaded with every reproach.

Reformed close: unending torment of rebels, figure for a reality that does not die. Same look on the dead; worship and warning stand together till the end.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on fire and sword, nations seeing his glory, and new heavens that remain—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 66:15–24.

Arminian Notable voices

“With fire—with terrible judgments, or with fire in a proper sense. Fury—with fervour; for fury properly taken is not in God. God at first pleads with sinners by words, but if he cannot so prevail, he will plead with them by fire, pestilence and blood. It shall come to pass that I will cast them off, and then I will gather all nations. My glory—my oracles, my ordinances, which hitherto have been locked up in the church of the Jews, shall be published to the Gentiles. A sign—Christ, Luke ii. 34, or the ministry of the word attended with miracles. They shall bring all your brethren, children of Abraham as father of many nations, into the church which began at Jerusalem. God will find among the converted Gentiles those who shall do the true work of priests and Levites. The new heavens—the new state of the church to be raised up under the Messiah. Remain—there shall be a daily succession of true believers. In the gospel-church there shall be as constant a course of worship as ever was in the Jewish church; New Testament worship is expressed by Old Testament phrases. The eternal punishment of the wicked.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 66:15–24)

Reformed Notable voices

“Jehovah will come in fire: that believers may not turn aside when worthless men laugh. He intends to include the last judgment along with temporal punishments. By fire and sword he confirms that this judgment will be dreadful. I will place in them a sign: none can escape except the elect, on whom the Lord has impressed his mark, as in Egypt. From the reserved he will send heralds among the Gentiles. They shall bring all your brethren: foreign nations ingrafted by faith into Abraham’s family. I will even take some of them for priests and Levites—the priesthood under Christ is very different; all are admitted without distinction. As the new heavens: restoration of the Church shall last for ever; he looks to the reign of Christ by whom all things have been renewed. From month to month, from Sabbath to Sabbath: under Christ a constant solemnity; our oblations are continued from day to day. They shall see the dead bodies: the wicked shall have a bad conscience as an executioner to torment them without end; the worm and the fire are metaphorical, yet the torment greater than all other torments, and they do not die.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 66:15–24)
