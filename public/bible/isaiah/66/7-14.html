Passage

Isaiah 66

Verses 7–14 · ESV · Voices

Before Zion is in labor she gives birth; a land is born in a day; the Lord who brings to birth will not shut the womb. Rejoice with Jerusalem, all who love her and mourn over her; nurse from her consoling breast. Peace flows like a river, the glory of the nations like a stream; he comforts as a mother comforts. Bones flourish; the hand of the Lord is known to his servants. Historic Zion after exile comes first; then the sudden gospel church. This is not a newspaper, and not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 66:7

7"Before she was in labor she gave birth; before her pain came upon her she delivered a son.

Arminian

Wesley: the whole verse is expressive of a great and sudden salvation which God would work for his church, like the delivery of a woman, and that of a man-child, before her travail, and without pain. Doubtless it refers to the coming of Christ, and the sudden propagation of the gospel.

Arminian birth: sudden gospel fruit, not a delayed millennial labor. Historic Zion first; then Christ’s and the apostles’ time. Shared christology with Calvin: this is the Church’s child. Not a newspaper birth announcement.

Reformed

Calvin: by the destruction of hypocrites he provides for believers’ safety. The whole Church is a woman; she shall obtain a numerous offspring though she appear childless. The work shall be sudden and unexpected; we must not judge restoration according to our own opinion. Women carry nine months; the Lord causes the child to see the light before pain is perceived. He claims the whole praise. A male—a noble, courageous offspring, regenerated by the Spirit of Christ (Rom 8:15).

Reformed birth: miracle that sets aside human industry. Same sudden son; the Church is mother, God the giver.

Isaiah 66:8

8Who has heard such a thing? Who has seen such things? Shall a land be born in one day? Shall a nation be brought forth in one moment? For as soon as Zion was in labor she brought forth her children.

Arminian

Wesley: as soon as the voice of the gospel put the church of the Jews into her travail, in Christ’s and the Apostles’ time, it presently brought forth.

Arminian moment: gospel voice, Jewish church in travail, children at once. Historic Zion; then the nations from that birth. Shared ground: who has heard such a thing? Not a millennial census.

Reformed

Calvin: he extols the greatness; believers shall not judge from the order of nature but from the grace of God. He does not mean the Church restored perfectly in a moment; the advancement is long-continued, and slow in the estimation of the flesh; yet even the beginning exceeds human capacity. The Church brings forth when she appeared barren, rendered fruitful by the preaching of the gospel. No nation ever came into the world in an instant; the Church, all at once and in more than one place, brings forth a vast number.

Reformed moment: gospel fruit that looks like a dream (Ps 126:1). Same one-day land; fulfilled in the return in some measure, far brighter in the gospel, and in our own times.

Isaiah 66:9

9Shall I bring to the point of birth and not cause to bring forth?" says the Lord; "shall I, who cause to bring forth, shut the womb?" says your God.

Arminian

Wesley: I, that in the ordinary course of my providence use to give a birth to women to whom I have given a power to conceive, shall I not give a birth to my people, whom by my promises I have made to conceive such expectations? Nor shall Zion once only bring forth; she shall go on, her womb shall not be shut, she shall every day bring forth more and more children, and my presence shall be with my church, to the end of the world.

Arminian womb: he will not stop mid-birth; the church keeps bearing. Promises make the expectation sincere. Shared ground with Calvin: he who brings to birth will bring forth. Not open theism: he is not unsure of the delivery.

Reformed

Calvin: lest good men be perplexed, consider his strength. Nothing so difficult in the eyes of men is not easily performed by him. In a woman’s bringing forth we see his wonderful power; shall he be less wonderful in enlarging the Church, the principal theater of his glory? It is wicked to limit his strength, as if he were less powerful when he acts by openly stretching out his hand than when he acts by natural means.

Reformed womb: the God of ordinary birth is not weaker in the Church’s birth. Same unshut womb; do not measure him by our slowness.

Isaiah 66:10

10"Rejoice with Jerusalem, and be glad for her, all you who love her; rejoice with her in joy, all you who mourn over her;

Arminian

Wesley: the unit’s joy is for those who love and have mourned over Jerusalem—the church’s friends, not her mockers (v. 5).

Arminian rejoice: lovers and mourners are invited; gladness is held out. Historic captivity’s grief first; gospel joy next. Shared ground: rejoice with Jerusalem. Not a millennial parade.

Reformed

Calvin: those who formerly were sad shall have a joyful condition. He has in view the captivity, during which believers groaned. He stimulates all who are moved by strong affection toward the Church and reckon nothing more desirable than her prosperity. None shall have a share but they who love her when she is contemptible (Ps 102:14). These promises extend from the return down to the reign of Christ and its full perfection.

Reformed rejoice: only the Church’s mourners taste this joy. Same be glad for her; the small company that loved her dust.

Isaiah 66:11

11that you may nurse and be satisfied from her consoling breast; that you may drink deeply with delight from her glorious abundance."

Arminian

Wesley: the gospel doctrine was their breasts of consolation. Her glory—Christ was the glory of the people of Israel, though he was also a light to the Gentiles.

Arminian breast: gospel doctrine and Christ himself the glory. Historic Jerusalem; the nations drink too. Shared christology with Calvin: consolation from the Church’s abundance is Christ. Not a millennial feast.

Reformed

Calvin: this verse is joined to the preceding. The occasion of joy is that the wretched condition of the Church shall be changed. By suck he compares all believers, whatever their age, to children, that they may remember their infirmity and be confirmed by the Lord’s strength. Consolations—what the Church has received and of which she makes her children partakers. Nothing can be more excellent than that ground of joy.

Reformed milk: children at the Church’s breast, fed with her received consolation. Same satisfied nursing; glory is hers as gift, not as source apart from God.

Isaiah 66:12

12For thus says the Lord: "Behold, I will extend peace to her like a river, and the glory of the nations like an overflowing stream; and you shall nurse, you shall be carried upon her hip, and bounced upon her knees.

Arminian

Wesley: it is plain this prophecy relates to a farther conversion of the Jews than hath been yet seen. Ye—ye Jews also. Her sides—the Gentiles were born upon the sides of Jerusalem and dandled upon her knees, as first hearing from the Apostles (who were members of the Jewish church) the glad tidings of salvation.

Arminian river: peace and nations’ glory, Jews also nursed, Gentiles from Jerusalem’s apostles. Historic then gospel; a farther Jewish turning under the same gospel, not a millennial camp fight against Calvin. Shared ground: peace like a river; the nations’ glory flows in.

Reformed

Calvin: he compares the children of God to infants carried in the arms and warmed in the bosom. He compares himself to a mother, whose love exceeds every other (Isa 49:15). Peace means prosperity. Glory of the Gentiles denotes every kind of riches for a happy life, as rivers run into the sea. Constant flowing: God is an inexhaustible fountain; his peace differs from the world’s, which quickly dries up. When the Church looks sad, remember he has rivers of peace.

Reformed river: he fondles us in his bosom; peace is from an unfailing fountain. Same overflowing nations; do not despair in wars.

Key contrast Both refuse a millennial timetable and a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s farther conversion of the Jews than yet seen, and Gentiles first hearing salvation from Jerusalem’s apostles. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s mother-God extending peace like a river to the Church, with the nations’ glory as her children’s supply. Shared ground: peace like a river; carried and bounced on Jerusalem’s knees; the gospel from Zion.

Isaiah 66:13

13As one whom his mother comforts, so I will comfort you; you shall be comforted in Jerusalem.

Arminian

Wesley: as one whom—that is, in the most tender and compassionate way.

Arminian comfort: he comforts as a mother; the place of comfort is Jerusalem. Historic city first; gospel church next. Shared ground with Calvin: I will comfort you. Not open theism: this is his chosen compassion, not a new feeling he just acquired.

Reformed

Calvin: it is wonderful that he dwells so largely on this renewal; he can neither express the greatness of the love God bears toward us, nor satisfy himself with speaking of it. I prefer reading that the restored Church shall gladden her children. This is not addressed to irreligious scorners, but to those who declare themselves her children. God is author of the joy; Jerusalem is his handmaid.

Reformed comfort: mother-love, Church as nurse, God as source. Same comforted in Jerusalem; only her children receive it.

Isaiah 66:14

14You shall see, and your heart shall rejoice; your bones shall flourish like the grass; and the hand of the Lord shall be known to his servants, and he shall show his indignation against his enemies."

Arminian

Wesley: the peace of the church and the propagation of the kingdom of Christ is always the cause of an heart rejoicing to such as fear God, so that they flourish like an herb in the spring. The hand—the power, protection, and influence of God.

Arminian close: seers rejoice; bones flourish; the hand known to servants, indignation to enemies. Historic then gospel. Shared close with Calvin: the hand of the Lord toward his servants. Not a millennial split of camps—servants versus enemies is already the text.

Reformed

Calvin: see denotes undoubted experience, that believers may not doubt the result. Bones shall flourish as grass—bones dried by a melancholy spirit (Prov 17:22) regain vigor; he alludes to captivity’s sadness. The hand of Jehovah shall be known toward his servants: at first he appeared to overlook them; when fine weather smiles there shall be a distinction between the good and the bad. If enemies have the superiority for a time, the time will come when the Lord will reveal himself.

Reformed close: hidden hand, then known. Same flourishing bones; wait through the apparent overlooking.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Zion’s sudden birth, Jerusalem’s consoling breast, and a mother’s comfort—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 66:7–14.

Arminian Notable voices

“Before—the whole verse is expressive of a great and sudden salvation, which God would work for his church, like the delivery of a woman, and that of a man-child, before her travail, and without pain. Doubtless it refers to the coming of Christ, and the sudden propagation of the gospel. As soon as the voice of the gospel put the church of the Jews into her travail, in Christ’s and the Apostles’ time, it presently brought forth. I, that in the ordinary course of my providence use to give a birth to women, shall I not give a birth to my people? She shall go on, her womb shall not be shut, to the end of the world. The gospel doctrine was their breasts of consolation. Christ was the glory of the people of Israel, though he was also a light to the Gentiles. It is plain this prophecy relates to a farther conversion of the Jews than hath been yet seen. Ye Jews also. The Gentiles were born upon the sides of Jerusalem, first hearing from the Apostles the glad tidings of salvation. As one whom—in the most tender and compassionate way. The peace of the church and the propagation of the kingdom of Christ is always the cause of an heart rejoicing to such as fear God.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 66:7–14)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Lord will punish in such a manner that by their destruction he will provide for the safety of believers. He compares the whole Church to a woman. She shall obtain a large and numerous offspring, though she appear for a time childless. This work of God shall be sudden and unexpected; he claims the whole praise for himself. She brought forth a male—a noble offspring, regenerated by the Spirit of Christ, not the spirit of timidity (Rom 8:15). Who hath heard such a thing? The Church all at once, and in more than one place, brings forth a vast number of children. Do I bring to the birth and not cause to bring forth? In enlarging the Church he is not less powerful than in the ordinary birth of a child. Rejoice ye with Jerusalem—none shall have a share but they who are prompted by a godly love of the Church. I cause peace to flow like a river; he compares himself to a mother, whose love exceeds every other. As a man whom his mother comforteth: he dwells on the greatness of the love which God bears toward us. Ye shall see, and your bones shall flourish as grass; the hand of Jehovah shall be known toward his servants.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 66:7–14)
