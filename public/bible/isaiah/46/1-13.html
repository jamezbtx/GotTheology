Isaiah 46

Verses 1–13 · ESV · Voices

After 45:14–25—the wealth of Egypt and the merchandise of Cush, and the Sabeans, men of stature, shall come over; Truly, you are a God who hides himself, O God of Israel, the Savior; Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth; every knee shall bow, every tongue swear; in the LORD all the offspring of Israel shall be justified and shall glory—the word turns: Bel bows down; Nebo stoops; their idols are on beasts and livestock, borne as burdens on weary beasts. Listen, O house of Jacob, borne from before birth; even to old age I will carry you; declaring the end from the beginning; calling a bird of prey from the east.

Isaiah 46:1

1Bel bows down; Nebo stoops; their idols are on beasts and livestock; these things you carry are borne as burdens on weary beasts.

Arminian

Bel bows down; Nebo stoops; their idols are on beasts and livestock; these things you carry are borne as burdens on weary beasts. Wesley: Bel—The chief idol of the Babylonians, called by profane historians Jupiter Belus. Boweth—As the Babylonians used to bow down to him to worship, so now he bows down to the victorious Persians. Nebo—Another of the famous idols, which used to deliver oracles. Their idols—Were taken and broken, and the materials of them, gold, silver, and brass, were carried upon beasts into Persia. Your carriages—O ye Persians, to whom he turns his speech.

Arminian hearing: Bel is Jupiter Belus, Nebo the oracle-idol; they bow to the Persians; gold, silver, and brass of the broken images are carried on beasts into Persia; “your carriages” turns the speech to the Persians. Shared loudly with Calvin: historic Bel and Nebo, Babylon’s gods laid low, Persians taking booty; the idols are burdens. Historic Babylon, not a newspaper, not a living politician, not a millennial camp fight. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Boweth” is his lemma for ESV “bows down”; “Your carriages” is his lemma for “these things you carry,” not a different verse. Prefer no keyContrast on 1: both camps take historic Bel/Nebo/Persians; Calvin’s farther look to Christ is the larger end, not a millennial fork. Match 45:20: wooden idols that cannot save. Match v. 2: they stoop together and go into captivity.

Reformed

Bel bows down; Nebo stoops. Calvin: Bel and Nebo are idols worshipped by the Babylonians, their chief patrons; Nebo perhaps inferior to Bel as Mercury to Jupiter. Fulfilled when Persians and Medes took Babylon by storm; idols taken as booty. Isaiah also looked farther to the coming of Christ, when all false worship falls (2 Cor 6:14)—the larger end, not a millennial camp fight. Eye on the captivity: Babylon’s gods laid low, Israel’s God rises.

Reformed hearing: historic Bel and Nebo, chief patrons of Babylon, taken as booty when Persians and Medes stormed the city; Christ’s coming is the farther look when false worship falls, not a millennial fork. Shared loudly with Wesley: historic Bel/Nebo/Persians; idols as burdens. Historic Babylon, not a newspaper, not a living politician. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “Bel and Nebo” names the ESV lemmas. Prefer no keyContrast on 1: both camps shout the same historic fall of Babylon’s gods; do not invent a millennial fight. Match 45:16: makers of idols confounded. Match v. 2: they cannot save the burden.

Isaiah 46:2

2They stoop; they bow down together; they cannot save the burden, but themselves go into captivity.

Arminian

They stoop; they bow down together; they cannot save the burden, but themselves go into captivity. Wesley: They—The Babylonians. Together—The Babylonians and their idols together, neither could help the other. Deliver—The Babylonians could not deliver their idols.

Arminian hearing: they are the Babylonians; together means Babylonians and idols, neither helping the other; they could not deliver their idols, and themselves go into captivity. Shared: they stoop together; they cannot save the burden; captivity. Historic Babylon, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Deliver” is his lemma for ESV “they cannot save the burden.” Prefer no keyContrast: both hear idols and worshippers falling together. Match v. 1: Bel and Nebo already bowing. Match v. 3: the God who carries Jacob, unlike these who must be carried.

Reformed

They stoop; they bow down together. Calvin: idols cannot withdraw from the burden; no strength or motion; contrast with the true God who needs nothing. Soul gone into captivity: ironic, they have neither soul nor understanding. The ark taken by the Philistines is not the same: the ark was a symbol leading up to God in heaven, not adored as God; he exposed the sanctuary to punish his people, as the prophets had said.

Reformed hearing: no strength, no motion; captivity of the “soul” is irony—they have neither soul nor understanding; the ark is not an idol. Shared with Wesley: they cannot save the burden; Babylonians and idols go down together. Historic Babylon, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “cannot withdraw from the burden” is his lemma for “they cannot save the burden.” Prefer no keyContrast: skip the ark-illustration rather than invent a Wesley fight. Match v. 1: Bel and Nebo. Match v. 3: the true God carries his people.

Isaiah 46:3

3“Listen to me, O house of Jacob, all the remnant of the house of Israel, who have been borne by me from before your birth, carried from the womb;

Arminian

Listen to me, O house of Jacob, all the remnant of the house of Israel, who have been borne by me from before your birth, carried from the womb. Wesley: Carried—Whom I have nourished, ever since you were a people, and came out of Egypt; and that as tenderly, as parents bring up their own children.

Arminian hearing: carried is nourished ever since they were a people and came out of Egypt, as tenderly as parents bring up their own children. Shared: Listen, O house of Jacob; borne from the womb; he carries his people, unlike Bel and Nebo who must be carried. Historic Israel, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Carried” is his lemma for “who have been borne by me…carried from the womb.” Match v. 1–2: idols are burdens on weary beasts. Match v. 4: even to old age I will carry you.

Reformed

Listen to me, O house of Jacob. Calvin: Hear me—vast difference: Babylon’s gods must be drawn on carts; the God of Israel carries his people like a mother who carries the child in her womb and then in her bosom. From the womb: not only nurse from birth but spiritually begot them (James 1:18) into new life. Remnant of the house of Israel: greater part alienated by revolt; hope of deliverance belonged only to a very small number. Hang on his mouth.

Reformed hearing: he carries like a mother in womb and bosom; not only from birth but spiritually begot (James 1:18); remnant—greater part alienated, hope only to a small number. Shared with Wesley: Listen, O house of Jacob; borne from the womb; he carries his people, unlike Bel and Nebo who must be carried. Historic Israel, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Calvin’s “from the womb” and “remnant of the house of Israel” name the ESV lemmas. Match v. 1–2: carts and beasts carry the idols. Match v. 4: even to old age, guardian till the end.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “Carried” as nourished ever since they were a people and came out of Egypt, as tenderly as parents bring up their own children. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take the carrying as a mother’s care in the womb and bosom; not only nurse from birth but spiritually begot them (James 1:18) into new life; remnant—the greater part alienated by revolt, hope of deliverance only to a very small number. Shared: Listen, O house of Jacob; borne from the womb; he carries his people, unlike Bel and Nebo who must be carried. Historic Israel, not a newspaper.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Bel bowing and Nebo stooping, idols borne as burdens on weary beasts, the house of Jacob carried from the womb even to old age, none like him, declaring the end from the beginning, a bird of prey from the east, and salvation put in Zion for Israel his glory—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 46:1–13.

Arminian Notable voices

“Bel—The chief idol of the Babylonians, called by profane historians Jupiter Belus. Boweth—As the Babylonians used to bow down to him to worship, so now he bows down to the victorious Persians. Nebo—Another of the famous idols, which used to deliver oracles. Their idols—Were taken and broken, and the materials of them, gold, silver, and brass, were carried upon beasts into Persia. Your carriages—O ye Persians, to whom he turns his speech. They—The Babylonians. Together—The Babylonians and their idols together, neither could help the other. Deliver—The Babylonians could not deliver their idols. Carried—Whom I have nourished, ever since you were a people, and came out of Egypt; and that as tenderly, as parents bring up their own children. Remove—He can stir neither hand nor foot to help his people. Bring it—Think of this again and again, ye who have been guilty of this foolish sin. Declaring—Foretelling from the beginning of the world, future events which should happen in succeeding ages, even to the end of the world. A bird—Cyrus, called a bird for his swiftness, and ravenous for his fierceness, and victoriousness over his enemies. I bring—Tho’ you are unrighteous, I will shew myself a righteous and faithful God, making good my promise of delivering you out of Babylon after seventy years. It—My work of saving you from the Babylonish captivity. Will place—I will bring my people to Zion, and save them from all their enemies. My glory—In whom I will once again glory as my people.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 46:1–13)

Reformed Notable voices

“Bel and Nebo: idols worshipped by the Babylonians, their chief patrons; Nebo perhaps inferior to Bel as Mercury to Jupiter. Fulfilled when Persians and Medes took Babylon by storm; idols taken as booty. Isaiah also looked farther to the coming of Christ, when all false worship falls (2 Cor 6:14). Idols cannot withdraw from the burden; no strength or motion; contrast with the true God who needs nothing. Soul gone into captivity: ironic, they have neither soul nor understanding. Ark taken by Philistines is not the same: ark was a symbol leading up to God in heaven, not adored as God. Hear me: vast difference—Babylon’s gods must be drawn on carts; the God of Israel carries his people like a mother who carries the child in her womb and then in her bosom. From the womb: not only nurse from birth but spiritually begot them (James 1:18) into new life. Remnant of the house of Israel: greater part alienated by revolt; hope of deliverance belonged only to a very small number. Even to old age: guardian till the end; he completes what he has begun (Ps 138:8; 22:10; 71:9). I am the same: always like himself, not only in essence but toward us. I have made and will carry: he created them to be his children, and will continue a father’s and mother’s care. To whom will ye liken me: they distrusted his power when they saw Babylon prosper, as if putting him on a level with idols. Two fathers of such gods: the rich man who lavishes gold and the workman who shapes it. They adore what they manufactured. Carry on the shoulder; it cannot answer or save. Remember this: addressed to the Jews as transgressors who, vanquished by a slight temptation, revolted. Return to the heart: forgetfulness of benefits was a sort of madness. Remember the former things: God has testified of himself by numerous proofs at all times. That I am God: he alone; if he admitted any companion, his throne would fall; either there is one God or there is none at all. Declaring from the beginning: taught by constant predictions. My counsel shall stand. Not only foreknowledge: prophecies have no certainty if the same God who declares events did not have the events themselves in his power. Calling a bird or a thought from the east: Cyrus shall come. It may be bird (true signification) or counsel/thought (Dan 2:14); Calvin prefers thought/decree but allows bird as metaphor of sudden approach. Man of my counsel: Cyrus executes the Lord’s decree. From the east / far country: no distance retards God’s work. I have spoken and will accomplish. Hear me, hardened in heart and far from righteousness: Jews almost overwhelmed yet proud; distresses render more rebellious; they refuse the medicine. I bring near my righteousness; it shall not be far off; salvation shall not tarry; I will place salvation in Zion, Israel my glory.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 46:1–13)
