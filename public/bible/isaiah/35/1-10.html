Passage

Isaiah 35

Verses 1–10 · ESV · Voices

The wilderness and the dry land shall be glad; the desert shall rejoice and blossom like the crocus; the glory of Lebanon, the majesty of Carmel and Sharon; they shall see the glory of the LORD; strengthen weak hands and feeble knees; say to the anxious heart, Be strong, fear not, your God will come with vengeance and save you; the eyes of the blind opened, the ears of the deaf unstopped; the lame leap like a deer, waters break forth in the wilderness; burning sand a pool, the haunt of jackals become reeds; a highway called the Way of Holiness, the unclean shall not pass, fools shall not go astray; no lion, the redeemed walk there; the ransomed of the LORD return to Zion with singing, sorrow and sighing flee. The unit stays with historic gladness after Edom’s waste in chapter 34—return from the waste, then gospel—as Wesley and Calvin actually read—not a newspaper, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 35:1

1The wilderness and the dry land shall be glad; the desert shall rejoice and blossom like the crocus.

Arminian

Wesley: the solitary place—Emmanuel's land, or the seat of God's church and people, which formerly was despised like a wilderness, and which the rage of their enemies had brought to desolation, shall flourish exceedingly. The glad wilderness is still that seat after Edom’s waste in chapter 34.

Arminian reading: they might yet have seen that desolate land glad. Historic Judah after Edom’s ruin is already named, then the church. This is not an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. The church is not to hunt another desert to make “the wilderness shall be glad” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: the wilderness and the solitary place shall be glad. Here the Prophet describes a wonderful change; for having in the former chapter described the destruction of Idumaea, and having said that it would be changed into a wilderness, he now promises, on the other hand, fertility to the wilderness. I pass by the dreams of the Jews, who apply all passages of this kind to the temporal reign of the Messiah, which they have contrived by their own imagination. The Lord began some kind of restoration when he brought his people out of Babylon; but that was only a slight foretaste, and therefore this passage must refer to the kingdom of Christ.

Reformed note: the gladness is still after historic Edom’s waste, then the return from Babylon as a taste, then Christ. Calvin will not let the wilderness become a newspaper or a chart of a temporal Messiah-reign.

Key contrast Both keep the glad wilderness as historic after Edom’s waste, then gospel, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads the solitary place as Emmanuel's land, the seat of God's church and people, despised like a wilderness and brought to desolation by the rage of their enemies, now to flourish exceedingly. Calvin reads the contrast with chapter 34’s Idumaea: fertility promised to the wilderness, Judea first then the world, Babylon’s return only a slight foretaste, the rest the kingdom of Christ begun here and completed at the last day. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight.

Isaiah 35:2

2It shall blossom abundantly and rejoice with joy and singing. The glory of Lebanon shall be given to it, the majesty of Carmel and Sharon. They shall see the glory of the LORD, the majesty of our God.

Arminian

Wesley: the excellency—the wilderness shall be as pleasant and fruitful as Lebanon, and Carmel, and Sharon. They—the inhabitants of the wilderness aforesaid. The glory—the glorious discoveries of God's power and goodness. Lebanon, Carmel, and Sharon are still that wilderness’s gift after the gladness of v. 1.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have seen those hills’ glory given to the waste. Historic fruit of that land is already named. The church is not to stretch “Lebanon” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: flourishing it shall flourish. The glory of Lebanon, the beauty of Carmel and Sharon. They shall see the glory of Jehovah. Till men learn to know God, they are barren and destitute of everything good; and consequently the beginning of our fertility is to be quickened by the presence of God, which cannot be without the inward perception of faith. Men might be satisfied with bread and wine and yet not acknowledge God to be the author of them, or cease to be wretched.

Reformed accent: Carmel and Sharon are still those historic hills’ beauty given to the waste. Seeing the LORD’s glory stays with that restored land, then with Christ, not a newspaper pasted onto “Lebanon.”

Isaiah 35:3

3Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble knees.

Arminian

Wesley: strengthen—ye ministers of God, comfort and encourage God's people, who are now ready to faint. Weak hands are still that people’s after the blossom of v. 2.

Arminian reading: they might yet have been comforted by that word. Historic Judah ready to faint after Edom’s waste is already named. Wesley and Calvin both give this charge to ministers; this is not an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. The church is not to hunt another pair of hands to make “strengthen the weak hands” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: strengthen ye the weak hands. The following verse shews that the whole of this passage relates to the ministers of the word; for he addresses the teachers of the Church, and enjoins them to exhort, arouse, and encourage weak men whose hearts are broken or cast down, that they may be rendered more firm and cheerful. So many tokens of God's anger could not do otherwise than fill even the strongest minds with alarm; the Prophet commands that they who are cast down and almost lifeless shall be enlivened.

Reformed note: the weak hands are still that flock’s after the waste. Calvin and Wesley both keep the charge with the teachers of the word, not a newspaper of later knees.

Isaiah 35:4

4Say to those who have an anxious heart, “Be strong; fear not! Behold, your God will come with vengeance, with the recompense of God. He will come and save you.”

Arminian

Wesley: your God—tho' he seems to be departed, he will come to you, and abide with you. He will shortly come in the flesh, to execute vengeance upon the enemies of God. The coming is still that God’s after the weak hands of v. 3.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have feared not, hearing that he would come and abide. Historic comfort to the faint after Edom’s waste is already named, then he comes in the flesh. This is not an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. The church is not to stretch “he will come and save you” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: say to them that are faint hearted. That strength is breathed into our hearts by God through his word, as by faith alone we stand and live; and therefore he adds the promise of grace yet to come. Behold, your God will come. If we are not supported by the word of the Lord, we must faint and despair. Nor is it superfluous that he adds your God; for if we do not know that he is our God, his approach will produce terror, instead of giving cause of joy. This vengeance is threatened against wicked men and enemies of the Church; to believers he will be a consolation, and he will come to save them. God is prepared and armed with vengeance, that believers may learn to lean on his aid, and not to fancy some deity unemployed in heaven.

Reformed accent: the coming is still the LORD’s for that flock, armed, not idle. Vengeance stays with the enemies of the Church after Edom’s waste, and salvation with the faint-hearted, not a newspaper pasted onto “behold, your God.”

Key contrast Both keep the coming as the LORD’s to save his people after the waste, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads: though he seems to be departed, he will come to you and abide with you; he will shortly come in the flesh, to execute vengeance upon the enemies of God. Calvin reads the strength as breathed in by God through his word; vengeance against the Church’s enemies joined to the godly’s salvation; God prepared and armed, not a deity unemployed in heaven. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight.

Isaiah 35:5

5Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped;

Arminian

Wesley: then—the poor Gentiles, who before were blind and deaf, shall now have the eyes and ears of their minds opened to see God's works, and to hear and receive his word. The opened eyes are still that “then” after God comes in v. 4.

Arminian reading: they might yet have heard and seen. Historic opening of Gentile minds to the word is already named. This is not an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. The church is not to hunt another blindness to make “the eyes of the blind” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: then shall the eyes of the blind be opened. Since a true restoration is accomplished by Christ, we must come to him. Isaiah probably alludes to a former prediction (Isaiah 29:10), in which he threatened against the Jews dreadful blindness; he now promises that, when Christ shall shine forth, those senses of which they were deprived for a time shall be renovated. So long as we are alienated from Christ, we are dumb, blind, and lame; we are renewed by the Spirit of Christ. Christ restored speech to the dumb, eyes to the blind, and perfect strength to the feeble and lame; but what he bestowed on their bodies was only a token of the far more abundant blessings which he imparts to our souls.

Reformed note: the opened eyes are still that restoration when Christ shines. Body-healings stay as tokens of the soul’s renewal, not a newspaper of later clinics.

Key contrast Both keep the opened eyes as gospel restoration after the waste, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads the poor Gentiles, who before were blind and deaf, now having the eyes and ears of their minds opened to see God's works and to hear and receive his word. Calvin reads the Church’s senses renovated when Christ shines, alluding to the threatened blindness of Isaiah 29:10; so long as alienated from Christ we are dumb, blind, and lame; the bodily miracles are tokens of the soul’s blessings. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight.

Isaiah 35:6

6Then shall the lame man leap like a deer, and the tongue of the mute sing for joy. For waters break forth in the wilderness, and streams in the desert.

Arminian

Wesley’s then of v. 5 still holds: the poor Gentiles, who before were blind and deaf, shall now have the eyes and ears of their minds opened to see God's works, and to hear and receive his word. The leaping lame and the singing mute are still that opening, and the streams prepare his v. 7 grace in the desert.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have leaped and sung when the waters broke forth. Historic streams in that wilderness after Edom’s waste are already named. The church is not to hunt another deer to make “the lame man leap” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: for waters shall be dug. He next adds other blessings with which believers shall be copiously supplied, as soon as the kingdom of Christ is set up; as if he had said, that there will be no reason to dread scarcity or want, when we have been reconciled to God through Christ, because perfect happiness flows to us from him. Where formerly all was barren, there the highest fertility shall be found. We are poor and barren, unless God bless us through Christ; for he alone brings with him the blessing of the Father. Wicked men, indeed, have often a great abundance of good things, but their wealth is wretched; for they have not Christ.

Reformed accent: the streams are still that kingdom’s water in the waste. The leaping lame stays with Christ’s restoration, not a newspaper pasted onto “waters break forth.”

Isaiah 35:7

7The burning sand shall become a pool, and the thirsty ground springs of water; in the haunt of jackals, where they lie down, the grass shall become reeds and rushes.

Arminian

Wesley: streams—the most dry and barren places shall be made moist and fruitful; which is principally meant of the plentiful effusion of God's grace upon such persons and nations, as had been wholly destitute of it. Rushes—those dry and parched deserts, in which dragons have their abode, shall yield abundance of grass, and reeds, and rushes, which grow only in moist ground. The pool is still that grace after the streams of v. 6, where chapter 34 left jackals on Edom’s waste.

Arminian reading: they might yet have received that grace in a dry place. Historic deserts destitute of it are already named. The church is not to stretch “burning sand” into a later headline.

Reformed

Calvin: the dry place shall be changed into a pool. Christ will come in order to enrich his people with all abundance of blessings; for waters shall flow out of dry places. An instance of this was given, when the Lord delivered his people and brought them out of Babylon; but the accomplishment of this prophecy must be looked for in Christ, through whom their ruinous condition is amended and restored; for that deliverance was but a feeble representation of it. And yet the full accomplishment of this promise ought not to be expected in the present life; our happiness, which is now in some respects concealed, must be an object of hope till the last day.

Reformed note: the pool is still that dry ground’s change, Babylon a taste, Christ the rest. Jackals’ haunt becoming reeds stays with that historic waste reversed, not a newspaper of later springs.

Key contrast Both keep the pool as grace after the waste, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads the dry places made moist as principally the plentiful effusion of God's grace upon persons and nations wholly destitute of it, dragons’ deserts yielding rushes. Calvin reads a picture of happy life under Christ: Babylon’s deliverance a feeble representation, the accomplishment in Christ, the full happiness hoped till the last day. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight.

Isaiah 35:8

8And a highway shall be there, and it shall be called the Way of Holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it. It shall belong to those who walk on the way; even if they are fools, they shall not go astray.

Arminian

Wesley: a way—the high-way and the way are not to be taken for two different ways, but for one and the same way, even a causey, which is raised ground, and a way. Holiness—the people (walking in it) shall be all righteous. For those—but this way shall be appropriated to those persons above-mentioned; the weak, and blind, and lame, whom God will lead and save. Though fools—the way shall be so plain and strait, that even the most foolish travellers cannot easily mistake it. The highway is still that raised way after the pool of v. 7.

Arminian pastoral: they might yet have walked that plain causey. Historic way for the weak, blind, and lame after the waste is already named. This is not a millennial chart and not a newspaper of later roads.

Reformed

Calvin: and a path shall be there. Here it is promised to the Jews that they shall be allowed to return to their native country, lest, when they were carried into Babylon, they should think that they were led into perpetual banishment. Yet this statement is extended much farther. It shall be called the holy way; the unclean person shall not pass through it; the Lord will separate believers in such a manner that they shall not be mingled with the reprobate. The full cleansing of the Church must be expected at the last day. The demonstrative pronoun is more correctly viewed as referring to God; as if he had said that God will go before them to lead and direct the way. Fools shall not go astray; they who are wise in their own eyes, and who rely on their own guidance, will be permitted by God to wander in uncertain courses.

Reformed accent: the highway is still the Jews’ return from Babylon, then God himself walking as leader. The holy way stays with that historic road, not a newspaper pasted onto “holiness.”

Key contrast Both keep the highway as the LORD’s holy way after the waste, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Wesley reads one causey, not two ways; the people walking in it all righteous; the way appropriated to the weak, blind, and lame, whom God will lead and save; so plain and strait that even the most foolish travellers cannot easily mistake it. Calvin reads the Jews’ return from Babylon lest they think perpetual banishment, then God himself going before as leader, as by cloud and fire; the unclean shall not pass; full cleansing at the last day; fools shall not go astray because they follow him, not because the road is obvious of itself. Neither comment is an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight.

Isaiah 35:9

9No lion shall be there, nor shall any ravenous beast come up on it; they shall not be found there, but the redeemed shall walk there.

Arminian

Wesley’s way of v. 8 still holds: this way shall be appropriated to those persons above-mentioned; the weak, and blind, and lame, whom God will lead and save. No lion is still that causey’s safety after the highway is named.

Arminian reading: they might yet have walked there without a beast. Historic redeemed on that way after Edom’s waste are already named. The church is not to hunt another lion to make “no lion shall be there” a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: there shall not be there a lion. Formerly he mentioned it (Isaiah 34:14) as one of the curses of God, that wild beasts would meet the Jews wherever they went; but now he declares that, when they have been received into favor, no lions and no beasts of prey shall attack them; because the Lord will ward them off, so as to open up a way for his people free from all danger and from all fear. God not only begins, but conducts to the end, the work of our salvation, that his grace in us may not be useless and unprofitable. He will not leave us in the midst of the journey, but at length will perfectly finish what he has begun in us by his Spirit.

Reformed note: the missing lion is still the reverse of chapter 34’s beasts on Edom. The redeemed walking stays with that guarded return, not a newspaper of later roads.

Isaiah 35:10

10And the ransomed of the LORD shall return and come to Zion with singing; everlasting joy shall be upon their heads; they shall obtain gladness and joy, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away.

Arminian

Wesley’s flourishing of v. 1 and his holy way of v. 8 still close the unit: Emmanuel's land, the seat of God's church and people, which the rage of their enemies had brought to desolation, shall flourish exceedingly; the people walking in that way shall be all righteous; the way appropriated to the weak, and blind, and lame, whom God will lead and save. The ransomed returning to Zion are still that people after the redeemed walk in v. 9.

Arminian close of the unit: they might yet have come to Zion with singing. Historic return after Edom’s waste, then the church, is already named. The church is not to stretch “the ransomed of the LORD shall return” into a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: therefore the redeemed of Jehovah shall return. The Prophet confirms the former doctrine, that God hath determined to redeem his people, and therefore that nothing can resist his decree. He calls them the redeemed of God, that they may consider his power, and may not estimate by human means the promise which he has made about their return. He says also that they will come to Zion, because God does not in vain wish to bring them out of Babylon and leave them when they have commenced their journey. We have no means of entering the Church but by the redemption of God; no man will redeem himself; Christ is our only deliverer. They shall obtain joy and gladness; the true and only ground of rejoicing is to know that we are reconciled to God. Sorrow and sighing shall flee away; the joy is everlasting, and all sadness flees away.

Reformed close: the return is still from Babylon to Zion, then the Church in Christ. Calvin and Wesley agree the ransomed come home by the LORD’s leading; Calvin will not let the decree be resisted, nor a man redeem himself. This return is already enough without a later newspaper.

Reformed Notable voices

“The wilderness and the solitary place shall be glad. Here the Prophet describes a wonderful change; for having in the former chapter described the destruction of Idumaea, and having said that it would be changed into a wilderness, he now promises, on the other hand, fertility to the wilderness. I pass by the dreams of the Jews, who apply all passages of this kind to the temporal reign of the Messiah, which they have contrived by their own imagination. Beginning appropriately with Judea, he calls the whole world a wilderness, because everywhere the wrath of God abounded. The Lord began some kind of restoration when he brought his people out of Babylon; but that was only a slight foretaste, and therefore this passage must refer to the kingdom of Christ; by the kingdom of Christ I mean not only that which is begun here, but that which shall be completed at the last day. This is done by Christ, by whom alone they can be renewed and made glad. Flourishing it shall flourish: the grace of Christ, by whose power those places which had been overgrown with filthy weeds flourish exceedingly. The glory of Lebanon, the beauty of Carmel and Sharon: they shall see the glory of Jehovah; till men learn to know God, they are barren and destitute of everything good. Strengthen ye the weak hands: the whole of this passage relates to the ministers of the word; he addresses the teachers of the Church, and enjoins them to exhort, arouse, and encourage weak men. Say to them that are faint hearted: that strength is breathed into our hearts by God through his word; behold, your God will come. If we are not supported by the word of the Lord, we must faint and despair. Nor is it superfluous that he adds your God; for if we do not know that he is our God, his approach will produce terror. This vengeance is threatened against wicked men and enemies of the Church; to believers he will be a consolation, and he will come to save them. God is prepared and armed with vengeance, that believers may learn to lean on his aid, and not to fancy some deity unemployed in heaven. Then shall the eyes of the blind be opened: so long as we are alienated from Christ, we are dumb, blind, and lame; we are renewed by the Spirit of Christ. What he bestowed on their bodies was only a token of the far more abundant blessings which he imparts to our souls. Waters shall be dug: where formerly all was barren, there the highest fertility shall be found. The dry place shall be changed into a pool: an instance was given when the Lord brought his people out of Babylon; but that deliverance was but a feeble representation; the accomplishment must be looked for in Christ. And a path shall be there: it is promised to the Jews that they shall be allowed to return to their native country, lest they should think they were led into perpetual banishment; yet this statement is extended much farther. It shall be called the holy way; the unclean person shall not pass through it. God will go before them to lead and direct the way. Fools shall not go astray. There shall not be there a lion: God not only begins, but conducts to the end, the work of our salvation; he will not leave us in the midst of the journey, but at length will perfectly finish what he has begun in us by his Spirit. Therefore the redeemed of Jehovah shall return, and come to Zion; nothing can resist his decree; we have no means of entering the Church but by the redemption of God; Christ is our only deliverer. They shall obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 35:1–10)
