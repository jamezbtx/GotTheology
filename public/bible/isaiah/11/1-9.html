Passage

Isaiah 11

Verses 1–9 · ESV · Voices

A shoot comes from the stump of Jesse; a branch from his roots bears fruit. The Spirit of the Lord rests on him: wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, fear of the Lord. His delight is that fear. He does not judge by eyes or ears, but in righteousness for the poor and meek. He strikes the earth with the rod of his mouth; the breath of his lips kills the wicked. Righteousness and faithfulness are his belt. Wolf with lamb, leopard with goat, calf and lion together; a little child leads them. Cow and bear graze; the lion eats straw. The nursing child plays at the cobra’s hole. They shall not hurt in all my holy mountain: the earth full of the knowledge of the Lord as waters cover the sea.

Isaiah 11:1

1There shall come forth a shoot from the stump of Jesse, and a branch from his roots shall bear fruit.

Arminian

A shoot from the stump of Jesse, a branch from his roots shall bear fruit. Wesley: the anointed of 10:27 explained. Stump—Messiah born of David’s house when it was cut down, nothing left but a root. Jesse, not David: the royal family reduced to a private man’s obscurity.

Arminian shoot: shared christology—this is Christ, not Hezekiah. Historic Jesse after Lebanon fell (10:34). The stump is still David’s, and the shoot still comes.

Reformed

Calvin: solely Christ. Not Hezekiah, Josiah, or Zerubbabel, who never reached this height. After the forest of 10:33–34, consolation begins here. Jesse, not David: the rank had sunk to a peasant’s. From a dry trunk a branch more excellent than all the forests. His beginning was mean; he grew like a large tree from a feeble seed.

Reformed shoot: the same Christ from the same cut house. Shared: shoot, stump of Jesse, branch, roots, fruit. Not a millennial camp: both name the man, not a timetable.

Isaiah 11:2

2And the Spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and the fear of the Lord.

Arminian

The Spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him: wisdom and understanding, counsel and might, knowledge and the fear of the Lord. Wesley: qualifications for a governor and a teacher; perfect knowledge for his people’s good; prudence to counsel and courage to execute; knowledge of God’s whole will and of men’s hearts; fear of reverence.

Arminian rest: the Spirit stays. Shared: this King is Spirit-anointed, the child of 9:6 now grown from Jesse. Historic gifts named, not a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: he now speaks of Christ without a figure. The spiritual kingdom consists in the gifts of the Spirit. This refers to His human nature, that we might be partakers; out of His fullness we draw (John 1:16). The anointing from which He is called Christ, which He imparts. Not the Papists’ sevenfold grace: six kinds here, and more elsewhere. Those barren and dry have no interest in Christ and falsely glory in His name. True faith makes us partakers. Ask all blessings from Christ alone.

Reformed rest: the gifts are in Him to be communicated. Shared: Spirit of the Lord, wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, fear of the Lord.

Isaiah 11:3

3And his delight shall be in the fear of the Lord. He shall not judge by what his eyes see, or decide disputes by what his ears hear,

Arminian

His delight shall be in the fear of the Lord. He shall not judge by what his eyes see, or decide disputes by what his ears hear. Wesley: not rashly or partially, but as the fear of God obliges judges. Not according to outward appearance; not by rumours.

Arminian delight: a real fear in the King, and a real refusal of surface judgment. Hearts are still His to search.

Reformed

Calvin: smell—so shrewd He need not learn from seeing or hearing. Fear of the Lord as the rule of judging. Outward mask of holiness is of no avail. He thoroughly knows inmost thoughts (John 2:25). None are true worshippers but those He approves, and they must offer a pure mind.

Reformed delight: He smells what eyes miss. Shared: delight in the fear of the Lord, not by eyes, not by ears.

Isaiah 11:4

4but with righteousness he shall judge the poor, and decide with equity for the meek of the earth; and he shall strike the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips he shall kill the wicked.

Arminian

With righteousness he shall judge the poor, and decide with equity for the meek of the earth; he shall strike the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips he shall kill the wicked. Wesley: defend and deliver the poor; condemn their malicious enemies. The rod of his mouth—His word, His scepter, sharper than a sword (Heb 4:12); by preaching He subdued the world and will destroy His enemies (2 Thess 2:8). This he adds to declare that Christ’s kingdom is not of this world.

Arminian rod: shared—this King rules by the word, not a sword of steel. The poor are still defended. Historic preaching that subdues, not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: guardian of the poor or meek—those humbled by a conviction of their poverty who have laid aside proud dispositions. Not all men: those who know they are poor. Implied contrast: He does not rule over the rich, those swelled with a false opinion of themselves. The poor alone allow themselves to be governed. If we want His protection, put on meekness. Rod of His mouth: a scepter of words; a mere breath is enough. The gospel wounds elect and reprobate differently. Paul: He will consume the Wicked with the breath of His mouth (2 Thess 2:8). Ministers’ lips as His. He does not wear a golden crown or earthly armor; where the gospel is preached in purity, Christ reigns.

Reformed rod: the same word; the poor who submit. Shared: righteousness, poor, meek, rod of his mouth, breath of his lips, kill the wicked.

Key contrast Both confess the shoot is the Spirit-resting King who judges by the word: the rod of His mouth, a kingdom not of this world (Wesley; Calvin). Shared christology; not a millennial camp fight as Arminian versus Reformed, and not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear Him defending the poor and condemning enemies by the preached word that subdues the world (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear the poor and meek as those humbled who alone allow themselves to be governed; He does not rule the proud (Calvin).

Isaiah 11:5

5Righteousness shall be the belt of his waist, and faithfulness the belt of his loins.

Arminian

Righteousness shall be the belt of his waist, and faithfulness the belt of his loins. Wesley: it shall adorn Him and be the glory of His government, as a girdle was an ornament and ensign of power, and shall constantly cleave to Him in all His administrations.

Arminian belt: the King wears justice, not purple. Historic figure for a real reign.

Reformed

Calvin: belt as royal emblem, like the scepter. Righteousness and truth: He shall not appear like kings in purple, but righteousness and truth shall shine. Not confined to Him: He comes girded, then imparts by the Spirit. Faithfulness: He never disappoints; He continues like Himself.

Reformed belt: what He wears He gives. Shared: righteousness, belt, faithfulness, loins.

Isaiah 11:6

6The wolf shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the young goat, and the calf and the lion and the fattened calf together; and a little child shall lead them.

Arminian

The wolf shall dwell with the lamb, and the leopard shall lie down with the young goat, and the calf and the lion and the fattened calf together; and a little child shall lead them. Wesley: the creatures restored to innocency before the fall; and men of fierce dispositions so transformed by the grace of Christ that they become gentle. A child—they will submit their rebellious wills to the meanest who speak in Christ’s name.

Arminian wolf: both the figure and the men. Historic hope after Assyria’s forest fell. Not a millennial camp as the split: Wesley himself names grace transforming the fierce. They still submit.

Reformed

Calvin: the character of those who have submitted to Christ. He looks beyond: restoration of the world, the order before apostasy; cruelty of brutes from sin. Christ came to reconcile. Chiefly he means the people of Christ will have no disposition to do injury: formerly lions, now sheep. From the less to the greater: if brutes into peace, much more brotherly harmony. He does not mean any are mild by nature before they are renewed. Little child: ministers of the word, who have no civil government. All are untamed till Christ subdues them by the gospel. Where the Spirit of Christ is not, no true meekness.

Reformed wolf: the same peace in the same people. Shared: wolf, lamb, leopard, goat, calf, lion, little child. Not a millennial timetable as Arminian versus Reformed.

Isaiah 11:7

7The cow and the bear shall graze; their young shall lie down together; and the lion shall eat straw like the ox.

Arminian

The cow and the bear shall graze; their young shall lie down together; and the lion shall eat straw like the ox. Wesley: together without danger; straw—the grass of the earth as at first; they shall not devour other living creatures.

Arminian straw: the predator’s mouth changed. Same King as v. 4. Historic figure for a people who stop devouring.

Reformed

Calvin: if sin had not polluted the world, no animal addicted to prey; the fruits of the earth would have sufficed (Gen 1:30). Under these figures: those who were like savage beasts will be mild, satisfied with ordinary food, abstaining from injury.

Reformed straw: the diet of a new people. Shared: cow, bear, young, lion, straw, ox.

Isaiah 11:8

8The nursing child shall play over the hole of the cobra, and the weaned child shall put his hand on the adder's den.

Arminian

The nursing child shall play over the hole of the cobra, and the weaned child shall put his hand on the adder's den. The smallest near the worst, unhurt.

Arminian play: the venomous no longer kill. Historic holy mountain (v. 9), not a newspaper zoo. They still play because the King still reigns by His word.

Reformed

Calvin: when brought into favor and cleansed by the Spirit of regeneration, free from every hurtful disposition. Animals injure even children because the race is stained from the womb. Those who carried malice like poison will do no harm even to little children. Safety even for those with no protection.

Reformed play: the hidden poison gone. Shared: nursing child, cobra, weaned child, adder's den.

Isaiah 11:9

9They shall not hurt or destroy in all my holy mountain; for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord as the waters cover the sea.

Arminian

They shall not hurt or destroy in all my holy mountain; for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord as the waters cover the sea. Wesley: my holy mountain—in Zion, in my church. The sea—the channel of the sea.

Arminian not hurt: the church as the mountain. Knowledge is why the wolf lies down. Historic Zion first, then the filling. Not a millennial camp fight: Wesley names the church.

Reformed

Calvin: men having laid aside depravity, inclined to do right. Believers regenerated; hypocrites mixed in will be cast out. Distinguishing mark of genuine members: free from desire of injury. This blessing flows from the knowledge of God, which abases flesh and teaches the same Father (Mal 2:10). Self-love otherwise. Christ’s government rests on faith and the gospel. Fullness of knowledge compared with the slender taste under the law (Jer 31:34). If we do not immediately possess full knowledge, we must advance, and fruit must spring from that root.

Reformed not hurt: knowledge that frees from malice. Shared: not hurt, not destroy, holy mountain, earth full of the knowledge of the Lord, waters cover the sea.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a dry stump of Jesse, a Spirit-resting King, and a holy mountain that does not hurt—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 11:1–9.

Arminian Notable voices

“Having said that the Assyrian yoke should be destroyed because of the anointing, he now explains who that anointed person was. The stem—or stump: the Messiah born of David’s house when it was in a most forlorn condition, like a tree cut down. Of Jesse—not of David, to intimate that at Christ’s birth the royal family should be reduced to its primitive obscurity. Wisdom and understanding—necessary qualifications for a governor and a teacher; perfect knowledge of all things necessary for his own and people’s good. Counsel—prudence to give good counsel, and might to execute it. Knowledge—of the whole will of God, and of the hearts of men. Fear—reverence, a care to please him. Judge—defend and deliver the poor. The rod of his mouth—his word, his scepter, sharper than a sword; by the preaching whereof he subdued the world to himself, and will destroy his enemies (2 Thess 2:8). This he adds to declare the nature of Christ’s kingdom, that it is not of this world. The wolf—the creatures restored to that state of innocency before the fall; and men of fierce dispositions so transformed by the grace of Christ that they become gentle. A child—they will submit their rebellious wills to the meanest persons that speak to them in Christ’s name. My holy mountain—in Zion, in my church.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 11:1–9)

Reformed Notable voices

“There shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse: this applies solely to the person of Christ. Not Hezekiah or Josiah or Zerubbabel. He does not call him David but Jesse, because that family had sunk so low it appeared to be a mean peasant’s. The Spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him: his whole strength consists in the gifts of the Spirit. This refers to Christ’s human nature, that we might be partakers; out of his fullness we draw. Those who are altogether barren and dry have no interest in Christ, and falsely glory in his name. True faith makes us partakers of all Christ’s benefits. He will judge the poor: he is the guardian of the poor or meek—those humbled by a conviction of their poverty. He does not rule over the rich, that is, those swelled with a false opinion of themselves. The poor alone allow themselves to be governed by him. The rod of his mouth: a scepter which consists in words. Christ will have no need to borrow aid; a mere breath or a word will be enough. Paul: the Lord shall consume the Wicked with the breath of his mouth (2 Thess 2:8). He does not wear a golden crown or employ earthly armor; the doctrine of the gospel is his royal banner. The wolf shall dwell with the lamb: he describes those who have submitted to Christ. Chiefly he means that the people of Christ will have no disposition to do injury. Formerly like lions, they will now be like sheep. Where the Spirit of Christ is not, there will be no true meekness. They shall not hurt: a distinguishing mark of the genuine members of the Church. This blessing flows from the knowledge of God, which abases all flesh and brings men into brotherly harmony.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 11:1–9)
