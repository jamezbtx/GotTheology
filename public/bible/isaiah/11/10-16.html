Passage

Isaiah 11

Verses 10–16 · ESV · Voices

In that day the root of Jesse stands as a signal for the peoples; the nations inquire of him; his resting place is glorious. The Lord extends his hand a second time to recover the remnant from Assyria, Egypt, Pathros, Cush, Elam, Shinar, Hamath, and the coastlands. He raises a signal, assembles the banished of Israel, gathers the dispersed of Judah from the four corners. Ephraim’s jealousy departs; they shall not harass one another. They swoop on Philistia and the east; Edom, Moab, Ammon. The Lord dries the Egyptian sea and the River into seven channels; a highway from Assyria for the remnant, as when Israel came up from Egypt.

Isaiah 11:10

10In that day the root of Jesse, who shall stand as a signal for the peoples—of him shall the nations inquire, and his resting place shall be glorious.

Arminian

In that day the root of Jesse, who shall stand as a signal for the peoples—of him shall the nations inquire, and his resting place shall be glorious. Wesley: a branch on the root; an ensign all nations may discern and resort to; rest—his temple or church; glory greater than the tabernacle, yet spiritual: gifts and graces of the Spirit.

Arminian signal: shared christology—the shoot of 11:1 is now a banner. Historic Jesse, then the nations. Not a millennial camp: Wesley already names spiritual glory in the church. They still inquire.

Reformed

Calvin: Gentiles who formerly abhorred the Jews will bow. Banner stretched aloft: fulfilled by preaching the gospel, more illustrious than if He had soared above the clouds. As Moses lifted the serpent (John 3:14). Sought: men flee to Him for salvation. Rest: the Church (Ps 132:14), once exposed to disgrace, raised again. God is pleased to dwell in His Church though this may not always be seen.

Reformed signal: the same root, the same gospel banner. Shared: that day, root of Jesse, signal for the peoples, nations inquire, resting place glorious. Not a millennial timetable as Arminian versus Reformed.

Isaiah 11:11

11In that day the Lord will extend his hand yet a second time to recover the remnant that remains of his people, from Assyria, from Egypt, from Pathros, from Cush, from Elam, from Shinar, from Hamath, and from the coastlands of the sea.

Arminian

In that day the Lord will extend his hand yet a second time to recover the remnant that remains of his people, from Assyria, from Egypt, from Pathros, from Cush, from Elam, from Shinar, from Hamath, and from the coastlands of the sea. Wesley: the first is Babylon; this second is in the days of the Messiah. Recover from all places the ten tribes or the two were carried. Pathros a province of Egypt.

Arminian second: historic names first—Assyria, Egypt, the coastlands—then Messiah’s day. The remnant of 10:20–22 still being gathered. Not a newspaper map, not a modern-nation chart.

Reformed

Calvin: God will display the power of His hand as for an uncommon exploit. Second time: as He delivered from Egypt. Cannot be Babylon only: they were not then brought back from Egypt, Ethiopia, and the rest. These words refer to the kingdom of Christ through the preaching of the gospel. Remnant: this deliverance belonged only to a small seed. The work belongs to God, not to men. From past benefits, hope for the future.

Reformed second: the gospel hand, a remnant. Shared: that day, hand, second time, recover, remnant, Assyria, Egypt, Pathros, Cush, Elam, Shinar, Hamath, coastlands.

Isaiah 11:12

12He will raise a signal for the nations and will assemble the banished of Israel, and gather the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth.

Arminian

He will raise a signal for the nations and will assemble the banished of Israel, and gather the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth. Wesley: all nations, Jews and Gentiles; outcasts driven into foreign parts; Israel—the ten tribes.

Arminian assemble: a real gathering of a real banished people, Jews and Gentiles under the same signal. Historic four corners, then the gospel. They still come. Match the hub: a King who lifts a signal, leaving this assembling as a people who may resort.

Reformed

Calvin: the former verse explained. Two readings of the ensign: terrify adversaries, or call the exiles. Among us the banner is the gospel, by which Christ is preached. We cannot be gathered unless we assemble to this ensign and be joined to Him by faith. My sheep hear my voice and follow me (John 10:27). The calling is efficacious: as soon as He indicates, He will restore. Dispersion: Moses promised He would gather from the four winds (Deut 30:3–4). Done under Christ. No hope of gathering the remnant but by looking to this ensign.

Reformed assemble: only those who come to the banner, the sheep who hear. Shared: signal for the nations, banished of Israel, dispersed of Judah, four corners of the earth.

Key contrast Both confess the root of Jesse is raised as a signal for the nations, historic then gospel, not a millennial camp fight as Arminian versus Reformed (Wesley; Calvin). Arminians typically hear all nations, Jews and Gentiles, the banished of the ten tribes and the dispersed of Judah gathered in Messiah’s days (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear that we cannot be gathered unless we assemble to this ensign by faith: His sheep hear His voice; the remnant looks to the banner (Calvin).

Isaiah 11:13

13The jealousy of Ephraim shall depart, and those who harass Judah shall be cut off; Ephraim shall not be jealous of Judah, and Judah shall not harass Ephraim.

Arminian

The jealousy of Ephraim shall depart, and those who harass Judah shall be cut off; Ephraim shall not be jealous of Judah, and Judah shall not harass Ephraim. Wesley: the ten tribes; of enemies they shall be made friends. Adversaries—not the body of Ephraim, who are reconciled, but those few who continue in enmity, with the rest of their foes.

Arminian jealousy: historic north and south, then friends under the signal. Not a newspaper civil war. They still stop harassing.

Reformed

Calvin: inward peace: Israelites and Jews no longer in civil broils. Envy the source of the strife, Judah and Ephraim contending for renown. Outward and inward peace. Objected that wars followed the gospel: he includes the whole of Christ’s kingdom, not a single age. We taste the beginning; He still defends. This belongs to the true children of Abraham, purified by the cross, who cease to desire renown.

Reformed jealousy: the house stops devouring itself (9:21). Shared: jealousy of Ephraim, harass Judah, cut off, not jealous, not harass.

Isaiah 11:14

14But they shall swoop down on the shoulder of the Philistines in the west, and together they shall plunder the people of the east. They shall put out their hand against Edom and Moab, and the Ammonites shall obey them.

Arminian

They shall swoop down on the shoulder of the Philistines in the west, and together they shall plunder the people of the east. They shall put out their hand against Edom and Moab, and the Ammonites shall obey them. Wesley: metaphor from birds of prey. Spoil—the spiritual victory the Messiah obtains by His apostles and ministers over all nations.

Arminian swoop: historic neighbors named, then the apostles’ victory. Not a millennial conquest, not a newspaper raid. Wesley already reads it as the word going out.

Reformed

Calvin: another way He assists: He conquers their enemies. Philistines, Ammon, Moab, Edom—continual molestors. The Church, though not without enemies, is victorious in the end. Jews who dream of an earthly kingdom of Christ interpret this carnally; judge it according to the nature of Christ’s kingdom. Partly seen at the return from captivity; a more splendid victory through the gospel. We vanquish when restored to liberty by Christ, lusts laid low, patience possessing the soul (Luke 21:19).

Reformed swoop: the same spiritual victory. Shared: Philistines, west, people of the east, Edom, Moab, Ammonites. Not a millennial camp as the split.

Isaiah 11:15

15And the Lord will utterly destroy the tongue of the Sea of Egypt, and will wave his hand over the River with his scorching breath, and strike it into seven channels, and he will lead people across in sandals.

Arminian

The Lord will utterly destroy the tongue of the Sea of Egypt, and will wave his hand over the River with his scorching breath, and strike it into seven channels, and he will lead people across in sandals. Wesley: not only divide as of old, but dry it for a highway. The sea—the Red Sea, the Egyptian sea. The river—Nile, famous for seven streams.

Arminian sandals: a real second Exodus figure. Historic sea and Nile, then Messiah’s road. He still dries what blocks the remnant.

Reformed

Calvin: by amazing power He opens a way that appeared shut. Tongue: a bay of the sea. The river: the Nile. Wind of the Lord: not chance; He directs the force. Seven streams: the Nile’s mouths. In shoes: so dry they need not pull them off. Nothing will stand in God’s way when He pleases to rescue. He glances at the former deliverance, that they may learn it will be the same.

Reformed sandals: the same God who split the sea. Shared: tongue of the Sea of Egypt, River, seven channels, across in sandals. No open theism: He will do it.

Isaiah 11:16

16And there will be a highway from Assyria for the remnant that remains of his people, as there was for Israel when they came up from the land of Egypt.

Arminian

There will be a highway from Assyria for the remnant that remains of his people, as there was for Israel when they came up from the land of Egypt. Wesley: all impediments removed; a way for God’s Israel from all parts of the world. Assyria named because the ten tribes were carried there, the most desperate case.

Arminian highway: historic Assyria first, then the remnant’s road. Same remnant as 10:21. Not a modern-nation chart. The signal of v. 10 is why the road exists.

Reformed

Calvin: explains v. 15. The same power as from Egypt: through seas, deserts, Jordan. What the Lord has once performed, let us expect. Direct thoughts to the final deliverance of the Church: though resurrection appear incredible, the Lord will easily find a way.

Reformed highway: Egypt’s pattern, Christ’s finish. Shared: highway, Assyria, remnant, as when Israel came up from Egypt.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a root lifted as a signal, a second recovery, and a highway from Assyria—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 11:10–16.

Arminian Notable voices

“A root—a branch growing upon the root. Ensign—an eminent ensign which not only the Jews but all nations may discern, and to which they shall resort. Rest—his temple or church, the place of his presence. Glorious—greater glory than the tabernacle, yet spiritual: plentiful gifts and graces of the Holy Spirit. The second—the first seems to be the deliverance out of Babylon; this second must be in the days of the Messiah. To recover—from all places far and near into which the ten tribes or the two tribes were carried. Nations—all nations, Jews and Gentiles. Outcasts—driven into foreign parts. Israel—the ten tribes. Fly—a metaphor from birds of prey. Spoil—the spiritual victory which the Messiah shall obtain by his apostles and ministers over all nations. Highway—all impediments removed, a way for the return of God’s Israel from all parts of the world. He mentions Assyria, because thither the ten tribes were carried, whose case seemed most desperate.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 11:10–16)

Reformed Notable voices

“The root of Jesse shall stand for an ensign of the peoples: to it shall the Gentiles seek. He compares it to a banner; this was fulfilled by the preaching of the gospel, more illustrious than if Christ had soared above the clouds. Sought: men flee to him to ask salvation. His rest shall be glory: the Church, as This is my everlasting rest; here will I dwell (Ps 132:14). The Lord will again set his hand: this cannot be understood of Babylon only; they were not brought back from Egypt and Ethiopia then. These words refer to the kingdom of Christ, under whom this deliverance was obtained through the preaching of the gospel. He expressly calls them a remnant. We cannot be gathered by the Lord unless we assemble to this ensign and be joined to him by faith. My sheep hear my voice and follow me (John 10:27). The Jews who dream of an earthly kingdom of Christ interpret all this in a carnal sense; they ought to judge of it according to the nature of Christ’s kingdom. Partly seen when they returned from captivity; a more splendid victory through the gospel. We vanquish our enemies when we are restored to liberty by Christ.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 11:10–14)

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
