Passage

Isaiah 3

Verses 1–15 · WEB · Voices

The Lord God of hosts takes from Jerusalem and Judah stay of bread and water, warrior, judge, prophet, and elder. Boys are princes; neighbor oppresses neighbor. A cloak is enough to be asked to rule a heap of ruins; the asked man will not be a healer. Jerusalem has stumbled because speech and deeds defy His glory. Faces proclaim sin like Sodom. Tell the righteous it shall be well; woe to the wicked. Guides mislead. The Lord stands to judge elders who have eaten the vineyard and grind the poor.

Isaiah 3:1

1For, behold, the Lord, GOD of Armies, takes away from Jerusalem and from Judah supply and support, the whole supply of bread, and the whole supply of water;

Arminian

Behold, the Lord, GOD of Armies, takes away from Jerusalem and Judah supply and support, the whole supply of bread, and water.

The Lord actually removes what Judah leaned on. Bread and water were stays. Agency remains in the speech and deeds of v. 8 that drew this taking.

Reformed

Calvin: behold presses the nearness of the hand. Wealth will not prevent wrath. Stay and staff: every kind of support, then bread and water first. God takes the strength of bread either by removing food or by withdrawing the hidden power that nourishes; without His blessing the full belly still hungers.

The secret blessing is His to give or withhold.

Isaiah 3:2

2the mighty man, the man of war, the judge, the prophet, the diviner, the elder,

Arminian

Wesley: judge—civil magistrates; ancient—wisdom increased by long experience.

Every rank in Judah’s gates will be taken away. When they go, the city feels it.

Reformed

Calvin: magistrates and teachers are the two eyes of the body. He mixes warrior, judge, prophet. Sometimes God takes them by death, sometimes by making them soft. A well-ordered commonwealth is a gift; fanatics who banish the sword oppose public safety.

He takes what He gave. Mighty, soldier, judge, prophet, elder.

Isaiah 3:3

3the captain of fifty, the honorable man, the counselor, the skilled craftsman, and the clever enchanter.

Arminian

Captain of fifty, man of rank, counselor, skillful magician, expert in charms. Judah’s full staff, including dark arts it should not have kept.

Offices and forbidden crafts together. Losing both is judgment on a land that filled itself from the east (2:6).

Reformed

Calvin: captains of fifty as the custom then. Artificers uphold a nation. Eloquent or skilled in muttering: some hear forbidden charms among the punishments—God takes even unlawful aids (cf. Hos 3:4).

Every ornament of a safe people is His to strip. The captain, the counselor, and the charmer are all removed.

Isaiah 3:4

4I will give boys to be their princes, and children shall rule over them.

Arminian

I will give boys to be their princes, and children shall rule over them. Wesley’s childish rulers begin here.

Unfit heads in Judah, not a modern gender or age slogan. The Lord makes them princes; the people still live under them.

Reformed

Calvin: children by age or by mind—delicate, without courage. No man is fit to govern unless God appoint and endow him. Vengeance in two ways: He stuns the able, or He gradually removes them and commits the reins to those who cannot rule a house.

He appoints kings in wrath (Hos 13:11). Neither side disputes this: boys, infants, rule.

Isaiah 3:5

5The people will be oppressed, everyone by another, and everyone by his neighbor. The child will behave himself proudly against the old man, and the wicked against the honorable.

Arminian

People oppress one another; youth insolent to the elder, the wicked against the honorable. Wesley: oppressed by command or permission of such childish rulers.

Neighbor against neighbor in streets. Insolence is still theirs under infant princes.

Reformed

Calvin: utmost confusion when order is destroyed. Wantonness unrestrained. They who sap civil government are open enemies of mankind. A child insulting an old man is the most preposterous of spectacles, and unavoidable when government has fallen.

Anarchy is a judgment, not a liberation. Common to both: oppress, youth, elder, despised, honorable.

Isaiah 3:6

6Indeed a man shall take hold of his brother in the house of his father, saying, “You have clothing, you be our ruler, and let this ruin be under your hand.”

Arminian

A man takes hold of his brother: You have clothing, you be our ruler, and let this heap of ruins under your rule. Wesley: we are undone, neither food nor raiment; thou hast something left; under thine hand—to heal it.

A rag of dignity in a ruined house. They still seize a brother.

Reformed

Calvin: no man will undertake to govern, though requested. Ambition usually contends for power; when dignity is refused, calamity has reached its depth. Take hold: they seize, not merely flatter. Ruin under thy hand: at least if you have compassion, aid our wretchedness.

The throne is a burden no one wants. Brother, cloak, leader, ruins.

Isaiah 3:7

7In that day he will cry out, saying, “I will not be a healer; for in my house is neither bread nor clothing. You shall not make me ruler of the people.”

Arminian

In that day he will say, I will not be a healer; for in my house neither bread nor clothing. You shall not make me ruler. Wesley: healer—a repairer of the ruins of the state.

The man asked to be a healer refuses. The man with a cloak still cannot bind the wound.

Reformed

Calvin: swear marks a firm refusal. Healer: metaphor from surgeons—I will not bind the wounds. House without bread or clothing: the state is past remedy.

The office is declined because the ruin is beyond him. Both traditions hear: not a healer, no bread, no cloak.

Isaiah 3:8

8For Jerusalem is ruined, and Judah is fallen; because their tongue and their doings are against the LORD, to provoke the eyes of his glory.

Arminian

Jerusalem has stumbled, Judah fallen; because their tongue and their doings are against the LORD, to provoke the eyes of his glorious presence. The taking of v. 1 has this because.

Judah fell because “their tongue and their doings” defied the LORD. Arminian readers note the provocation was deliberate, done in the very presence of his glory.

Reformed

Calvin: lest God seem cruel, the reason is their words and actions against Him, to provoke the eyes of His glory. Open insult in His presence. Glory: extraordinary madness to have no reverence when majesty is displayed. The cause of calamities is in their own hands.

The fall is His righteous sentence on that defiance. Jerusalem, Judah, speech, deeds, glory.

Isaiah 3:9

9The look of their faces testify against them. They parade their sin like Sodom. They don’t hide it. Woe to their soul! For they have brought disaster upon themselves.

Arminian

The look of their faces bears witness; they parade their sin like Sodom. They have brought disaster upon themselves. Wesley: pride and impiety show in their looks; they act it publicly; procured a fit recompense, even utter ruin.

Sodom is moral resemblance (1:10). They fetched this evil. Faces still their own.

Reformed

Calvin: countenance answers against them—it will not be necessary to bring distant witnesses. They declare their sin as Sodom, boasting without shame. Woe unto their soul: they have rewarded evil unto themselves. God cannot be accused; give glory to a righteous judge and lay the blame on yourselves.

The fetching is theirs; the woe is His verdict.

Isaiah 3:10

10Tell the righteous that it will be well with them, for they will eat the fruit of their deeds.

Arminian

Tell the righteous that it will be well with them, for they will eat the fruit of their deeds. Wesley: say ye, O priests and Levites, that God will be their safeguard and portion.

An earnest word to righteous in this crashing city. Fruit of deeds is not a works-gospel that buys 1:18’s snow; it is well with them because God is their portion, and what they have done is not nothing. Creaturely fruit under a King who actually safeguards.

Reformed

Calvin: say—hold it a settled point. Public calamity tempts the godly to think they perish with the wicked. Providence never confounds; even in apparent confusion He distinguishes. He does not merely bid them tell the righteous, but every man be convinced it shall be well, though he appear unhappy. Fruit of his doings: he shall not be defrauded of the reward of good conduct.

The distinction is God’s, not chance. The fruit is a reward He will not deny, not a claim that justifies. On this both agree: righteous, well, fruit of deeds.

Key contrast Both confess it will be well with the righteous in a falling Judah, and ill with the wicked (3:10–11). Arminians catch a telling: God is their safeguard and portion, and they eat the fruit of their deeds (Wesley). Reformed interpreters typically hear a settled conviction that providence never confounds good and bad, and that the righteous will not be defrauded of the reward of good conduct (Calvin), and not a works-gospel.

Isaiah 3:11

11Woe to the wicked! Disaster is upon them, for the deeds of their hands will be paid back to them.

Arminian

Disaster is upon them, for the deeds of their hands will be paid back to them. What his hands have done will be paid back to him. The pair of v. 10. Hands still deal.

Ill matching hands. Not a fake warning.

Reformed

Calvin: contrast to comfort the godly and terrify the wicked. Do not think chance rules, or that God punishes without justice, or that good and bad have the same reward. Hold that the wicked man’s condition will be most wretched; he brings on himself the evil that falls on his head.

The dealing is repaid by a just judge. The wicked receive what their hands have done.

Isaiah 3:12

12As for my people, children are their oppressors, and women rule over them. My people, those who lead you cause you to err, and destroy the way of your paths.

Arminian

My people, children are their oppressors, and women rule over them. Guides mislead and swallow the way of your paths. Wesley: women—weak and effeminate rulers; rulers civil and ecclesiastical.

The boy-princes of v. 4 in another key. Unfit heads, weak where they should have been fathers. Guides still mislead; the people can still refuse the path.

Reformed

Calvin: madness that they obstinately shake off God’s yoke and yield abject submission to men of no estimation. God has struck them with cowardice. They who govern cause thee to err: the rulers are the plague; no worse destruction than bad princes. He does not here mean flattering prophets so much as heads who corrupt by example.

Wrath appointed these heads. Both confess: my people, infants, women as unfit rule, guides mislead.

Isaiah 3:13

13The LORD stands up to contend, and stands to judge the peoples.

Arminian

The LORD stands up to contend, and stands to judge the peoples. Wesley: he will shortly stand as judge to enquire and give sentence; to judge—to defend and deliver them.

The LORD holds court over the peoples. He contends; elders still have to answer.

Reformed

Calvin: so long as wickedness rages, we think He is idle. After complaining of princes, he adds that the Lord will do what His authority demands. He sits as judge in heaven and will call them to account.

He was never absent. Either reading: contend, judge, peoples.

Isaiah 3:14

14The LORD will enter into judgment with the elders of his people and their leaders: “It is you who have eaten up the vineyard. The plunder of the poor is in your houses.

Arminian

The Lord comes to judge the elders and princes of his people: you have eaten up the vineyard. The plunder of the poor is in your houses. Wesley: ancients—princes chosen from ripe years; eaten—destroyed instead of preserving; spoil—goods violently taken from the poor.

Judah as the Lord’s planting (soon Isa 5). Spoil in houses is theft in the gates of 1:23. They ate what they should have dressed.

Reformed

Calvin: God contends with the ancients—not a lawful assembly in which they sit as deputies, but a suit against them. Psalm 82: He stands in the assembly of the gods. You have destroyed the vineyard: vine-dressers who devoured like wild beasts. Spoil of the poor in palaces that ought to be a sanctuary is sacrilege.

“Vineyard”—double crime—they wasted what He loved. Elders, princes, vineyard, spoil, poor.

Isaiah 3:15

15What do you mean that you crush my people, and grind the face of the poor?” says the Lord, GOD of Armies.

Arminian

What do you mean that you crush my people, grind the face of the poor? “Says the Lord, GOD of Armies.” The last word of the range is His question.

An interrogation of grinders. My people, the poor—Judah’s crushed, not a slogan. They can still stop the millstone.

Reformed

Calvin: other particulars of haughty, cruel rule. The poor ought to betake themselves to the magistrate as father. What? What effrontery to abuse the mean. God speaks: the accusation is not from men. Majesty meets princes who think themselves beyond advice. From the greater to the less: if He punishes princes who destroyed the vineyard, the lowest will not escape.

The Lord of hosts is the plaintiff. Crushing, grinding, poor, Lord God of hosts.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What older interpreters in each camp said about a city stripped of stay, a face like Sodom, and a word to the righteous.

Arminian Notable voices

“The judge—the civil magistrates. The ancient—whose wisdom was increased by long experience. Oppressed—by thy command or permission of such childish rulers. The shew—their pride and impiety manifestly shews itself in their very looks. They declare—they act it publicly, casting off all fear of God. Rewarded—procured a fit recompense for their wickedness, even utter ruin. Say ye—O ye priests and Levites, that God will be their safeguard and portion. Women—weak and effeminate rulers. Ancients—the princes; eaten—destroyed instead of preserving; spoil—the goods violently taken from the poor.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 3:2–14)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Lord Jehovah of hosts will take away from Jerusalem every help by which you think you are upheld. Say, it shall be well with the righteous: hold it to be a settled point. He does not bid them tell the righteous man, but bids every man be fully convinced that happy will be the condition of the righteous, though he may only appear to be unhappy. He shall eat the fruit of his doings: he shall not be defrauded of the reward of his good conduct. Woe unto the wicked: think not that blind chance rules, or that God punishes without regard to justice. They have rewarded evil unto themselves. Acknowledge that it has been done by yourselves; give glory to a righteous judge.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 3:1, 9–11)
