Passage

Isaiah 1

Verses 10–20 · ESV · Voices

The remnant of 1:9 does not cancel the charge. Rulers of Judah are named Sodom, the people Gomorrah: hear the word. Multitudes of sacrifice, new moons, and spread hands cannot cover blood. Wash; cease evil; learn good; seek justice for fatherless and widow. Come, let us reason: scarlet may be snow. If willing and obedient, eat the good of the land; if they refuse, the sword—for the mouth of the Lord has spoken.

Isaiah 1:10

10Hear the word of the Lord, you rulers of Sodom! Give ear to the teaching of our God, you people of Gomorrah!

Arminian

Hear the word of the Lord, rulers of Sodom; give ear to the teaching of our God, people of Gomorrah. Wesley: so called for their resemblance of them in wickedness; the law is the message from God, their great lawgiver.

Arminian hear: 1:9’s survivors are still addressed as Sodom. Historic Judah, not a newspaper. The resemblance is moral, not a millennial chart. A real word still finds real ears.

Reformed

Calvin: milder treatment than Sodom is mercy, not lesser guilt. Rulers named Sodom, people Gomorrah: not two different crimes, but one bundle. He strips disguises—Abraham’s name, ceremonies, national grandeur.

Reformed hear: the word unmasks. Shared: Sodom and Gomorrah as names for Judah’s rulers and people. Sacrifices are next.

Isaiah 1:11

11“What to me is the multitude of your sacrifices? says the Lord; I have had enough of burnt offerings of rams and the fat of well-fed beasts; I do not delight in the blood of bulls, or of lambs, or of goats.

Arminian

What to me is the multitude of your sacrifices? Enough of rams and fat; no delight in the blood of bulls, lambs, or goats. Wesley: God is a spirit, and cannot be satisfied with carnal oblations; he expects hearts and lives as well as bodies and sacrifices. Even the fat and blood reserved for Him are rejected here.

Arminian multitude: real offerings, really refused. Quantity is not devotion. The command was never “more rams.”

Reformed

Calvin: God speaks so the lawgiver interprets His own law. Sacrifices were appointed not as a satisfaction by which He should be appeased, but as aids to piety. Hypocrites make all holiness consist of the services.

Reformed multitude: ceremonies without the word as their soul are empty. Shared: the Lord is full of burnt offerings. No culture-war on liturgy; Judah’s altar is the scene.

Isaiah 1:12

12When you come to appear before me, who has required of you this trampling of my courts?

Arminian

When you come to appear before me, who has required this trampling of my courts? Wesley: the three solemn feasts or other occasions; what He commanded was not chiefly external sacrifice, but true repentance, faith in the promises, and sincere resolutions of devotion.

Arminian who required: a real appearing that misses the thing required. Courts can be trodden by feet that will not turn. Agency under a God who actually required the heart.

Reformed

Calvin: who hath required this at your hand? He will not be worshipped in any way but what He has enjoined. Obedience is what remains (1 Sam 15:22). Their close attendance is only treading pavements.

Reformed who required: will-worship is labor thrown away. Shared: appearing, trampling. Vain offerings follow.

Isaiah 1:13

13Bring no more vain offerings; incense is an abomination to me. New moon and Sabbath and the calling of convocations—I cannot endure iniquity and solemn assembly.

Arminian

Bring no more vain offerings; incense an abomination; new moon, Sabbath, convocations—He cannot endure iniquity and solemn assembly. Wesley: the solemn meeting is the last and most solemn day of each of the three feasts.

Arminian vain: appointed days, emptied. Sabbath and assembly are not the problem; iniquity in them is. Historic feasts of Judah, not a modern worship fight.

Reformed

Calvin: not only unprofitable but detested, because the name of God is falsely employed. Ceremonies were for training children toward faith; hypocrites keep the form and leave out faith, repentance, and Christ.

Reformed vain: a deceitful mask. Shared: incense, new moon, Sabbath. His soul hates is v. 14.

Isaiah 1:14

14Your new moons and your appointed feasts my soul hates; they have become a burden to me; I am weary of bearing them.

Arminian

Your new moons and appointed feasts my soul hates; they have become a burden; I am weary of bearing them. The calendar is theirs now—“your” moons—because they have made it a cloak.

Arminian hates: a real weariness in God toward a real burden. The feasts remain His appointments; the soul-hate falls on the use. Judah can still keep a day and lose the Day-giver.

Reformed

Calvin: not merely useless but wicked when spiritual truth is absent. Superstition is either inventing what God has not commanded, or fixing on the outward form of what He has. He heaps expressions because men are wanton in contriving worship.

Reformed hates: God detests His glory infringed. Shared: burden, weary. Spread hands are v. 15.

Isaiah 1:15

15When you spread out your hands, I will hide my eyes from you; even though you make many prayers, I will not listen; your hands are full of blood.

Arminian

When you spread out your hands, I will hide my eyes; many prayers, I will not listen; your hands are full of blood. Wesley: blood—you are guilty of murder and oppression.

Arminian hands: real prayer-posture, real deafness. Blood here is oppression in Judah’s courts, not a slogan. Many words do not pry the eyes open.

Reformed

Calvin: spreading hands is not condemned; hypocrisy is. Faith is the mother of calling on God; without it, empty mockery. Hands full of blood: not only open killers but those who take a neighbor’s life by fraud and starve the simple. God judges hidden tricks as murder.

Reformed hands: He is deaf to the cry that will not drop the knife. Shared: hide, not listen, blood. Wash is the next word.

Isaiah 1:16

16Wash yourselves; make yourselves clean; remove the evil of your deeds from before my eyes; cease to do evil,

Arminian

Wash yourselves; make yourselves clean; remove the evil of your deeds from before my eyes; cease to do evil. Wesley: wash—cleanse your hearts and hands.

Arminian wash: a real command to these rulers and this people. Hearts and hands, not a new pile of rams. The call is not a fake warning and not a works-gospel: scarlet will be snow in v. 18. Match the hub: they are to cease, under a King who actually commands.

Reformed

Calvin: nothing pleases God unless it proceed from a pure conscience. He does not mean that men repent by their own free-will; there is no other remedy than that they appear pure before God. Sacred writers attribute to men what the Spirit works; Ezekiel’s clean water is His, and to Him belongs repentance.

Reformed wash: the imperative names the gift’s fruit. Shared: wash, clean, cease. Learn good is v. 17.

Isaiah 1:17

17learn to do good; seek justice, correct oppression; bring justice to the fatherless, plead the widow's cause.

Arminian

Learn to do good; seek justice, correct oppression; bring justice to the fatherless, plead the widow’s cause. Wesley: begin to live soberly, righteously, and godly; show religion to God by practising justice to men; defend and deliver them.

Arminian learn: neighbor-justice is the evidence, not a culture-war plank. Fatherless and widow in Judah’s gates. They can begin—this is the same people told to wash.

Reformed

Calvin: fruits of repentance in the whole life. Two heads: cease to do evil, and do well. He will not have us keep from harm and skip kindness, nor rob one to endow another. Fatherless and widow: those from whom men expect no reward; the Lord takes their charge.

Reformed learn: outward fruit of an inward washing the Spirit gives. Shared: justice, oppression, fatherless, widow. Reason together is v. 18.

Isaiah 1:18

18“Come now, let us reason together, says the Lord: though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they are red like crimson, they shall become like wool.

Arminian

Come now, let us reason together: though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though red like crimson, like wool. Shared gospel in the verse: the Lord invites a stained people, and names a whitening He performs.

Arminian come: a real invitation to reason, not a trap. Scarlet is theirs; snow is His. The next verse’s willing is not payment for the wash. Pardon is offered to these same hands that were full of blood.

Reformed

Calvin: He does not refuse to dispute; hypocrites will still be speechless. He does not pursue offenses to the utmost. If we sincerely turn, He immediately returns to favor and blots out all remembrance of sins—makes us as white as snow. He is satisfied with cleanness of heart.

Reformed come: the reasoning is His kindness, not a bargain among equals. Shared: scarlet, snow, crimson, wool. The if of v. 19 is the fork.

Isaiah 1:19

19If you are willing and obedient, you shall eat the good of the land;

Arminian

If you are willing and obedient, you shall eat the good of the land. Wesley: if you are fully resolved to obey all my commands; together with pardon, you shall receive temporal and worldly blessings.

Arminian if: a real condition under the Lord’s rule. Willing is not Pelagian leftover: grace has spoken in v. 18; they must still resolve. Historic land of Judah—the good of that land—not a newspaper prosperity. Match the hub: a King who governs and provides, leaving this willing as a real second cause.

Reformed

Calvin: papists torture this as if happiness were at the disposal of free will. The Lord does not here describe the extent of our ability. Sinners are justly the voluntary agents of their calamities. It is a special gift of God when a man aims at what is good; it is their own wickedness that hinders the reprobate. Nothing but our ingratitude keeps us from the goodness He freely offers.

Reformed if: the willing is inside the gift, not a bare permission after snow is offered. Shared: willing, obedient, eat the good. Refuse is v. 20.

Key contrast Both confess the call is real and the pardon of v. 18 is the Lord’s: scarlet may be snow. Arminians typically hear a real “if you are willing” under that grace—fully resolved to obey (Wesley), not a fake warning and not a purchase of snow. Reformed readers typically hear no free-will proof: the Spirit washes (Calvin on 1:16); it is a special gift when a man aims at good, and the reprobate are hindered by their own wickedness.

Isaiah 1:20

20but if you refuse and rebel, you shall be eaten by the sword; for the mouth of the Lord has spoken.”

Arminian

If you refuse and rebel, you shall be eaten by the sword; for the mouth of the Lord has spoken. The fork of v. 19 still stands: refusing is as real as willing. The sword is the Lord’s spoken end, not a slogan against empires.

Arminian refuse: they can still say no after snow is offered. Historic Judah under Assyria’s coming blade. The mouth has spoken; the rebel still refuses.

Reformed

Calvin: there will be no end of calamities till they are destroyed. Heavier judgments await if they imagine they have already suffered enough. The mouth of the Lord: not a mortal’s word, not changeable. He mentions that mouth to terrify the slumber of vice.

Reformed refuse: obstinacy is theirs; the sword is His decree. Shared: refuse, rebel, sword, the mouth has spoken.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on vain offerings, a real washing, and an “if you are willing”—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 1:10–20.

Arminian Notable voices

“Of Sodom—so called for their resemblance of them in wickedness. To me—who am a spirit, and therefore cannot be satisfied with such carnal oblations, but expect to have your hearts and lives, as well as your bodies and sacrifices, presented unto me. Who required—the thing I commanded was not only, nor chiefly, that you should offer external sacrifices, but that you should do it with true repentance, with faith in my promises, and sincere resolutions of devoting yourselves to my service. Wash—cleanse your hearts and hands. If—if you are fully resolved to obey all my commands. Shall eat—together with pardon, you shall receive temporal and worldly blessings.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 1:10–19)

Reformed Notable voices

“Wash you, make you clean. He does not mean that men repent by their own exercise of free-will; but he shows that there is no other remedy but this, that they shall appear pure in the sight of God. The sacred writers attribute to men what is wrought in them by the Spirit of God, whom Ezekiel calls clean water, because to him belongs the work of repentance. If ye be willing and obedient: it is a special gift of God when a man aims at what is good; but it is equally true that it is their own wickedness that hinders the reprobate from applying their mind to it. Come now, and let us reason together: if we sincerely turn to him, he will immediately return to favor with us, and will blot out all remembrance of our sins.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 1:16–19)
