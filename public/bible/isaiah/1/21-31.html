Passage

Isaiah 1

Verses 21–31 · ESV · Voices

After the offer of snow, the charge turns to the city: faithful Jerusalem has become a whore; silver is dross; princes love bribes and ignore widow and fatherless. The Mighty One of Israel will get relief from foes, smelt dross, restore judges, and name her righteous again. Zion is redeemed by justice; those who repent, by righteousness. Rebels are broken. Oaks and gardens of idolatry wither; the strong and his work burn, with none to quench.

Isaiah 1:21

21How the faithful city has become a whore, she who was full of justice! Righteousness lodged in her, but now murderers.

Arminian

How the faithful city has become a whore, she who was full of justice; righteousness lodged in her, but now murderers. Wesley: Jerusalem, faithful in former reigns; an harlot filled with idolatry; murderers names all unrighteous practice under one gross kind.

Arminian city: historic Jerusalem, not a newspaper. Real lodging of righteousness, real fall. The whoredom is idolatry and blood in her gates—not a culture-war on cities.

Reformed

Calvin: astonishment joined with grief. The Church is the wife of God; Jerusalem held that rank while she kept spiritual chastity. Murderers: not only assassins, but men who under pretense of justice gained a neighbor’s property.

Reformed city: a monstrous revolt from pledged allegiance. Shared: faithful, then whore; justice, then murderers. Dross is v. 22.

Isaiah 1:22

22Your silver has become dross, your best wine mixed with water.

Arminian

Your silver has become dross, your best wine mixed with water. The metal still shines; the cup still looks like wine. The city has not yet been openly overturned.

Arminian silver: real debasement they can still see if they will. Hypocrisy keeps the color. The smelting of v. 25 will not be a fake warning.

Reformed

Calvin: two comparisons: dross instead of gold, wine that keeps its color and has lost its flavor. Outward affairs not yet overthrown, yet the condition widely different. Hypocrites assume a false color of silver.

Reformed silver: God is not fooled by the sheen. Shared: dross, mixed wine. Princes are next.

Isaiah 1:23

23Your princes are rebels and companions of thieves. Everyone loves a bribe and runs after gifts. They do not bring justice to the fatherless, and the widow's cause does not come to them.

Arminian

Princes are rebels and companions of thieves; everyone loves a bribe; fatherless get no justice, the widow’s cause does not come. Wesley: rebellious against their sovereign Lord; companions of thieves by connivance and by the same violence; gifts are bribes that pervert justice.

Arminian princes: real rulers in Judah’s gates, real choices. The ignored widow of 1:17 is still ignored. No culture-war on “elites”; these are named thieves.

Reformed

Calvin: he points to the source of the evil. No disease worse than what spreads from the head. Companions of thieves: by taking part of the booty they grant permission to steal. A judge corrupted by a bribe cannot but let crimes abound.

Reformed princes: avarice has unbound the city. Shared: rebels, bribes, fatherless, widow. Therefore is v. 24.

Isaiah 1:24

24Therefore the Lord declares, the Lord of hosts, the Mighty One of Israel: “Ah, I will get relief from my enemies and avenge myself on my foes.

Arminian

Therefore the Lord, the Lord of hosts, the Mighty One of Israel: Ah, I will get relief from my enemies and avenge myself on my foes. The city’s own rebels are now named His enemies.

Arminian therefore: after ignored widow and loved bribe, the Mighty One speaks. Relief is His; the foes are still Judah’s princes who would not hear. Historic title, not a slogan against empires.

Reformed

Calvin: having shown the crime, He takes the names that mark power enough to avenge. Those who wasted the city are treated as adversaries of God, not merely of the poor.

Reformed therefore: the mouth that offered snow (1:18) now eases Himself of foes. Shared: Lord of hosts, Mighty One, enemies, foes. The hand turns in v. 25.

Isaiah 1:25

25I will turn my hand against you and will smelt away your dross as with lye and remove all your alloy.

Arminian

I will turn my hand against you and will smelt away your dross as with lye and remove all your alloy. Wesley: purge out the incorrigible; for those who are curable, by my word and by the furnace of affliction, purge out the corruption that yet remains.

Arminian smelt: a real furnace on a real city. Two outcomes under one hand—incorrigible taken out, curable actually cleaned. Not a works-gospel: they do not smelt themselves. Not a fake warning: dross burns. Match the hub: a King who judges, leaving this remaining corruption as theirs to lose in the fire He lights.

Reformed

Calvin: an alleviation—the Church will be preserved. Purification is God’s own work. Rods would be of no avail if He did not touch hearts inwardly. This is a special favor to His elect; repentance is a peculiar work of the Holy Spirit, or chastisement only hardens. Not that the Church is entirely without stain in this world, but her holiness will shine like pure silver.

Reformed smelt: the hand that turns does the purging. Shared: dross, lye, alloy. Judges restored is v. 26.

Key contrast Both confess the Lord Himself smelts Jerusalem’s dross (1:25) and that rebels are not spared (1:28). Arminians typically hear a real divide in the furnace: incorrigible purged out, curable purged by word and affliction (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear purification as God’s own work and repentance as the Spirit’s peculiar gift to the elect; rods do not avail unless He touches the heart (Calvin).

Isaiah 1:26

26And I will restore your judges as at the first, and your counselors as at the beginning. Afterward you shall be called the city of righteousness, the faithful city.”

Arminian

I will restore your judges as at the first, and your counselors as at the beginning; afterward called the city of righteousness, the faithful city. Wesley: princes shall hearken to wise and faithful counselors; thou shalt be such.

Arminian restore: a real afterward for this city. The name she lost in v. 21 is spoken back. Judges are still men under the restored name.

Reformed

Calvin: restoration of the Church in the city’s old titles. What men ruined in the gates, God puts back. The faithful name is His gift after the smelting, not a vote of the princes.

Reformed restore: He names her righteous when He has purged. Shared: judges, counselors, faithful city. Zion redeemed is v. 27.

Isaiah 1:27

27Zion shall be redeemed by justice, and those in her who repent, by righteousness.

Arminian

Zion shall be redeemed by justice, and those in her who repent, by righteousness. Wesley: delivered from enemies by God’s righteous judgment, purging out the incorrigible; converts—her returners from captivity; righteousness is His faithfulness or His goodness.

Arminian repent: a real class in her—those who turn. Historic returners to the land, not a millennial chart. The snow of 1:18 still meets a people who repent. Justice is the Lord’s; the repenting is not a purchase of it.

Reformed

Calvin: restoration does not depend on the will of men but is founded on the justice of God. Do not look at yourselves for hope. It is a great mistake to take justice and judgment as the Church’s well-ordered civic life. They that return: dispersed exiles gathered again.

Reformed repent: the returning is inside His justice, not a contribution to it. Shared: Zion redeemed, those who return. Rebels in v. 28 are the other half.

Isaiah 1:28

28But rebels and sinners shall be broken together, and those who forsake the Lord shall be consumed.

Arminian

Rebels and sinners shall be broken together; those who forsake the Lord shall be consumed. The furnace has two ends. Wesley’s incorrigible land here.

Arminian broken: real forsaking, real consuming. The offer of snow did not make revolt imaginary. Historic Judah, not a party label.

Reformed

Calvin: the same justice that redeems Zion breaks those who will not return. Special grace to the remnant does not cancel the breaking of the forsakers.

Reformed broken: effectual end of revolt. Shared: rebels, sinners, forsake, consumed. Oaks are next.

Isaiah 1:29

29For they shall be ashamed of the oaks that you desired, and you shall blush for the gardens that you have chosen.

Arminian

They shall be ashamed of the oaks you desired, and blush for the gardens you have chosen. Wesley: oaks consecrated after the manner of the heathen to idolatry; gardens in which they committed idolatry.

Arminian oaks: real groves in Judah, real choosing. Shame is theirs when the thing chosen cannot save. Not a culture-war on trees.

Reformed

Calvin: the objects of their desire become their disgrace. Chosen gardens: will-worship they picked. The blush is the Lord’s doing in the collapse of the idol.

Reformed oaks: He shames what they loved. Shared: oaks, gardens, desired, chosen. The likeness is v. 30.

Isaiah 1:30

30For you shall be like an oak whose leaf withers, and like a garden without water.

Arminian

You shall be like an oak whose leaf withers, and like a garden without water. The worshiper becomes the thing worshiped: dry leaf, dry plot.

Arminian like: a real withering of people who chose the grove. Second causes still drink or thirst. The Lord who left survivors (1:9) can also dry a garden.

Reformed

Calvin: they become what they trusted—an oak that cannot keep its leaf, a garden that cannot keep its spring. Judgment fits the idol.

Reformed like: the likeness is decreed, not accidental. Shared: withered oak, waterless garden. Tinder is v. 31.

Isaiah 1:31

31And the strong shall become tinder, and his work a spark; both of them shall burn together, with none to quench them.

Arminian

The strong shall become tinder, and his work a spark; both burn together, with none to quench them. Wesley: the strong are your idols, thought able to defend you; consumed as tow by fire; the maker of the idol can save neither himself nor his workmanship.

Arminian both: idol and maker, historic Judah’s strength and its product. None to quench is the Lord’s last word in the chapter, not a slogan. Agency remains in the making; fire remains His.

Reformed

Calvin: the mighty and what they wrought kindle each other. No quencher: when God lights this, neither prince nor idol puts it out.

Reformed both: the spark is in the work itself under His hand. Shared: tinder, spark, burn, none to quench.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a faithful city turned whore, a furnace that smelts dross, and Zion redeemed—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 1:21–31.

Arminian Notable voices

“The city—Jerusalem, which in the reign of former kings was faithful to God. An harlot—is filled with idolatry. Murderers—under that one gross kind he comprehends all sorts of unrighteous men and practices. Companions of thieves—partly by connivance, partly by practising the same violence. And purge—I will purge out of thee those wicked men that are incorrigible, and for those of you that are curable, I will by my word, and by the furnace of affliction, purge out all that corruption that yet remains in you. Redeemed—delivered from enemies and calamities, by God’s righteous judgment, purging out the incorrigible. Converts—her returners, those who shall come out of captivity into their own land.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 1:21–27)

Reformed Notable voices

“How is the faithful city become an harlot! She who was a chaste wife is now a harlot. I will turn my hand upon thee: an alleviation, that the Church would be preserved. I will purge away thy dross: the purification of the Church is God’s own work. Rods would be of no avail if he did not make them useful by touching their hearts inwardly. Repentance is a true and peculiar work of the Holy Spirit; otherwise the sinner would be more hardened by chastisements. Zion shall be redeemed with judgment: the restoration does not depend on the will of men, but is founded on the justice of God. It is a great mistake to consider justice and judgment to refer to the Church, as if Isaiah were speaking about the well-ordered condition of a city. They that return: the exiles, widely dispersed, will again be gathered.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 1:21–27)
