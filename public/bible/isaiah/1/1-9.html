Passage

Isaiah 1

Verses 1–9 · ESV · Voices

Isaiah son of Amoz sees concerning Judah and Jerusalem in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. Heaven and earth are summoned: the Lord has reared children who rebel. Ox and donkey know; Israel does not. A sinful nation is struck from head to foot. Country and cities lie desolate before foreigners. Zion is left like a booth. If the Lord of hosts had not left a few survivors, they would have been Sodom.

Isaiah 1:1

1The vision of Isaiah the son of Amoz, which he saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah.

Arminian

The vision of Isaiah son of Amoz, concerning Judah and Jerusalem, in the days of four named kings of Judah. Wesley: visions, because they were as clearly represented to the prophets’ minds as bodily objects to men’s eyes; principally Judah, yet not exclusively.

Arminian vision: a real seer in real reigns. Historic Judah, not a newspaper. The fork is not whether Isaiah saw; it is how the Lord governs what he saw.

Reformed

Calvin: vision here denotes the certainty of the doctrine—nothing in this book not made known to Isaiah by God. Concerning Judah and Jerusalem as the chief subject; other nations are not outside his office, but Judah is who he is sent to.

Reformed vision: God opened the prophet’s eyes. Shared: Uzziah through Hezekiah, Judah and Jerusalem. Assyria’s shadow is the history, not a modern chart.

Isaiah 1:2

2Hear, O heavens, and give ear, O earth; for the Lord has spoken: “Children have I reared and brought up, but they have rebelled against me.

Arminian

Hear, O heavens, and give ear, O earth: the Lord has spoken. Children have I reared and brought up, but they have rebelled against me. Wesley: he calls in the whole creation to bear witness against Israelites past hearing; this is the Lord’s plea, of whose equity all creatures may be judges.

Arminian children: real rearing, real revolt. The Most High actually brought them up; the rebellion is still theirs. Match the hub: a King who governs and judges, leaving this revolt as a real second cause—not a puppet-string.

Reformed

Calvin: Isaiah imitates Moses (Deut 32:1). A prodigy that should strike even the elements: children nourished, then in revolt. God had exhausted kindness toward this people as toward a vineyard (Isa 5:4). They have revolted, not merely despised.

Reformed children: the rearing is the Father’s; the revolt does not make Him a spectator. Shared: heavens and earth hear; Judah rebelled. The ox is next.

Isaiah 1:3

3The ox knows its owner, and the donkey its master's crib, but Israel does not know, my people do not understand.”

Arminian

The ox knows its owner, and the donkey its master’s crib; Israel does not know, my people do not understand. Wesley: know me their owner and master; knowing is practical and includes reverence and obedience.

Arminian know: they ought to have known. Crib and owner are given; the not-knowing is guilt, not missing data. Creaturely Israel under a real Owner.

Reformed

Calvin: worse than the Gentiles would have been charge enough; he sends them to ox and ass. Israel is emphatic: the name of the wrestler is a reproach in bastard mouths. They do not know their Owner or their crib.

Reformed know: dullness after such feeding is monstrous. Shared: ox knows; Israel does not. The ah of v. 4 names the nation.

Isaiah 1:4

4Ah, sinful nation, a people laden with iniquity, offspring of evildoers, children who deal corruptly! They have forsaken the Lord, they have despised the Holy One of Israel, they are utterly estranged.

Arminian

Ah, sinful nation, laden with iniquity, offspring of evildoers, children who deal corruptly. They have forsaken the Lord, despised the Holy One of Israel, utterly estranged. Wesley: a seed—children of wicked parents, whose guilt they inherit and whose example they follow; corrupters of themselves or others; backward instead of growing in grace.

Arminian ah: real forsaking. Inherited example does not cancel this generation’s corrupt dealing. The Holy One of Israel is despised by children He named.

Reformed

Calvin: hoi is astonishment and sorrow. Laden: not a slip, but sold to act wickedly. Degenerate: unlike their parents. They have forsaken the Lord—the source of all evils. They have despised the Holy One of Israel, who had chosen them alone.

Reformed ah: apostasy, not one crime. Shared: sinful nation, Holy One despised. Why still struck is v. 5.

Isaiah 1:5

5Why will you still be struck down? Why will you continue to rebel? The whole head is sick, and the whole heart faint.

Arminian

Why will you still be struck down? Why continue to rebel? Whole head sick; whole heart faint. Wesley: the very head and heart of the body politic, from which the plague is derived to the members.

Arminian why: the blows are real, and the continuing is theirs. Striking has not yet made them unwilling to rebel. No culture-war on “the state”; Judah’s head and heart are sick.

Reformed

Calvin: why should you be stricken any more? Every remedy tried; obstinacy past hope of cure. You will grow more faithless. Head and heart: vital parts injured, so the body cannot be healed.

Reformed why: chastisements that do not humble prove the height of wickedness. Shared: still struck, still rebelling. The sores are v. 6.

Isaiah 1:6

6From the sole of the foot even to the head, there is no soundness in it, but bruises and sores and raw wounds; they are not pressed out or bound up or softened with oil.

Arminian

From sole to head, no soundness: bruises, sores, raw wounds, not pressed out, bound up, or softened with oil. The figure stays medical. Judah is a body untreated.

Arminian wounds: historic sores of a people who would not take the oil. Agency remains in the refusing of the bandage. The Lord still offers a physician; they still bleed.

Reformed

Calvin: putrifying sores, a fountain of venom that cannot be stopped. No closing, binding, or ointment: without hope of relief, without comfort, without remedy. This is the display of severity after long patience.

Reformed wounds: the nation almost wasted, and still impenitent. Shared: no soundness. Desolate country is v. 7.

Isaiah 1:7

7Your country lies desolate, your cities are burned with fire; in your very presence foreigners devour your land; it is desolate, as overthrown by foreigners.

Arminian

Country desolate; cities burned; in your presence foreigners devour your land, as overthrown by foreigners. Wesley: the eye sees it to torment you, with no power in the hands to deliver; the overthrow of strangers who spare no persons because the land will not stay theirs.

Arminian foreigners: historic invaders of Judah—Syria, Israel, Assyria in these reigns—not a newspaper. Real cities, real fire. The devouring is under the Lord’s rule without emptying the armies of agency.

Reformed

Calvin: not a threat only but calamities already in view. Strangers from a distant country lay waste with more recklessness than neighbors who hope to garrison. If we do not repent, He does not desist.

Reformed foreigners: providence using second causes, including cruel ones. Shared: desolate land, burned cities. Not a modern-nation chart. Zion left is v. 8.

Isaiah 1:8

8And the daughter of Zion is left like a booth in a vineyard, like a lodge in a cucumber field, like a besieged city.

Arminian

The daughter of Zion is left like a booth in a vineyard, a lodge in a cucumber field, a besieged city. Wesley: left solitary, neighboring villages and country laid waste.

Arminian left: a real leftover city under siege. Booth and lodge are seasonal and frail. Zion is still called daughter. Mercy has not yet said Sodom.

Reformed

Calvin: vine-keepers’ cottage when grapes ripen; a night-watch over cucumbers. Either the country wasted and the city like a cottage, or the city itself brought down to a mean hut. Daughter of Zion: Jerusalem by the dignity of the temple.

Reformed left: the leftover is already a judgment. Shared: booth, lodge, besieged. The unless of v. 9 is the fork.

Isaiah 1:9

9If the Lord of hosts had not left us a few survivors, we should have been like Sodom, and become like Gomorrah.

Arminian

If the Lord of hosts had not left us a few survivors, we should have been like Sodom, and become like Gomorrah. Wesley has no separate note here; his “left solitary” (v. 8) and practical knowing (v. 3) still hold: survivors are the Lord’s leaving, and the revolt that nearly ended in Sodom was theirs.

Arminian remnant: historic few in Judah after fire and foreigners—not a modern-nation chart, not a millennial timetable. The Lord of hosts actually left them. Children reared (v. 2) really rebelled; a remnant is still mercy under that rule, a real leftover, not a fake warning. Match the hub: a King who governs and judges, leaving this sparing as compassion that does not cancel the revolt.

Reformed

Calvin: the Lord snatched as it were from the burning a very small remnant. You would be Sodom, were it not that God in compassion preserved a remnant. Special grace, that out of the general ruin He rescues His people. Paul, the best interpreter: through tender mercies a remnant shall be saved, that the Church may not utterly perish (Rom 9:27–29). Do not judge by numbers; God acknowledges a small chosen people.

Reformed remnant: effectual sparing, not a bare permission after the fire. Shared: Lord of hosts, few survivors, else Sodom. Historic Judah; Paul reads the same line as mercy that keeps a church in the world—not a newspaper, not a modern-nation chart.

Key contrast Both confess the remnant is the Lord’s leaving, not Judah’s luck: without it they would be Sodom (1:9). Arminians typically hear real revolt of reared children and a mercy that actually leaves survivors under that rule. Reformed readers typically hear special grace that snatches a seed so the Church not perish (Calvin; Paul in Rom 9:29). Not a modern-nation chart, and not a dispensational-versus-amillennial fight.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a vision over Judah, children who rebel, and a remnant the Lord of hosts left—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 1:1–9.

Arminian Notable voices

“Vision—or, the visions: this is the book of the visions or prophecies. As prophets were called Seers, so prophecies are called visions, because they were as clearly represented to the prophets’ minds as bodily objects are to men’s eyes. Hear—he directs his speech to those senseless creatures, that he might awaken the Israelites, whom he hereby proclaims to be so dull and stupid that they were past hearing, and therefore calls in the whole creation of God to bear witness against them. Know—me their owner and master. Knowing is here taken practically, and includes reverence and obedience. A seed—the children of wicked parents, whose guilt they inherit, and whose evil example they follow. Backward—instead of proceeding forward and growing in grace.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 1:1–4)

Reformed Notable voices

“I have nourished and brought up children, and they have rebelled against me. He relates a prodigy which fills him with such horror that he is compelled to summon dead creatures as witnesses. Except the Lord of hosts had left unto us a very small remnant, we should have been as Sodom. The Lord snatched as it were from the burning a very small remnant. You would be in the same condition that Sodom and Gomorrah now are, were it not that God, in compassion on you, has preserved a remnant. Through his tender mercies a remnant shall be saved, that the Church may not utterly perish.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 1:2, 9)
