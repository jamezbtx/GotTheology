Passage

Isaiah 6

Verses 1–13 · ESV · Voices

In the year King Uzziah died, Isaiah sees the Lord on a throne, high and lifted up; seraphim cry Holy, holy, holy. Thresholds shake; the house fills with smoke. Woe is me—unclean lips among an unclean people. A coal from the altar touches his mouth: guilt taken away, sin atoned. Whom shall I send, who will go for us? Here am I. Go: keep hearing, do not understand; make the heart dull, lest they turn and be healed. How long? Until cities lie waste. A tenth remains and is burned again; the holy seed is the stump.

Isaiah 6:1

1In the year that King Uzziah died I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up; and the train of his robe filled the temple.

Arminian

In the year King Uzziah died I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up; the train of his robe filled the temple. Wesley: in a vision; the Divine Majesty as he subsisteth in three persons; train—royal and judicial robe, for he is represented as a judge.

Arminian saw: historic year, historic temple. Shared: this is the Holy One of 1:4 now seen. John 12:41 will name the glory as Christ’s. Not a millennial camp.

Reformed

Calvin: not the first prophecy; Uzziah dead, Jotham succeeding; a new sealing when kings change. I saw the Lord: God never appeared as He is, but as men could receive. John says it was Christ; God never revealed Himself to the Fathers but in the Word; yet do not limit the title to Christ’s person—Isaiah saw the glory of Christ, the image of the invisible God. Throne: a Judge, that majesty might strike terror.

Reformed saw: the same Lord, present without deceit. Shared: Uzziah, throne, temple.

Isaiah 6:2

2Above him stood the seraphim. Each had six wings: with two he covered his face, and with two he covered his feet, and with two he flew.

Arminian

Above him stood the seraphim. Six wings: two covered the face, two the feet, two flew. Wesley: ministers attending; seraphim from fire—bright nature or fervent zeal; covered out of profound reverence.

Arminian seraphim: real attendants in the vision. Covering is worship, not a puzzle.

Reformed

Calvin: seraphim from fervor. Two wings to fly: ready obedience. Two covering the face: even angels cannot endure His brightness; how rash for men to intrude. Two lower: either not to contract earth’s defilement, or to conceal them from our view.

Reformed seraphim: the vision teaches distance. Shared: six wings, face, feet, flew.

Isaiah 6:3

3And one called to another and said: “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory!”

Arminian

One called to another: Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory. Wesley: singing in consort; thrice to intimate the Trinity; glory of His holiness as well as power, wisdom, and goodness.

Arminian holy: shared confession of the Holy One. Earth full—not a glory locked in Judah’s boast (2:6). Trinity is named, not a millennial scheme.

Reformed

Calvin: ancients used this for three persons; I do not disagree, and have no doubt the angels describe One God in Three Persons, but would rather employ stronger proofs against heretics. The repetition also marks unwearied praise. Whole earth full: implied contrast with Jews who thought His glory was only among themselves. This agrees with the blinding that opened Gentile admission.

Reformed holy: the same thrice-holy Lord. Shared: holy, holy, holy, Lord of hosts, earth full of glory.

Isaiah 6:4

4And the foundations of the thresholds shook at the voice of him who called, and the house was filled with smoke.

Arminian

Foundations of the thresholds shook at the voice, and the house was filled with smoke. Wesley: posts and door; violent motions tokens of anger; smoke here of anger, elsewhere of presence.

Arminian shook: a real temple quake in the vision. Historic house, historic smoke.

Reformed

Calvin: no mortal voice shakes lintels. Smoke the ordinary sign of meeting Him; here of power in executing judgment, and a restraint on curious research into His purpose.

Reformed shook: the voice is His. Shared: thresholds, smoke.

Isaiah 6:5

5And I said: “Woe is me! For I am lost; for I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips; for my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts!”

Arminian

Woe is me! I am lost; a man of unclean lips, among a people of unclean lips; my eyes have seen the King, the Lord of hosts. Wesley: a great sinner, particularly by the lips; unclean branch of an unclean tree; involved in their guilt; the sight gives cause to fear judgment.

Arminian woe: a real prophet undone. Holiness exposes lips. He still speaks the woe.

Reformed

Calvin: so terrified he expected destruction. Until God reveals Himself we think we are gods; when we have seen Him we know what we are. Hence true humility. Unclean lips: he names what he regarded as most valuable, the tongue consecrated to prophecy. Human doctrines have an uncleanness; nothing pure but what came from God. Sight of God brings death accidentally, through our fault, not His nature.

Reformed woe: the Judge’s presence kills self-confidence. Shared: lost, unclean lips, King, Lord of hosts.

Isaiah 6:6

6Then one of the seraphim flew to me, having in his hand a burning coal that he had taken with tongs from the altar.

Arminian

One of the seraphim flew, a burning coal taken with tongs from the altar. Wesley: by God’s command; coal a token and instrument of purification; altar of burnt-offering.

Arminian coal: from the altar, not from Isaiah. Cleansing is given.

Reformed

Calvin: purity of lips comes from God alone. Fire from the altar: no strange fire (Lev 10:1); all purity flows from God. The coal had no magic virtue; the angel administered, God authored.

Reformed coal: the altar’s fire, His. Shared: seraphim, coal, tongs, altar.

Isaiah 6:7

7And he touched my mouth and said: “Behold, this has touched your lips; your guilt is taken away, and your sin atoned for.”

Arminian

He touched my mouth: this has touched your lips; your guilt is taken away, and your sin atoned for. Wesley: only to touch, not to burn, which God could easily effect; a sign that I have pardoned and purged the uncleanness of thy lips.

Arminian touched: real atonement spoken over these lips. Guilt taken, then sending. Not a works-gospel.

Reformed

Calvin: the sign was not empty; the blessing signified was bestowed. In the sacraments the reality is given with the sign, distinguished but not separated. Doctrine is added; without the word, no sacrament. Free from pollution, he may appear as God’s representative.

Reformed touched: God purges, then employs. Shared: lips, guilt taken away, sin atoned.

Isaiah 6:8

8And I heard the voice of the Lord saying, “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” Then I said, “Here I am! Send me.”

Arminian

Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Here I am! Send me. Wesley: to deliver the following message; I and us—plurality of persons in the Godhead.

Arminian send: a real offer after atonement. He still says here. Shared Trinity in the us, with Calvin.

Reformed

Calvin: on a revolting occasion—blinding the Jews—he is more fully assured. Whom shall I send: not that God hesitates, but a teacher of uncommon excellence is needed. Who will go for us: I am rather favorable to Three Persons, as in Let us create man. Here am I: cheerfulness from faith; he who lately lay like a dead man dreads no difficulty.

Reformed send: the call is His, the here is given after the coal. Shared: I, us, here I am, send me.

Isaiah 6:9

9And he said, “Go, and say to this people: “‘Keep on hearing, but do not understand; keep on seeing, but do not perceive.’

Arminian

Go, say to this people: Keep on hearing, but do not understand; keep on seeing, but do not perceive. Wesley: imperative, yet not a command of what they ought to do, but a prediction of what they would do.

Arminian go: a real sending to historic Judah. This people—not my people in the address. Hearing continues; understanding does not. The prediction meets a will already hard (5:19, 24).

Reformed

Calvin: necessary that Isaiah not fail: obstinacy for more than sixty years. Teach without good effect; do not regret it; leave consequences to God. Openly reprove their blind obstinacy. Preaching a sweet smell to God even when it brings death (2 Cor 2:15–16).

Reformed go: the mission is obedience, not success. Shared: this people, hear, not understand, see, not perceive.

Isaiah 6:10

10Make the heart of this people dull, and their ears heavy, and blind their eyes; lest they see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their hearts, and turn and be healed.”

Arminian

Make the heart of this people dull, and their ears heavy, and blind their eyes; lest they see, hear, understand, turn and be healed. Wesley: fat—stupid; ascribed to the prophet as to God in John 12:40, because God inflicted it by the prophet’s ministry—prediction of the effect, and withdrawing the Spirit. Lest they be able, as before they were not willing. Convert—turn to God.

Arminian make: they were unwilling first. The sending meets that will; then the Spirit’s light is withdrawn, and they become unable. Not a fake warning: turn and be healed is still the gospel’s native end. Match the hub: a King who sends, leaving the first hardness as theirs.

Reformed

Calvin: not only fruitless labor but by instruction he will blind them. Blame lies with the people; they refuse His kindness. Hardening does not arise from the nature of the word but from depravity—accidental, as a weak eye blames the sun. If you inquire into the first cause, predestination; but that purpose is hidden; consider the cause before our eyes: rebellion. John: they could not believe, because Isaiah said this; the prediction was not the cause of unbelief. He did not send the Prophet because He intended to save the whole body already condemned; yet the word always has a few who find salvation. Healing’s first step is repentance.

Reformed make: judicial hardening on a rebel people; the word remains a savor of life to the remnant. Shared: dull heart, heavy ears, blind eyes, lest they turn and be healed.

Key contrast Both confess the Holy One on the throne, the coal that atones, and a real sending (6:1–8). Arminians typically hear 6:9–10 as prediction of what they would do, unwilling first, then the Spirit withdrawn so they become unable (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear a mission that judicially blinds: the word’s hardening is accidental to man’s depravity; first cause is hidden predestination, the open cause is rebellion (Calvin). Not a millennial fight.

Isaiah 6:11

11Then I said, “How long, O Lord?” And he said: “Until cities lie waste without inhabitant, and houses without people, and the land is a desolate waste,

Arminian

How long, O Lord? Until cities lie waste without inhabitant, houses without people, the land a desolate waste. Wesley: abrupt from passion; until destroyed first by Babylonians, afterward by Romans.

Arminian how long: a real prophet’s grief. Historic sequence, not a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: prophets sustain two characters—unshaken courage for God’s glory, and compassion for brethren. How long: if this blindness must come, let it not be permanent. The reply: no light punishment, but utter destruction.

Reformed how long: mercy asked; severity answered. Shared: how long, cities waste, houses empty.

Isaiah 6:12

12and the Lord removes people far away, and the forsaken places are many in the midst of the land.

Arminian

The Lord removes people far away, and the forsaken places are many in the midst of the land. Wesley: carried captive into far countries; houses and lands forsaken of their owners.

Arminian removes: historic exile, the far nations of 5:26. Forsaken land is Judah’s.

Reformed

Calvin: survivors of war are not exempt; they are led into captivity. The land desolate, some fled, some banished, some slain. Reward of obstinate rebellion.

Reformed removes: the stretched-out hand of 5:25 still. Shared: far away, forsaken places.

Isaiah 6:13

13And though a tenth remain in it, it will be burned again, like a terebinth or an oak, whose stump remains when it is felled.” The holy seed is its stump.

Arminian

Though a tenth remain, it will be burned again, like a terebinth or oak whose stump remains when felled. The holy seed is its stump. Wesley: a tenth—small remnant; return from Babylon; that remnant eaten a second time (Syria, then Romans); yet another remnant, a holy seed, who shall look on him whom they have pierced and mourn; winter trees keep a vital principle in the root; for their sake the land is not finally rooted out.

Arminian tenth: historic leftover, then another leftover. Holy seed is real remaining life, and Wesley names those who look on the Pierced—shared christology, not a millennial camp. The stump still lives.

Reformed

Calvin: diminished to a tenth. Like trees that cast leaves in winter and keep hidden vigor till spring: the Church in captivity appears dead timber, yet energy remains in the word. Holy seed: the elect, preserved by free mercy; banishment a cleansing that took away the ungodly. Some refer this to Christ; too far-fetched—extend it to all the godly; the holy seed is the substance of the Church.

Reformed stump: elect substance, not numbers. Shared: tenth, burned again, oak, stump, holy seed. Differ on remnant: Wesley’s sequential leftovers and a seed that looks on the Pierced; Calvin’s elect preserved by mercy as the Church’s substance.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a throne, a coal that atones, a sending that hardens, and a stump called holy seed—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 6:1–13.

Arminian Notable voices

“The Lord—the Divine Majesty as he subsisteth in three persons. Holy—repeated thrice, to intimate the Trinity of persons united in the Divine essence. Perceive not—the Hebrew words are imperative; yet not a command what the people ought to do, but a prediction what they would do. Because you have so long heard my words to no purpose, and have hardened your hearts, I will punish you in your own kind: I will still continue my word, but will withdraw my Spirit, so that you shall be as unable, as now you are unwilling, to understand. This making of their hearts fat is ascribed to the prophet, as to God in John 12:40, because God inflicted this judgment by the ministry of the prophet, partly by prediction, and partly by withdrawing the light of his Spirit. A tenth—a small remnant reserved. Yet there shall be another remnant, an holy seed, who shall afterwards look upon him whom they have pierced, and mourn over him. When their leaves are cast in winter, they have a vital principle in the root.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 6:1–13)

Reformed Notable voices

“John tells us that it was Christ (John 12:41), and justly, for God never revealed himself to the Fathers but in his eternal Word. Yet it is wrong to limit this to the person of Christ; the Prophet calls him God indefinitely, and yet Isaiah saw the glory of Christ, who was the image of the invisible God. Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? I am rather favorable to Three Persons in the Godhead. Harden the heart of this people: by his instruction he will also blind them. The blame of this blindness lies with the people; they refuse the amazing kindness of God. Blinding does not arise out of the nature of the word, but is accidental, and must be ascribed to the depravity of man. If you inquire into the first cause, we must come to the predestination of God; but that purpose is hidden, and we ought to consider the cause which lies plainly before our eyes, namely rebellion. The holy seed: the elect, who would be preserved by the free mercy of God. Some refer this to Christ; it will be more consistent to extend it to all the godly; the holy seed is the substance of the Church.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 6:1–13)
