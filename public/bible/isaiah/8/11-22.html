Passage

Isaiah 8

Verses 11–22 · ESV · Voices

The Lord, with a strong hand, warns Isaiah not to walk in this people’s way. Do not call conspiracy what they call conspiracy, or fear their fear. Honor the Lord of hosts as holy: He is sanctuary and a stone of offense to both houses, a snare to Jerusalem; many stumble. Bind the testimony among disciples. Isaiah waits while the Lord hides His face. He and the children given him are signs. Do not inquire of mediums; to the teaching and the testimony. The rest pass hungry through the land into thick darkness.

Isaiah 8:11

11For the Lord spoke thus to me with his strong hand upon me, and warned me not to walk in the way of this people, saying:

Arminian

The Lord spoke thus with his strong hand upon me, and warned me not to walk in the way of this people. Wesley: vehement, more than ordinary inspiration; the way of the generality of Judah, whose danger he foretells.

Arminian not to walk: a real warning to a real prophet. Historic Judah’s way—running to Assyria (7:9; 8:12)—not a newspaper. He can still refuse that road.

Reformed

Calvin: two temptations, outward (Assyria) and inward (the sacred people relying on man). The Lord seizes the hand, as a father a scholar’s, that Isaiah not throw the office away when faith appears banished. We would be driven every moment were we not held by His government.

Reformed strong hand: He holds the servant to the word. Shared: Lord spoke, strong hand, not walk in this people’s way.

Isaiah 8:12

12“Do not call conspiracy all that this people calls conspiracy, and do not fear what they fear, nor be in dread.

Arminian

Do not call conspiracy all that this people calls conspiracy, and do not fear what they fear, nor be in dread. Wesley: Isaiah and his children, do not consent to this confederacy with Assyria. Their fear: if they do not call Assyria, the two kings will destroy them.

Arminian conspiracy: historic Ahaz’s league, not a modern plot. They still name it; you still need not fear it. The two stumps of 7:4 are still smoke.

Reformed

Calvin: conspiracy as the league unbelievers longed to make with Assyria. Do not regard the counsels of the wicked though the whole people vie in them. Blind fear hurries to unlawful aid and does not look to the Lord. The godly are alarmed but compose their minds; the ungodly think of nothing but Assyrian help.

Reformed conspiracy: their fear is not yours. Shared: conspiracy, do not fear what they fear, do not dread. No open theism: the end of the league is already spoken (8:10).

Isaiah 8:13

13But the Lord of hosts, him you shall honor as holy. Let him be your fear, and let him be your dread.

Arminian

The Lord of hosts, him you shall honor as holy. Let him be your fear, and let him be your dread. Wesley: give him the glory of his power, goodness, and faithfulness by trusting his promises. Let God, not the kings of Syria and Israel, be the object of your fear.

Arminian honor: a real command. They can sanctify Him by trusting what He has said. Historic Lord of hosts, not a slogan. Match the hub: a King who is to be feared, leaving this honoring as theirs under that rule.

Reformed

Calvin: God is robbed of His holiness when we do not immediately betake ourselves to Him. No higher affront than to give way to fear as if He were not exalted above all events. Let Him be your fear: not horror that flees, but reverence that stays. Free from solicitude if sincere fear is engraven and does not pass away. The ungodly He harasses with uneasiness as revenge for their indifference.

Reformed honor: fearing Him is ascribing lawful government. Shared: Lord of hosts, honor as holy, your fear, your dread.

Isaiah 8:14

14And he will become a sanctuary and a stone of offense and a rock of stumbling to both houses of Israel, a trap and a snare to the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

Arminian

He will become a sanctuary and a stone of offense and a rock of stumbling to both houses of Israel, a trap and a snare to the inhabitants of Jerusalem. Wesley: sanctuary—a sure refuge to all that truly fear him and rely upon him. A stone—an occasion of sin and ruin, at whom they will take offence and stumble. Both houses. Jerusalem named as a wonder: the seat of the temple, yet it would reject Immanuel; the prediction keeps faith from shaking when it happens.

Arminian sanctuary: shared christology—this is the Lord, and Immanuel, refuge to those who actually fear and rely. They take offence; that is still theirs. Historic both houses, not a millennial camp. Not a fake warning: Jerusalem can stumble.

Reformed

Calvin: covering the true worshippers, a citadel in a lofty place. Contrast implied: sanctuary for the godly, destruction for the ungodly who stumble against Him. Both houses: the whole nation, Ephraim and Judah; some exceptions, but he speaks of the body. It belongs to God to receive men into His favor; stumbling is accidental. More clearly in Christ. Paul: it is God of whom the Prophet speaks (Rom 9:33)—the same God who created heaven and earth. Builders rejected the chief corner-stone (Ps 118:22).

Reformed sanctuary: He receives; they stumble against what was meant to save. Shared: sanctuary, stone of offense, both houses, Jerusalem.

Key contrast Both confess the Lord of hosts is sanctuary and stone: refuge and stumbling, and that this is fulfilled in Immanuel (Wesley; Calvin; Rom 9:33). Historic both houses, not a newspaper, and not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear a sure refuge to all that truly fear and rely, and a stone at which they take offence (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear God covering those He receives; stumbling is accidental to that receiving, and both houses as a body are broken (Calvin). Cause and effect, not open theism.

Isaiah 8:15

15And many shall stumble on it. They shall fall and be broken; they shall be snared and taken.”

Arminian

Many shall stumble on it. They shall fall and be broken; they shall be snared and taken. Wesley: many—not all; there shall be a remnant (4:2; 6:13). Stumble at that stone. Accomplished at the coming of the Messiah, whom the Jews rejected to their own destruction.

Arminian many: a real fall, and a real leftover. Not all. Shared remnant, as in 6:13. Historic rejection of Immanuel, not a chart.

Reformed

Calvin: they will find He excels them in strength and wisdom, to their destruction. Either utterly bruised or snared so they cannot extricate. The threatening also regards the godly, that they not hesitate to withdraw from the multitude. Peter: though many stumble, Christ is still a chosen and precious stone (1 Pet 2:4).

Reformed many: the body falls; the stone remains precious. Shared: many, stumble, fall, broken, snared, taken.

Isaiah 8:16

16Bind up the testimony; seal the teaching among my disciples.

Arminian

Bind up the testimony; seal the teaching among my disciples. Wesley: testimony and law are one—the word of God, especially the doctrine of the Messiah, which the generality will disown when He comes. Bind and seal: it shall certainly be fulfilled, yet kept secret from the unbelieving. Disciples: those taught of God.

Arminian disciples: a real company who are taught and keep the word. The seal is not a fake closed book: they can still be taught. Historic Isaiah’s circle, and those who will own Messiah.

Reformed

Calvin: a sealed letter, handled by many, read by few—the elect. Sealed to those who derive no advantage; unsealed to His own by the Spirit. Teachers must persevere though all revolt; the Lord will reserve some disciples. Isaiah is not to shut himself up: cry aloud to all, but be satisfied with the few.

Reformed disciples: reserved, unsealed by the Spirit. Shared: bind, testimony, seal, teaching, disciples.

Isaiah 8:17

17I will wait for the Lord, who is hiding his face from the house of Jacob, and I will hope in him.

Arminian

I will wait for the Lord, who is hiding his face from the house of Jacob, and I will hope in him. Wesley: notwithstanding this dreadful prophecy of Israel’s rejection. Wait—cast my care upon Him, expect the Messiah and His mercies on me and all believing Israelites. Hideth—withdraws favour from Israel.

Arminian wait: a real waiting under a real hiding. Hope is still theirs. Historic house of Jacob, not a modern-nation chart.

Reformed

Calvin: therefore—after the seal among disciples. Though the whole world revolt, and God hide His face from His people, wait with unshaken hope. The only remedy left. Offenses commonly turn us aside; this consolation is necessary when the Church is oppressed or the multitude is treacherous.

Reformed wait: stand when the face is hid. Shared: wait, Lord, hiding his face, house of Jacob, hope.

Isaiah 8:18

18Behold, I and the children whom the Lord has given me are signs and portents in Israel from the Lord of hosts, who dwells on Mount Zion.

Arminian

Behold, I and the children whom the Lord has given me are signs and portents in Israel from the Lord of hosts, who dwells on Mount Zion. Wesley: literally Isaiah concerning himself, mystically Christ (Heb 2:13). Children—spiritual children, begotten or brought up by his ministry. Wonders—a gazing-stock for our folly in believing God’s promises. Zion—where the temple was, and where Messiah was to set up His kingdom.

Arminian children: given, and also begotten by the ministry. Shared: Hebrews reads this as Christ and His. Signs in historic Israel, not a millennial camp. They can still believe the promises and look like fools for it.

Reformed

Calvin: I bring those preserved through my agency. Children: disciples. Whom the Lord hath given: undeserved election. He taught all; the doctrine advantages only those God drew by an inward operation of the Spirit. Readiness to believe does not depend on the will of men; some believe because they had been foreordained (Acts 13:48). Christ: the elect were given to Him by the Father (John 17:6; 6:44). Signs and wonders: the godly regarded as monsters. From the Lord of hosts who dwells in Zion: God on his side, against hypocrites who boast the temple contrary to the word.

Reformed children: given, drawn, kept. Shared: I and the children, given me, signs, Lord of hosts, Mount Zion.

Isaiah 8:19

19And when they say to you, “Inquire of the mediums and the necromancers who chirp and mutter,” should not a people inquire of their God? Should they inquire of the dead on behalf of the living?

Arminian

When they say, Inquire of the mediums and the necromancers who chirp and mutter—should not a people inquire of their God? Should they inquire of the dead on behalf of the living? Wesley: Israelites fallen into superstition; you—my children, armed against the common temptation. Mutter—low voice, as in their bellies. Living men should inquire of the living God, not dead idols or the spirits of dead men.

Arminian inquire: a real fork in a real famine of the word. Historic necromancy in Judah, not a newspaper occult war. They can still seek their God.

Reformed

Calvin: arm the godly with this answer: God alone ought to be consulted. Alludes to Deut 18: they would not lack a prophet. Depend entirely on His word. From the living to the dead: one God sufficient for living and dead; search heaven, earth, and hell, one God is enough.

Reformed inquire: the word, not the dead. Shared: mediums, chirp and mutter, inquire of their God, the dead, the living.

Isaiah 8:20

20To the teaching and to the testimony! If they will not speak according to this word, it is because they have no dawn.

Arminian

To the teaching and to the testimony! If they will not speak according to this word, it is because they have no dawn. Wesley: let the dispute be determined by God’s word, called law because they are obliged to believe and obey, and testimony because it witnesses God’s will and man’s duty. No light—darkness of their minds; they are blind and cannot see.

Arminian teaching: a real standard they ought to obey. No dawn is still their blindness. Not a works-gospel: the word is the Lord’s. Not a fake warning: if they will not speak it, they have no dawn.

Reformed

Calvin: the law contains the testimony of His will. A high commendation: doctrine of salvation and the rule of a good life. Forsake superstitions. Everything added to the word is a corruption of it; if we lend our ears to others we take a liberty He has forbidden. If they shall not speak according to this word: they evince their own blindness. Despise their folly so it not obstruct you (Matt 15:14). Even an angel otherwise is accursed (Gal 1:8).

Reformed teaching: the sealed word of v. 16, now the public test. Shared: teaching, testimony, this word, no dawn.

Isaiah 8:21

21They will pass through the land, greatly distressed and hungry. And when they are hungry, they will be enraged and will speak contemptuously against their king and their God, and turn their faces upward.

Arminian

They will pass through the land, greatly distressed and hungry; when hungry they will be enraged and speak contemptuously against their king and their God, and turn their faces upward. Wesley: their own land; destitute of food; their king—because he does not relieve, or by foolish counsels brought them here; their God—the idol they trusted, now unable; look to heaven for help.

Arminian pass: historic hunger in Judah after the refused word. They still curse. Not a newspaper famine.

Reformed

Calvin: wandering in the land promised as a perpetual inheritance. Hunger as part for every calamity. Fret: beginning of repentance in some; in the reprobate, rage without humility. Curse king and God: false confidence in idols, then in earthly defense; the king was their standard-bearer against the prophet.

Reformed pass: the land itself will not hold them. Shared: distressed, hungry, enraged, king, their God, faces upward.

Isaiah 8:22

22And they will look to the earth, but behold, distress and darkness, gloom of anguish. And they will be thrust into thick darkness.

Arminian

They will look to the earth, but behold, distress and darkness, gloom of anguish. And they will be thrust into thick darkness. Wesley: finding no help from heaven, they look downward, hither and thither for comfort.

Arminian look: heaven failed them because they would not have the word (v. 20). Historic gloom, not a millennial night. The disciples of v. 16 still have the sealed teaching.

Reformed

Calvin: whether conversion or reprobate rage, they see nothing but frightful calamities. Darkness: adversity. Driven to darkness: a heavier push, more danger of stumbling. Judgments so dreadful they are constrained to look up—and still the earth answers with gloom.

Reformed thrust: the end of walking in this people’s way. Shared: earth, distress, darkness, gloom, thick darkness.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a conspiracy not to fear, a Lord who is sanctuary and stone, and a testimony sealed among disciples—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 8:11–22.

Arminian Notable voices

“Spake—with a vehement and more than ordinary inspiration. Say not—thou Isaiah, and my children, do not consent to this confederacy with the king of Assyria. Their fear—that if they do not call in the Assyrian succours, they shall be destroyed by those two potent kings. Sanctify—give him the glory of his power, and goodness, and faithfulness, by trusting to his promises. Let God, and not the kings of Syria and Israel, be the object of your fear. Sanctuary—a sure refuge to all that truly fear him, and rely upon him. A stone—an occasion of sin and ruin, at whom they will take offence and stumble. Many—not all; for there shall be a remnant. This was accomplished at the coming of the Messiah, whom the Jews rejected to their own destruction. Disciples—those who were taught of God. Wait—I will cast my care upon him, and expect the accomplishment of his promise, in sending the Messiah. Behold—literally spoken by Isaiah concerning himself, but mystically concerning Christ (Heb 2:13). The children—his spiritual children, whom he had either begotten or brought up by his ministry.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 8:11–18)

Reformed Notable voices

“With a strong hand: the Lord lays hold, as a father seizes the hand of a scholar, that Isaiah not throw everything away when the sacred people rely on man rather than God. Say ye not, a conspiracy: the league unbelievers were desirous to contract with the Assyrian. Neither fear ye their fear: the ungodly, overcome by terror, think of nothing but the assistance of the Assyrians; the godly compose their minds, knowing God takes care of their preservation. Sanctify Jehovah of hosts: God is robbed of his holiness when we do not immediately betake ourselves to him. Let him be your fear: they will be free from solicitude if a sincere fear of God be deeply engraven. He shall be for a sanctuary: covering the true worshippers. But a stone of stumbling to both houses of Israel: he includes the whole nation. It belongs to God to receive men into his favor; stumbling is, as we would say, accidental. Paul shows it is God of whom the Prophet speaks (Rom 9:33). Bind up the testimony: a sealed letter, felt by many, read by few—the elect. The Lord unseals it to his own by the Spirit. I and the children whom the Lord hath given me: readiness to believe does not depend on the will of men; some of the multitude believe because they had been foreordained. In like manner Christ declares that the elect were given to him by the Father.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 8:11–18)
