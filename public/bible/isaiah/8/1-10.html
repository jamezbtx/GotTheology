Passage

Isaiah 8

Verses 1–10 · ESV · Voices

The Lord tells Isaiah to write Maher-shalal-hash-baz on a large tablet in common characters, with Uriah and Zechariah as witnesses. The prophetess bears a son by that name: before the boy can cry father or mother, Damascus’s wealth and Samaria’s spoil go before the king of Assyria. This people refuses Shiloah’s gentle waters and rejoices in Rezin; so the Lord brings the River—Assyria—over its banks, through Judah, to the neck, filling Immanuel’s land. Peoples, be shattered. Counsel will not stand: God is with us.

Isaiah 8:1

1Then the Lord said to me, “Take a large tablet and write on it in common characters, ‘Belonging to Maher-shalal-hash-baz.’”

Arminian

Take a large tablet and write on it in common characters, Belonging to Maher-shalal-hash-baz. Wesley: a great volume, large and legible; concerning that which the child’s name signifies.

Arminian write: a public name, Speed-spoil, haste-prey. Historic Damascus and Samaria, not a newspaper, and not a spiritualized raid on the devil. The tablet is for Judah to read.

Reformed

Calvin: nothing new, but a seal of 7:16. A great roll so it may be read at a distance; a common pen so the uneducated can read. Four words to carry home the swiftness of wrath. Those who make this Christ spoiling the prince of this world cannot harmonize with the text.

Reformed write: confirmation of the dated spoil, not a new gospel. Shared: large tablet, common characters, Maher-shalal-hash-baz.

Isaiah 8:2

2And I got reliable witnesses, Uriah the priest and Zechariah the son of Jeberechiah, to attest for me.

Arminian

Reliable witnesses: Uriah the priest and Zechariah son of Jeberechiah. The name is attested before it is born.

Arminian witnesses: historic priests and names. The word is meant to be checked, not whispered.

Reformed

Calvin: I called-to-witness witnesses, as on important occasions. Faithful means worthy of credit by office, not that Uriah was a good man—the same priest who later built Ahaz’s Damascus altar (2 Kgs 16:11). The prophecy affixed to the temple gates.

Reformed witnesses: office enough to bind the public word. Shared: Uriah, Zechariah, attest.

Isaiah 8:3

3And I went to the prophetess, and she conceived and bore a son. Then the Lord said to me, “Call his name Maher-shalal-hash-baz;

Arminian

I went to the prophetess; she conceived and bore a son. Call his name Maher-shalal-hash-baz. Wesley: his own wife, so called because the wife of a prophet.

Arminian conceived: a real child with a real name, timed to the spoil. Historic house of Isaiah, next to Shear-jashub (7:3).

Reformed

Calvin: likely a vision sealing the prediction, though he will not dispute an actual son. Prophetess: she sustained a public character in the vision, not a courtesy title.

Reformed conceived: the name is the point, whether vision or birth. Shared: prophetess, son, Maher-shalal-hash-baz.

Isaiah 8:4

4for before the boy knows how to cry ‘My father’ or ‘My mother,’ the wealth of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria will be carried away before the king of Assyria.”

Arminian

Before the boy knows how to cry My father or My mother, the wealth of Damascus and the spoil of Samaria will be carried away before the king of Assyria. Wesley: within two years; this agrees with 7:16. Shear-jashub, born earlier, could refuse evil as soon as this one could name his parents.

Arminian before: a dated clock, historic Rezin and Pekah emptied by Assyria. Same near-term as 7:16, not Immanuel’s childhood.

Reformed

Calvin: before children soon born can speak, Israel and Syria are spoiled before the Assyrian’s face, as booty in a triumph. Spoken to comfort the godly and to hold up the king’s foolish dread. The Lord by His goodness rises above the wickedness of king and people.

Reformed before: the same two kingdoms fall on a short clock. Shared: father, mother, Damascus, Samaria, king of Assyria.

Isaiah 8:5

5The Lord spoke to me again:

Arminian

The Lord spoke to me again. A second word after the child’s name: not only spoil up north, but a river over Judah.

Arminian again: He still speaks. The tablet is not the last line.

Reformed

Calvin: he breaks off from general safety of the godly to threaten unbelievers, lest Ahaz slumber in unfounded expectation.

Reformed again: consolation does not cancel the flood. Shared: the Lord spoke again.

Isaiah 8:6

6“Because this people has refused the waters of Shiloah that flow gently, and rejoice over Rezin and the son of Remaliah,

Arminian

This people has refused the waters of Shiloah that flow gently, and rejoice over Rezin and the son of Remaliah. Wesley: this people—Israel, who rejoiced in their king and in Rezin; Shiloah—the small brook by Jerusalem, the Jews’ strength which enemies derided.

Arminian refused: a real no to a real brook. Historic Shiloah, historic Rezin. They could have stayed with the gentle waters. Match the hub: a King who offers Shiloah, leaving this refusing as theirs.

Reformed

Calvin: not a party but the great body of the nation—Judah distrusting weakness, longing for Israel’s wealth and a powerful king. They despised Shiloah, their lot with a promise. Shiloah ought to be our defense, preferred to the highest power of kings. He excepts a few servants of God.

Reformed refused: unbelief looking at nakedness, not at the promise. Shared: Shiloah, flow gently, Rezin, son of Remaliah. Wesley hears Israel deriding Judah’s brook; Calvin hears Judah despising her own.

Isaiah 8:7

7therefore, behold, the Lord is bringing up against them the waters of the River, mighty and many, the king of Assyria and all his glory. And it will rise over all its channels and go over all its banks,

Arminian

Therefore the Lord is bringing up against them the waters of the River, mighty and many—the king of Assyria and all his glory. It will rise over its channels and go over its banks. Wesley: Euphrates; the Assyrian forces; this monarch enlarges his dominions, adding Syria and Israel.

Arminian therefore: they asked for a great river; they get it. Historic Assyria, not a newspaper. The Lord brings it.

Reformed

Calvin: because they are not satisfied with their condition, I will show them what it is to have a powerful king. The more powerful, the more they oppress. Euphrates against Shiloah: the Assyrians waste Judea like a deluge. The sacred history assures these things were fulfilled: the Assyrians they called in destroyed them. He does not condemn human aid, but the wicked fear that no promise can restrain. If we have outward help we may use it; if not, let God alone suffice.

Reformed therefore: the ally is the flood. Shared: River, mighty, king of Assyria, channels, banks.

Isaiah 8:8

8and it will sweep on into Judah, it will overflow and pass on, reaching even to the neck, and its outspread wings will fill the breadth of your land, O Immanuel.”

Arminian

It will sweep on into Judah, overflow and pass on, reaching even to the neck; its outspread wings will fill the breadth of your land, O Immanuel. Wesley: great danger of being destroyed; Sennacherib took the fenced cities (2 Kgs 18:13). Wings—his forces. Judah so called because the Messiah, Immanuel, should be born there. Consolation: Israel should not be a people; Judah restored for the sake of the Messiah, the place of His birth and ministry.

Arminian O Immanuel: shared christology—this land is His. Historic flood to the neck, not a millennial camp. Judah kept for His birth, as at 7:14.

Reformed

Calvin: not only water Judah but drown it, up to the neck: Jerusalem the metropolis. Why address Christ instead of calling it God’s holy land? That desolation would not prevent the Redeemer. Nevertheless the land shall be thine, O Immanuel. A remnant of hope: the land, though torn, belongs to God and not to men. The Jews too are punished for unbelief, having despised promises and signs.

Reformed O Immanuel: same Christ, same land. The flood is to the neck, not over the head, because the land is His. Not a millennial timetable. Shared: Judah, neck, wings, your land, Immanuel.

Isaiah 8:9

9Be broken, you peoples, and be shattered; give ear, all you far countries; strap on your armor and be shattered; strap on your armor and be shattered.

Arminian

Be broken, you peoples, and be shattered; give ear, all you far countries; strap on your armor and be shattered. Wesley: Syrians and Israelites; whoever conspires against Immanuel’s land; gird for war; repeated for assurance and the comfort of God’s people.

Arminian be broken: a real taunt after a real flood. Historic peoples against this land, not a newspaper coalition. They still strap on armor; it still shatters.

Reformed

Calvin: associate, gird, form a plan—pressed together as a dense mass. After mentioning Immanuel he rises into confidence and taunts enemies as if he had vanquished them. The Assyrian’s army was of various peoples. Not one stroke but incessant contests. Direct your eyes to Christ, by whose sight we triumph.

Reformed be broken: the watchman sees Immanuel and forgets the distress. Shared: peoples, far countries, armor, shattered.

Isaiah 8:10

10Take counsel together, but it will come to nothing; speak a word, but it will not stand, for God is with us.

Arminian

Take counsel together, but it will come to nothing; speak a word, but it will not stand, for God is with us. Wesley has no separate note here; his 8:8–9 still hold: plots against Immanuel’s land fail because Judah is kept for the Messiah, and God is with us.

Arminian God is with us: a real with-us under the flood to the neck. Counsel is still theirs to take; it still comes to nothing. Not a fake warning: Rezin’s league already failed (7:7). Match the hub: a King who is with His land, leaving this standing as His promise received.

Reformed

Calvin: forces and counsels both vanish. For God is with us—Hebrew Immanuel. He alludes to the name given to Christ. The majesty of God is not of itself sufficient to support us; he contemplates God in the person of the Mediator, in whom alone He has promised to assist us. Wicked men also boast that God is with them; their glorying is idle. The valor of the godly rests on the word and proceeds from true faith. Make sure God is with us, which cannot be unless we embrace the promises by faith. If God be for us, who shall be against us? (Rom 8:31).

Reformed God is with us: Immanuel in the Mediator, not a bare slogan. Shared: counsel, come to nothing, word will not stand, God is with us.

Key contrast Both confess the counsel against Judah shall not stand, because God is with us—Immanuel (8:8, 10). Historic Assyria to the neck, not a newspaper, and not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear a real with-us that keeps Judah for Messiah’s birth and shatters whoever conspires against His land (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear God contemplated in the Mediator: idle when the ungodly boast it; sure only when the promises are embraced by faith (Calvin).

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a child’s name that hurries spoil, a brook refused, and Immanuel’s land under the River—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 8:1–10.

Arminian Notable voices

“A roll—a great volume, written in large and legible characters, concerning that which is signified by the child’s name. Prophetess—his own wife, so called because the wife of a prophet. To cry—to speak and know his parents, within two years; this agrees with 7:16. This people—the people of Israel, who rejoiced not only in their own king but in so powerful an ally as Rezin. Shiloah—that small brook which ran by Jerusalem; hereby the munitions and strength of the Jews, which their enemies derided. Reach—Judah in great danger of being destroyed; Sennacherib took the fenced cities and sent his army against Jerusalem. My land—Judah, so called because the Messiah, who is called Immanuel, should be born there. God would make a difference between Israel and Judah: Israel should not be a people; Judah should be restored, for the sake of the Messiah. Ye people—Syrians and Israelites, whosoever conspire against Immanuel’s land.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 8:1–9)

Reformed Notable voices

“This prophecy contains nothing new, but confirms the former: both countries deprived of their kings before children can distinguish. Write with a common pen, that the most ignorant may read. Make speed to spoil, hasten to the prey: four words to carry home. Many improperly explain this of Christ spoiling the prince of this world; that ingenuity cannot harmonize with the text. This people: not a few, but the great body of the nation; they despised Shiloah—their condition—and their joy was to Rezin. Shiloah ought to be our defense. If we rely on human aid, we must look for the punishment here threatened. He does not condemn human aid, but that wicked fear by which we distrust, so that no promise of God can keep us. O Immanuel: he directs his discourse to Christ, that desolation would not prevent the coming of the Redeemer. The land shall be thine. Take counsel: they will accomplish nothing. For God is with us—Immanuel. He contemplates God in the person of the Mediator. Wicked men also boast that God is with them; their glorying is idle. Make sure that God is with us, which cannot be unless we embrace the promises by faith. If God be for us, who shall be against us?”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 8:1–10)
