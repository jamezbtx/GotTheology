Passage

Isaiah 7

Verses 1–9 · ESV · Voices

In the days of Ahaz, Rezin of Syria and Pekah of Israel come up against Jerusalem and cannot yet attack. The house of David shakes like trees in the wind. The Lord sends Isaiah with Shear-jashub to the upper pool: be quiet; do not fear two smoldering stumps. Their counsel—to set the son of Tabeel in Judah—shall not stand. Damascus remains Rezin’s head; within sixty-five years Ephraim is shattered. If you are not firm in faith, you will not be firm at all.

Isaiah 7:1

1In the days of Ahaz the son of Jotham, son of Uzziah, king of Judah, Rezin the king of Syria and Pekah the son of Remaliah the king of Israel came up to Jerusalem to wage war against it, but could not yet mount an attack against it.

Arminian

In the days of Ahaz son of Jotham, son of Uzziah, Rezin of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah of Israel came up to Jerusalem to wage war, but could not yet mount an attack. Wesley: a most wicked king; yet comfortable words for the faithful under his reign.

Arminian days: historic Syro-Ephraimite war, not a newspaper. The city still stands. Uzziah’s year of death (6:1) has become Ahaz’s war.

Reformed

Calvin: a second war after a first defeat, so the deliverance is the more a miracle. They could not prevail: the result first, then the design. Unthankful men would not have seen the hand if the details were not recorded. Kindness conferred that the Church might be preserved and Christ afterwards appear.

Reformed days: historic Ahaz, historic failure to take the city. Shared: Ahaz, Rezin, Pekah, Jerusalem, could not yet attack.

Isaiah 7:2

2When the house of David was told, “Syria is in league with Ephraim,” the heart of Ahaz and the heart of his people shook as the trees of the forest shake before the wind.

Arminian

When the house of David was told, Syria is in league with Ephraim, the heart of Ahaz and of his people shook as trees of the forest shake before the wind. Wesley: house of David—Ahaz and his relations; the message for David’s sake, not Ahaz’s; Ephraim—the ten tribes; fear from guilt and the enemies’ strength.

Arminian shook: real panic in a real palace. Trees still shake. Historic league, not a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: house of David is the court. Terror spread from the palace to the nation. Un godly ease till danger; then they are seized so as to lose their senses. Two fears: the godly run to God as a harbour; the ungodly find no remedy.

Reformed shook: the leaf is the punishment of slumber. Shared: house of David, Syria, Ephraim, trees, wind.

Isaiah 7:3

3And the Lord said to Isaiah, “Go out to meet Ahaz, you and Shear-jashub your son, at the end of the conduit of the upper pool on the highway to the Washer's Field.

Arminian

Go out to meet Ahaz, you and Shear-jashub your son, at the end of the conduit of the upper pool on the highway to the Washer's Field. Wesley: Shear-jashub—The remnant shall return; Fuller’s field—waters for the city, as Hezekiah later (2 Chr 32).

Arminian go: a real meeting at a real conduit. The boy’s name already preaches remnant (6:13). Ahaz is still to be met.

Reformed

Calvin: God anticipates this wicked king; He does not wait for prayers. Shear-jashub: a seal of approaching captivity and of return, by secret inspiration. The place authenticates the history; the hypocrite running about when Isaiah comes to soothe him.

Reformed go: remnant in the son’s name, before Immanuel is offered. Shared: Isaiah, Shear-jashub, upper pool, Washer's Field.

Isaiah 7:4

4And say to him, ‘Be careful, be quiet, do not fear, and do not let your heart be faint because of these two smoldering stumps of firebrands, at the fierce anger of Rezin and Syria and the son of Remaliah.

Arminian

Be careful, be quiet, do not fear, do not let your heart be faint because of these two smoldering stumps of firebrands—Rezin and Syria and the son of Remaliah. Wesley: settle thy mind by belief of the joyful message; firebrands—small ends, more smoke than fire, more shew than strength; Pekah called only son of Remaliah, usurper and murderer (2 Kgs 15:25).

Arminian quiet: a real command to a shaking king. He can still be quiet. The stumps are historic kings, not a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: refrain—do not wander in uncertainty. Ungodliness is never at rest; where faith exists the mind is composed. Fear not: not every feeling forbidden; two fears in the children of God. Tails of smoking firebrands: not a devouring fire but fragments going out, slight smoke of short duration. Form a different opinion of the violence of the ungodly than what it appears.

Reformed quiet: faith keeps watch without being tossed. Shared: be quiet, do not fear, two stumps, Rezin, son of Remaliah.

Isaiah 7:5

5Because Syria, with Ephraim and the son of Remaliah, has devised evil against you, saying,

Arminian

Because Syria, with Ephraim and the son of Remaliah, has devised evil against you, saying— The plot is named so the no of v. 7 is heard against a real scheme.

Arminian devised: real counsel of real kings. Evil is still theirs under a God who will not let it stand.

Reformed

Calvin: he does not hide that their devices are cruel if the Lord do not restrain. Evil counsel: destructive. He relates their very words to set the plot before the eyes.

Reformed devised: the scheme is theirs; the restraint is His. Shared: Syria, Ephraim, evil against you.

Isaiah 7:6

6“Let us go up against Judah and terrify it, and let us conquer it for ourselves, and set up the son of Tabeel as king in the midst of it,”

Arminian

Let us go up against Judah and terrify it, and conquer it for ourselves, and set up the son of Tabeel as king in the midst of it. Wesley: break their power and subdue it to ourselves.

Arminian let us: historic coup—replace David’s house with Tabeel. Not a newspaper. The house of David is the target; the remnant name already walked to the pool.

Reformed

Calvin: open it—make a breach so entrance is easy when loyalty breaks. They hoped the sight of so vast an army would make Jerusalem surrender without a long siege. Tabeal: probably some Israelite, a dependent they would set up against David’s line.

Reformed let us: giants against the decree. Shared: Judah, terrify, son of Tabeel.

Isaiah 7:7

7thus says the Lord God: “‘It shall not stand, and it shall not come to pass.

Arminian

Thus says the Lord God: It shall not stand, and it shall not come to pass. Wesley: their evil counsel.

Arminian not: a real no over a real plot. The word is enough to meet two armies. Historic Jerusalem still David’s.

Reformed

Calvin: he first showed the greatness of the danger, then the decree. Whatever men attempt after the manner of giants against God shall not stand. It shall not be: reduced to nothing, as if it had never existed. The bare word contrasted with the vast army.

Reformed not: His sentence, not Judah’s odds. Shared: Lord God, shall not stand, shall not come to pass.

Isaiah 7:8

8For the head of Syria is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin. And within sixty-five years Ephraim will be shattered from being a people.

Arminian

The head of Syria is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin. Within sixty-five years Ephraim will be shattered from being a people. Wesley: Damascus remains Syria’s capital; Jerusalem will not become Rezin’s; he keeps his own bounds.

Arminian head: historic limits. Sixty-five years is a dated shattering of Ephraim, not a millennial chart. Rezin stays Rezin.

Reformed

Calvin: those kings shall have their limits. Broken: Israel annihilated, mixed with foreign nations, its name blotted out. Sixty-five years: reckoned from Amos’s known word, not from this day; Isaiah confirms a prediction already public, and mocks Israel for thinking the word had grown old.

Reformed head: bounds He set. Shared: Damascus, Rezin, sixty-five years, Ephraim shattered.

Isaiah 7:9

9And the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah. If you are not firm in faith, you will not be firm at all.’”

Arminian

The head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah. If you are not firm in faith, you will not be firm at all. Wesley: Samaria remains Israel’s chief city; Pekah shall not conquer Jerusalem. If you do not believe this, but seek to the Assyrians for succour, ye shall not be established.

Arminian if: a real condition under a word that shall not stand (v. 7). Believe this—do not run to Assyria—and you will be established. Not a works-gospel: the standing is the Lord’s promise received. Not a fake warning: unbelief still collapses. Match the hub: a King who promises, leaving this believing as a real second cause.

Reformed

Calvin: this is the only support. Wait calmly for the promised deliverance. If you do not wait, destruction. God will stand, though they disbelieve his word and as far as they can destroy its stability; they will not stand unless they rely on the promise. When we have departed from the word, though we suppose we are firmly established, ruin is at hand. Either we must believe the promises of God, or it is in vain to expect salvation.

Reformed if: He stands anyway; they stand only by relying. Shared: Samaria, son of Remaliah, firm in faith, firm at all.

Key contrast Both confess the plot against David’s house shall not stand, and that Ahaz must be firm in faith (7:7, 9). Arminians typically hear a real if: believe this instead of running to Assyria, and you will be established (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear that God will stand though they disbelieve, and that they will not stand unless they rely on the promise—the only support (Calvin). Historic Ahaz, not a newspaper.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a shaking house of David, two smoking stumps, and a faith that stands—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 7:1–9.

Arminian Notable voices

“Ahaz—a most wicked king: yet no prophecies are more comfortable than those delivered in his time, for the encouragement of the faithful under his impious reign. House of David—the message was sent to Ahaz not for his own sake, but for the sake of his worthy progenitor David. Shear-jashub—The remnant shall return. Firebrands—not whole, but small ends, more smoke than fire; more of shew and terror than of strength. If—if you do not believe this, but seek to the Assyrians for succour, ye shall not be established.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 7:1–9)

Reformed Notable voices

“Take heed, and be quiet. Ungodliness is never at rest; where faith exists, the mind is composed. Fear not: faith does not deprive us of all feeling; the children of God are moved by two kinds of fear, one from human nature, the other from the weakness of faith. If you do not believe, surely ye shall not stand. This is the only support on which you can rely. Wait calmly for what the Lord has promised. If you do not wait for it, what else remains than destruction? God will stand, though they disbelieve his word; but they will not stand, unless they rely on the promise. When we have departed from the word of God, though we may suppose that we are firmly established, still ruin is at hand. Either we must believe the promises of God, or it is in vain for us to expect salvation.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 7:4, 9)
