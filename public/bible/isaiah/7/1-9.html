Passage

Isaiah 7

Verses 1–9 · WEB · Voices

In the days of Ahaz, Rezin of Syria and Pekah of Israel come up against Jerusalem and cannot yet attack. The house of David shakes like trees in the wind. The Lord sends Isaiah with Shear-jashub to the upper pool: be quiet; do not fear two smoldering stumps. Their counsel—to set the son of Tabeel in Judah—shall not stand. Damascus remains Rezin’s head; within sixty-five years Ephraim is shattered. If you will not believe, surely you shall not be firm at all.

Isaiah 7:1

1In the days of Ahaz the son of Jotham, the son of Uzziah, king of Judah, Rezin the king of Syria and Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem to war against it, but could not prevail against it.

Arminian

In the days of Ahaz son of Jotham, the son of Uzziah, Rezin of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah of Israel, went up to Jerusalem to war, but could not yet mount an attack. Wesley: a most wicked king; yet comfortable words for the faithful under his reign.

“Days”—Syro-Ephraimite war. The city still stands. Uzziah’s year of death (6:1) has become Ahaz’s war.

Reformed

Calvin: a second war after a first defeat, so the deliverance is the more a miracle. They could not prevail: the result first, then the design. Unthankful men would not have seen the hand if the details were not recorded. Kindness conferred that the Church might be preserved and Christ afterwards appear.

“Days”—Ahaz, failure to take the city. Ahaz, Rezin, Pekah, Jerusalem, could not yet attack.

Isaiah 7:2

2David’s house was told, “Syria is allied with Ephraim.” His heart trembled, and the heart of his people, as the trees of the forest tremble with the wind.

Arminian

When the house was told, Syria is allied with Ephraim. His heart trembled, and of his people, as trees of the forest tremble with the wind. Wesley: house of David—Ahaz and his relations; the message for David’s sake, not Ahaz’s; Ephraim—the ten tribes; fear from guilt and the enemies’ strength.

Panic in a palace. Trees still shake.

Reformed

Calvin: house of David is the court. Terror spread from the palace to the nation. Un godly ease till danger; then they are seized so as to lose their senses. Two fears: the godly run to God as a harbour; the ungodly find no remedy.

The leaf is the punishment of slumber. House of David, Syria, Ephraim, trees, wind.

Isaiah 7:3

3Then the LORD said to Isaiah, “Go out now to meet Ahaz, you, and Shearjashub your son, at the end of the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway of the fuller’s field.

Arminian

Go out now to meet Ahaz, you, and Shearjashub your son, at the end of the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway of the fuller’s field. Wesley: Shear-jashub—The remnant shall return; Fuller’s field—waters for the city, as Hezekiah later (2 Chr 32).

“Go”—a meeting at a conduit. The boy’s name already preaches remnant (6:13). Ahaz is still to be met.

Reformed

Calvin: God anticipates this wicked king; He does not wait for prayers. Shear-jashub: a seal of approaching captivity and of return, by secret inspiration. The place authenticates the history; the hypocrite running about when Isaiah comes to soothe him.

Remnant in the son’s name, before Immanuel is offered. Isaiah, Shear-jashub, upper pool, Washer's Field.

Isaiah 7:4

4Tell him, ‘Be careful, and keep calm. Don’t be afraid, neither let your heart be faint because of these two tails of smoking torches, for the fierce anger of Rezin and Syria, and of the son of Remaliah.

Arminian

Be careful and keep calm; don’t be afraid, and don’t let your heart faint because of these two smoking stubs of firebrands—Rezin with Syria and the son of Remaliah. Wesley: settle thy mind by belief of the joyful message; firebrands—small ends, more smoke than fire, more shew than strength; Pekah called only son of Remaliah, usurper and murderer (2 Kgs 15:25).

An earnest command to a shaking king. He can still be quiet. The stumps are kings.

Reformed

Calvin: refrain—do not wander in uncertainty. Ungodliness is never at rest; where faith exists the mind is composed. Fear not: not every feeling forbidden; two fears in the children of God. Tails of smoking firebrands: not a devouring fire but fragments going out, slight smoke of short duration. Form a different opinion of the violence of the ungodly than what it appears.

Faith keeps watch without being tossed. Be quiet, do not fear, two stumps, Rezin, son of Remaliah.

Isaiah 7:5

5Because Syria, Ephraim, and the son of Remaliah, have plotted evil against you, saying,

Arminian

Because Syria, Ephraim, and the son of Remaliah, have plotted evil against you, saying— The plot is named so the no of v. 7 is heard against a scheme.

These are the plans of real kings. Evil is still theirs under a God who will not let it stand.

Reformed

Calvin: he does not hide that their devices are cruel if the Lord do not restrain. Evil counsel: destructive. He relates their very words to set the plot before the eyes.

The scheme is theirs; the restraint is His.

Isaiah 7:6

6“Let’s go up against Judah, and tear it apart, and let’s divide it among ourselves, and set up a king within it, even the son of Tabeel.”

Arminian

Let us go up against Judah, tear it apart, divide it among ourselves, and set up a king within it, the son of Tabeel. Wesley: break their power and subdue it to ourselves.

Coup—replace David’s house with Tabeel. The house of David is the target; the remnant name already walked to the pool.

Reformed

Calvin: open it—make a breach so entrance is easy when loyalty breaks. They hoped the sight of so vast an army would make Jerusalem surrender without a long siege. Tabeal: probably some Israelite, a dependent they would set up against David’s line.

Giants against the decree. Judah, terrify, son of Tabeel.

Isaiah 7:7

7This is what the Lord GOD says: “It shall not stand, neither shall it happen.”

Arminian

Thus says the Lord GOD says: It shall not stand, neither shall it shall not come to pass. Wesley: their evil counsel.

“Not”—a no over a plot. The word is enough to meet two armies.

Reformed

Calvin: he first showed the greatness of the danger, then the decree. Whatever men attempt after the manner of giants against God shall not stand. It shall not be: reduced to nothing, as if it had never existed. The bare word contrasted with the vast army.

His sentence, not Judah’s odds. Lord God, shall not stand, shall not come to pass.

Isaiah 7:8

8For the head of Syria is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin. Within sixty-five years Ephraim shall be broken in pieces, so that it shall not be a people.

Arminian

The head of Syria is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin. Within sixty-five years Ephraim shall be broken in pieces, so that it shall not be a people. Wesley: Damascus remains Syria’s capital; Jerusalem will not become Rezin’s; he keeps his own bounds.

God sets limits by naming each nation’s head. Sixty-five years is a dated shattering of Ephraim. Rezin stays Rezin.

Reformed

Calvin: those kings shall have their limits. Broken: Israel annihilated, mixed with foreign nations, its name blotted out. Sixty-five years: reckoned from Amos’s known word, not from this day; Isaiah confirms a prediction already public, and mocks Israel for thinking the word had grown old.

Bounds He set. Damascus, Rezin, sixty-five years, Ephraim shattered.

Isaiah 7:9

9The head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is Remaliah’s son. If you will not believe, surely you shall not be established.’”

Arminian

The head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is Remaliah’s son. If you will not believe, surely you shall not be firm at all. Wesley: Samaria remains Israel’s chief city; Pekah shall not conquer Jerusalem. If you do not believe this, but seek to the Assyrians for succour, ye shall not be established.

The promise of v. 7 is unconditional: the northern alliance will fail. Ahaz’s own stability, though, depends on believing it. Arminians see a condition attached to a sure promise—refuse to believe, and you will not stand.

Reformed

Calvin: this is the only support. Wait calmly for the promised deliverance. If you do not wait, destruction. God will stand, though they disbelieve his word and as far as they can destroy its stability; they will not stand unless they rely on the promise. When we have departed from the word, though we suppose we are firmly established, ruin is at hand. Either we must believe the promises of God, or it is in vain to expect salvation.

He stands anyway; they stand only by relying. Samaria, son of Remaliah, firm in faith, firm at all.

Key contrast Both confess the plot against David’s house shall not stand, and that Ahaz must be firm in faith (7:7, 9). Arminians detect a true if: believe this instead of running to Assyria, and you will be established (Wesley). Reformed Christians hear that God will stand though they disbelieve, and that they will not stand unless they rely on the promise—the only support (Calvin).

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What older interpreters in each camp said about a shaking house of David, two smoking stumps, and a faith that stands.

Arminian Notable voices

“Ahaz—a most wicked king: yet no prophecies are more comfortable than those delivered in his time, for the encouragement of the faithful under his impious reign. House of David—the message was sent to Ahaz not for his own sake, but for the sake of his worthy progenitor David. Shear-jashub—The remnant shall return. Firebrands—not whole, but small ends, more smoke than fire; more of shew and terror than of strength. If—if you do not believe this, but seek to the Assyrians for succour, ye shall not be established.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 7:1–9)

Reformed Notable voices

“Take heed, and be quiet. Ungodliness is never at rest; where faith exists, the mind is composed. Fear not: faith does not deprive us of all feeling; the children of God are moved by two kinds of fear, one from human nature, the other from the weakness of faith. If you do not believe, surely ye shall not stand. This is the only support on which you can rely. Wait calmly for what the Lord has promised. If you do not wait for it, what else remains than destruction? God will stand, though they disbelieve his word; but they will not stand, unless they rely on the promise. When we have departed from the word of God, though we may suppose that we are firmly established, still ruin is at hand. Either we must believe the promises of God, or it is in vain for us to expect salvation.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 7:4, 9)
