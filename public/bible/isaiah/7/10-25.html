Passage

Isaiah 7

Verses 10–25 · ESV · Voices

The Lord speaks again to Ahaz: ask a sign, deep as Sheol or high as heaven. Ahaz will not ask, will not put the Lord to the test. The house of David wearies God. Therefore the Lord himself gives a sign: the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, Immanuel. Curds and honey until he knows to refuse evil. Before the boy knows, the land of the two kings is deserted. Then days worse than Ephraim’s split—the king of Assyria. The Lord whistles for fly and bee; a hired razor shaves head to beard. Survivors keep a cow and two sheep among briers.

Isaiah 7:10

10Again the Lord spoke to Ahaz:

Arminian

Again the Lord spoke to Ahaz. The first word (7:1–9) was not the last. He speaks again to a king who would not be firm in faith.

Arminian again: a real second address. Historic Ahaz at the pool, not a newspaper. The offer of a sign is still grace after 7:9.

Reformed

Calvin: as the Lord knew Ahaz was so wicked as not to believe the promise, He enjoins Isaiah to confirm him by adding a sign. When His promises do not satisfy us, He makes additions suitable to our weakness: miracles added to the word are seals.

Reformed again: the second speech is for weakness, not a new gospel. Shared: the Lord, Ahaz, again.

Isaiah 7:11

11“Ask a sign of the Lord your God; let it be deep as Sheol or high as heaven.”

Arminian

Ask a sign of the Lord your God; let it be deep as Sheol or high as heaven. The range is unrestricted: grave or sky. Your God is still said to this king.

Arminian ask: a real invitation. He may ask. Depth and height are the Lord’s to give. Historic Ahaz is not being toyed with.

Reformed

Calvin: an unrestricted choice, either above or below. Astonishing forbearance toward this wicked king, that He was willing to give any pledge of His power they chose. He had in view not unbelievers only, but the weak in whom there was a seed of godliness.

Reformed ask: forbearance that still offers a seal. Shared: sign, Lord your God, Sheol, heaven.

Isaiah 7:12

12But Ahaz said, “I will not ask, and I will not put the Lord to the test.”

Arminian

Ahaz will not ask, and will not put the Lord to the test. Wesley: as if I questioned the truth of his word—but this was deep hypocrisy.

Arminian will not: a real refusal dressed as piety. He could have asked. Unbelief here is still his. Not a works-gospel: the sign was offered, not earned. Not a fake warning: this no still wearies God (v. 13).

Reformed

Calvin: by a plausible excuse he refuses. Do we tempt God when we accept what He offers? Certainly not. Ahaz speaks falsehood. If God add anything to His word, it is no virtue to reject it as superfluous. He who rejects the signs when offered must also reject the grace. We must not separate those things which the Lord has commanded us to join.

Reformed will not: refusing the offered seal is refusing the word it seals. Shared: I will not ask, I will not put the Lord to the test.

Isaiah 7:13

13And he said, “Hear then, O house of David! Is it too little for you to weary men, that you weary my God also?

Arminian

Hear then, O house of David! Is it too little for you to weary men, that you weary my God also? Wesley: he reproves them all, the king’s counsellors; is it not wickedness enough; my God—to vex prophets and people, and by ingratitude, unbelief, and disobedience.

Arminian weary: a real insult to God after a real offer. House of David is still addressed for David’s sake (7:2). Historic court, not a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: under pretense of honor he excluded the power of God. The Prophet kindles. House of David is named as reproach: they had degenerated from the family from which the world’s salvation would proceed. They thought they had to deal with men. My God: Ahaz and his like have no right to boast the name; God is on the prophet’s side, not the hypocrites’.

Reformed weary: thy God (v. 11) has become my God. Shared: house of David, weary men, weary my God.

Isaiah 7:14

14Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.

Arminian

Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel. Wesley: because you despise me, God of his own free grace will send a nobler sign. How was a birth many ages later a sign of present deliverance? This promised birth supposed the preservation of that city, nation, and tribe in and of which the Messiah was to be born. Immanuel—God with us; God dwelling among us, in our nature (John 1:14); God and man meeting in one person, a mediator. The name describes His nature and office, not merely what He would commonly be called.

Arminian therefore: shared christology—the virgin’s son is God with us, God and man in one person. Free grace still gives the sign after the refusal. Historic Judah is kept so Messiah can be born there. Not a millennial camp fight, not a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: an illustrious prediction concerning the Messiah, called Immanuel. Those who apply it to Hezekiah are excessively impudent; Isaiah’s son cannot bear a title so illustrious. Ahaz having rejected the sign, the Prophet reminds him of the foundation of the covenant: the Messiah must be born. True, thou dost not believe, but God will fulfill the promises; He will send His Christ, for whose sake He determines to preserve this city. Though thou art unworthy, God will have regard to His own honor. The discourse is to the adopted body, not as if Ahaz were numbered with the chosen. God with us: the only-begotten clothed Himself with our flesh; a union of person, Christ God-man. All the promises of God in Christ are Yea and Amen.

Reformed therefore: same Christ, same virgin, same God with us. The city stands for Christ’s sake, and the covenant for the remnant, though the king is a hypocrite. Not a millennial timetable. Shared: Lord himself, sign, virgin, son, Immanuel.

Key contrast Both confess the sign is the virgin’s son, Immanuel, God with us—God and man in one person (Wesley; Calvin). Shared christology; not a millennial camp fight, and not a newspaper. Arminians typically hear free grace sending a nobler sign whose promised birth preserves the city, nation, and tribe so Messiah can be born (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear God fulfilling the covenant for the remnant though the king is unworthy and not among the chosen: He sends Christ for whose sake He keeps the city (Calvin). Historic Ahaz’s sign first.

Isaiah 7:15

15He shall eat curds and honey when he knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good.

Arminian

He shall eat curds and honey when he knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good. Wesley: butter—the common food of children in that country; he—the virgin’s son; know—to discern between good and evil.

Arminian eat: the same child is truly a child. Shared: Immanuel is man enough to eat and to learn. Not a phantom, not a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: marks of human nature, lest we think the prophet presents an apparition. Christ will actually appear in flesh, reared as children commonly are. For a time He was willing to be deprived of understanding and to endure our weaknesses (Luke 2:52; Heb 2:14). This relates to His human nature, not His divinity.

Reformed eat: true humanity of the same Immanuel. Shared: curds, honey, refuse the evil, choose the good.

Isaiah 7:16

16For before the boy knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good, the land whose two kings you dread will be deserted.

Arminian

Before the boy knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good, the land whose two kings you dread will be deserted. Wesley: not only this land preserved until the virgin’s son is born, but the enemies’ land scourged and the two kings destroyed in a very little time. This child—Shear-jashub, pointed at, brought by special command (7:3) for this use. Pekah and Rezin lose their own lands and lives within two years, slain by the king of Assyria.

Arminian before: historic near-term emptying of Syria and Israel. The boy at the pool is a clock. Ahaz’s dread still has a dated end. Not a millennial fight.

Reformed

Calvin: not the same child as v. 14. He returns from the general doctrine of the Mediator to the immediate subject. The child: children in general, the age marked, not a particular son. Before children born hereafter can distinguish, Israel and Syria are forsaken of their kings. Rezin slain; Pekah gone in Ahaz’s twelfth year. Consolation that makes Ahaz more inexcusable.

Reformed before: the two kings fall on a short clock, distinct from the virgin’s son. Shared: before the boy knows, two kings, land deserted. Wesley names Shear-jashub; Calvin names the age. Either way, historic Rezin and Pekah, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 7:17

17The Lord will bring upon you and upon your people and upon your father's house such days as have not come since the day that Ephraim departed from Judah—the king of Assyria.”

Arminian

The Lord will bring upon you and upon your people and upon your father's house days that have not come since Ephraim departed from Judah—the king of Assyria. Wesley: although God will deliver you at this time, yet He will requite all your wickedness. Part of the storm in Ahaz’s reign; the rest on sons and successors. The king is their plague, the rod of God’s anger (10:5).

Arminian bring: a real deliverance now, and a real requital. Assyria is historic, the ally they ran to (7:9). Not a newspaper empire.

Reformed

Calvin: what Ahaz hopes will be his preservation—the Assyrian—will be utterly destructive to him. He might have remained at ease and received God’s help; he chose to call them in. Family of David not exempt. God regulates judgments so as to spare His Church and not let the wicked mixed with the good escape. Amidst the most dreadful deaths the Church remains safe.

Reformed bring: the hired help is the judgment. Shared: upon you, your people, father's house, Ephraim’s departure, king of Assyria.

Isaiah 7:18

18In that day the Lord will whistle for the fly that is at the end of the streams of Egypt, and for the bee that is in the land of Assyria.

Arminian

In that day the Lord will whistle for the fly at the end of the streams of Egypt, and for the bee in the land of Assyria. Wesley: flies—great numbers; rivers of Nile; bee—Assyrian army, numerous, orderly, fierce. Assyria or Babylon, later one empire, names used promiscuously.

Arminian whistle: He calls them. Historic Egypt and Assyria, not a modern chart. The hiss is the Lord’s.

Reformed

Calvin: the Jews thought Assyria bound by league; he ridicules that. Instead of command, a hiss: Egypt abounds in flies, Assyria in bees. No difficulty; as soon as He gives the sign they run. He shows what efficacy belongs to His secret operation. Everything is governed by the hand of God.

Reformed whistle: a hiss is enough. Shared: that day, fly, Egypt, bee, Assyria.

Isaiah 7:19

19And they will all come and settle in the steep ravines, and in the clefts of the rocks, and on all the thornbushes, and on all the pastures.

Arminian

They will all come and settle in the steep ravines, and in the clefts of the rocks, and on all the thornbushes, and on all the pastures. Wesley: valleys they found fruitful but made desolate; rocks to which Israelites fled; bushes—flies and bees rest there; no place escapes.

Arminian settle: the land is covered. Historic invasion, not a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: bees nest in caverns, valleys, bushes. There would not be a corner in which the enemy would not settle. Whether they hide in caverns or seek concealment in valleys, no escape. A concealed bridle on furious beasts.

Reformed settle: every corner under the hiss. Shared: ravines, rocks, thornbushes, pastures.

Isaiah 7:20

20In that day the Lord will shave with a razor that is hired beyond the River—with the king of Assyria—the head and the hair of the feet, and it will sweep away the beard also.

Arminian

In that day the Lord will shave with a razor that is hired beyond the River—with the king of Assyria—the head and the hair of the feet, and it will sweep away the beard also. Wesley: shave—utterly spoil; hired by Ahaz (2 Kgs 16:7–8); scourging them with a rod of their own making. Successive Assyrian kings, and especially Nebuchadnezzar. Head to foot: total destruction of their state, highest to lowest.

Arminian hired: they paid for this razor. Historic Ahaz’s tribute, not a newspaper. The Lord still holds the blade.

Reformed

Calvin: a hired razor is used freely, not spared as one’s own. He will send the Assyrians with mad violence, even if the razor is blunted. Euphrates will not hinder them. From the top of the head to the feet; kings and princes not exempt. The Lord took such vengeance; we ought to fear the razor with which He has already begun to shave us.

Reformed hired: His instrument, their contract. Shared: razor, hired, beyond the River, king of Assyria, head, feet, beard.

Isaiah 7:21

21In that day a man will keep alive a young cow and two sheep,

Arminian

In that day a man will keep alive a young cow and two sheep. Wesley: they who used to keep great herds shall esteem it happiness if they can keep one cow and two sheep.

Arminian keep alive: scarcity after the shave. Historic Judah emptied, not a culture-war on farming.

Reformed

Calvin: not every man, but those who formerly reared large numbers will be satisfied with a few. All reduced to deep poverty. A country wanting inhabitants is a heavier affliction than few herds.

Reformed keep alive: remnant poverty, not plenty. Shared: that day, young cow, two sheep.

Isaiah 7:22

22and because of the abundance of milk that they give, he will eat curds, for everyone who is left in the land will eat curds and honey.

Arminian

Because of the abundance of milk they give, he will eat curds; everyone left in the land will eat curds and honey. Wesley: large pastures from scarcity of cattle; butter the poor used to sell—now none to sell to, so few people left.

Arminian left: curds and honey here are famine-fare for a leftover, not the child’s diet of v. 15 reused as a slogan. Historic depopulation.

Reformed

Calvin: men so few that a small quantity of milk suffices for all. He does not comfort here; he threatens. Everyone that shall be left: smallness of number again.

Reformed left: the land’s leftover eats what the herds can spare because the people are gone. Shared: abundance of milk, curds, honey, left in the land.

Isaiah 7:23

23In that day every place where there used to be a thousand vines, worth a thousand shekels of silver, will become briers and thorns.

Arminian

Every place where there used to be a thousand vines, worth a thousand shekels of silver, will become briers and thorns. Wesley: each of a thousand vineyards might have been sold or let for a thousand shekels, yearly rent of excellent vineyards.

Arminian thousand: historic vineyards of Judah (5:1–7) gone to thorn. Not a newspaper real-estate chart.

Reformed

Calvin: where the richest cultivation was, briers. Sold for a trifle because there is none to cultivate. No vinedressers after the invading army.

Reformed thousand: the vineyard of ch. 5 under the razor. Shared: thousand vines, thousand shekels, briers, thorns.

Isaiah 7:24

24With bow and arrows a man will come there, for all the land will be briers and thorns.

Arminian

With bow and arrows a man will come there, for all the land will be briers and thorns. Wesley: either to hunt, or to defend themselves from wild beasts that abide in desolate grounds.

Arminian bow: hunting ground where vines were. Historic desolation, not a millennial battlefield.

Reformed

Calvin: where the richest cultivation existed, opportunity for hunting; wild beasts have their dens. A wretched change: fields turned into woods. Not approached by husbandmen but by hunters.

Reformed bow: frightful desolation. Shared: bow, arrows, briers, thorns.

Isaiah 7:25

25And as for all the hills that used to be hoed with a hoe, you will not come there for fear of briers and thorns, but they will become a place where cattle are let loose and where sheep tread.

Arminian

As for all the hills that used to be hoed with a hoe, you will not come there for fear of briers and thorns; they will become a place where cattle are let loose and where sheep tread. Wesley: hills that used to be dug for vines or choice fruit; fences broken, owners slain or carried away; all sorts of cattle enter and feed.

Arminian hoe: the tended hill is pasture because the owners are gone. Historic captivity’s landscape. Immanuel was the sign; this is the requital after the refusal.

Reformed

Calvin: hills once remote become pasturage because men flee there. A most wretched state, when men cannot escape death but by resorting to thorns. Ahaz would despise this, settled on his lees after the siege lifted, relying on Assyria. Isaiah perseveres: destruction would come from the quarter from which he expected preservation.

Reformed hoe: the ally is the ruin. Shared: hills, hoe, fear of briers, cattle, sheep.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a refused sign, a virgin’s son named Immanuel, and a hired razor—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 7:10–25.

Arminian Notable voices

“I will not—by asking a sign, as if I questioned the truth of his word: but this was deep hypocrisy. Therefore—because you despise me, and the sign which I now offer to you, God of his own free grace will send you a more honourable messenger, and give you a nobler sign. A sign—of your deliverance. This promised birth supposed the preservation of that city, and nation and tribe, in and of which the Messiah was to be born. Immanuel—God with us; God dwelling among us, in our nature. God and man meeting in one person, and being a mediator between God and men. This child—Shear-jashub, whom in all probability the prophet pointed at. Shall bring—although God will deliver you at this time, yet he will requite all your wickedness. Hired—by Ahaz; scourging them with a rod of their own making.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 7:12–20)

Reformed Notable voices

“Ahaz refuses the sign by a plausible excuse. Do we tempt God, when we accept what he offers? Certainly not. He who shall reject the signs when offered must also reject the grace of God. We must not separate those things which the Lord has commanded us to join. Behold, a virgin shall conceive. An illustrious prediction concerning the Messiah, called Immanuel. Those who apply this to Hezekiah are excessively impudent. True, thou dost not believe the promises of God, but yet God will fulfill them; for he will at length send his Christ, for whose sake he determines to preserve this city. Though thou art unworthy, yet God will have regard to his own honor. He is called God with us: the only-begotten Son of God clothed himself with our flesh; a union of person, by which Christ became God-man. Butter and honey: marks of human nature, that he will actually appear in flesh. Before the child shall know: not the same child as the virgin’s son, but children in general; both countries deprived of their kings. The Lord shall bring upon thee the king of Assyria: what he hopes to be his preservation will be utterly destructive to him.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 7:12–17)
