Passage

Isaiah 19

Verses 16–25 · ESV · Voices

In that day the Egyptians will be like women, trembling before the hand the LORD of hosts shakes over them; the land of Judah a terror because of the purpose the LORD has purposed. Five cities in Egypt speak the language of Canaan and swear allegiance to the LORD of hosts; an altar to the LORD in the midst of Egypt, a pillar at its border; a sign and witness; when they cry, he will send a savior and defender. The LORD will make himself known to the Egyptians; they will worship, strike and heal. A highway from Egypt to Assyria; Israel the third, a blessing in the midst of the earth: Blessed be Egypt my people, and Assyria the work of my hands, and Israel my inheritance. The unit stays with historic Egypt, Assyria, and Israel—Judah a terror to Egypt, then gospel mercy Wesley and Calvin name in Christ—not a newspaper of later states, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 19:16

16In that day the Egyptians will be like women, and tremble with fear before the hand that the LORD of hosts shakes over them.

Arminian

Wesley: Women—feeble and fearful. Because—because they shall perceive that they do not fight with men only, but with the Lord of hosts, who now lifts up his hand against them, as he did against their forefathers. Shared: tremble at the shaken hand.

Arminian pastoral: the hand is still the LORD’s over historic Egypt. Do not turn “like women” into a culture-war speech. The next verses will name mercy; this fear is the door.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day shall Egypt be like women. He again repeats that the Egyptians will have nothing that is manly. They shall be afraid and fear, because of the shaking of the hand of the Lord of hosts, which he shaketh over them. The same hand that emptied the spirit now makes them tremble.

Reformed pastoral: the tremble is still Egypt’s before that hand. Shared: like women, fear. Historic Egypt, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 19:17

17And the land of Judah will become a terror to the Egyptians. Everyone to whom it is mentioned will fear because of the purpose that the LORD of hosts has purposed against them.

Arminian

Wesley: A terror—because of their manifold injuries against Judah, for which they now apprehend God is calling them to account. Determined—because God is now about to execute his appointed judgments. It—against Egypt. Shared: Judah a terror because of the LORD’s purpose.

Arminian pastoral: the purpose is already appointed, not open theism. Historic Judah named against historic Egypt. Do not hunt another land to make “terror” a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: and the land of Judah shall be a terror to the Egyptians. Some explain it simply of military fear; he means that they will dread Judah because they perceive that God fights for his people, and that the purpose of the Lord of hosts against Egypt is the true cause of the terror. Mention of Judah will be enough to make them fear.

Reformed note: the purpose is still that decree’s. Shared: Judah a terror; the LORD has purposed. Historic neighbors, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 19:18

18In that day there will be five cities in the land of Egypt that speak the language of Canaan and swear allegiance to the LORD of hosts. One of these will be called the City of Destruction.

Arminian

Wesley: In that day—after that time. In the times of the gospel. Five—a considerable number of their chief cities: a certain number being put for an uncertain. Speak—profess the Jewish religion, agree with them in the same mind; which is fitly signified by speaking the same language. Swear—the dedication and yielding up of a person or thing to the Lord by a solemn vow. One—not one of the five, but another city, the sixth city. As divers cities shall be converted and saved, so some other cities shall continue in their impenitency, and be destroyed.

Arminian pastoral: gospel speech in historic Egypt, not a newspaper of later towns. This is not an Arminian–Reformed millennial fight. Five cities swear; a sixth remains for destruction.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day there shall be five cities. After threatening, he promises restoration. Five cities out of six would be saved; the cities in Egypt were very numerous, so that only a sixth part perishes. Speaking with the lip of Canaan: he means agreement with the people of God in the truth revealed to the fathers, confession of the name of God. It is not every kind of agreement that is sufficient. One shall be called the city of destruction, or of the sun, as some read; in any case a remnant of refusal stands beside the swearing cities.

Reformed pastoral: Canaan’s lip is true religion, not a later dialect. Shared: five cities swear to the LORD of hosts. Historic Egypt in gospel mercy, not a millennial chart.

Key contrast Both hear five cities in Egypt speaking Canaan’s language in gospel times—historic Egypt, not a newspaper and not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s five as a round number of chief cities converted, and the City of Destruction as a sixth that remains impenitent. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: five out of six restored to worship, the lip of Canaan as confession of the fathers’ truth, not any merely human agreement.

Isaiah 19:19

19In that day there will be an altar to the LORD in the midst of the land of Egypt, and a pillar to the LORD at its border.

Arminian

Wesley: An altar—the altar is put for the worship of God, as it is in many places both of the Old and New Testament. And nothing is more common in the prophets than to speak of gospel-worship in the phrases of the law. Pillar—a monument of the true religion. Here also he alludes to the ancient custom of erecting pillars to God. The border—as before, in the midst of it. The meaning is, there shall be evidences of their piety in all places. Shared: altar and pillar to the LORD in Egypt.

Arminian pastoral: gospel worship in law’s phrases, not a rebuilt Egyptian temple as a headline. Historic Egypt’s midst and border, not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day shall there be an altar to the Lord in the midst of the land of Egypt, and a pillar at the border. He continues the promise of worship. The altar does not bind us to a new Levitical house in Egypt; the prophets speak of gospel worship in the phrases of the law. Midst and border: piety through the land, not one corner.

Reformed note: the altar is still Egypt’s confession. Shared: a pillar to the LORD. Historic land, not a newspaper of later shrines. Wesley and Calvin share this christological reading of the phrases.

Isaiah 19:20

20It will be a sign and a witness to the LORD of hosts in the land of Egypt. When they cry to the LORD because of oppressors, he will send them a savior and defender, and deliver them.

Arminian

Wesley: It—the altar or pillar last mentioned. A witness—to testify that they own the Lord for their God. Cry—being sorely distressed, they shall turn unto the true God. A great one—a great or mighty saviour, even Christ. Shared: a savior sent when they cry.

Arminian pastoral: the cry is real, and the savior is Christ—shared with Calvin, not open theism. Historic Egypt’s oppressors, not a living politician. Do not invent another deliverer to make this a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: and he will send them a Savior. We cannot serve God unless he first bestow his grace upon us. They will cry unto the Lord: the cry proceeds from faith after they have been allured by his goodness. When the Lord promises that he will send a Savior, by whose hand the Egyptians will be delivered, this can mean no other than Christ; for Egypt was not delivered from its distresses before the doctrine of Christ reached it. Thus Christ delivered that country when it had begun to know him.

Reformed pastoral: the savior is Christ. Shared: Wesley’s mighty saviour, even Christ, and Calvin’s no other than Christ. Historic Egypt, gospel mercy, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 19:21

21And the LORD will make himself known to the Egyptians, and the Egyptians will know the LORD in that day and worship with sacrifice and offering, and they will make vows to the LORD and perform them.

Arminian

Wesley: Shall sacrifice—shall worship God spiritually; which yet is signified by typical phrases. Shared: known, and they know; vows performed.

Arminian pastoral: spiritual worship in sacrificial speech, as v. 19. Historic Egyptians knowing the LORD, not a later state church. Do not restage the vows as a headline.

Reformed

Calvin: and the Lord shall be known to the Egyptians. Isaiah now adds what was most important: not only that they shall be delivered, but that they shall know the Lord, and shall do sacrifice and oblation, and shall vow a vow to the Lord and perform it. Knowledge of God is the fruit; outward acts of devotion follow, but we have no right to invent them. Away with the inventions of men, that we may listen to God alone on this subject.

Reformed note: known by the Egyptians is still that gospel day. Shared: worship with offering; vows kept. Historic Egypt, not a newspaper.

Isaiah 19:22

22And the LORD will strike Egypt, striking and healing, and they will return to the LORD, and he will listen to their pleas for mercy and heal them.

Arminian

Wesley: Smite—God will afflict them and by those afflictions will convert and save them. Shared: strike and heal; they return; he hears.

Arminian pastoral: affliction may yet turn them. Historic Egypt under the rod, then mercy. Do not invent a limited God from “he will listen”; the listening is covenant mercy, not open theism.

Reformed

Calvin: therefore Jehovah will smite Egypt: he shall smite and heal. From what has been already said, the Prophet shews that the same God who wounds also binds up. They shall return to the Lord, and he shall be entreated of them, and shall heal them. Conversion is his work; the healing answers the cry of v. 20.

Reformed pastoral: smiting that heals is still Egypt’s. Shared: they return; he heals. Historic people, not a millennial decoder.

Isaiah 19:23

23In that day there will be a highway from Egypt to Assyria, and Assyria will come into Egypt, and Egypt into Assyria, and the Egyptians will worship with the Assyrians.

Arminian

Wesley: Assyria—they who were implacable enemies one to another, and both to the church of God, shall now be reconciled and united together in the service of God, and love to his church. Serve—the Lord. Shared: a highway, and worship together.

Arminian pastoral: the road is gospel peace between historic enemies, not a twenty-first-century trade corridor. Do not turn Egypt-to-Assyria into a newspaper. This is not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day shall there be a highway out of Egypt to Assyria. The Lord will diffuse his goodness toward nations formerly hostile. The Egyptians shall serve with the Assyrians: not a political merger for its own sake, but joint worship of the true God, the wall of hostility broken in the knowledge of the Lord.

Reformed note: the highway is still that day’s. Shared: Egypt and Assyria worship together. Historic enemies of Judah, now in the church’s hope, not a later map.

Isaiah 19:24

24In that day Israel will be the third with Egypt and Assyria, a blessing in the midst of the earth,

Arminian

Wesley: The third—the third party, in that sacred league, whereby all of them oblige themselves to serve God. Egypt—these are named, because they were the most obstinate enemies to God’s church, but they are here put for all the Gentiles. A blessing—this is peculiar to Israel, who is not only a third party, but is the most eminent of the three, as being the fountain, by which the blessing is conveyed to the other two; because Christ was to be born of them, and the gospel-church and ordinances were first established among them, and from them derived to the Gentiles. The land—or, of those lands, Egypt and Assyria, between which Israel lay.

Arminian pastoral: Christ born of Israel, blessing to Gentiles—shared christology with Calvin. Historic three, not a millennial chart.

Reformed

Calvin: in that day shall Israel be the third with Egypt and with Assyria, even a blessing in the midst of the land. Isaiah concludes the promise: Israel holds her place among the nations now brought in, a blessing in the midst of the earth because from her the knowledge of God went forth. Egypt and Assyria, once the rod and the refuge, now stand with her in worship.

Reformed pastoral: the third is still that sacred company. Shared: a blessing in the midst of the earth. Historic Israel, Egypt, Assyria—not a newspaper of later blocs.

Isaiah 19:25

25whom the LORD of hosts has blessed, saying, “Blessed be Egypt my people, and Assyria the work of my hands, and Israel my inheritance.”

Arminian

Wesley: Whom—that is, which people, Israel, Egypt, and Assyria; of whom he speaks as of one people, because they are all united into one church. My people—this title, and those which follow, that were peculiar to the people of Israel, shall now be given to these and all other nations. Shared: Egypt my people; Assyria the work of my hands; Israel my inheritance.

Arminian close: the titles of Israel spill over to historic enemies now in one church. Gospel mercy, not a twenty-first-century coalition. Wesley and Calvin share the christological reading. Do not stretch “my people” into a millennial camp fight or a newspaper.

Reformed

Calvin: because the Lord of hosts will bless him, saying, Blessed be Egypt my people, and Assyria the work of my hands, and Israel mine inheritance. He assigns a reason: the Lord will not be ashamed to bestow on former enemies the titles that belonged to Israel, when they are united in his worship. The work of his hands and his inheritance stand together. This is the gospel’s reach, not a chart of later states.

Reformed close: the blessing is still the LORD of hosts’. Shared: Egypt, Assyria, and Israel named as one people in the church. Historic nations around Judah—already enough without a later decoder.
