Passage

Isaiah 43

Verses 22–28 · ESV · Voices

After the new thing of 43:14–21—Babylon brought down, a way in the wilderness, rivers in the desert, a people formed for himself that they might declare his praise—the word turns on Jacob: Yet you did not call upon me; you have been weary of me, O Israel. They have not brought sheep or honored him with sacrifices; they have burdened him with their sins and wearied him with their iniquities. He himself blots out their transgressions for his own sake and will not remember their sins, then summons them to argue their case; their first father sinned and their mediators transgressed, and therefore he will profane the princes of the sanctuary and deliver Jacob to utter destruction and Israel to reviling.

Isaiah 43:22

22“Yet you did not call upon me, O Jacob; but you have been weary of me, O Israel!

Arminian

Yet you did not call upon me, O Jacob; but you have been weary of me, O Israel. Wesley: For—God called to the Gentiles to be his people, because the Jews forsook him. Weary—thou hast not esteemed my service to be a privilege, but a burden and bondage.

Arminian hearing: the Jews’ forsaking is why God called the Gentiles; weariness is treating his service as bondage, not privilege. Shared: Jacob did not call; Israel was weary of the LORD. Historic Jacob and Israel, not a newspaper. Do not invent open theism: they forsook him; he is not discovering whether they would call. The Gentile-turn is Wesley’s reading of this verse’s “For,” not a millennial timetable.

Reformed

Thou hast not called on me. Calvin: Confirms they have no merits that induced him to act kindly. Calling on the name includes the whole worship of God, the chief part of which is calling upon him (Ps 50:15). Thou hast been wearied of me: they obeyed reluctantly; the Lord loves a cheerful giver (2 Cor 9:7); reluctant obedience is not sincere worship.

Reformed hearing: the charge is meritless, reluctant worship; calling-on-me is the whole of worship, not a Gentile-turn in this verse. Shared with Wesley: Jacob did not call; Israel was weary of the LORD. Historic Israel’s worship, not a newspaper. No millennial fight. Match v. 25: the same people who would not call are later pardoned for his own sake, not theirs.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take the “For” of this charge as the reason God called the Gentiles to be his people—because the Jews forsook him—and take “weary” as treating his service as a burden and bondage, not a privilege. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take the same charge as proof they have no merits that induced him to act kindly: calling on the name is the whole worship of God (Ps 50:15); they obeyed reluctantly; the Lord loves a cheerful giver (2 Cor 9:7); there is no Gentile-turn in this verse. Shared: Jacob did not call; Israel was weary of the LORD. Historic worship of Jacob, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 43:23

23You have not brought me your sheep for burnt offerings, or honored me with your sacrifices. I have not burdened you with offerings, or wearied you with frankincense.

Arminian

You have not brought me your sheep for burnt offerings, or honored me with your sacrifices. I have not burdened you with offerings, or wearied you with frankincense. Wesley: Honoured—either thou didst neglect sacrificing to me; or didst perform it merely out of custom or didst dishonour me, and pollute thy sacrifices by thy wicked life. Although—God had not laid such heavy burdens upon them, nor required such costly offerings, as might give them cause to be weary, nor such as idolaters did freely perform in the service of their idols.

Arminian hearing: neglect, custom, or a wicked life pollutes the altar; God had not wearied them with costly demands the way idolaters wearied themselves. Shared: empty cult; he had not burdened them. Historic sheep, frankincense, and sanctuary, not a newspaper. Match v. 24: niggardly offerings and the burden of sins complete the same charge. Do not invent a millennial fight.

Reformed

Thou hast not brought. Calvin: why this reproach when they were careful to offer according to the Law? Not limited to captivity. They sacrificed but were wanting in faith and obedience; hands full of blood (Isa 1:15). God rejects services rendered in a slavish spirit.

Reformed hearing: the altar was busy and still rejected; faith and obedience were wanting; slavish spirit is not worship. Shared with Wesley: they did not honor him with sacrifices; empty cult. Historic Law and Isa 1:15, not a newspaper. Largely shared with v. 24’s ceremonies without faith. No keyContrast: both camps read neglected or polluted offering, not a fight over whether sheep were ever brought.

Isaiah 43:24

24You have not bought me sweet cane with money, or satisfied me with the fat of your sacrifices. But you have burdened me with your sins; you have wearied me with your iniquities.

Arminian

You have not bought me sweet cane with money, or satisfied me with the fat of your sacrifices. But you have burdened me with your sins; you have wearied me with your iniquities. Wesley: Sweet cane—used in the precious ointment, Exod 30:34, and for the incense, Exod 30:7. Thou hast been niggardly in my service, when thou hast spared for no cost in the service of thine idols. Nor filled me—thou hast not multiplied thy thank-offerings and free-will offerings, though I have given thee sufficient occasion. But—thou hast made me to bear the load and burden of thy sins.

Arminian hearing: niggardly toward the LORD, lavish toward idols; thank-offerings withheld; he bears the load of their sins. Shared: empty cult; burden of sins. Historic sweet cane of the sanctuary (Exod 30), not a living market. Match v. 23: he had not wearied them with frankincense; they have wearied him with iniquities.

Reformed

Thou hast not bought cane. Calvin: They lost pains in ceremonies without faith. Made me to serve with thy sins: they endeavored to make God submit to them; treated him as a slave by rebellion. Ye have wearied me: God suffered much uneasiness; we wound him when we reject his voice.

Reformed hearing: ceremonies without faith waste the cane; rebellion treats God as a slave; wearied means wounded by a rejected voice. Shared with Wesley: niggardly or empty cult; he bears the burden of their sins. Historic incense and fat of sacrifices, not a newspaper. No keyContrast: 23–24 are largely shared—empty cult versus the burden of sins. Match v. 25: the one they wearied is the one who blots out for his own sake.

Isaiah 43:25

25“I, I am he who blots out your transgressions for my own sake, and I will not remember your sins.

Arminian

I, I am he who blots out your transgressions for my own sake, and I will not remember your sins. Wesley: I—I whom thou hast thus provoked. Mine own sake—not for thy merits, but my own mere goodness. Wesley has no separate lemma on “I will not remember”; hear the second clause with his mere goodness—do not invent a unique Wesley sentence on forgetting.

Arminian hearing: the same “I” they provoked is the one who blots; pardon is mere goodness, not their merits. Shared loudly with Calvin: he blots out for his own sake, not for merits. Do not invent an unlimited-versus-limited atonement fight here; both camps say blot-out for his own sake. No open theism: he blots because he is he, already provoked, for his own sake—not because he learned whether to forgive. Historic Jacob’s transgressions, not a newspaper.

Reformed

I, I am he that blotteth out thy iniquities for mine own sake. Calvin: Contrasts his mercy with all other causes; not induced by anything else; wrong to ascribe redemption to merits or sacrifices. Freely bestowed redemption; pardon before deliverance. Speaks of punishment remitted, not guilt only—against sophists’ satisfactions, indulgences, purgatory. For mine own sake: he pays no regard to us; Ezekiel 36:22, not for your sakes. I will not remember thy sins: consolation to the godly; no one can be certain of pardon unless he rely on the absolute goodness of God.

Reformed hearing: mercy has no other cause; Ezekiel 36:22, not for your sakes; certainty of pardon rests on absolute goodness. Shared loudly with Wesley: blot out for his own sake, not merits—Wesley’s mere goodness and Calvin’s absolute goodness name the same fountain. Calvin’s polemic against sophists’ satisfactions is Rome, not an Arminian-versus-Reformed atonement split. Do not invent unlimited versus limited atonement on this verse. Historic pardon of Jacob, not a newspaper. Match v. 22: the people with no merits are pardoned for his sake.

Isaiah 43:26

26Put me in remembrance; let us argue together; set forth your case, that you may be proved right.

Arminian

Put me in remembrance; let us argue together; set forth your case, that you may be proved right. Wesley: Put me—I remember nothing by which thou hast deserved my favour.

Arminian hearing: the courtroom is empty; they have no remembered desert of favor. Shared: argue together; they will not be proved right by merits. Historic lawsuit with Jacob, not a newspaper. Match v. 25: if he blots for his own sake, they cannot set forth a case of desert. Do not invent open theism: he remembers nothing of their merit because there is none, not because he is still learning the file.

Reformed

Put me in remembrance. Calvin: liberty to say whatever they please in their cause; vehement mockery that shuts mouths more completely than a sentence. Must strip the Jews of the mask of personal worth so they receive grace humbly.

Reformed hearing: the invitation to argue is mockery that closes the mouth; personal worth is a mask to be stripped so grace is received. Shared with Wesley: they have no desert; the case is empty. Historic courtroom with Israel, not a newspaper. No keyContrast: 26 is shared emptiness of the docket. Match v. 27: the first father’s sin will be the exhibit when they try to speak.

Isaiah 43:27

27Your first father sinned, and your mediators transgressed against me.

Arminian

Your first father sinned, and your mediators transgressed against me. Wesley: Thy father—this may be put for their forefathers; as they were sinners, so also were their progenitors, yea even the best of them. Teachers—thy priests and prophets, who were their intercessors with God; if these were transgressors, the people had no reason to fancy themselves innocent.

Arminian hearing: even the best fathers sinned; priests and prophets as intercessors were transgressors, so the people cannot plead innocence. Shared: first father and teachers; forefathers even the best; priests and prophets. Historic Jacob’s line and sanctuary mediators, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Teachers” names the ESV’s “mediators” as priests and prophets, not a different verse. Match v. 26: the empty case now has an exhibit of ancestral and priestly sin.

Reformed

Thy first father sinned. Calvin: Not only Adam or Abraham; under Father he includes many ancestors. Teachers: priests and prophets who ought to have been guides were first to stumble. No class free from vices. Let them produce the smallest reason why I ought to protect them except my own goodness.

Reformed hearing: first father is many ancestors, not Adam-only or Abraham-only; guides stumbled first; no class is clean; the only remaining reason to protect them is his own goodness. Shared with Wesley: forefathers even the best; priests and prophets as teachers. Historic ancestors and sanctuary guides, not a newspaper. No keyContrast: 27 is shared. Calvin’s “not only Adam or Abraham” is a refusal to pin the verse to one name, not a fight with Wesley’s “even the best of them.” Match v. 28: those same sanctuary princes will be profaned.

Isaiah 43:28

28Therefore I will profane the princes of the sanctuary, and deliver Jacob to utter destruction and Israel to reviling.”

Arminian

Therefore I will profane the princes of the sanctuary, and deliver Jacob to utter destruction and Israel to reviling. Wesley: Therefore—I have exposed them to contempt and destruction. Princes—the highest and best of your priests. Curse—to utter destruction, to which persons or things accursed were devoted.

Arminian hearing: the highest priests are exposed; curse here is devoted destruction, the old devoted-thing. Shared: princes of the sanctuary profaned; Jacob delivered to destruction and Israel to reviling. Historic sanctuary and Jacob, not a newspaper. Quote-honesty: Wesley’s “Curse” is his lemma for utter destruction, matching devoted things, not a different English Bible. Skip a thin fork with Calvin’s Deut 28 byword: both still read profaned priests and Jacob given over. Match v. 27: the mediators who transgressed are the princes now profaned.

Reformed

Therefore I will pollute the princes of the sanctuary. Calvin: God is said to pollute, that is, profane, his Church when he throws it aside. Jacob a curse and Israel a reproach: borrowed from Deut 28:37. So they might not complain the punishments were too severe.

Reformed hearing: profane is God throwing his Church aside; curse and reproach echo Deut 28:37; the sentence is not too severe. Shared with Wesley: princes of the sanctuary exposed; Jacob to destruction, Israel to reviling. Historic sanctuary and Deuteronomy’s byword, not a newspaper. No keyContrast: the devoted-destruction versus Deut 28 byword is too thin a fork; both read the same therefore. Match v. 22: the people who would not call are the Jacob now delivered to reviling—and v. 25 still stands: blot-out remains for his own sake, not theirs.
