Passage

Isaiah 43

Verses 14–21 · ESV · Voices

After the witnesses of 43:1–13—Fear not, I am he, none can turn back his work—the Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel, sends to Babylon and brings the Chaldeans down as fugitives in the ships in which they rejoice. He is the LORD, Creator of Israel, their King. The same God who made a way in the sea and quenched Pharaoh’s chariot and horse now says, Remember not the former things: he is doing a new thing, a way in the wilderness and rivers in the desert, water for the wild beasts and for his chosen people, whom he formed for himself that they might declare his praise.

Isaiah 43:14

14Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: “For your sake I send to Babylon and bring them all down as fugitives, even the Chaldeans, in the ships in which they rejoice.

Arminian

Thus says the LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: For your sake I send to Babylon and bring them all down as fugitives, even the Chaldeans, in the ships in which they rejoice. Wesley: Sent—I have sent Cyrus against Babylon, to this very end, that he might deliver you out of captivity. Chaldeans—the common people of Chaldea, who make fearful outcries as they flee away from the Persians in ships.

Arminian hearing: Cyrus is sent for Israel’s sake; the Chaldeans flee in ships with outcries. Shared: historic Persian conquest of Babylon; the Redeemer acts for his people. Historic Babylon, Cyrus, Chaldeans—not a newspaper, not a living politician. Do not invent open theism: he has sent Cyrus to this very end—he is not discovering the outcome as the ships put out.

Reformed

Thus saith Jehovah. Calvin: Cyrus will be but a hired soldier to render his services to the Lord for delivering his people. The prophet does not name Cyrus here but speaks of the army under his command for subduing the Babylonians; accomplished by Cyrus and Darius under God’s direction. Addresses captives who appeared beyond hope. For your sake I have sent to Babylon: while Cyrus was instigated by ambition, nothing was generally believed than that the Jews would return. Easy victories granted so they subdue the East because God reconciled to his Church. They are all fugitives. Cry of the Babylonians in the ships: the Euphrates dried up, they could not escape by land.

Reformed hearing: Cyrus is hired for the Lord’s purpose; the army, not the name, is in this verse; fugitives in ships when the Euphrates is dry. Shared with Wesley: historic Cyrus against Babylon for Israel’s sake. No keyContrast: both camps stay in the old story of Persian conquest. Match the unit: the Redeemer of v. 14 is the Holy One and King of v. 15.

Isaiah 43:15

15I am the LORD, your Holy One, the Creator of Israel, your King.”

Arminian

I am the LORD, your Holy One, the Creator of Israel, your King. Wesley has no extra note on this verse. Paraphrase from Redeemer, Holy One, and King honestly: the same God who in v. 14 sends to Babylon for their sake now seals the word as their Holy One, the Creator of Israel, their King; the titles of v. 14 still govern.

Arminian hearing: Redeemer, Holy One, Creator, King name the one who sent Cyrus. Shared: he is Israel’s Holy One and King. Historic Israel’s God, not a newspaper. Do not invent a unique Wesley sentence here. Do not invent open theism: the King who sends already is their Redeemer.

Reformed

I Jehovah, your Holy One, the Creator of Israel, your King. Calvin: seal of the promise. Holy because he has chosen and separated a people. Creator not universal creation but new creation (Ephesians 2:10). King: even when no semblance of a kingdom was visible; hope for restoration of the kingdom.

Reformed hearing: the titles seal v. 14; Creator is new creation, King is hoped restoration when no kingdom is seen. Shared with Wesley’s titles: Holy One, Creator, King. Match v. 14: the same Redeemer now names himself. Skip a fork on Creator as nation-making versus new creation: both still rest the seal on the LORD himself. Historic Israel’s King, not a living politician.

Isaiah 43:16

16Thus says the LORD, who makes a way in the sea, a path in the mighty waters,

Arminian

Thus says the LORD, who makes a way in the sea, a path in the mighty waters. Wesley has no extra note on this verse. Paraphrase from v. 17 honestly: the way in the sea is the Exodus path; the same LORD who will quench Pharaoh’s chariot and horse in v. 17 is the one who made a path in the mighty waters.

Arminian hearing: Red Sea memory grounds the new word. Shared: a way in the sea is the former deliverance. Historic Exodus, not a newspaper. Do not invent a unique Wesley sentence here. Match v. 17: chariot and horse name who was brought through that path to their end.

Reformed

Who maketh a way in the sea. Calvin: reminds of former benefits (Red Sea, Jordan) so they believe he will be no less gracious. Same God who opened a way through the sea.

Reformed hearing: former path through the waters is the ground of present hope. Shared with Wesley’s Exodus reading of v. 17: Red Sea, not a new sea. Historic Red Sea and Jordan, not a newspaper. No keyContrast: 43:16–17 are shared Exodus memory. Match v. 17: the same God who opened the sea quenched the host.

Isaiah 43:17

17who brings forth chariot and horse, army and warrior; they lie down, they cannot rise, they are extinguished, quenched like a wick:

Arminian

Who brings forth chariot and horse, army and warrior; they lie down, they cannot rise, they are extinguished, quenched like a wick. Wesley: The chariot—Pharaoh and his chariots and horses and army. Lay down—in the bottom of the sea. They never rose again to molest the Israelites. Quenched—as the wick of a candle when it is put into the water is extinguished.

Arminian hearing: Pharaoh’s host at the bottom of the sea; never rose to molest Israel again. Shared: Exodus memory; quenched like a wick. Historic Pharaoh and Egypt, not a newspaper. Match v. 16: the way in the sea is where they lay down. Do not invent a millennial fight as the camp split.

Reformed

When he bringeth out chariot and horse. Calvin: no power hinders. Pharaoh drowned. Continual victory; enemies quenched like flax, immediately consumed.

Reformed hearing: no power hinders; the drowning is immediate consumption. Shared with Wesley: Pharaoh; quenched like a wick or flax. Historic Egypt, not a living army. No keyContrast: both camps read the Exodus host extinguished. Match v. 18: this former thing is what they are told not to remember in comparison.

Isaiah 43:18

18“Remember not the former things, nor consider the things of old.

Arminian

Remember not the former things, nor consider the things of old. Wesley: Remember not—though your former deliverance out of Egypt was glorious: yet in comparison of that inestimable mercy of sending the Messiah, all your former deliverances are scarce worthy of your remembrance and consideration.

Arminian hearing: Egypt was glorious, yet scarce worthy beside sending the Messiah. Shared: the former thing is Egypt; they are not told to despise it as false, but not to rest there. Historic Exodus versus the new mercy, not a newspaper. The fork on how far the new thing reaches waits at v. 19; no keyContrast here. Do not invent open theism: he tells them what not to remember because he already knows the greater mercy.

Reformed

Remember not former things. Calvin: all miracles of the first redemption of little importance compared with the more remarkable miracle soon to be wrought. Not that they should forget Egypt (Exodus 20:2) but by comparison (Jeremiah 23:7–8). Latter redemption far more illustrious. Do not limit this prediction to a small number of years; he does not separate commencement and progress but extends the blessed consequences of their return till Christ.

Reformed hearing: do not forget Egypt, but by comparison the latter is far more illustrious; the return’s blessed consequences run till Christ. Shared with Wesley: Egypt is the former thing; a greater mercy outstrips it. Historic first redemption, not a millennial timetable fight. Match v. 19: Calvin’s one-redemption line and Wesley’s Messiah comparison meet there.

Isaiah 43:19

19Behold, I am doing a new thing; now it springs forth, do you not perceive it? I will make a way in the wilderness and rivers in the desert.

Arminian

Behold, I am doing a new thing; now it springs forth, do you not perceive it? I will make a way in the wilderness and rivers in the desert. Wesley: A new thing—such a work as was never yet done in the world. Now—the scripture often speaks of things at a great distance of time as if they were now at hand; to make us sensible of the inconsiderableness of time and all temporal things in comparison of God and eternal things; upon which account it is said that a thousand years are in God’s sight but as one day. Hear with his v. 18: the inestimable mercy of sending the Messiah makes former Egypt scarce worthy of remembrance.

Arminian hearing: the new thing is a work never yet done; “now” may be a great distance because a thousand years are as one day. Shared: God does a new thing; a way in the wilderness and rivers in the desert. Historic wilderness, not a newspaper. Do not invent open theism: the new thing springs because he declared it; “now” is God’s timing, not ignorance of when it will rise.

Reformed

Behold I do a new thing. Calvin: unheard of, uncommon; the sun making stars disappear. Now it shall arise: time shall not be long; seventy years of captivity versus God. Way in the wilderness, rivers. Almost all Christian commentators interpret this as referring absolutely to the coming of Christ—they are undoubtedly mistaken; the Jews are also wrong when they limit it to the redemption from Babylon. Include the whole period which followed the redemption from Babylon down to the coming of Christ. The two are one redemption. Second redemption leaves the first far behind. Haggai 2:6–9: glory of the latter house greater because of the Desire of nations.

Reformed hearing: not Christ-only, not Babylon-only; Babylon-to-Christ is one redemption; seventy years are short beside God. Shared with Wesley: unheard-of new thing; way in the wilderness. Historic return and Christ, not a millennial or dispensational fight. Do not invent open theism: time shall not be long because he said so.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take the “new thing” as a work never yet done in the world, and “now” as Scripture’s way of speaking of things at a great distance as if they were at hand—because a thousand years are in God’s sight but as one day—and they hear it with his v. 18: former Egypt is scarce worthy of remembrance compared with sending the Messiah. Reformed readings, with Calvin, refuse both an interpretation that refers this absolutely only to the coming of Christ and a limit to Babylon only: include the whole period from the redemption from Babylon down to the coming of Christ as one redemption; the latter more illustrious; time shall not be long (seventy years versus God). Shared: Egypt is the former thing; God does a new thing; a way in the wilderness. Historic and christological accent, not a millennial camp fight.

Isaiah 43:20

20The wild beasts will honor me, the jackals and the ostriches, for I give water in the wilderness, rivers in the desert, to give drink to my chosen people,

Arminian

The wild beasts will honor me, the jackals and the ostriches, for I give water in the wilderness, rivers in the desert, to give drink to my chosen people. Wesley: The beast—shall have cause, if they had abilities, to praise me for their share in this mercy. Dragons—which live in dry and barren deserts.

Arminian hearing: even desert beasts would praise if they could; the water is for his chosen people. Shared: water in the wilderness; chosen people drink. Historic wilderness, jackals, ostriches—not a newspaper. Match v. 19: the rivers promised there now water beasts and people. Do not invent open theism: he gives the water he announced.

Reformed

The beast of the field shall honor me. Calvin: power so visible brute animals acknowledge God. My people, my chosen: reminds them of adoption; he who once chose them does not change his purpose.

Reformed hearing: visible power even beasts acknowledge; chosen is adoption that does not change purpose. Shared with Wesley: beasts honor; the drink is for his people. Historic desert creatures, not a newspaper. Match v. 21: the chosen of v. 20 are the people formed for his praise. Skip a fork on “if they had abilities” versus brute acknowledgment: both still give the honor to God for water in the desert.

Isaiah 43:21

21the people whom I formed for myself that they might declare my praise.”

Arminian

The people whom I formed for myself that they might declare my praise. Wesley has no extra note on this verse. Paraphrase from v. 7 and v. 19 honestly: formed for myself, they shall shew forth my praise—the same people created for his glory in 43:7, now given rivers in the desert so that they declare that praise.

Arminian hearing: forming is for his praise; the new thing of v. 19 serves that end. Shared: formed for himself; they declare his praise. Historic Israel formed for praise, not a newspaper. Do not invent a unique Wesley sentence here. Do not invent open theism: he formed them for this end and will have the praise he formed them to declare.

Reformed

This people have I created for myself: they shall declare my praise. Calvin: he will necessarily do what he said because it concerns his glory to preserve the people he has chosen. End of our election: that we may shew forth the glory of God (Ephesians 1:4–6; 1 Peter 2:9).

Reformed hearing: preservation is necessary because his glory is bound to the people he chose; election’s end is to shew forth his glory. Shared with Wesley: formed for myself; they shall shew forth my praise. Historic chosen people, not a living politician. No keyContrast: 43:21 is shared. Match v. 20: my chosen drink so that the people formed for himself declare his praise.
