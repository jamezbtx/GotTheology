Passage

Isaiah 10

Verses 20–34 · WEB · Voices

A remnant of Israel will stop leaning on the Assyrian who struck them and will rely truly on the LORD. Only a remnant will return, but Zion need not fear. The Assyrian army marches town by town toward Jerusalem, only to be felled like a forest by the LORD.

Isaiah 10:20

20It will come to pass in that day that the remnant of Israel, and those who have escaped from the house of Jacob will no more again lean on him who struck them, but shall lean on the LORD, the Holy One of Israel, in truth.

Arminian

In that day that the remnant of Israel, and those who have escaped from the house of Jacob will no more again lean on him who struck them, but shall lean on the LORD, the Holy One of Israel, in truth. Wesley: Jews preserved from the sweeping Assyrian scourge; they shall no more trust the Assyrians for help.

“Remnant”—leftover after the rod of 10:5. They still stop leaning on the one who struck them (7:9; 8:6). In truth is still a lean. The remnant here is not a chart of modern nations.

Reformed

Calvin: the result of the blow: a small portion survives and learns to hope in God. Until afflicted, all wished to be Abraham’s children; it was pretense. Hope will be true when cleansed from impostures. We cannot trust God unless we withhold the heart from creatures; otherwise the heart is divided and double.

True lean after the false one is burned off. Both traditions agree: that day, remnant of Israel, house of Jacob, no more again lean on him who struck them, Holy One of Israel, in truth.

Isaiah 10:21

21A remnant will return, even the remnant of Jacob, to the mighty God.

Arminian

A remnant will return, even the remnant of Jacob, to the mighty God. The name in Isaiah’s son (7:3) now spoken over Jacob. Mighty God is the child’s name (9:6).

“Return”—a genuine turning of a leftover to the same Mighty God. They still return.

Reformed

Calvin: confirms the former; alludes to Shear-jashub. To the mighty God: the people, after apostasy, will acknowledge Him as guardian of their salvation. They ran to Assyria because they did not think God sufficient. This attribute is to excite confidence.

“Return”—the son’s name kept. Held in common: remnant will return, remnant of Jacob, mighty God.

Isaiah 10:22

22For though your people, Israel, are like the sand of the sea, only a remnant of them will return. A destruction is determined, overflowing with righteousness.

Arminian

Though your people, Israel, are like the sand of the sea, only a remnant of them will return. A destruction is determined, overflowing with righteousness. Wesley: a remnant only. The destruction was already decreed by the fixed counsel of God, and therefore must needs be executed, like a deluge. Righteousness—with justice, and yet with clemency: He has spared a considerable remnant when He might have destroyed them utterly.

Only a remnant returns, and the destruction is decreed. Arminian readers see the decree as a righteous judgment that still leaves a remnant, spared by clemency they had not earned.

Reformed

Calvin: he casts down hypocrites from confidence in flesh-descent from Abraham. Consumption: a diminution almost to ruin. O Israel: the true, not the spurious. Overflowing with righteousness: this small company will overflow righteousness; a remnant sufficient to fill the world with rivers of it. Paul quotes this (Rom 9:27–28) as fulfilled when the Jews were cut off and a small remnant preserved. Not a promiscuous multitude that had apostatized.

The sand is not the Church; the remnant is. Paul reads this line in Romans 9.

Key contrast Both confess only a remnant of Jacob returns, and that destruction is determined, overflowing with righteousness (10:22). Arminians detect a remnant only, yet considerable clemency: He spared when He might have destroyed utterly (Wesley). Reformed readers hear hypocrites cast down from Abrahamic sand: the consumption is determined, and Paul reads the remnant as those preserved when the rest are cut off (Calvin; Rom 9:27–28).

Isaiah 10:23

23For the Lord, GOD of Armies, will make a full end, and that determined, throughout all the earth.

Arminian

The Lord, GOD of Armies, will make a full end, and that determined, throughout all the earth. Wesley: in all parts of the land, not excepting Jerusalem, which was to be preserved in the Assyrian invasion.

“Full end”—the decree of v. 22 done. Jerusalem still sits inside it.

Reformed

Calvin: repetition to wound self-complacency. The Lord is the author of this consumption, against those who thought promises made it impossible they should perish. In the midst: the heart of the land, its best-defended places. Paul (Rom 9:28) repeats this as fulfilled when the Jews were cut off from the kingdom of God and a small remnant preserved.

He does what He decreed. Lord God of hosts, full end, as decreed, midst of all the earth.

Isaiah 10:24

24Therefore the Lord, GOD of Armies, says, “My people who dwell in Zion, don’t be afraid of the Assyrian, though he strike you with the rod, and lift up his staff against you, as Egypt did.

Arminian

Says, My people who dwell in Zion, don’t be afraid of the Assyrian, though he strike you with the rod, and lift up his staff against you, as Egypt did. Wesley: inference from the whole prophecy; seeing the Assyrian shall be destroyed. He shall afflict, but not destroy. As Egypt formerly did.

A word to a Zion under a rod. Sennacherib, as Egypt was historic. The remnant of v. 20 is this people.

Reformed

Calvin: consolation for the godly few, not all who gloried in the name. The oppression is a rod, not destruction. Pattern of Egypt: wretched, not deadly. As He was victorious over Pharaoh, so over the Assyrian.

The rod of 10:5, now limited. Neither side disputes this: my people, Zion, be not afraid, Assyrian, rod, staff, Egyptians.

Isaiah 10:25

25For yet a very little while, and the indignation against you will be accomplished, and my anger will be directed to his destruction.”

Arminian

For yet a very little while, and the indignation against you will be accomplished, and my anger will be directed to his destruction. Wesley: indignation—mine anger towards the Assyrian; cease as anger does when vengeance is fully executed.

A dated end to this fury. It will end, then turn on them.

Reformed

Calvin: includes not only Sennacherib’s siege but later calamities, that the godly be fortified. Consumption: Babylon as a grave. Yet in the urgent danger, the Lord will spare Zion and destroy the Assyrian. Finish: until He has discharged His anger on them.

The timing is God’s, not theirs. Common ground: very little while, fury come to an end, anger, their destruction.

Isaiah 10:26

26The LORD of Armies will stir up a scourge against him, as in the slaughter of Midian at the rock of Oreb. His rod will be over the sea, and he will lift it up like he did against Egypt.

Arminian

The LORD of Armies will stir up a scourge against him, as in the slaughter of Midian at the rock of Oreb. His rod will be over the sea, and he will lift it up like he did against Egypt. Wesley: a destroying angel; Midian slain suddenly in the night; the sea divided for deliverance and Egypt’s ruin.

Midian and the sea are the pattern, as at 9:4. He still lifts the staff.

Reformed

Calvin: scourge, not rod: He will treat the enemies more harshly than they treated the Jews. Two examples: Midian (Judg 7) and Egypt at the sea. Unexpected means. Afflictions are His rods; He does not permit Satan to inflict deadly ones on us. Awful destruction awaits the enemies.

Same hand, other end of the stick. Lord of hosts, whip, Midian, rock of Oreb, staff, sea, Egypt.

Isaiah 10:27

27It will happen in that day that his burden will depart from off your shoulder, and his yoke from off your neck, and the yoke shall be destroyed because of the anointing oil.

Arminian

In that day that his burden will depart from off your shoulder, and his yoke from off your neck, and the yoke shall be destroyed because of the fat. Wesley reads the Hebrew as anointing: possibly David, for whose sake many deliverances; but the Messiah is principally intended, of whom David was a type; foundation of all the promises (2 Cor 1:20) and of all deliverances in all ages. The WEB’s “anointing oil” stands close to Wesley’s reading.

Shared christology if the oil is the Anointed; yoke of Assyria first. The child of 9:6 is why the yoke snaps.

Reformed

Calvin: tyranny described two ways to show the blessing. Because of the anointing: he reminds them of Christ; the kingdom set apart, a type of Christ. This benefit depends on the power of Christ, not on the ability of man. Through His kindness they will be delivered from that tyranny.

The oil is why, not the ox’s neck. Wesley and Calvin both land the breaking on the Anointed, David’s greater Son.

Isaiah 10:28

28He has come to Aiath. He has passed through Migron. At Michmash he stores his baggage.

Arminian

He has come to Aiath; he has passed through Migron; at Michmash he stores his baggage. Wesley: the prophet returns to the invasion as a thing present, and sets down the stages toward Jerusalem. Sennacherib; leaving less necessary things for speed.

The invader’s march is traced through named towns.

Reformed

Calvin: the siege at hand; the whole march set before their eyes so the godly see the Assyrian does not move a step but by appointment. He had described it by the Prophet’s mouth.

“Come”—every stage already spoken. Aiath, Migron, Michmash, baggage.

Isaiah 10:29

29They have gone over the pass. They have taken up their lodging at Geba. Ramah trembles. Gibeah of Saul has fled.

Arminian

They have gone over the pass. They have taken up their lodging at Geba. Gibeah of Saul has fled. Wesley: the people fled to Jerusalem for fear of the Assyrian.

Town after town falls within a single night.

Reformed

Calvin: they crossed the strait of Michmash without opposition—no stand in the open country. Terror so great the country surrenders at the first attack. He passes from king to army.

The pass is lost. Pass, Geba, Ramah, Gibeah of Saul.

Isaiah 10:30

30Cry aloud with your voice, daughter of Gallim! Listen, Laishah! You poor Anathoth!

Arminian

Cry aloud with your voice, daughter of Gallim! Listen, Laishah! You poor Anathoth. Wesley: Jerusalem the mother city; lesser towns her daughters.

Named villages on the road.

Reformed

Calvin: neigh—howling heard at a distance. Cities called daughters. The howling heard even in neighboring towns: groans of Anathoth at Laish.

The road is loud. Gallim, Laishah, Anathoth.

Isaiah 10:31

31Madmenah is a fugitive. The inhabitants of Gebim flee for safety.

Arminian

Madmenah is a fugitive. The inhabitants of Gebim flee for safety. The next names empty.

The towns are evacuated as the people flee.

Reformed

Calvin: the city shaken as if removed. Disorder of a people in flight. Gebim crowd together, or rush without a free passage.

The map is emptying toward Zion. Madmenah and Gebim are emptied.

Isaiah 10:32

32This very day he will halt at Nob. He shakes his hand at the mountain of the daughter of Zion, the hill of Jerusalem.

Arminian

This very day he will halt at Nob. He shakes his hand at the mountain of the daughter of Zion, the hill of Jerusalem. Wesley: by way of commination.

“Nob”—the last ridge, the fist at the temple hill. The remnant of v. 20 is inside that hill.

Reformed

Calvin: one day longer in Nob, a village by Jerusalem; great part of the day left to prepare the siege. Rapid march; Zion near utter destruction. Shake the hand: he thinks he can storm it by the slightest will. None ventured to oppose. That it was preserved is extraordinary favor, a sheep from a lion’s jaws.

As far as the rod is allowed. Common to both: this very day, Nob, shake his fist, daughter of Zion, hill of Jerusalem.

Isaiah 10:33

33Behold, the Lord, GOD of Armies, will lop the boughs with terror. The tall will be cut down, and the lofty will be brought low.

Arminian

Behold, the Lord, GOD of Armies, will lop the boughs with terror. The tall will be cut down, and the lofty will be brought low. Wesley: the top-bough, Sennacherib, with a most terrible stroke.

The fist of v. 32 is answered. The lofty of 2:12–17, now this forest.

Reformed

Calvin: almost all apply this to the Assyrians; I think it threatens the Jews’ own calamity, to which chapter 11 is the consolation. Tall trees: all that is powerful. The next chapter’s comfort fits this grief. (Wesley hears Sennacherib; Calvin hears Judah’s own height coming down. Either way the Lord of hosts hews, and 11:1’s stump is next.)

He wields the axe of v. 15. Lord God of hosts, lop the boughs, terrifying power, great in height, lofty brought low.

Isaiah 10:34

34He will cut down the thickets of the forest with iron, and Lebanon will fall by the Mighty One.

Arminian

He will cut down the thickets of the forest with iron, and Lebanon will fall by the Mighty One. Wesley: as trees are cut with iron; his Lebanon—the Assyrian army, the forest of v. 18.

Host on the ridge, falling by the Holy One who was a flame (10:17). The remnant that returns (v. 21) is not this forest. Next unsplit: a shoot from Jesse’s stump.

Reformed

Calvin: if this were the Assyrian, Lebanon would not fit; he reads it as Judah stripped of ornaments, to which “a shoot from the stump of Jesse” is the remedy. Thick places cleared. The Majestic One does it.

The forest falls; the stump is 11:1. Thickets, forest, axe, Lebanon, Majestic One.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

A remnant that returns to the mighty God, a decreed consumption, and boughs lopped at Nob: older Arminian and Reformed writers, each in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“And such—Jews preserved from that sweeping Assyrian scourge. Stay—shall no more trust to the Assyrians for help. A remnant—or, a remnant only. The consumption—the destruction of Israel was already decreed by the fixed counsel of God, and therefore must needs be executed, and like a deluge overflow them. Righteousness—with justice, and yet with clemency, inasmuch as he has spared a considerable remnant, when he might have destroyed them utterly. Be not afraid—the Assyrian shall be destroyed; he shall afflict, but not destroy thee. The anointing—possibly David, for whose sake God gave many deliverances; but the Messiah is principally intended, of whom David was a type. He is the foundation of all the promises, and of all the deliverances granted to God’s people in all ages. The bough—the top-bough, Sennacherib. Lebanon—the Assyrian army, before compared to a forest.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 10:20–34)

Reformed Notable voices

“In that day: the result of the chastisement. You would wish the Assyrians far off; this blow is as necessary as medicine. Until the Lord afflicted them, all wished to be accounted children of Abraham; it was mere pretense. Their hope will be true and sincere when they have been cleansed from impostures. We cannot place our confidence in God unless we altogether withhold our heart from creatures. A remnant shall return: an allusion to Shear-jashub. To the mighty God: they betook themselves to Assyria because they did not think God sufficient. Though thy people be as the sand of the sea: he casts down hypocrites from foolish confidence in descent from Abraham. A consumption decreed: they were not far from utter extermination. Overflowing with righteousness: this very small company will overflow righteousness; this remnant, though small, will be sufficient to cause rivers of righteousness to flow. Paul quotes this (Rom 9:27–28) as fulfilled when the Jews were cut off and a small remnant preserved. Because of the anointing: he reminds them of Christ; through his kindness they will be delivered. This benefit depends on the power of Christ, not on the ability of man. Lop the bough: I think this threatens the Jews’ own calamity, to which the consolation of the next chapter is the remedy.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 10:20–33)
