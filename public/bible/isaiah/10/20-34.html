Passage

Isaiah 10

Verses 20–34 · ESV · Voices

In that day the remnant of Israel, survivors of Jacob, will no more lean on him who struck them, but on the Lord, the Holy One of Israel, in truth. A remnant will return to the mighty God. Though Israel be as the sand of the sea, only a remnant returns. Destruction is decreed, overflowing with righteousness. Zion, be not afraid of the Assyrian: a little while, then the whip as at Midian and Egypt. The yoke broken because of the fat. He comes Aiath to Nob, shakes his fist at Zion—then the Lord lops the boughs; Lebanon falls by the Majestic One.

Isaiah 10:20

20In that day the remnant of Israel and the survivors of the house of Jacob will no more lean on him who struck them, but will lean on the Lord, the Holy One of Israel, in truth.

Arminian

In that day the remnant of Israel and the survivors of the house of Jacob will no more lean on him who struck them, but will lean on the Lord, the Holy One of Israel, in truth. Wesley: Jews preserved from the sweeping Assyrian scourge; they shall no more trust the Assyrians for help.

Arminian remnant: historic leftover after the rod of 10:5. They still stop leaning on the one who struck them (7:9; 8:6). In truth is still a real lean. Not a modern-nation chart.

Reformed

Calvin: the result of the blow: a small portion survives and learns to hope in God. Until afflicted, all wished to be Abraham’s children; it was pretense. Hope will be true when cleansed from impostures. We cannot trust God unless we withhold the heart from creatures; otherwise the heart is divided and double.

Reformed remnant: true lean after the false one is burned off. Shared: that day, remnant of Israel, house of Jacob, no more lean on him who struck them, Holy One of Israel, in truth.

Isaiah 10:21

21A remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob, to the mighty God.

Arminian

A remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob, to the mighty God. The name in Isaiah’s son (7:3) now spoken over Jacob. Mighty God is the child’s name (9:6).

Arminian return: a real turning of a real leftover to the same Mighty God. Historic Jacob, not a millennial camp. They still return.

Reformed

Calvin: confirms the former; alludes to Shear-jashub. To the mighty God: the people, after apostasy, will acknowledge Him as guardian of their salvation. They ran to Assyria because they did not think God sufficient. This attribute is to excite confidence.

Reformed return: the son’s name kept. Shared: remnant will return, remnant of Jacob, mighty God.

Isaiah 10:22

22For though your people Israel be as the sand of the sea, only a remnant of them will return. Destruction is decreed, overflowing with righteousness.

Arminian

Though your people Israel be as the sand of the sea, only a remnant of them will return. Destruction is decreed, overflowing with righteousness. Wesley: a remnant only. The destruction was already decreed by the fixed counsel of God, and therefore must needs be executed, like a deluge. Righteousness—with justice, and yet with clemency: He has spared a considerable remnant when He might have destroyed them utterly.

Arminian only: a real leftover, not the sand. The decree stands; the clemency is still a sparing they did not earn. Not a fake warning: only a remnant. Not a modern-nation chart. Match the hub: a King whose counsel is fixed, leaving this returning as a real remnant under that decree.

Reformed

Calvin: he casts down hypocrites from confidence in flesh-descent from Abraham. Consumption: a diminution almost to ruin. O Israel: the true, not the spurious. Overflowing with righteousness: this small company will overflow righteousness; a remnant sufficient to fill the world with rivers of it. Paul quotes this (Rom 9:27–28) as fulfilled when the Jews were cut off and a small remnant preserved. Not a promiscuous multitude that had apostatized.

Reformed only: the sand is not the Church; the remnant is. Shared: sand of the sea, only a remnant, destruction decreed, overflowing with righteousness. Paul reads this line; neither camp makes it a newspaper.

Key contrast Both confess only a remnant of Jacob returns, and that destruction is decreed, overflowing with righteousness (10:22). Historic leftover after Assyria, not a modern-nation chart, and not a millennial camp fight. Arminians typically hear a remnant only, yet considerable clemency: He spared when He might have destroyed utterly (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear hypocrites cast down from Abrahamic sand: the consumption is determined, and Paul reads the remnant as those preserved when the rest are cut off (Calvin; Rom 9:27–28).

Isaiah 10:23

23For the Lord God of hosts will make a full end, as decreed, in the midst of all the earth.

Arminian

The Lord God of hosts will make a full end, as decreed, in the midst of all the earth. Wesley: in all parts of the land, not excepting Jerusalem, which was to be preserved in the Assyrian invasion.

Arminian full end: the decree of v. 22 done. Historic land, not a globe-chart. Jerusalem still sits inside it.

Reformed

Calvin: repetition to wound self-complacency. The Lord is the author of this consumption, against those who thought promises made it impossible they should perish. In the midst: the heart of the land, its best-defended places. Paul (Rom 9:28) repeats this as fulfilled when the Jews were cut off from the kingdom of God and a small remnant preserved.

Reformed full end: He does what He decreed. Shared: Lord God of hosts, full end, as decreed, midst of all the earth.

Isaiah 10:24

24Therefore thus says the Lord God of hosts: “O my people, who dwell in Zion, be not afraid of the Assyrian when he strikes you with his rod and lifts up his staff against you as the Egyptians did.

Arminian

O my people, who dwell in Zion, be not afraid of the Assyrian when he strikes you with his rod and lifts up his staff against you as the Egyptians did. Wesley: inference from the whole prophecy; seeing the Assyrian shall be destroyed. He shall afflict, but not destroy. As Egypt formerly did.

Arminian be not afraid: a real word to a real Zion under a real rod. Historic Sennacherib, as Egypt was historic. The remnant of v. 20 is this people.

Reformed

Calvin: consolation for the godly few, not all who gloried in the name. The oppression is a rod, not destruction. Pattern of Egypt: wretched, not deadly. As He was victorious over Pharaoh, so over the Assyrian.

Reformed be not afraid: the rod of 10:5, now limited. Shared: my people, Zion, be not afraid, Assyrian, rod, staff, Egyptians.

Isaiah 10:25

25For in a very little while my fury will come to an end, and my anger will be directed to their destruction.

Arminian

For in a very little while my fury will come to an end, and my anger will be directed to their destruction. Wesley: indignation—mine anger towards the Assyrian; cease as anger does when vengeance is fully executed.

Arminian little while: a dated end to this fury. Not open theism: it will end, then turn on them. Historic night coming (2 Kgs 19).

Reformed

Calvin: includes not only Sennacherib’s siege but later calamities, that the godly be fortified. Consumption: Babylon as a grave. Yet in the urgent danger, the Lord will spare Zion and destroy the Assyrian. Finish: until He has discharged His anger on them.

Reformed little while: His clock, not theirs. Shared: very little while, fury come to an end, anger, their destruction.

Isaiah 10:26

26And the Lord of hosts will wield against them a whip, as when he struck Midian at the rock of Oreb. And his staff will be over the sea, and he will lift it as he did in Egypt.

Arminian

The Lord of hosts will wield against them a whip, as when he struck Midian at the rock of Oreb. His staff will be over the sea, and he will lift it as he did in Egypt. Wesley: a destroying angel; Midian slain suddenly in the night; the sea divided for deliverance and Egypt’s ruin.

Arminian whip: Midian and the sea are the pattern, as at 9:4. Historic angel, historic night. He still lifts the staff.

Reformed

Calvin: scourge, not rod: He will treat the enemies more harshly than they treated the Jews. Two examples: Midian (Judg 7) and Egypt at the sea. Unexpected means. Afflictions are His rods; He does not permit Satan to inflict deadly ones on us. Awful destruction awaits the enemies.

Reformed whip: same hand, other end of the stick. Shared: Lord of hosts, whip, Midian, rock of Oreb, staff, sea, Egypt.

Isaiah 10:27

27And in that day his burden will depart from your shoulder, and his yoke from your neck; and the yoke will be broken because of the fat.”

Arminian

In that day his burden will depart from your shoulder, and his yoke from your neck; and the yoke will be broken because of the fat. Wesley reads the Hebrew as anointing: possibly David, for whose sake many deliverances; but the Messiah is principally intended, of whom David was a type; foundation of all the promises (2 Cor 1:20) and of all deliverances in all ages. ESV’s fat is the ox grown too strong for the yoke.

Arminian broken: shared christology if the oil is the Anointed; historic yoke of Assyria first. The child of 9:6 is why the yoke snaps. Not a millennial camp fight.

Reformed

Calvin: tyranny described two ways to show the blessing. Because of the anointing: he reminds them of Christ; the kingdom set apart, a type of Christ. This benefit depends on the power of Christ, not on the ability of man. Through His kindness they will be delivered from that tyranny.

Reformed broken: the oil is why, not the ox’s neck. Shared: that day, burden, shoulder, yoke, neck, broken. Wesley and Calvin both land the breaking on the Anointed, David’s greater Son.

Isaiah 10:28

28He has come to Aiath; he has passed through Migron; at Michmash he stores his baggage;

Arminian

He has come to Aiath; he has passed through Migron; at Michmash he stores his baggage. Wesley: the prophet returns to the invasion as a thing present, and sets down the stages toward Jerusalem. Sennacherib; leaving less necessary things for speed.

Arminian come: historic march, named towns. Not a newspaper column.

Reformed

Calvin: the siege at hand; the whole march set before their eyes so the godly see the Assyrian does not move a step but by appointment. He had described it by the Prophet’s mouth.

Reformed come: every stage already spoken. Shared: Aiath, Migron, Michmash, baggage.

Isaiah 10:29

29they have crossed over the pass; at Geba they lodge for the night; Ramah trembles; Gibeah of Saul has fled.

Arminian

They have crossed over the pass; at Geba they lodge for the night; Ramah trembles; Gibeah of Saul has fled. Wesley: the people fled to Jerusalem for fear of the Assyrian.

Arminian trembled: real towns, real night. Historic Benjamin’s ridge.

Reformed

Calvin: they crossed the strait of Michmash without opposition—no stand in the open country. Terror so great the country surrenders at the first attack. He passes from king to army.

Reformed trembled: the pass is lost. Shared: pass, Geba, Ramah, Gibeah of Saul.

Isaiah 10:30

30Cry aloud, O daughter of Gallim! Give attention, O Laishah! O poor Anathoth!

Arminian

Cry aloud, O daughter of Gallim! Give attention, O Laishah! O poor Anathoth. Wesley: Jerusalem the mother city; lesser towns her daughters.

Arminian cry: named villages on the road. Historic Anathoth, Jeremiah’s later town. Not a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: neigh—howling heard at a distance. Cities called daughters. The howling heard even in neighboring towns: groans of Anathoth at Laish.

Reformed cry: the road is loud. Shared: Gallim, Laishah, Anathoth.

Isaiah 10:31

31Madmenah is in flight; the inhabitants of Gebim flee for safety.

Arminian

Madmenah is in flight; the inhabitants of Gebim flee for safety. The next names empty.

Arminian flight: historic evacuation. They still flee.

Reformed

Calvin: the city shaken as if removed. Disorder of a people in flight. Gebim crowd together, or rush without a free passage.

Reformed flight: the map is emptying toward Zion. Shared: Madmenah, Gebim.

Isaiah 10:32

32This very day he will halt at Nob; he will shake his fist at the mount of the daughter of Zion, the hill of Jerusalem.

Arminian

This very day he will halt at Nob; he will shake his fist at the mount of the daughter of Zion, the hill of Jerusalem. Wesley: by way of commination.

Arminian Nob: the last ridge, the fist at the temple hill. Historic Sennacherib, not a millennial siege. The remnant of v. 20 is inside that hill.

Reformed

Calvin: one day longer in Nob, a village by Jerusalem; great part of the day left to prepare the siege. Rapid march; Zion near utter destruction. Shake the hand: he thinks he can storm it by the slightest will. None ventured to oppose. That it was preserved is extraordinary favor, a sheep from a lion’s jaws.

Reformed Nob: as far as the rod is allowed. Shared: this very day, Nob, shake his fist, daughter of Zion, hill of Jerusalem.

Isaiah 10:33

33Behold, the Lord God of hosts will lop the boughs with terrifying power; the great in height will be hewn down, and the lofty will be brought low.

Arminian

Behold, the Lord God of hosts will lop the boughs with terrifying power; the great in height will be hewn down, and the lofty will be brought low. Wesley: the top-bough, Sennacherib, with a most terrible stroke.

Arminian lop: the fist of v. 32 is answered. Historic hewing of that host. The lofty of 2:12–17, now this forest.

Reformed

Calvin: almost all apply this to the Assyrians; I think it threatens the Jews’ own calamity, to which chapter 11 is the consolation. Tall trees: all that is powerful. The next chapter’s comfort fits this grief. (Wesley hears Sennacherib; Calvin hears Judah’s own height coming down. Either way the Lord of hosts hews, and 11:1’s stump is next.)

Reformed lop: He wields the axe of v. 15. Shared: Lord God of hosts, lop the boughs, terrifying power, great in height, lofty brought low.

Isaiah 10:34

34He will clear the thickets of the forest with an axe, and Lebanon will fall by the Majestic One.

Arminian

He will clear the thickets of the forest with an axe, and Lebanon will fall by the Majestic One. Wesley: as trees are cut with iron; his Lebanon—the Assyrian army, the forest of v. 18.

Arminian Lebanon: historic host on the ridge, falling by the Holy One who was a flame (10:17). The remnant that returns (v. 21) is not this forest. Not a millennial camp. Next unsplit: a shoot from Jesse’s stump.

Reformed

Calvin: if this were the Assyrian, Lebanon would not fit; he reads it as Judah stripped of ornaments, to which “a shoot from the stump of Jesse” is the remedy. Thick places cleared. The Majestic One does it.

Reformed Lebanon: the forest falls; the stump is 11:1. Shared: thickets, forest, axe, Lebanon, Majestic One.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a remnant that returns to the mighty God, a decreed consumption, and boughs lopped at Nob—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 10:20–34.

Arminian Notable voices

“And such—Jews preserved from that sweeping Assyrian scourge. Stay—shall no more trust to the Assyrians for help. A remnant—or, a remnant only. The consumption—the destruction of Israel was already decreed by the fixed counsel of God, and therefore must needs be executed, and like a deluge overflow them. Righteousness—with justice, and yet with clemency, inasmuch as he has spared a considerable remnant, when he might have destroyed them utterly. Be not afraid—the Assyrian shall be destroyed; he shall afflict, but not destroy thee. The anointing—possibly David, for whose sake God gave many deliverances; but the Messiah is principally intended, of whom David was a type. He is the foundation of all the promises, and of all the deliverances granted to God’s people in all ages. The bough—the top-bough, Sennacherib. Lebanon—the Assyrian army, before compared to a forest.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 10:20–34)

Reformed Notable voices

“In that day: the result of the chastisement. You would wish the Assyrians far off; this blow is as necessary as medicine. Until the Lord afflicted them, all wished to be accounted children of Abraham; it was mere pretense. Their hope will be true and sincere when they have been cleansed from impostures. We cannot place our confidence in God unless we altogether withhold our heart from creatures. A remnant shall return: an allusion to Shear-jashub. To the mighty God: they betook themselves to Assyria because they did not think God sufficient. Though thy people be as the sand of the sea: he casts down hypocrites from foolish confidence in descent from Abraham. A consumption decreed: they were not far from utter extermination. Overflowing with righteousness: this very small company will overflow righteousness; this remnant, though small, will be sufficient to cause rivers of righteousness to flow. Paul quotes this (Rom 9:27–28) as fulfilled when the Jews were cut off and a small remnant preserved. Because of the anointing: he reminds them of Christ; through his kindness they will be delivered. This benefit depends on the power of Christ, not on the ability of man. Lop the bough: I think this threatens the Jews’ own calamity, to which the consolation of the next chapter is the remedy.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 10:20–33)
