Passage

Isaiah 10

Verses 1–4 · ESV · Voices

Woe to those who decree iniquitous decrees, and writers who keep writing oppression: they turn the needy from justice, rob the poor of their right, make widows spoil and the fatherless prey. What will you do on the day of punishment, the ruin from afar? To whom will you flee, and where leave your wealth? Nothing remains but to crouch among prisoners or fall among the slain. His anger has not turned away; his hand is stretched out still.

Isaiah 10:1

1Woe to those who decree iniquitous decrees, and the writers who keep writing oppression,

Arminian

Woe to those who decree iniquitous decrees, and the writers who keep writing oppression. Wesley: magistrates who make unjust laws and give unjust sentences; grievous things that vex their subjects.

Arminian woe: historic Israel’s bench, not a newspaper about modern statutes. They still write it. The poor of v. 2 are this people’s poor.

Reformed

Calvin: he comes to particulars so hypocrisy cannot hide. Not two classes of scribes, but princes and magistrates whose decrees approach robbery. Those on the highest rank imagine they are exempt and not bound to give account; they will be first punished.

Reformed woe: the governors of 9:15–16 under a first woe. Shared: decree, iniquitous, writers, oppression. Historic Ephraim, not a culture-war.

Isaiah 10:2

2to turn aside the needy from justice and to rob the poor of my people of their right, that widows may be their spoil, and that they may make the fatherless their prey!

Arminian

To turn aside the needy from justice and to rob the poor of my people of their right, that widows may be their spoil, and that they may make the fatherless their prey. Wesley: judgment—from obtaining a just sentence.

Arminian turn aside: a real theft in a real court. Widows and fatherless are still His concern (9:17). Historic Israel, not a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: keep the poor from their cause. Magistrates ought to have assisted them more, yet indulge more contemptuously in oppressing them. The Lord takes peculiar care of the poor (Ex 22:23; Deut 24:15) and will not let this pass unpunished. He is their protector (Ps 68:5). The powerful are warned: no avenger now seen, still He will avenge.

Reformed turn aside: His poor, His day. Shared: needy, justice, poor of my people, widows, fatherless.

Isaiah 10:3

3What will you do on the day of punishment, in the ruin that will come from afar? To whom will you flee for help, and where will you leave your wealth?

Arminian

What will you do on the day of punishment, in the ruin that will come from afar? To whom will you flee for help, and where will you leave your wealth? Wesley: from far—the Assyrians; Israelites were secure, in amity with them after weakening Judah. Glory—your wealth, to be kept safe for your use.

Arminian what: a real question to a secure court. Historic Assyria from afar, not a newspaper empire. They still have nowhere to park the glory they wrote for themselves.

Reformed

Calvin: princes careless, intoxicated by prosperity. God has fixed a time. From afar: they had beaten neighbors and allied with a powerful nation. Visitation is judgment—He visits in mercy and in judgment. In vain they rely on resources. Judges appointed to defend the poor: they will have judgment without mercy who showed no mercy (Jas 2:13). Where deposit your glory: where find a hiding-place to preserve it? If princes fall, what of the lowest? No one has reason to flatter himself.

Reformed what: the day is fixed. Shared: day of punishment, ruin from afar, flee for help, wealth.

Isaiah 10:4

4Nothing remains but to crouch among the prisoners or fall among the slain. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still.

Arminian

Nothing remains but to crouch among the prisoners or fall among the slain. For all this his anger has not turned away, and his hand is stretched out still. Wesley: without me—without my favour and help, which you have forfeited. Shall bow down—notwithstanding all your succours.

Arminian still: the refrain of 9:12, 17, 21. They forfeited help they might have had. Historic prisoners of Assyria, not a millennial camp. Match the hub: a King whose favour can be forfeited, leaving this crouching as the end of the decrees they would not unsay.

Reformed

Calvin: without me they shall fall—they have revolted from God. The cause of all distresses is to forsake the fountain of life. Ironical: they vaunt of being forsaken as if nothing were more pleasant than distance from Him. Or: no hiding-place but among the bound and the slain. Wrath not yet apparent; more frightful punishments reserved. Nothing more desirable than sincere repentance, that we may obtain pardon.

Reformed still: they left the fountain; the hand is not done. Shared: prisoners, slain, anger not turned away, hand stretched out still.

Key contrast Both confess the woe on those who write oppression, and that after the ruin from afar His hand is stretched out still (10:1, 4). Historic Israel’s statutes and Assyria from far, not a newspaper, and not open theism: the day is fixed. Arminians typically hear help forfeited—without His favour they bow, though they might have had it (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear revolt from the fountain of life: calamity is His absence, and more punishments remain till they are moved to sincere repentance (Calvin).

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on unjust statutes, a day of visitation from afar, and a hand still stretched out—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 10:1–4.

Arminian Notable voices

“Woe—unto those magistrates who make unjust laws, and give unjust sentences. Grievousness—unjust decrees as cause grief and vexation to their subjects. Judgment—from obtaining a just sentence. From far—from the Assyrians. The Israelites, having weakened the Jews and being in amity with the Assyrians, were secure. Glory—your wealth. Without me—without my favour and help, which you have forfeited. Shall bow down—notwithstanding all your succours.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 10:1–4)

Reformed Notable voices

“Woe to them that decree: he attacks princes and magistrates who oppressed by unjust and tyrannical decrees approaching robbery. Those elevated imagine they are exempt and not bound to give account; they will be the first that are punished. To keep back the poor from judgment: magistrates ought to have assisted them more than others. The Lord takes peculiar care of the poor and will not allow oppression inflicted on them to pass unpunished. What will you do in the day of visitation? God has fixed a time for judgment. Visitation is judgment: He visits in mercy and in judgment. From afar: He can shake heaven and earth from east to west. They will have judgment without mercy who have showed no mercy. Without me they shall fall: the cause of their destruction is that they have revolted from God. The cause of all our distresses is to forsake the fountain of life. For all this his anger is not turned away: a dreadful destruction; He will find out more frightful punishments. Nothing is more truly desirable than to be moved by a sincere feeling of repentance, that we may obtain pardon.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 10:1–4)
