Passage

Isaiah 10

Verses 5–19 · ESV · Voices

Ah, Assyria, the rod of the Lord’s anger; the staff in their hands is His fury. He sends him against a godless nation, to spoil and tread them like mire. But Assyria does not so intend: his heart is to destroy nations. He boasts his commanders are kings; Calno, Hamath, Samaria like the rest; he will do to Jerusalem’s idols as to Samaria’s. When the Lord has finished His work on Zion, He punishes the arrogant heart. The axe must not boast over the hewer. Therefore wasting and fire: the light of Israel becomes a flame; the forest’s remnant so few a child can write them.

Isaiah 10:5

5Ah, Assyria, the rod of my anger; the staff in their hands is my fury!

Arminian

Ah, Assyria, the rod of my anger; the staff in their hands is my fury. Wesley: God’s invitation to take the charge; the instrument wherewith I shall chastise my people; mine anger puts the weapons into their hand.

Arminian rod: historic Assyria, not a newspaper empire. He is still a rod, not the hand. The fury is the Lord’s.

Reformed

Calvin: God calls the Assyrian as a judge calls an officer. The staff they carry is His anger: everything in their hand is wrath. All their strength proceeds from His wrath. Blind attacks of enemies are regulated by a heavenly providence. Do not suppose anything happens by chance.

Reformed rod: their swords are His anger. Shared: Assyria, rod of my anger, staff, fury. Historic Sennacherib, not a millennial camp.

Isaiah 10:6

6Against a godless nation I send him, and against the people of my wrath I command him, to take spoil and seize plunder, and to tread them down like the mire of the streets.

Arminian

Against a godless nation I send him, and against the people of my wrath I command him, to take spoil and seize plunder, and to tread them down like the mire of the streets. Wesley: send him—by my providence, giving him both occasion and inclination to this expedition.

Arminian send: a real sending against historic hypocritical Israel (9:17). Occasion and inclination are still given; the treading is still theirs to do. Not open theism: He sends.

Reformed

Calvin: the rod is not uncertain; appointed for the unthankful. Hypocritical nation: no uprightness. People of His indignation: nothing in them but grounds of anger. I will command him: two ways of commanding—secret decree, and the law that demands voluntary obedience. Fanatics who excuse their wickedness by the decree are guilty of falsehood. The Assyrian has the law; he serves his own lusts. Accidental, as to men, that God acts by the reprobate.

Reformed send: His charge, their plunder. Shared: godless nation, people of my wrath, spoil, mire of the streets.

Isaiah 10:7

7But he does not so intend, and his heart does not so think; but it is in his heart to destroy, and to cut off nations not a few;

Arminian

But he does not so intend, and his heart does not so think; but it is in his heart to destroy, and to cut off nations not a few. Wesley: he doth not design the execution of my will, but only to enlarge his own empire. Seasonably added, to justify God in the judgments threatened to the Assyrian. To cut off—to sacrifice multitudes to his own ambition and covetousness.

Arminian intend: a real other intent. God’s will and Assyria’s will are not the same act. He is still guilty of the empire he meant. Match the hub: a King who uses a rod, leaving this intending as the rod’s own.

Reformed

Calvin: he will not think so. Wicked men vomit rage as if God could not restrain them. Step forth: they are justly chastised by a secret judgment. Nothing farther from the Assyrian than to serve God; yet God works with amazing skill, bringing men to yield obedience without their knowledge or will. Their attempts are totally different; this will not prevent God from executing what He has decreed. The Assyrians will have their turn. God restrains their presumption from carrying into effect whatever they think fit. Lift your eyes to God, whose decree is far beyond that blind fury.

Reformed intend: He still does His work by a heart that never meant it. Shared: does not so intend, heart does not so think, destroy, cut off nations.

Key contrast Both confess Assyria is the rod of the Lord’s anger, sent, yet he does not so intend: his heart is empire, not the Lord’s will (10:5–7). Historic Assyria, not a newspaper, and not open theism: the decree stands. Arminians typically hear providence giving occasion and inclination, while Assyria’s own design of empire remains his—and that other intent justifies the later blow (Wesley). Reformed readers typically hear God impelling the wicked to an object unknown to them: they serve their lusts; He regulates particular acts without dragging the will; they cannot plead the decree against the law (Calvin).

Isaiah 10:8

8for he says: “Are not my commanders all kings?

Arminian

Are not my commanders all kings? Wesley: equal for power, wealth, and glory to the kings of other nations.

Arminian says: historic boast. The rod talks like a throne.

Reformed

Calvin: the reason he will not think he is the rod: blinded by pride, he acknowledges no authority superior to his own. Whoever imagines he can do anything, however small, assumes the power of God. Princes so intoxicated they scarcely remember they are men.

Reformed says: the boast is why he cannot see the hand. Shared: commanders, kings.

Isaiah 10:9

9Is not Calno like Carchemish? Is not Hamath like Arpad? Is not Samaria like Damascus?

Arminian

Is not Calno like Carchemish? Hamath like Arpad? Samaria like Damascus? Wesley: have I not conquered one as another, stronger as weaker? Shall not Samaria be as Damascus?

Arminian like: a real list of real cities. Historic map, not a newspaper. Samaria already in his mouth as done.

Reformed

Calvin: shall it not happen to the unconquered as to the subdued? Wicked men talk of former actions as if they will break through every obstruction without the will of God. He who exalted us will be able to cast us down.

Reformed like: the list is his proof; it is not. Shared: Calno, Carchemish, Hamath, Arpad, Samaria, Damascus.

Isaiah 10:10

10As my hand has reached to the kingdoms of the idols, whose carved images were greater than those of Jerusalem and Samaria,

Arminian

As my hand has reached to the kingdoms of the idols, whose carved images were greater than those of Jerusalem and Samaria. Wesley: kingdoms that worshipped their own idols and thought they could protect them. He calls even Jerusalem’s gods idols by contempt, because none delivered their people, and he judged them inferior to the sun, the Assyrian god.

Arminian idols: historic taunt. He ranks Zion with the rest. That ranking is the blasphemy of v. 12.

Reformed

Calvin: he breaks into more outrageous language: not only men but God and the gods he worshipped. First he exalts himself above God; second he makes no distinction between God and false gods. If he despised Apollo, he despised him as having something divine. He placed the living God on a level with heathen gods.

Reformed idols: the living God is not in that list. Shared: hand, kingdoms of the idols, Jerusalem, Samaria.

Isaiah 10:11

11shall I not do to Jerusalem and her idols as I have done to Samaria and her images?”

Arminian

Shall I not do to Jerusalem and her idols as I have done to Samaria and her images? The next city on the list is Zion.

Arminian shall I: historic threat against Jerusalem. Samaria’s fall does not prove Zion’s God is an idol. The child of 9:6 still sits.

Reformed

Calvin: he ridicules Israel’s confidence as if no greater power belonged to God than to idols. The second blasphemy: the living God leveled with the false.

Reformed shall I: he will try; v. 12 answers. Shared: Jerusalem, her idols, Samaria, her images.

Isaiah 10:12

12When the Lord has finished all his work on Mount Zion and on Jerusalem, he will punish the speech of the arrogant heart of the king of Assyria and the boastful look in his eyes.

Arminian

When the Lord has finished all his work on Mount Zion and on Jerusalem, he will punish the speech of the arrogant heart of the king of Assyria and the boastful look in his eyes. Wesley: because of this impudent blasphemy. His work—chastising his people so long as he sees fit. Looks—insolent words and carriage.

Arminian when: Zion first, then the rod. Historic order, not a millennial timetable. The work on Zion is still His, and the punishment of the boast is still His.

Reformed

Calvin: two clauses—God will punish the king; the time is not yet, that believers may wait. When He shall have chastised the sins of His Church, as a master settling his family, then the rod. Do not despond when the tyrant is exalted. Fruit of the stout heart: pride’s seat. God cannot endure the arrogance of proud men. Whole work: our haste would drag Him off a work only begun. Judgment begins at the house of God, then extends to those without.

Reformed when: Church first, then the tool. Shared: finished his work, Zion, Jerusalem, arrogant heart, boastful look.

Isaiah 10:13

13For he says: “By the strength of my hand I have done it, and by my wisdom, for I have understanding; I remove the boundaries of peoples, and plunder their treasures; like a bull I bring down those who sit on thrones.

Arminian

By the strength of my hand I have done it, and by my wisdom, for I have understanding; I remove the boundaries of peoples, and plunder their treasures; like a bull I bring down those who sit on thrones. Wesley: invaded their lands and added them; put down—deprived of former glory.

Arminian by my hand: the axe claiming the hewer’s work. Historic expansion, not a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: he ascribes all victories to his power and wisdom. Strength of hand: vast armies. Wisdom: tricks and dishonest cunning by which he gained on his neighbors. Removed boundaries: extended dominion. He claims what belongs to God.

Reformed by my hand: the speech v. 12 will punish. Shared: strength of my hand, wisdom, boundaries, treasures, thrones.

Isaiah 10:14

14My hand has found like a nest the wealth of the peoples; and as one gathers eggs that have been left, so I have gathered all the earth; and there was none that moved a wing or opened its mouth or chirped.”

Arminian

My hand has found like a nest the wealth of the peoples; as one gathers eggs that have been left, so I have gathered all the earth; and there was none that moved a wing or opened its mouth or chirped. Wesley: eggs the dam left; all the riches of the earth—an hyperbole usual in such mouths; peeped—as birds when the nest is robbed.

Arminian nest: historic plunder-talk. No chirp is his lie; Zion will chirp by the Holy One (v. 17).

Reformed

Calvin: it cost him no trouble. A deserted nest, eggs taken without the parent birds. No one ventured to open the mouth. He attributes this to his wisdom and makes no acknowledgment of providence. These boasts are bellows to kindle judgment.

Reformed nest: the earth was not ownerless. Shared: nest, eggs, all the earth, none moved a wing, chirped.

Isaiah 10:15

15Shall the axe boast over him who hews with it, or the saw magnify itself against him who wields it? As if a rod should wield him who lifts it, or as if a staff should lift him who is not wood!

Arminian

Shall the axe boast over him who hews with it, or the saw magnify itself against him who wields it? As if a rod should wield him who lifts it, or as if a staff should lift him who is not wood. Wesley: how absurd for thee, an instrument, to blaspheme thy Lord, who has as great power over thee as a man over the ax wherewith he heweth.

Arminian axe: the rod of v. 5, named. He is still wood. The boast is still sin. Not a fake warning: this question has an answer in v. 16.

Reformed

Calvin: as if an axe despised the hand that moves it. Men rise against God whenever they ascribe to themselves more than is proper. God presides over individual acts, moving men like rods—not a mere general upholding of nature. The question of free-will is unseasonably introduced: if God turns their thoughts, they do not therefore cease to plan. Not violent compulsion; in a wonderful manner He regulates all movements, so they still have the exercise of their will. All efforts are fruitless if God do not grant success.

Reformed axe: particular providence, not a dead staff that still chooses. Shared: axe, hews, saw, rod, staff, not wood.

Isaiah 10:16

16Therefore the Lord God of hosts will send wasting sickness among his stout warriors, and under his glory a burning will be kindled, like the burning of fire.

Arminian

Therefore the Lord God of hosts will send wasting sickness among his stout warriors, and under his glory a burning will be kindled, like the burning of fire. Wesley: the sovereign Lord of thine and all armies shall strip him and his princes of wealth, might, glory, and destroy his numerous army as fire does combustibles.

Arminian therefore: because of the boast. Historic wasting of that host, not a newspaper. The Lord of hosts outranks this host.

Reformed

Calvin: therefore—because he trusted in wealth and forces. Fatness made lean. Under his glory a burning: the greater the splendor, the more fuel. Nothing left, as a tree cut from the root.

Reformed therefore: the hewer kindles the axe. Shared: Lord God of hosts, wasting, stout warriors, glory, burning, fire.

Isaiah 10:17

17The light of Israel will become a fire, and his Holy One a flame, and it will burn and devour his thorns and briers in one day.

Arminian

The light of Israel will become a fire, and his Holy One a flame, and it will burn and devour his thorns and briers in one day. Wesley: that God who is a comfortable light to his people; a fire to the Assyrians; thorns—his vast army, no more able to resist than dry thorns to fire.

Arminian light: shared—the Holy One of 6:3 is this flame. Historic one day (the night of the angel, 2 Kgs 19), not a millennial camp. Light to Israel, fire to the rod.

Reformed

Calvin: in fire, light and heat. He consumes enemies by heat and enlightens the godly by light. God is a devouring fire and, in another view, light: contrary effects on godly and ungodly. Holy One: He protects the people He has chosen. In one day: sudden, when they think danger far off. Defenses like tow.

Reformed light: the same Holy One, two effects. Shared: light of Israel, fire, Holy One, flame, thorns, briers, one day.

Isaiah 10:18

18The glory of his forest and of his fruitful land the Lord will destroy, both soul and body, and it will be as when a sick man wastes away.

Arminian

The glory of his forest and of his fruitful land the Lord will destroy, both soul and body, and it will be as when a sick man wastes away. Wesley: his great army like a forest for numbers; soldiers thick as ears of corn; soul and body—totally, inwardly and outwardly; like an army when the standard-bearer is slain, panic in the whole.

Arminian forest: historic host thinned. Soul and body: the man and the ranks. Not a chart.

Reformed

Calvin: highest and lowest consumed. From the soul even to the flesh: both unhealthy, most dangerous. Vengeance falls on them also. The flame only scorches the godly; it consumes the ungodly, in whom it finds only fuel. Fainting of a standard-bearer: ensigns taken, great slaughter.

Reformed forest: nothing sound left. Shared: glory, forest, fruitful land, soul and body, wastes away.

Isaiah 10:19

19The remnant of the trees of his forest will be so few that a child can write them down.

Arminian

The remnant of the trees of his forest will be so few that a child can write them down. Wesley: the remainder of that mighty host.

Arminian remnant: Assyria’s leftover, not Israel’s (that is 10:20). A child can number what was a forest. Historic few, after the flame of v. 17.

Reformed

Calvin: a Hebrew idiom for few. Once a vast forest; after the trees are hewn, those left are scattered, countable by a child who struggles past three or four.

Reformed remnant: the rod, numbered. Shared: remnant, trees of his forest, child can write them down.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a rod that does not mean so, an axe that must not boast, and a Holy One who becomes a flame—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 10:5–19.

Arminian Notable voices

“O Assyrian—God’s invitation to take the charge and set upon the work. The rod—the instrument of mine anger, wherewith I shall chastise my people. Send him—by my providence, giving him both occasion and inclination to this expedition. Howbeit—he doth not design the execution of my will, but only to enlarge his own empire. Which is seasonably added, to justify God in his judgments threatened to the Assyrian. The ax—how absurd is it, for thee, who art but an instrument in God’s hand, to blaspheme thy Lord and master, who has as great power over thee as a man hath over the ax wherewith he heweth. The light—that God who is and will be a comfortable light to his people. A fire—to the Assyrians. Thorns—his vast army, no more able to resist God than dry thorns are to oppose the fire. Wherefore—because of this impudent blasphemy. His work—of chastising his people so long as he sees fit.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 10:5–17)

Reformed Notable voices

“O Assyrian: God calls him as an officer to put the malefactor in chains. The staff in their hand is mine indignation: everything in their hand is His wrath. All the strength the enemy possesses proceeds from the wrath of God. Three ways God acts by men: all move and exist by Him; He impels and directs the wicked according as He thinks fit, though nothing is farther from their thoughts; and by His Spirit of sanctification, peculiar to the elect. Two ways of commanding: His secret decree, and His law. Fanatics who excuse wickedness by the decree are guilty of falsehood. The Assyrians had no such intention; they were hurried by lust and ambition. Accidental, so far as relates to men, that He acts by the wicked. He does not so intend: nothing farther from the Assyrian than to serve God; yet God brings men to yield obedience even without their knowledge or will. Shall the axe boast? Men rise up against God whenever they ascribe to themselves more than is proper. He presides over individual acts, and moves men like rods. Not a violent compulsion, as if God dragged them against their will; in a wonderful manner He regulates all the movements of men, so that they still have the exercise of their will. When the Lord hath finished his whole work on Zion: first He chastises the Church; then He visits the pride of the rod. Judgment begins at the house of God.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 10:5–15)
