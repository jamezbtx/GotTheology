Passage

Isaiah 60

Verses 1–9 · ESV · Voices

After the Redeemer of 59:20, Zion is told to arise: the Lord’s glory has risen, while darkness covers the peoples. Nations and kings come to her light; sons and daughters return; sea-wealth, Midian’s camels, Sheba’s gold and frankincense, Kedar’s flocks, and Tarshish ships bring children and praise to the Holy One who has made her beautiful.

Isaiah 60:1

1Arise, shine, for your light has come, and the glory of the Lord has risen upon you.

Arminian

Wesley: arise out of a low and comfortless condition; shine with a full and heavenly light. Thy light is the light of the gospel; the glory is the glorious God.

Arminian summons: Zion can still get up. The light has come—sincerely held out—so shine is a real command under grace. Historic Jerusalem first, then the church that received the gospel’s dawn. No millennial chart is required to obey arise.

Reformed

Calvin: he raises a prostrate Church by command, as if putting her in possession of the happier condition he promised. Words are not scattered in the air. Arise stands against the former lie down. Brightness denotes salvation; it will arise from God’s smiling countenance.

Reformed effect: he speaks with effect. Everything goes well when the Lord shines; when he turns away, nothing more wretched can befall us. The glory is his, risen upon the Church he lifts.

Isaiah 60:2

2For behold, darkness shall cover the earth, and thick darkness the peoples; but the Lord will arise upon you, and his glory will be seen upon you.

Arminian

Wesley: darkness is a thick cloud of ignorance and idolatry; upon thee, O Jerusalem, the church of God.

Arminian contrast: the earth’s dark is real, and so is the rising on Zion. Gentiles still sit in that cloud until they come to the light (v. 3). The glory seen upon the church is gospel-wide, not a later earthly timetable. The invitation to walk out of thick darkness remains sincere.

Reformed

Calvin: by comparison he shows how extraordinary the privilege of the elect is. While the world sinks, God takes care of his people. This brightness is not indiscriminately enjoyed. Do not judge the Church’s condition from outward appearance; the discourse relates to spiritual light.

Reformed peculiarity: never was there a period when all Gentiles were in affliction while Jews enjoyed mere political peace. The happiness promised is different from meat and drink. These things relate to Christ’s spiritual kingdom, when gospel light shone and foreign nations were enlightened. We are in darkness till God shines with the testimony of adoption. The Church alone partakes of this brightness.

Isaiah 60:3

3And nations shall come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your rising.

Arminian

Wesley: Gentiles—all nations. Kings as well as common people walk to the brightness.

Arminian mission: the light is Zion’s because the gospel went out from Jerusalem (Isa 2:3). Nations come—they are not pushed as scenery. Coming is a real approach under grace, the same house of prayer for all peoples (56:7). Do not invent a millennial fight; Wesley and Calvin both read gospel nations here.

Reformed

Calvin: no other light of men but when the Lord shines by his word. This grace is supernatural, distinguished from nature by the repeated upon thee. It comes from God alone; all are not indiscriminately partakers, only the elect. This is done by Christ, the Sun of Righteousness (Mal 4:2).

Reformed from Zion: the daybreak was first in Judea; the lamp was kindled at Jerusalem. There is no light but from the doctrine of the prophets. Kings are named so we may not imagine only the common people come. The Church shines by borrowed light, as the moon from the sun.

Key contrast Both see gospel nations walking to Zion’s light in this age. Arminians typically stress a sincere, worldwide coming of all who will; Reformed readers typically stress that this light is supernatural, peculiar to the elect, and borrowed from Christ. Neither needs a millennial chart to honor the verse.

Isaiah 60:4

4Lift up your eyes all around, and see; they all gather together, they come to you; your sons shall come from afar, and your daughters shall be carried on the hip.

Arminian

Wesley: from far—from the most distant parts of the earth. Sons and daughters are the returning family, not a census of one ethnicity only.

Arminian sight: lift up and see is a command to believe what is coming. Gathering is the Lord’s, and coming is theirs. Historic restoration after exile is the first frame; the wider church is the same gathering of 56:8—yet others besides those already gathered.

Reformed

Calvin: a variety of expressions confirms an incredible restoration. The Jews were to look above human thought, round about, nations from every quarter united in one body. Not only an end of dispersion, but a wonderful revolution: strangers and dispersed united. The Church extends to the farthest boundaries.

Reformed family: both sons and daughters run to the Church—at home and abroad. The womb is not limited to a corner of the world. Do not fix eyes on the outward condition or you cannot obtain the fruit of these promises.

Isaiah 60:5

5Then you shall see and be radiant; your heart shall thrill and exult, because the abundance of the sea shall be turned to you, the wealth of the nations shall come to you.

Arminian

Wesley: see with delight; overflow with vast increase; fear—or worship; enlarged with inward joy. Sea-abundance and nations’ wealth are tribute to the Lord who beautifies Zion, not a license for greed.

Arminian joy: the heart’s thrill is worship. Nations bringing wealth is the consecration of their powers (v. 6), the same spiritual sacrifices of converted peoples. Historic Zion first; then every church that sees the nations come.

Reformed

Calvin: he spoke as present to the eyes of faith; now as future so believers may exercise patience. Thou shalt shine or overflow—joy that fills the Church, or the ornament with which she dazzles. Trembling is astonishment at unexpected honor, agreeing with joy.

Reformed wealth: when he says the Church shall be enriched, this must not be understood of persons’ luxury. Because of the unity of Head and members, what belongs to God and Christ is transferred to the Church. Foolishly do some devour the earth’s riches under this prophecy; papists torture it for magnificence. All will consecrate whatever they have.

Isaiah 60:6

6A multitude of camels shall cover you, the young camels of Midian and Ephah; all those from Sheba shall come. They shall bring gold and frankincense, and shall bring good news, the praises of the Lord.

Arminian

Wesley: they bring gold and frankincense—Matthew 2:11 is a first drop of this rain, not the whole cistern. Praises of the Lord are the real cargo. Midian, Ephah, Sheba are named nations walking to the light.

Arminian gospel: Magi at the manger show the nations already on the road. Good news and praises are what Sheba was always for. Do not turn camels into a millennial trade route; the point is worship arriving from afar.

Reformed

Calvin describes figuratively, accommodating to time and customary ceremonies, that under figures he might describe spiritual worship. He mentions camels, frankincense, gold, sheep according to what each country produces: all will consecrate to God whatever they have. We cannot be converted without offering all our faculties as spiritual sacrifices (1 Pet 2:5; Rom 12:1).

Reformed Magi: papists heap every gold-and-incense text onto the magi’s gifts and onto Roman luxury. The Prophet means that everywhere men shall call upon God, and foreigners assemble to worship him. Wicked men abuse gifts; good men dedicate them.

Isaiah 60:7

7All the flocks of Kedar shall be gathered to you; the rams of Nebaioth shall minister to you; they shall come up with acceptance on my altar, and I will beautify my beautiful house.

Arminian

Wesley: Kedar—Arabians, a heathenish people. Minister—as they did Solomon. Come up as a sacrifice acceptable. House—the temple, or my church.

Arminian altar: acceptance is the Lord’s word, the same as 56:7. Former outsiders minister. Beautifying the house is God’s work; the flocks are their offering. Historic names (Kedar, Nebaioth) keep the text from becoming a slogan.

Reformed

Calvin: Kedar and Nebaioth point east, Arabia and neighbors. Sacrifices shall ascend to the good pleasure of the altar—acceptable, distinguished from profane Gentile offerings without faith. Three things: they lift the heart on high; they are acceptable; they are on the altar which alone sanctifies (Matt 23:19).

Reformed Christ: Christ is the altar; on him we offer. Glorifying the house is true restoration; God reigns among us. This was not completed immediately after the return (Haggai, Malachi). Dignity was manifested when God opened heaven to Jerusalem and called the Gentiles to salvation.

Isaiah 60:8

8Who are these that fly like a cloud, and like doves to their windows?

Arminian

Wesley: metaphors of great flocks of converts flying to the church with great speed and in great numbers.

Arminian wonder: the question is astonishment, not doubt. Cloud and doves are people coming, as 56:8 gathered yet others. Speed and number are the gospel’s ordinary miracle when the light has risen. Zion is told to look, not to delay them with a chart.

Reformed

Calvin cannot satisfy himself and breaks into admiration: Who are those? More forcible than a count. Some think of apostles making their way to the world’s end (Mark 16:15); he speaks of a universal assemblage. From every quarter men run readily. Doves: scattered they seem untamed, yet they have a house to which they fly.

Reformed windows: believers, enlightened by faith, perceive their assembly and fly from frightful dispersion. Necessary warning: if prophets gained little from Jews, what from Gentiles alienated from God? He is filled with amazement that we may admire it.

Isaiah 60:9

9For the coastlands shall hope for me, the ships of Tarshish first, to bring your children from afar, their silver and gold with them, for the name of the Lord your God, and for the Holy One of Israel, because he has made you beautiful.

Arminian

Wesley: Tarshish—the sea-faring men. Coastlands hope; ships bring children, silver, and gold for the Name, because he has made Zion beautiful.

Arminian close: hope for me is the nations’ waiting, the same far-off peace of 57:19. Beauty is God’s gift, not Zion’s makeup. Children from afar are the point; metal is cargo for the Name. Historic isles and ships, gospel gathering—no millennial navy required.

Reformed

Calvin: islands shall wait—observe my will, as servants comply. Ships of Tarshish, once proud, shall be subjected and bring sons from distant countries. Silver and gold are a sacred offering to God, not the Church’s prey. Nothing should be desired more than that the world bow to God’s authority.

Reformed beauty: God himself promises he will glorify thee, lest believers ascribe it to merits. Papists display luxury and claim the Spirit as author of that wickedness; Jews rise at gold and cannot raise understandings to heaven. Such stupidity needs little refutation. God intends to elevate his Church and adorn her with necessary ornaments—for his name.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Zion’s rising light and the nations who walk to it—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 60:1–9.

Arminian Notable voices

“Arise—Out of thy low and comfortless condition. Shine—With a full and heavenly light. Thy light—The light of the gospel. Glory—The glorious God. Darkness—Ignorance and idolatry. Gentiles—All nations. Who are these that fly as a cloud—These metaphors represent the great flocks of converts flying to the church with great speed and in great numbers.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 60:1–3, 8)

Reformed Notable voices

“He raises up a prostrate and afflicted Church… For thy brightness is come… this light will arise from no other quarter than from God’s smiling countenance. Darkness shall cover the earth… that benefit… is spiritual, and the brightness which he promises is spiritual; and consequently, these things relate to the spiritual kingdom of Christ, when the light of the Gospel shone in every part of the world. And the Gentiles shall walk to thy brightness… There is, therefore, no light but from the doctrine of the prophets.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 60:1–3)
