Passage

Isaiah 60

Verses 10–22 · ESV · Voices

Foreigners build Zion’s walls and kings minister, after wrath, in favor. Gates stay open for the nations’ wealth; Lebanon beautifies the sanctuary; afflicters bow and name her the City of the Lord. God himself is everlasting light. The people are his planting, and he will hasten it in its time.

Isaiah 60:10

10Foreigners shall build up your walls, and their kings shall minister to you; for in my wrath I struck you, but in my favor I have had mercy on you.

Arminian

Wesley: minister—administer all necessaries. As I afflicted thee in anger, so out of compassion I will abundantly bless thee. Strangers building walls is gospel gathering: Gentiles in the work, not a later political masonry.

Arminian mercy: wrath and favor are both real. Historic Zion was struck; the church is the same people under mercy. Kings can still serve rather than hinder. The turn from strike to pity is the Lord’s, and it invites trust.

Reformed

Calvin: as foreigners help build the temple, so sons of the stranger labor on the walls. Cyrus supplied gold; in Christ it is fulfilled when he raises pastors from strangers and would have foreign princes be nursing-fathers. Papists twist this into papal tyranny over kings; Christ’s kingdom is not of this world (John 18:36). Kings remain kings, using power to preserve worship.

Reformed cause: do not imagine chance. Wrath smote; undeserved kindness delivers. Calamities do not happen by chance, nor is he angry without leaving room for compassion (Hab 3:2).

Isaiah 60:11

11Your gates shall be open continually; day and night they shall not be shut, that people may bring to you the wealth of the nations, with their kings led in procession.

Arminian

Wesley: for that purpose—the conflux of people continually flocking thither. Open gates are welcome, not a millennial checkpoint. Revelation 21:25 borrows the same peace.

Arminian reading: day and night means the gathering does not clock out. Wealth of nations is consecration, as in 60:5–9. Kings led in procession are those who come to the light (v. 3), not a newspaper parade.

Reformed

Calvin: not merely safety, but gates open so riches may be brought from every quarter. Day will not suffice; carrying will be constant. Riches of the Gentiles belong to the Church as obedience rendered to God, not carnal luxury; what is offered to God belongs to the Church because he has nothing separate from her.

Reformed kings: he keeps the participle—kings naturally haughty shall be led, submitting to God and the Church. They do not cease to be kings.

Isaiah 60:12

12For the nation and kingdom that will not serve you shall perish; those nations shall be utterly laid waste.

Arminian

Wesley: serve—that will not submit to Christ’s scepter. The warning is moral and missionary: refusal of the King, not a map of later geopolitics.

Arminian gravity: nations can still serve. Perish is what unbelief earns. The scepter is Christ’s already (59:16–20). Gospel-wide light (v. 3) does not make this verse optional.

Reformed

Calvin: Gentiles will aid restoration; kingdoms that will not serve the Church shall be destroyed. If the careless do not pass unpunished, what of tyrants who labor to destroy her? Even the whole world, if involved in the same guilt, shall perish; multitude is no excuse.

Reformed serve: kings and nations serve the Church not because she lords over them, but because God committed to her the scepter of his word by which he rules.

Isaiah 60:13

13The glory of Lebanon shall come to you, the cypress, the plane, and the pine, to beautify the place of my sanctuary, and I will make the place of my feet glorious.

Arminian

Wesley: kings and great ones, and persons of a lower rank, shall be the materials and members of Christ’s church. Sanctuary—the temple. Place of my feet—the ark. All this is made good in the gospel-church.

Arminian house: beautifying is God’s end. Trees of Lebanon are people, not lumber millennialism. The footstool lifts worshipers toward the Holy One, as 57:15 already stooped to the contrite.

Reformed

Calvin: fir, pine, box from Lebanon—excellent materials for the building. Under figures, spiritual worship. He holds out the temple pattern that under it we may contemplate the spiritual temple of which we are living stones (Eph 2:21; 1 Pet 2:5).

Reformed feet: he dwells so that majesty is not confined; feet only, that we may ascend to heaven and not fix on outward signs. Worship the footstool (Ps 99:5)—not that God is divided, but that he lifts servants from feet to head.

Isaiah 60:14

14The sons of those who afflicted you shall come bending low to you, and all who despised you shall bow down at your feet; they shall call you the City of the Lord, the Zion of the Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

Wesley: sons—posterity or themselves. Bending—humbling as penitents. Call thee—they shall acknowledge her to be so.

Arminian honor: the name City of the Lord is confession, the honor of the Head shining in the church. Bowing is repentance toward Christ in Zion, not a millennial footstool of flesh. Afflicters can still bend.

Reformed

Calvin: persecutors shall submit with their whole heart. Partly fulfilled at the return; that return was a shadow of deliverance through Christ. Accomplished under Christ’s reign, with full accomplishment at his second coming. Honor is rendered to the Head, who is worshipped in the Church by those who formerly hated him.

Reformed name: they obey his doctrine. Jerusalem was almost obliterated; it shall be restored so all acknowledge the city of God. High rank is for the temple’s sake—the worship established there.

Isaiah 60:15

15Whereas you have been forsaken and hated, with no one passing through, I will make you majestic forever, a joy from age to age.

Arminian

Wesley: forsaken both of God and of her inhabitants; streets left desolate. Forever and age to age are the church’s comfort after exile’s empty streets.

Arminian hope: forsaken is not the last tense. Majestic forever is gift. Historic Zion’s hate-season does not cancel the joy promised to generations who come to the light.

Reformed

Calvin eyes the captivity at hand, lest they despair. Grievous calamities cannot prevent God from restoring them. If the splendor was not of long duration, those things were still fulfilled: under the cross the glory of Christ shines, the name of God remains, a people calls on him by faith.

Reformed ingratitude: we interrupt God’s works by malice. The Prophet embraces the whole course of redemption from the end of captivity through the gospel to the end of Christ’s reign.

Isaiah 60:16

16You shall suck the milk of nations; you shall nurse at the breast of kings; and you shall know that I, the Lord, am your Savior and your Redeemer, the Mighty One of Jacob.

Arminian

Wesley: the church shall receive the wealth of nations and the power of kings freely and affectionately, as milk from the mother. Knowledge of the Savior is the end.

Arminian knowing: Redeemer and Mighty One of Jacob (cf. 49:24–26; 59:20) is christology already shared. Milk is support for Zion’s children, not a license to drain the world. Kings still may nurse rather than strike.

Reformed

Calvin: from a slender remnant the seed spread through the world. Milk and breasts are service for supporting her offspring. Kings are named because harder to believe, and they are reminded of duty: servants of the Church (Ps 2:10–11) or the Lord will call them to account.

Reformed suck: she may not be an insatiable gulf. These things relate to spiritual condition—pure worship, the ministry, discipline. It is more blessed to give (Acts 20:35). Then they shall know by experience the Redeemer, the mighty one of Jacob—what he was to the fathers.

Isaiah 60:17

17Instead of bronze I will bring gold, and instead of iron I will bring silver; instead of wood, bronze, instead of stones, iron. I will make your overseers peace and your taskmasters righteousness.

Arminian

Wesley: an allusion to Solomon, when gold was as brass. Peace—loving, meek, peaceable. Righteousness—most righteous; even thy tax-gatherers shall be such.

Arminian officers: the upgrade is moral before it is mineral. Peace and righteousness in government answer the unjust gain of 57:17 and the true fast of 58. Historic restoration first; gospel holiness of rule in the church.

Reformed

Calvin compares the ancient temple with the spiritual. This was never completed in the external restoration; the later temple was inferior. Hence he discourses of the spiritual temple, the Church of Christ. Gifts of the Spirit are more excellent than gold.

Reformed magistrates: peace and righteousness contrast unjust Babylonian rule. More fully when through Christ we were delivered from the devil’s tyranny; the gospel set up a kingdom of righteousness not yet completed. Look to his last coming; be satisfied with firstfruits.

Isaiah 60:18

18Violence shall no more be heard in your land, devastation or destruction within your borders; you shall call your walls Salvation, and your gates Praise.

Arminian

Wesley: they shall be safe; thou shalt be as safe as salvation itself can make thee. Within and upon gates and walls, thou shalt sing praises. He adds that this will be fulfilled during the thousand years wherein Christ shall reign upon earth—his own note on the safety-language, not a camp to force on the whole Arminian tradition.

Shared ground: walls named Salvation and gates Praise are the Lord’s doing. Historic Zion’s violence ends in worship. Do not turn gates into a millennial fight between the two notes; both want a people safe enough to praise.

Reformed

Calvin: he contrasts former miseries. They shall not afterwards be subjected to such afflictions so as to have no remaining form of the Church. Not exemption from every annoyance; God spares his Church. Under the cross there is peace, kept by his hand.

Reformed names: safety with joy; she rejoices at being safe. Keep in remembrance that these things are experienced only in part; Christ’s kingdom has not yet been completed. Walls and gates here are not a newspaper, and not an Arminian-versus-Reformed millennial chart.

Isaiah 60:19

19The sun shall be no more your light by day, nor for brightness shall the moon give you light; but the Lord will be your everlasting light, and your God will be your glory.

Arminian

Wesley: these shall not be esteemed in comparison of the spiritual light of the church. God will be not only a shield but a sun. Christ shall scatter darkness and ignorance; this light shall not wax and wane, suffer eclipses and settings, but be constant. The glory—always matter of glorying in him.

Arminian comfort: everlasting light is Christ in the gospel church, already, not postponed to a later sun. Revelation 21 sings the same song. No millennial fight is required to prefer the Lord to the moon.

Reformed

Calvin: prosperity shall not be temporary. Distinguish the Church from the ordinary lot under the sun. Do not judge safety from present appearance; it is laid up in God. He will be thy sun. This does not deprive God’s children of ordinary benefits; it names a still greater blessing—heavenly light which ungodly men hate.

Reformed glory: even if elements cease or threaten, it is enough that God is reconciled. Sun and moon include the whole changing condition of man. The fatherly kindness of God shines on believers in thick darkness.

Key contrast Both read God himself as Zion’s light in the gospel, not a lesser lamp. Arminians typically stress Christ’s constant spiritual light already given to the church; Reformed readers typically stress that this light is the elect’s peculiar adoption, not to be judged from outward sun and moon. Neither needs a millennial chart to honor everlasting light.

Isaiah 60:20

20Your sun shall no more go down, nor your moon withdraw itself; for the Lord will be your everlasting light, and your days of mourning shall be ended.

Arminian

Wesley’s former verse still governs: no eclipse of this light. Mourning ended is the church’s comfort after 57:18’s mourners and 59:11’s groans.

Arminian hope: days of mourning have a term. The Lord as light is enough to end them. Historic exile’s grief, gospel consolation, and the last day agree without splitting the camps over a thousand years.

Reformed

Calvin continues: the children of God are distinguished from the ordinary lot. Mourning ended does not mean a church without a cross in history; it means God himself is the unchanging light when sun and moon fail as measures.

Reformed patience: part now, fullness later. The same Lord who rose on Zion (v. 1) will be everlasting light until mourning’s days are actually done.

Isaiah 60:21

21Your people shall all be righteous; they shall possess the land forever, the branch of my planting, the work of my hands, that I might be glorified.

Arminian

Wesley: the branch—thy people being of my planting, the work of my hands. All righteous is the gospel people, planted, not self-grown.

Arminian planting: forever in the land is the family of God kept in the promise. Work of my hands keeps boasting out. He is glorified when a people actually walks righteous—the true fast’s fruit (58) under the Redeemer (59:20).

Reformed

Calvin: the Church is established when purged of the ungodly. Hypocrites have always been mingled; this describes Christ’s whole reign in its perfection, not one moment. He began at his coming, goes on till harvest. Thy people excludes hypocrites who snatch promises.

Reformed land: not a code for heaven only. Canaan meant remaining in God’s family. The new branch is his work; we were planted before the world (Eph 1:4) and ingrafted. Wicked men are not his planting (Matt 15:13). End: that he may be glorified (Eph 1:12; 1 Pet 2:9).

Isaiah 60:22

22The least one shall become a clan, and the smallest one a mighty nation; I am the Lord; in its time I will hasten it.

Arminian

Wesley: in due time, the time that I have appointed. Small becoming a nation is the Gentile church’s story (Matt 13:31 sits nearby). Hasten is his verb.

Arminian close: the least may still become a clan. Appointed time is not delay as denial. The mouth that said arise (v. 1) will not be late. Trust the schedule; do the shining.

Reformed

Calvin: though few, the Church will be populous. After diminution, a vast body. What was said to the Jews is said to us: the Church cannot perish; it is God’s planting. I Jehovah—not like men whose efforts pass away. He speaks of a wonderful work, not ordinary government.

Reformed time: hasten it in her time—the Church’s season. Exhorts to patience; do not plunge headlong. He delays for our benefit. He appears idle; he hastens to the proper season he knows.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on open gates, the Lord as light, and a people all righteous—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Isaiah 60:10–22.

Arminian Notable voices

“Minister—Shall administer all necessaries to thee. Therefore—by reason of the conflux of people, that shall be continually flocking thither. Serve—That will not submit to Christ’s scepter. All this is made good in the gospel-church. The sun—These shall not be esteemed in comparison of the spiritual light of the church… Christ shall scatter all darkness and ignorance, and this light shall not wax, and wane… The branch—Thy people being of my planting, the work of my hands. In time—In due time, the time that I have appointed.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Isa 60:10–13, 19, 21–22)

Reformed Notable voices

“Christ employed a few apostles… afterwards he raised up strangers, from among whom he chose pastors, and wished that their foreign princes should be nursing-fathers of the Church. Christ’s kingdom is not of this world. Thou shalt no longer have the sun for the light of days… The Lord will be thy sun… that benefit… is spiritual. Thy people also are all righteous… when she is purged of the ungodly… Christ began to do this at his coming… but he goes on from day to day in purifying it, and will go on till the day of harvest. I Jehovah will hasten it in her time.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Isaiah (on Isa 60:10, 19–22)
