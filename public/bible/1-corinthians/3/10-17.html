Passage

1 Corinthians 3

Verses 10–17 · WEB

Paul describes himself as a wise master builder who laid the only foundation, Jesus Christ. Others build on it with materials that will be tested by fire on the Day; some work will survive and be rewarded, and some will be burned, though the builder himself will be saved as through fire. Paul then warns that God will destroy anyone who destroys God’s temple, the church. The passage raises questions about rewards, judgment, and warnings that both traditions answer with care.

1 Corinthians 3:10

10According to the grace of God which was given to me, as a wise master builder I laid a foundation, and another builds on it. But let each man be careful how he builds on it.

Arminian

By God’s grace Paul laid a foundation as a skilled master builder, and others build on it. Arminian readers see grace enabling Paul’s work and a warning that each builder must take care. Faithfulness in ministry is required.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul’s ability as entirely the gift of grace. Builders are accountable for how they build because the work belongs to God.

1 Corinthians 3:11

11For no one can lay any other foundation than that which has been laid, which is Jesus Christ.

Arminian

No one can lay any foundation other than Jesus Christ. Arminian readers see Christ as the only basis of salvation and church life, offered to all. Every ministry must build on him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as the exclusive foundation of the church, grounding the sufficiency of his saving work. Any other foundation is no gospel at all.

1 Corinthians 3:12

12But if anyone builds on the foundation with gold, silver, costly stones, wood, hay, or stubble;

Arminian

Builders may use gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, or straw. Arminian readers see the varying quality of ministers’ work, which is a matter of faithfulness and choice. Some build with lasting materials, others with flimsy ones.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the materials as the doctrine and fruit of ministry, whether sound or shallow. The verse calls ministers to build with God’s truth.

1 Corinthians 3:13

13each man’s work will be revealed. For the Day will declare it, because it is revealed in fire; and the fire itself will test what sort of work each man’s work is.

Arminian

Each one’s work will be revealed by fire on the Day. Arminian readers see final judgment testing the quality of believers’ service. Nothing hidden will escape God’s evaluation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Day of the Lord exposing the true character of ministry. Reformed and most Protestant readers reject using this verse for purgatory, since it concerns the testing of works, not purification of persons.

1 Corinthians 3:14

14If any man’s work remains which he built on it, he will receive a reward.

Arminian

If the work survives, the builder will receive a reward. Arminian readers see God’s real reward for faithful labour, which motivates diligent service. Rewards differ according to faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see rewards as God’s gracious recognition of the work he himself enabled. They encourage faithfulness without grounding salvation in works.

1 Corinthians 3:15

15If any man’s work is burned, he will suffer loss, but he himself will be saved, but as through fire.

Arminian

If the work is burned, he will suffer loss, though he himself will be saved, as through fire. Arminian readers note that this builder is a genuine believer who keeps building on Christ, so loss of reward is distinct from loss of salvation. Arminian readers nevertheless hold that apostasy, abandoning the foundation, is a different and real danger.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the security of the believer even when his works are poor. Salvation rests on the foundation, Christ, not on the quality of one’s building, though such a narrow escape is nothing to aim for.

Key contrast Both agree that this verse distinguishes loss of reward from loss of salvation for a builder on Christ. Arminian readers add that departing from the foundation itself would be another matter. Reformed readers see here the security of all who are truly on the foundation.

1 Corinthians 3:16

16Don’t you know that you are a temple of God, and that God’s Spirit lives in you?

Arminian

Do you not know you are God’s temple and God’s Spirit dwells in you? Arminian readers see the church as the dwelling of God, a reality that calls for holiness and unity. The you is plural, the congregation together.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the indwelling Spirit as the mark of God’s people. The church’s identity as temple is God’s gift and the ground for its holiness.

1 Corinthians 3:17

17If anyone destroys God’s temple, God will destroy him; for God’s temple is holy, which you are.

Arminian

If anyone destroys God’s temple, God will destroy him, for the temple is holy. Arminian readers take this as a serious warning, possibly addressed to people within the church who wreck it by division. Such warnings are real and must not be explained away.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a solemn warning that may describe false teachers or those whose destructive work shows they never truly belonged. Warnings remain one of the means by which God preserves his people.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
