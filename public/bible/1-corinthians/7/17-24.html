Passage

1 Corinthians 7

Verses 17–24 · WEB

Paul states a general principle: each person should live the life the Lord assigned and continue in the condition in which God called him. Circumcision or uncircumcision counts for nothing; keeping God’s commands is what matters. A slave called in the Lord is the Lord’s freedman, and a free person is Christ’s slave, though slaves should take freedom if they can. Believers were bought with a price and must not become slaves of men. Both traditions see here a theology of vocation shaped by God’s call. They speak of calling with slightly different emphases.

1 Corinthians 7:17

17Only, as the Lord has distributed to each man, as God has called each, so let him walk. So I command in all the assemblies.

Arminian

Each should live as the Lord assigned and as God called him. Arminian readers see God’s call coming into people’s existing situations. Faith transforms how one lives in a station, not necessarily the station itself.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s providence assigning each person’s circumstances, and his call meeting them there. This text is central to the Reformation doctrine of vocation.

1 Corinthians 7:18

18Was anyone called having been circumcised? Let him not become uncircumcised. Has anyone been called in uncircumcision? Let him not be circumcised.

Arminian

Was anyone circumcised when called? Let him not remove the marks. Arminian readers see ethnic markers made irrelevant for salvation. Jewish and Gentile believers stand on equal footing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the end of circumcision as a covenant requirement for salvation. Neither state brings standing with God.

1 Corinthians 7:19

19Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of God.

Arminian

Circumcision and uncircumcision are nothing; keeping God’s commands is what counts. Arminian readers see obedience as the mark of genuine faith. Externals do not save; faithful living matters.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the moral law’s continuing role in Christian life. Obedience flows from faith, as Galatians 5:6 says, faith working through love.

Key contrast Both affirm obedience to God’s commands as essential to the Christian life. Arminian readers stress obedience as the continuing response of faith. Reformed readers stress obedience as the fruit of grace, with the moral law as a guide.

1 Corinthians 7:20

20Let each man stay in that calling in which he was called.

Arminian

Each should remain in the condition in which he was called. Arminian readers see a principle for contentment, not a ban on change. Christians need not change status to serve God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see vocation as a place of service under God’s providence. Every honest calling can glorify God.

1 Corinthians 7:21

21Were you called being a bondservant? Don’t let that bother you, but if you get an opportunity to become free, use it.

Arminian

Were you a slave when called? Do not worry, but if you can gain freedom, take it. Arminian readers see Paul encouraging freedom where possible while dignifying the slave’s standing in Christ. The gospel relativizes social status.

Reformed

Reformed readers also see freedom encouraged, though the Greek can be read either way. Both readings place status beneath the call of Christ.

1 Corinthians 7:22

22For he who was called in the Lord being a bondservant is the Lord’s free man. Likewise he who was called being free is Christ’s bondservant.

Arminian

A slave called in the Lord is the Lord’s freedman; the free person is Christ’s slave. Arminian readers see a reversal that equalizes believers. Every Christian is both free and bound to Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see true freedom as belonging to Christ. Social status does not define one’s standing before God.

1 Corinthians 7:23

23You were bought with a price. Don’t become bondservants of men.

Arminian

You were bought with a price; do not become slaves of men. Arminian readers see redemption, offered in Christ’s death, as freeing believers from ultimate loyalty to anyone else. Christ alone is Lord.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s purchase securing believers for himself. Liberty of conscience grows from this belonging.

1 Corinthians 7:24

24Brothers, let each man, in whatever condition he was called, stay in that condition with God.

Arminian

Each should remain with God in the condition in which he was called. Arminian readers see God’s presence in every circumstance. One’s station is a place to walk with God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the phrase with God as key: callings are lived before God’s face. Ordinary life becomes holy service.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
