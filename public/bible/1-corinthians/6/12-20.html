Passage

1 Corinthians 6

Verses 12–20 · WEB

Paul answers Corinthian slogans such as all things are lawful for me and food is for the stomach. Freedom has limits: not everything is beneficial, and believers must not be mastered by anything. The body is for the Lord and will be raised, so union with a prostitute is unthinkable for those whose bodies are members of Christ. He calls them to flee sexual immorality because the body is a temple of the Holy Spirit, and they are not their own but were bought with a price. Both traditions find a rich theology of the body here.

1 Corinthians 6:12

12“All things are lawful for me,” but not all things are expedient. “All things are lawful for me,” but I will not be brought under the power of anything.

Arminian

All things are lawful for me, but not all things are helpful or should master me. Arminian readers see Christian freedom limited by love and wisdom. Freedom used wrongly becomes slavery.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christian liberty rightly understood. Freedom from the law’s condemnation is not freedom to sin.

1 Corinthians 6:13

13“Foods for the belly, and the belly for foods,” but God will bring to nothing both it and them. But the body is not for sexual immorality, but for the Lord; and the Lord for the body.

Arminian

Food is for the stomach, but the body is for the Lord and the Lord for the body. Arminian readers see Paul rejecting the view that bodily acts are morally indifferent. The body belongs to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the goodness and dignity of the body in God’s purpose. The body is not a disposable shell but redeemed for the Lord.

1 Corinthians 6:14

14Now God raised up the Lord, and will also raise us up by his power.

Arminian

God raised the Lord and will raise us by his power. Arminian readers see resurrection hope shaping present conduct. What we do with our bodies matters because they have a future.

Reformed

Reformed readers see bodily resurrection as the fruit of union with Christ’s resurrection. God’s power guarantees the future of the body.

1 Corinthians 6:15

15Don’t you know that your bodies are members of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ, and make them members of a prostitute? May it never be!

Arminian

Your bodies are members of Christ; shall they become members of a prostitute? Never! Arminian readers see union with Christ as a reason to choose holiness. Sexual sin contradicts belonging to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see union with Christ as the deepest ground of sexual ethics. The believer’s body is joined to Christ himself.

1 Corinthians 6:16

16Or don’t you know that he who is joined to a prostitute is one body? For, “The two”, he says, “will become one flesh.”

Arminian

Joined to a prostitute becomes one body, citing Genesis: the two become one flesh. Arminian readers see sex as creating a real union, not a casual act. God designed it for covenant marriage.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the creational order of marriage reaffirmed. Sexual union carries meaning given by God that cannot be emptied.

1 Corinthians 6:17

17But he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit.

Arminian

Whoever is joined to the Lord is one spirit with him. Arminian readers see the intimate relationship believers have with Christ through faith. This union is the believer’s highest loyalty.

Reformed

Reformed readers see mystical union with Christ by the Spirit. The union is real, spiritual, and the source of every benefit of salvation.

Key contrast Both treasure the believer’s union with Christ. Arminian readers stress the union as a living relationship maintained by faith. Reformed readers stress the union as the Spirit’s secure bond that grounds all saving benefits.

1 Corinthians 6:18

18Flee sexual immorality! “Every sin that a man does is outside the body,” but he who commits sexual immorality sins against his own body.

Arminian

Flee sexual immorality; it is a sin against one’s own body. Arminian readers see a decisive call to action, not passivity. Believers must actively run from temptation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sexual sin as uniquely damaging because of the body’s union with Christ. Fleeing is the God-given means of resisting it.

1 Corinthians 6:19

19Or don’t you know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God? You are not your own,

Arminian

Your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you; you are not your own. Arminian readers see the Spirit’s indwelling as both privilege and responsibility. Believers must honour God’s presence in how they live.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s ownership of believers through the Spirit’s indwelling. Here the individual body is a temple, alongside the corporate temple of 3:16.

1 Corinthians 6:20

20for you were bought with a price. Therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God’s.

Arminian

You were bought with a price; so glorify God in your body. Arminian readers see the price of redemption as Christ’s death for all, calling those who receive it to holy living. Gratitude for redemption motivates obedience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s purchase of his people, which secures their belonging to God. Glorifying God in the body is the purpose of redemption.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
