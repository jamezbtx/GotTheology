Passage

1 Corinthians 15

Verses 1–11 · WEB

Paul reminds the Corinthians of the gospel he preached, which they received and in which they stand, and by which they are saved if they hold firmly to it. He delivers what he received as of first importance: Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared to Cephas, the Twelve, more than five hundred, James, all the apostles, and last of all to Paul. Paul calls himself the least of the apostles, yet by God’s grace he worked harder than all of them, though it was the grace of God with him. Both traditions treasure this passage as the core of the gospel. They read the conditional in verse 2 and the grace in verse 10 in distinctive ways.

1 Corinthians 15:1

1Now I declare to you, brothers, the Good News which I preached to you, which also you received, in which you also stand,

Arminian

Paul reminds them of the gospel he preached, which they received and in which they stand. Arminian readers see a gospel received by faith and stood in through continued faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same gospel received and stood in. They stress that standing in grace is God’s gift, as Romans 5:2 says. Reformed readers see the gospel both as the means of conversion and as the ground on which believers continue to stand.

1 Corinthians 15:2

2by which also you are saved, if you hold firmly the word which I preached to you—unless you believed in vain.

Arminian

They are saved by this gospel, if they hold firmly to the word, unless they believed in vain. Arminian readers see a real condition: final salvation depends on continuing in faith, and believing in vain is a real danger.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the condition as the way true faith shows itself. Those who truly believe will hold fast, and “in vain” describes a faith that was never genuine or a gospel emptied of the resurrection.

Key contrast Arminian readers see “if you hold firmly” as a genuine condition that a believer could fail to meet. Reformed readers see it as describing the perseverance that marks true faith, which God secures.

1 Corinthians 15:3

3For I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures,

Arminian

Paul delivered as of first importance what he received: Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures. Arminian readers see Christ’s death as provision for the sins of all, received through faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see substitutionary atonement at the heart of the gospel. Many connect “for our sins” to Christ’s definite work for his people. Others in the tradition affirm a sufficiency for all while the intent is for the elect. Reformed readers agree with Arminians that “according to the Scriptures” points to Isaiah 53 and the whole Old Testament pattern.

1 Corinthians 15:4

4that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures,

Arminian

He was buried and raised on the third day according to the Scriptures. Arminian readers see the resurrection as God’s vindication of Christ and the ground of hope.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same. The burial confirms the reality of death, and the resurrection fulfills Scripture. Reformed readers see the Scriptures fulfilled in the burial and the third day, often linking this to Psalm 16 and Jonah. The resurrection is God's vindication of the Son and the ground of the believer's justification.

1 Corinthians 15:5

5and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve.

Arminian

He appeared to Cephas, then to the Twelve. Arminian readers see historical witnesses as the basis of the Christian faith. Paul names Cephas first, the one who denied the Lord. The appearances rest the gospel on public testimony, not private visions.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the emphasis on eyewitness testimony. Peter’s restoration after his denial also displays the grace of the risen Lord. The Twelve are named as a group of formal witnesses.

1 Corinthians 15:6

6Then he appeared to over five hundred brothers at once, most of whom remain until now, but some have also fallen asleep.

Arminian

Then he appeared to more than five hundred at once, most of whom were still alive. Arminian readers see a public, verifiable claim. Most of the five hundred were still alive and could be questioned. The resurrection is presented as an event in history, open to inquiry.

Reformed

Reformed readers see public evidence that could be checked, which matters for Christian apologetics. Paul invites inquiry rather than blind belief. Some have fallen asleep, a gentle phrase for believers' death.

1 Corinthians 15:7

7Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles,

Arminian

Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles. Arminian readers see further witnesses, including the Lord’s brother. James had not believed during Jesus' ministry, according to John 7:5. Arminian readers see the risen Christ appearing to a skeptic, who later became a leader in the Jerusalem church.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that James, once an unbeliever according to John 7:5, became a leader in the church. His conversion is often cited as evidence of the resurrection.

1 Corinthians 15:8

8and last of all, as to the child born at the wrong time, he appeared to me also.

Arminian

Last of all, as to one untimely born, he appeared to Paul. Arminian readers see grace reaching even the persecutor. Paul calls himself one untimely born, an abrupt and unexpected birth into apostleship. Arminian readers see grace confronting Paul in a way he then embraced in faith and obedience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereign grace in Paul’s conversion. Paul himself describes it as God’s calling in Galatians 1:15, given to a persecutor who was not seeking Christ.

1 Corinthians 15:9

9For I am the least of the apostles, who is not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the assembly of God.

Arminian

Paul is the least of the apostles, unworthy to be called one, because he persecuted the church. Arminian readers see humility and grace to the undeserving.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Paul’s unworthiness highlights unmerited grace. His past as a persecutor shows that God’s choice does not rest on human worthiness. Paul calls himself the least of the apostles because he persecuted the church.

1 Corinthians 15:10

10But by the grace of God I am what I am. His grace which was given to me was not futile, but I worked more than all of them; yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me.

Arminian

By the grace of God he is what he is, and that grace was not in vain; he worked harder than all, yet not he but the grace of God with him. Arminian readers see grace that works together with Paul’s own effort, which he really exerted.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace as the cause of Paul’s labor. Augustine and Calvin both used this verse to show that grace produces good works rather than merely assisting them.

Key contrast Arminian readers see grace with which Paul cooperated, working alongside his real effort. Reformed readers see grace as the decisive cause of Paul’s labor, so that the credit belongs to God alone.

1 Corinthians 15:11

11Whether then it is I or they, so we preach, and so you believed.

Arminian

Whether Paul or the others, so they preached and so the Corinthians believed. Arminian readers see one shared gospel among all the apostles. The gospel does not depend on which preacher brings it. What matters is the message believed, which is the same for all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the unity of the apostolic preaching. This supports the consistency of the witness preserved in the New Testament. Whether Paul or others preached, the Corinthians believed the same message.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
