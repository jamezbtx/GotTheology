Passage

1 Corinthians 15

Verses 12–19 · WEB

Some in Corinth were saying there is no resurrection of the dead. Paul answers by showing the consequences. If there is no resurrection, then Christ has not been raised; if Christ has not been raised, preaching is empty, faith is futile, the apostles are false witnesses, believers are still in their sins, and those who have died in Christ have perished. If our hope in Christ is only for this life, we are the most pitiable of all people. Both traditions read this as a defense of the bodily resurrection, central to Christian faith. Neither tradition finds much here to divide over.

1 Corinthians 15:12

12Now if Christ is preached, that he has been raised from the dead, how do some among you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?

Arminian

If Christ is preached as raised, how can some say there is no resurrection of the dead? Arminian readers see Paul confronting a serious error in the church. Denying the general resurrection undermines Christ’s own.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the error likely came from Greek thought that despised the body. The gospel affirms the redemption of the whole person. Some at Corinth said there is no resurrection of the dead, perhaps because they could not imagine a bodily future. Paul answers by pointing to Christ's own bodily resurrection.

1 Corinthians 15:13

13But if there is no resurrection of the dead, neither has Christ been raised.

Arminian

If there is no resurrection, then Christ has not been raised. Arminian readers see Christ’s resurrection tied to the resurrection of believers. Paul argues that denying resurrection in general denies Christ's resurrection in particular. The two stand or fall together.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the union of Christ and his people. What is true of the head is true of the body, so the two resurrections stand or fall together.

1 Corinthians 15:14

14If Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain, and your faith also is in vain.

Arminian

If Christ has not been raised, preaching and faith are in vain. Arminian readers see faith resting on historical fact. Arminian readers see that the gospel is not merely a moral or spiritual idea. If the event did not happen, the apostles' preaching and the church's believing would both be empty.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that faith without a risen Christ has no object. The value of faith lies entirely in the one it trusts. Preaching is empty if its central claim is false.

1 Corinthians 15:15

15Yes, we are also found false witnesses of God, because we testified about God that he raised up Christ, whom he didn’t raise up, if it is so that the dead are not raised.

Arminian

The apostles would be false witnesses of God. Arminian readers see the integrity of the witnesses at stake. Paul’s argument assumes the apostles were in a position to know.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s own truthfulness at stake. The apostles testified that God raised Christ, so a false resurrection would make them misrepresent God. Paul does not allow the resurrection to be treated as a symbol. Either God raised Christ, or the witnesses lied about God.

1 Corinthians 15:16

16For if the dead aren’t raised, neither has Christ been raised.

Arminian

If the dead are not raised, Christ has not been raised. Arminian readers see Paul repeating the argument for emphasis. The repetition presses the Corinthians to see what follows from their denial. Arminian readers see Paul appealing to reason so that hearers will reconsider and believe rightly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul tightening the logic. Christ’s resurrection is the beginning of the general resurrection, not an isolated exception. Denying the general resurrection would deny Christ's.

1 Corinthians 15:17

17If Christ has not been raised, your faith is vain; you are still in your sins.

Arminian

If Christ has not been raised, faith is futile and you are still in your sins. Arminian readers see the resurrection as essential to forgiveness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the resurrection as God’s declaration that Christ’s atonement was accepted, as Romans 4:25 says. Without it, justification would have no public seal.

1 Corinthians 15:18

18Then they also who are fallen asleep in Christ have perished.

Arminian

Those who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished. Arminian readers see that hope for the dead depends on the resurrection. If Christ is not raised, believers who have died have no hope. Arminian readers see Paul confronting the Corinthians with what their denial would mean for their own loved ones.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that the comfort of believers in grief rests entirely on Christ’s resurrection. Without it, the dead in Christ would be lost. The phrase “fallen asleep in Christ” assumes they will wake.

1 Corinthians 15:19

19If we have only hoped in Christ in this life, we are of all men most pitiable.

Arminian

If we have hope in Christ only in this life, we are most pitiable. Arminian readers see the Christian life oriented toward eternal hope. Paul does not say the Christian life has no present value. He says that its hope reaches beyond death, and without the resurrection that hope would be empty.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Christian suffering makes sense only in light of the resurrection. Faith that looks only to this life would be a cruel delusion.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
