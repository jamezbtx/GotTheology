Passage

1 Corinthians 12

Verses 27–31 · WEB

Paul applies the image of the body directly: the Corinthians are the body of Christ, and each is a member of it. God has appointed in the church first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then miracles, healings, helps, administrations, and kinds of tongues. Not all are apostles, prophets, or teachers, and not all speak in tongues. Paul urges them to earnestly desire the greater gifts and then promises to show them a still more excellent way, the way of love in chapter 13. Arminian and Reformed readers agree that the ranking of gifts favors those that build up the church through the word, though they differ over which gifts continue.

1 Corinthians 12:27

27Now you are the body of Christ, and members individually.

Arminian

You are the body of Christ, and members individually. Arminian readers see each believer belonging to Christ and to one another.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the church as Christ’s body united to him by the Spirit. Each member has a place given by God, and none is a member by its own choosing alone.

1 Corinthians 12:28

28God has set some in the assembly: first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then miracle workers, then gifts of healings, helps, governments, and various kinds of languages.

Arminian

God has appointed in the church first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then other gifts. Arminian readers see a ranking that puts the ministry of the word first, and many in Pentecostal and Holiness traditions see all these gifts continuing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see apostles and prophets as foundational, as Ephesians 2:20 says. Many Reformed interpreters see these offices as limited to the founding of the church, with teachers continuing in pastors and elders.

1 Corinthians 12:29

29Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Are all miracle workers?

Arminian

Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Are all workers of miracles? Arminian readers see that no single gift is required of all believers. The questions expect the answer no.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same. The questions expect the answer no, which shows that gifts differ by God’s appointment.

1 Corinthians 12:30

30Do all have gifts of healings? Do all speak with various languages? Do all interpret?

Arminian

Do all have gifts of healing? Do all speak with tongues? Do all interpret? Many Arminians read this verse as showing that not all speak in tongues. Some Pentecostal traditions distinguish the gift of tongues here from tongues as an initial sign of Spirit baptism.

Reformed

Reformed readers see this verse as clear evidence that tongues are not required of all believers. The form of the question in Greek expects a negative answer.

Key contrast Classical Pentecostals, who are mostly Arminian, often distinguish tongues as initial evidence from tongues as a gift for ministry. Reformed readers, and many other Arminians, see verse 30 as showing that tongues are not expected of every believer.

1 Corinthians 12:31

31But earnestly desire the best gifts. Moreover, I show a most excellent way to you.

Arminian

But earnestly desire the greater gifts, and Paul shows a more excellent way. Arminian readers see believers encouraged to seek gifts that build up the church, while love is the path that governs them all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same balance. Gifts are given by the Spirit’s will, yet believers may desire them; love is the way in which every gift must be used.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
