Passage

Mark 15

Verses 42–47 · WEB

As evening approaches on the day before the Sabbath, Joseph of Arimathea, a respected council member who was waiting for God’s kingdom, boldly asks Pilate for Jesus’ body. Pilate confirms Jesus is dead, then grants the body. Joseph wraps it in linen, lays it in a rock tomb, and rolls a stone across the entrance, while two of the women watch. The burial confirms that Jesus truly died and sets up the empty tomb. Both traditions read this passage in essentially the same way.

Mark 15:42

42When evening had now come, because it was the Preparation Day, that is, the day before the Sabbath,

Arminian

Evening was coming on the Preparation Day, the day before the Sabbath, so burial had to be quick (Deuteronomy 21:22–23). Arminian readers note the time pressure.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same. This verse is shared narrative ground.

Mark 15:43

43Joseph of Arimathaea, a prominent council member who also himself was looking for God’s Kingdom, came. He boldly went in to Pilate, and asked for Jesus’ body.

Arminian

Joseph, a respected council member who was waiting for God’s kingdom, boldly asks for the body. Arminian readers see a man stepping out in courage at real risk.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God raising up an unexpected servant. Both traditions honor Joseph’s courage.

Mark 15:44

44Pilate was surprised to hear that he was already dead; and summoning the centurion, he asked him whether he had been dead long.

Arminian

Pilate is surprised that Jesus is already dead and checks with the centurion. Arminian readers note that this confirms Jesus truly died.

Reformed

Reformed readers make the same point, which counters any claim that Jesus only fainted. Both traditions treat the reality of his death as essential.

Mark 15:45

45When he found out from the centurion, he granted the body to Joseph.

Arminian

Pilate grants the body to Joseph. Arminian readers note the official confirmation of the death.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same. This verse is shared ground.

Mark 15:46

46He bought a linen cloth, and taking him down, wound him in the linen cloth and laid him in a tomb which had been cut out of a rock. He rolled a stone against the door of the tomb.

Arminian

Joseph wraps the body in linen, lays it in a rock-cut tomb, and rolls a stone over the entrance. Arminian readers see a respectful burial that also makes the tomb easy to identify.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same, often linking it to Isaiah 53:9 (with the rich in his death). Both traditions see the burial as part of the gospel itself (1 Corinthians 15:3–4).

Mark 15:47

47Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses, saw where he was laid.

Arminian

Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses see where he is laid. Arminian readers note that they are witnesses who know the right tomb.

Reformed

Reformed readers make the same point. Both traditions see their witness preparing for the resurrection account.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
