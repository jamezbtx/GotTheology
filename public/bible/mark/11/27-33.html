Passage

Mark 11

Verses 27–33 · WEB

The temple authorities demand to know by what authority Jesus acts. He answers with a question about John’s baptism: from heaven or from men? Trapped between their unbelief and their fear of the crowd, they plead ignorance, and Jesus declines to answer them. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the passage exposes leaders unwilling to follow the truth they already have.

Mark 11:27

27They came again to Jerusalem, and as he was walking in the temple, the chief priests, the scribes, and the elders came to him,

Arminian

In the temple the chief priests, scribes, and elders approach him. Arminian and Reformed readers agree these represent the Sanhedrin’s leadership.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the very groups Jesus predicted would reject him (Mark 8:31) now confront him.

Mark 11:28

28and they began saying to him, “By what authority do you do these things? Or who gave you this authority to do these things?”

Arminian

By what authority do you do these things, and who gave it to you? Arminian and Reformed readers agree these things include the temple action.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the question of Jesus’ authority is central to Mark’s Gospel.

Mark 11:29

29Jesus said to them, “I will ask you one question. Answer me, and I will tell you by what authority I do these things.

Arminian

Jesus answers with one question of his own. Arminian and Reformed readers agree this is a recognized teaching method that exposes motives.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus’ wisdom in refusing a trap.

Mark 11:30

30The baptism of John—was it from heaven, or from men? Answer me.”

Arminian

Was John’s baptism from heaven or from men? Arminian and Reformed readers agree the question links Jesus’ authority with John’s witness to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that accepting John meant accepting the one he announced.

Mark 11:31

31They reasoned with themselves, saying, “If we should say, ‘From heaven;’ he will say, ‘Why then did you not believe him?’

Arminian

They reason that if they say from heaven, he will ask why they did not believe John. Arminian readers note that they reason pragmatically rather than seeking truth.

Reformed

Reformed readers see hearts unwilling to submit, more concerned with the consequences than with the truth.

Mark 11:32

32If we should say, ‘From men’”—they feared the people, for all held John to really be a prophet.

Arminian

If from men, they fear the people, who held John to be a prophet. Arminian and Reformed readers agree fear of the crowd controls them.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the fear of man as a snare (Proverbs 29:25).

Mark 11:33

33They answered Jesus, “We don’t know.” Jesus said to them, “Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things.”

Arminian

They say we don’t know, and Jesus refuses to tell them his authority. Arminian and Reformed readers agree that those who refuse light already given do not receive more.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the refusal exposes their unbelief while the parable that follows answers the question indirectly.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
