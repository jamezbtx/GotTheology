Passage

Mark 11

Verses 1–11 · WEB

Jesus approaches Jerusalem from the Mount of Olives and arranges, with prophetic foreknowledge, to ride a young donkey never ridden before. The crowds spread garments and branches and cry out words from Psalm 118, hailing the coming kingdom of David. Mark ends the scene quietly: Jesus looks around the temple and withdraws to Bethany. Arminian and Reformed readers agree this is a deliberate messianic act that echoes Zechariah 9:9, a humble king coming in peace.

Mark 11:1

1When they came near to Jerusalem, to Bethsphage and Bethany, at the Mount of Olives, he sent two of his disciples

Arminian

Jesus sends two disciples from near Bethsphage and Bethany on the Mount of Olives. Arminian readers note the deliberate staging: Jesus chooses the place and time of his entry.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the Mount of Olives’ link with the Lord’s coming in Zechariah 14:4. Jesus moves toward Jerusalem knowingly, fulfilling his mission.

Mark 11:2

2and said to them, “Go your way into the village that is opposite you. Immediately as you enter into it, you will find a young donkey tied, on which no one has sat. Untie him and bring him.

Arminian

He tells them they will find an unridden colt tied in the village. Arminian and Reformed readers agree this shows Jesus’ foreknowledge and recalls Zechariah 9:9.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that an animal never ridden suits sacred use (compare Numbers 19:2). The king directs every detail.

Mark 11:3

3If anyone asks you, ‘Why are you doing this?’ say, ‘The Lord needs him;’ and immediately he will send him back here.”

Arminian

If asked, they are to say the Lord needs it and will send it back. Arminian readers see Jesus’ authority joined with courtesy: he borrows and returns.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the title Lord. Whether it means Jesus himself or God, interpreters differ, but the claim of authority is clear.

Mark 11:4

4They went away, and found a young donkey tied at the door outside in the open street, and they untied him.

Arminian

They find the colt tied by a door in the street and untie it. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the fulfillment matches Jesus’ word exactly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the reliability of Jesus’ word demonstrated in small things.

Mark 11:5

5Some of those who stood there asked them, “What are you doing, untying the young donkey?”

Arminian

Bystanders ask why they are untying the colt. Arminian and Reformed readers agree Mark records the scene as Jesus predicted.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God’s providence works through ordinary people and questions.

Mark 11:6

6They said to them just as Jesus had said, and they let them go.

Arminian

They answer as Jesus said, and the people let them go. Arminian readers note the disciples’ simple obedience.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that obedience to Christ’s word meets with the outcome he promised.

Mark 11:7

7They brought the young donkey to Jesus and threw their garments on it, and Jesus sat on it.

Arminian

They lay garments on the colt and Jesus sits on it. Arminian and Reformed readers agree this is an act of royal homage (compare 2 Kings 9:13).

Reformed

Reformed readers see the humble king of Zechariah 9:9, coming not on a war horse but a donkey.

Mark 11:8

8Many spread their garments on the way, and others were cutting down branches from the trees and spreading them on the road.

Arminian

Many spread garments and leafy branches on the road. Arminian and Reformed readers agree this is a festive, royal welcome.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the enthusiasm of the crowd, which Mark will soon contrast with the rejection of Jesus.

Mark 11:9

9Those who went in front and those who followed cried out, “Hosanna! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord!

Arminian

The crowds cry Hosanna, blessed is he who comes in the Lord’s name, from Psalm 118. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the crowd hails Jesus as the coming one, though perhaps without understanding the cross ahead.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Hosanna, meaning save us, is a prayer that Jesus will answer in a way the crowd does not expect.

Mark 11:10

10Blessed is the kingdom of our father David that is coming in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest!”

Arminian

Blessed is the coming kingdom of our father David. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the crowd expects a Davidic restoration.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the kingdom truly arriving in Jesus, though through suffering rather than political triumph.

Mark 11:11

11Jesus entered into the temple in Jerusalem. When he had looked around at everything, it being now evening, he went out to Bethany with the twelve.

Arminian

Jesus enters the temple, looks around at everything, and leaves for Bethany at evening. Arminian readers note the quiet inspection that sets up the next day’s action.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Lord coming to his temple (Malachi 3:1) and surveying it before judgment.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
