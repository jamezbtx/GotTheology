Passage

Mark 16

Verses 1–8 · WEB

After the Sabbath, three women bring spices to the tomb at sunrise, wondering who will roll away the large stone. They find it already moved, and inside a young man in white tells them that Jesus of Nazareth has risen, that he is not there, and that they should tell his disciples and Peter he is going ahead of them to Galilee. The women flee in trembling and fear and say nothing to anyone. In the two oldest complete Greek manuscripts, Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, the Gospel ends here. Many interpreters across both traditions think Mark intended this abrupt ending, while others think the original ending was lost. Arminian and Reformed readers share the same resurrection faith.

Mark 16:1

1When the Sabbath was past, Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James, and Salome bought spices, that they might come and anoint him.

Arminian

When the Sabbath ends, three women buy spices to anoint the body. Arminian readers note their devotion, though they do not yet expect a resurrection.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same: their love is real, even while their faith is still unformed. Both traditions honor them as the first witnesses.

Mark 16:2

2Very early on the first day of the week, they came to the tomb when the sun had risen.

Arminian

They arrive very early on the first day of the week. Arminian readers note that the first day becomes the church’s day of worship in honor of the resurrection.

Reformed

Reformed readers make the same point. This verse is shared ground.

Mark 16:3

3They were saying among themselves, “Who will roll away the stone from the door of the tomb for us?”

Arminian

They wonder who will roll away the stone. Arminian readers see sincere disciples facing an obstacle they cannot remove.

Reformed

Reformed readers often see a picture of human helplessness before God acts. Both traditions see the question answered before they arrive.

Mark 16:4

4for it was very big. Looking up, they saw that the stone was rolled back.

Arminian

They look up and see that the huge stone has already been rolled away. Arminian readers note that God has gone ahead of them.

Reformed

Reformed readers make the same point, emphasizing that God acted first. Both traditions agree that the stone was moved to show that the tomb was empty, not to let Jesus out.

Mark 16:5

5Entering into the tomb, they saw a young man sitting on the right side, dressed in a white robe; and they were amazed.

Arminian

Inside they see a young man in a white robe, whom the other Gospels describe as an angel, and they are alarmed. Arminian readers note the normal human fear in the presence of the heavenly.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same. This verse is shared ground.

Mark 16:6

6He said to them, “Don’t be amazed. You seek Jesus, the Nazarene, who has been crucified. He has risen! He is not here. See the place where they laid him!

Arminian

“He has risen! He is not here.” The one who was crucified is the one who is raised, and the empty place is evidence. Arminian readers stress that the resurrection vindicates Jesus and opens the way of salvation to all who believe.

Reformed

Reformed readers stress that the resurrection is God’s vindication of his Son and the guarantee that his saving work is complete. Both traditions stand together on the bodily resurrection.

Mark 16:7

7But go, tell his disciples and Peter, ‘He goes before you into Galilee. There you will see him, as he said to you.’ ”

Arminian

The women must tell the disciples “and Peter” that Jesus is going ahead to Galilee, as he promised (Mark 14:28). Arminian readers see restoration offered to Peter, who had denied him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same grace to Peter and the faithfulness of Christ to his promise. Both traditions find great comfort in Jesus naming the one who failed.

Mark 16:8

8They went out, and fled from the tomb, for trembling and astonishment had come on them. They said nothing to anyone; for they were afraid.

Arminian

They flee in trembling and amazement and say nothing, because they are afraid. If this is Mark’s intended ending, it leaves the reader with a challenge: will you tell what you have heard? Arminian readers often stress the call to respond in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers often stress that the promise of verse 7 stands, despite human fear, and the message will still go out because it depends on God. Whether Mark ended here, or the ending was lost, remains debated in both traditions. The WEB notes that one manuscript adds a short ending after this verse.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
