Passage

Mark 12

Verses 13–27 · WEB

Two traps follow. Pharisees and Herodians ask about paying taxes to Caesar; Jesus answers that what bears Caesar’s image belongs to Caesar and what belongs to God must be given to God. Sadducees, who deny the resurrection, pose a riddle about a woman married to seven brothers; Jesus says they know neither the Scriptures nor God’s power, since the resurrected do not marry and God is the God of the living. Arminian and Reformed readers agree on both answers.

Mark 12:13

13They sent some of the Pharisees and the Herodians to him, that they might trap him with words.

Arminian

They send Pharisees and Herodians to trap him in his words. Arminian and Reformed readers agree this alliance of rivals shows shared hostility (compare 3:6).

Reformed

Reformed readers note the cunning of the attempt to entangle him.

Mark 12:14

14When they had come, they asked him, “Teacher, we know that you are honest, and don’t defer to anyone; for you aren’t partial to anyone, but truly teach the way of God. Is it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar, or not?

Arminian

They flatter him as truthful and impartial, then ask whether to pay taxes to Caesar. Arminian and Reformed readers agree either answer seemed dangerous: rebellion or collaboration.

Reformed

Reformed readers note their flattery is ironically true.

Mark 12:15

15Shall we give, or shall we not give?” But he, knowing their hypocrisy, said to them, “Why do you test me? Bring me a denarius, that I may see it.”

Arminian

Knowing their hypocrisy, he asks for a denarius. Arminian and Reformed readers agree Jesus sees their motives.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that asking for the coin exposes that they carry Caesar’s money.

Mark 12:16

16They brought it. He said to them, “Whose is this image and inscription?” They said to him, “Caesar’s.”

Arminian

Whose image and inscription? Caesar’s. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the image detail sets up the answer.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the implied contrast with humans who bear God’s image (Genesis 1:27).

Mark 12:17

17Jesus answered them, “Give to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s.” They marveled greatly at him.

Arminian

Give Caesar what is Caesar’s and God what is God’s. Arminian and Reformed readers agree this affirms limited civil duty while placing all of life under God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the basis for a theology of the state under God; traditions differ in how they apply it, but share the priority of God’s claim.

Mark 12:18

18Some Sadducees, who say that there is no resurrection, came to him. They asked him, saying,

Arminian

Sadducees, who deny the resurrection, come to him. Arminian and Reformed readers agree Mark identifies them by this denial.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the Sadducees accepted the books of Moses as authoritative, which shapes Jesus’ reply.

Mark 12:19

19“Teacher, Moses wrote to us, ‘If a man’s brother dies and leaves a wife behind him, and leaves no children, that his brother should take his wife and raise up offspring for his brother.’

Arminian

They cite Moses’ levirate law (Deuteronomy 25:5 to 6). Arminian and Reformed readers agree they use Scripture to mock the resurrection.

Reformed

Reformed readers note how Scripture can be misused to support unbelief.

Mark 12:20

20There were seven brothers. The first took a wife, and dying left no offspring.

Arminian

Seven brothers marry in turn and die childless. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the scenario is meant to be absurd.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the constructed case aimed at ridicule.

Mark 12:21

21The second took her, and died, leaving no children behind him. The third likewise;

Arminian

The second and third die likewise. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the repetition heightens the riddle.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the rhetorical buildup.

Mark 12:22

22and the seven took her and left no children. Last of all the woman also died.

Arminian

All seven die childless, and finally the woman. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the story is complete for the trap.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the setup for Jesus’ answer.

Mark 12:23

23In the resurrection, when they rise, whose wife will she be of them? For the seven had her as a wife.”

Arminian

In the resurrection, whose wife will she be? Arminian and Reformed readers agree the question assumes the resurrection life merely continues earthly arrangements.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that this assumption is the error Jesus corrects.

Mark 12:24

24Jesus answered them, “Isn’t this because you are mistaken, not knowing the Scriptures nor the power of God?

Arminian

You are wrong, not knowing the Scriptures or God’s power. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the error is both a misreading of Scripture and a too-small view of God.

Reformed

Reformed readers stress the two sources of right belief: Scripture and the power of God.

Mark 12:25

25For when they will rise from the dead, they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels in heaven.

Arminian

The resurrected neither marry nor are given in marriage but are like angels. Arminian and Reformed readers agree this does not say people become angels, but that the age to come transcends marriage.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the resurrection is a transformed life, not a mere extension of this one.

Mark 12:26

26But about the dead, that they are raised, haven’t you read in the book of Moses about the Bush, how God spoke to him, saying, ‘I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob’?

Arminian

God at the bush said, I am the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Arminian and Reformed readers agree Jesus argues from the books of Moses the Sadducees accepted.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s covenant faithfulness to the patriarchs as a guarantee of their resurrection.

Mark 12:27

27He is not the God of the dead, but of the living. You are therefore badly mistaken.”

Arminian

He is God of the living, not the dead; you are badly mistaken. Arminian and Reformed readers agree the covenant relationship death cannot end implies resurrection.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the firmness of Jesus’ rebuke.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
