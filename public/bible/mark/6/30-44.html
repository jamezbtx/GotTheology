Passage

Mark 6

Verses 30–44 · WEB

The apostles return and report, and Jesus invites them to rest, but crowds follow. Moved with compassion for people like sheep without a shepherd, Jesus teaches them and then feeds five thousand men with five loaves and two fish. Twelve baskets of leftovers remain. Both traditions see Jesus as the compassionate Shepherd and provider; Old Testament echoes of Moses, manna, and the shepherd psalm are widely noted.

Mark 6:30

30The apostles gathered themselves together to Jesus, and they told him all things, whatever they had done, and whatever they had taught.

Arminian

The apostles report all they did and taught. Arminian readers see faithful service returning to Jesus. Only here does Mark call them apostles, those who have been sent.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the mission accountable to Christ, who sent them. Their report returns all the credit for the work to the one whose authority they carried.

Mark 6:31

31He said to them, “Come away into a deserted place, and rest awhile.” For there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat.

Arminian

Come away and rest. Arminian readers see Jesus caring for His workers’ needs. Ministry demands do not cancel the need for rest.

Reformed

Reformed readers see rest as part of faithful service. Jesus cares for the whole person of His servants.

Mark 6:32

32They went away in the boat to a deserted place by themselves.

Arminian

They go by boat to a deserted place. Arminian readers see the search for rest. Even their retreat becomes an occasion for Jesus’ compassion.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the setting prepare for a wilderness feeding, echoing Israel’s manna. The wilderness setting recalls God feeding Israel in the desert.

Mark 6:33

33They saw them going, and many recognized him and ran there on foot from all the cities. They arrived before them and came together to him.

Arminian

Crowds recognize them and run ahead on foot. Arminian readers see real spiritual hunger drawing people to Jesus, even when it interrupts His plans for rest.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the demand on Jesus’ compassion. Their rush overtakes the plan for rest.

Mark 6:34

34Jesus came out, saw a great multitude, and he had compassion on them because they were like sheep without a shepherd; and he began to teach them many things.

Arminian

He has compassion on them, like sheep without a shepherd. Arminian readers see Jesus’ love for all the crowd.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus as the promised Shepherd, compare Numbers 27:17 and Ezekiel 34.

Mark 6:35

35When it was late in the day, his disciples came to him and said, “This place is deserted, and it is late in the day.

Arminian

It is late and the place deserted. Arminian readers see practical concern. The disciples see the problem but not the solution.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the setting for divine provision. Their concern is reasonable, yet they forget who is with them.

Mark 6:36

36Send them away, that they may go into the surrounding country and villages and buy themselves bread, for they have nothing to eat.”

Arminian

Send them away to buy bread. Arminian readers see the disciples thinking in human terms. Their suggestion is practical but leaves Jesus out.

Reformed

Reformed readers see limited faith. They plan as though the crowd’s need is beyond Jesus’ concern.

Mark 6:37

37But he answered them, “You give them something to eat.” They asked him, “Shall we go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give them something to eat?”

Arminian

“You give them something to eat.” Arminian readers see Jesus calling disciples to participate in provision. Two hundred denarii was a large sum, roughly two hundred days’ wages for a laborer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Jesus exposing human inadequacy before providing. Their answer shows how impossible the task is by human reckoning.

Mark 6:38

38He said to them, “How many loaves do you have? Go see.” When they knew, they said, “Five, and two fish.”

Arminian

Five loaves and two fish. Arminian readers see small resources offered to Jesus. Jesus works with what is available.

Reformed

Reformed readers see insufficiency that makes the miracle plain. Jesus begins with what they have so that the result is plainly His work.

Mark 6:39

39He commanded them that everyone should sit down in groups on the green grass.

Arminian

He has them sit on the green grass. Arminian readers see order and care. The green grass also suggests springtime.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Psalm 23 echoes of green pastures. The Shepherd makes His flock lie down and feeds them.

Mark 6:40

40They sat down in ranks, by hundreds and by fifties.

Arminian

They sit in groups of hundreds and fifties. Arminian readers see order in provision. The crowd is organized so that all can be fed.

Reformed

Reformed readers note possible echoes of Israel’s camp order in Exodus 18:21. The arrangement turns a crowd into an ordered people.

Mark 6:41

41He took the five loaves and the two fish; and looking up to heaven, he blessed and broke the loaves, and he gave to his disciples to set before them, and he divided the two fish among them all.

Arminian

He blesses, breaks, and gives. Arminian readers see thanksgiving and provision. Jesus looks to heaven, showing dependence on the Father.

Reformed

Reformed readers see language that anticipates the Lord’s Supper. The disciples distribute what Jesus provides.

Mark 6:42

42They all ate and were filled.

Arminian

All ate and were filled. Arminian readers see provision offered to the whole crowd, with no one left out.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s sufficiency for His people. The hungry are fully satisfied, as Israel was fed in the wilderness.

Mark 6:43

43They took up twelve baskets full of broken pieces and also of the fish.

Arminian

Twelve baskets of pieces remain. Arminian readers see God’s generosity going beyond what is needed, so that even the leftovers are more than they started with.

Reformed

Reformed readers see twelve perhaps pointing to Israel’s tribes. The leftovers witness to the abundance of the miracle.

Mark 6:44

44Those who ate the loaves were five thousand men.

Arminian

Five thousand men ate. Arminian readers see the scale of grace. Mark’s count shows the scale of the provision.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Matthew adds women and children. The numbers underline that this is no private event.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
