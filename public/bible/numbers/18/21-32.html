Passage

Numbers 18

Verses 21–32 · WEB

The final section gives the Levites the tithe of Israel as their inheritance in return for their service at the Tent of Meeting, since they will have no land of their own. The Levites in turn must give a tithe of that tithe, from the best part, to the LORD through Aaron the priest; once they have done so, the rest is credited to them like the produce of their own threshing floor and wine press, and they may eat it anywhere. The section ends with a warning not to profane the holy gifts. Readers in both traditions commonly find shared ground here: God provides for those who serve, everyone, including those who receive offerings, is to give back to God from the best, and holy gifts call for reverent handling. How the tithe applies to Christians today is discussed within each tradition, rather than dividing them from one another.

Numbers 18:21

21“To the children of Levi, behold, I have given all the tithe in Israel for an inheritance, in return for their service which they serve, even the service of the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the tithe given to the Levites as fair provision in return for their service. God links support to faithful work.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God assigning the tithe as the Levites’ inheritance, since they receive no land (compare Hebrews 7:5). Readers in both traditions commonly see a principle of supporting those who serve God’s people (compare 1 Timothy 5:18).

Numbers 18:22

22Henceforth the children of Israel shall not come near the Tent of Meeting, lest they bear sin, and die.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the restriction on ordinary Israelites approaching the Tent as protecting them from death. God’s boundaries serve their good.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the verse as answering the fear of 17:13 through God’s appointed order. Both traditions generally see this restricted access as contrasted with the access believers now have through Christ (Hebrews 10:19).

Numbers 18:23

23But the Levites shall do the service of the Tent of Meeting, and they shall bear their iniquity. It shall be a statute forever throughout your generations. Among the children of Israel, they shall have no inheritance.

Arminian

Arminian readers note that the Levites bear the responsibility for the Tent, a demanding calling. Their service protects the rest of the people.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Levites as bearing iniquity in a representative way, as the priests did. Readers in both traditions commonly read this as part of the mediating structure of the old covenant.

Numbers 18:24

24For the tithe of the children of Israel, which they offer as a wave offering to the LORD, I have given to the Levites for an inheritance. Therefore I have said to them, ‘Among the children of Israel they shall have no inheritance.’ ”

Arminian

Arminian readers see the tithe as a wave offering to the LORD, then given to the Levites. Giving to God’s servants is first giving to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the repeated statement that the Levites have no inheritance as pointing to their dependence on God. Both traditions generally read the verse as uniting worship and provision.

Numbers 18:25

25The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

Arminian readers note the new instruction begins with God speaking to Moses. Even those who receive offerings are addressed by God’s word.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the Levites placed under God’s command. Readers in both traditions commonly see the verse as introducing a fair and balanced system.

Numbers 18:26

26“Moreover you shall speak to the Levites, and tell them, ‘When you take of the children of Israel the tithe which I have given you from them for your inheritance, then you shall offer up a wave offering of it for the LORD, a tithe of the tithe.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the tithe of the tithe as teaching that those who receive must also give. No one is exempt from offering back to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the principle that all of God’s people, including those supported by offerings, owe God the first portion (compare Nehemiah 10:38). Both traditions generally find here a lasting lesson about giving.

Numbers 18:27

27Your wave offering shall be credited to you, as though it were the grain of the threshing floor, and as the fullness of the wine press.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the Levites’ offering credited as though it were their own harvest. God counts their giving as real worship, even though they do not farm.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God graciously reckoning the Levites’ gift as equivalent to the farmer’s produce. Readers in both traditions commonly see God valuing the heart of the giver.

Numbers 18:28

28Thus you also shall offer a wave offering to the LORD of all your tithes, which you receive of the children of Israel; and of it you shall give the LORD’s wave offering to Aaron the priest.

Arminian

Arminian readers note that the Levites’ offering goes to Aaron the priest. The flow of giving supports each part of the ministry.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the ordered structure of giving, from people to Levites to priests. Both traditions generally see God’s design for mutual support in his service.

Numbers 18:29

29Out of all your gifts, you shall offer every wave offering to the LORD, of all its best parts, even the holy part of it.’

Arminian

Arminian readers see the instruction to give from the best parts as calling for wholehearted giving. God deserves the holy part, not leftovers.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the best given to God as honoring him as Lord of all. Readers in both traditions commonly connect this with cheerful, generous giving (compare 2 Corinthians 9:7).

Numbers 18:30

30“Therefore you shall tell them, ‘When you heave its best from it, then it shall be credited to the Levites as the increase of the threshing floor, and as the increase of the wine press.

Arminian

Arminian readers note the reassurance that, once the best is given, the remainder is credited to the Levites as their own increase. Giving first frees them to enjoy the rest.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s gracious accounting, which treats the Levites as full participants in the harvest. Both traditions generally find encouragement in God’s generosity toward those who give.

Numbers 18:31

31You may eat it anywhere, you and your households, for it is your reward in return for your service in the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the freedom to eat the remainder anywhere as a sign that it is truly the Levites’ reward. Their work is honored.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the provision as a fitting wage for service in the Tent of Meeting. Readers in both traditions commonly see the principle that the worker deserves wages (1 Timothy 5:18).

Numbers 18:32

32You shall bear no sin by reason of it, when you have heaved from it its best. You shall not profane the holy things of the children of Israel, that you not die.’ ”

Arminian

Arminian readers see the closing warning as a reminder that holy gifts must be handled faithfully. Giving the best first removes guilt.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the warning against profaning holy things as underlining God’s holiness. Both traditions generally read the chapter’s end as calling for reverent stewardship of what belongs to God.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
