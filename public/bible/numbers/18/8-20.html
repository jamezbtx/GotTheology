Passage

Numbers 18

Verses 8–20 · WEB

The LORD now assigns the priests their portions: the most holy offerings, wave offerings, the best of the oil, wine and grain, first fruits, devoted things, and firstborn animals, with the firstborn of humans and of unclean animals redeemed. These gifts are confirmed as a “covenant of salt forever,” and the section closes with the LORD’s declaration to Aaron that he will have no inheritance in the land, because the LORD himself is his portion and inheritance. Arminian and Reformed readers commonly read this as shared ground: God provides for those who serve him, holy things are to be treated with reverence, and the deepest inheritance is God himself (compare Psalm 16:5; Lamentations 3:24). Many in both traditions also see a principle carried into the church, that those who serve in the gospel may be supported by it (1 Corinthians 9:13–14).

Numbers 18:8

8The LORD spoke to Aaron, “Behold, I myself have given you the command of my wave offerings, even all the holy things of the children of Israel. I have given them to you by reason of the anointing, and to your sons, as a portion forever.

Arminian

Arminian readers see God personally giving Aaron and his sons charge of the holy offerings as their portion forever. Those who serve at the altar are provided for by God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the gift tied to the anointing, showing that the priests’ support rests on God’s appointment. Readers in both traditions commonly see the principle reflected in 1 Corinthians 9:13.

Numbers 18:9

9This shall be yours of the most holy things from the fire: every offering of theirs, even every meal offering of theirs, and every sin offering of theirs, and every trespass offering of theirs, which they shall give to me, shall be most holy for you and for your sons.

Arminian

Arminian readers note that the most holy offerings, including sin and trespass offerings, become the priests’ portion. What was given to God sustains those who serve him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the holiness of these offerings marked by who may eat them and how. Both traditions generally read the verse as part of the ceremonial system fulfilled in Christ.

Numbers 18:10

10You shall eat of it like the most holy things. Every male shall eat of it. It shall be holy to you.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the instruction to eat the most holy portions as most holy things as a call to reverence. Even eating is part of holy service.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the restriction to males of the priestly family as marking the highest degree of holiness. Readers in both traditions commonly treat the rule as specific to the Levitical priesthood.

Numbers 18:11

11“This is yours, too: the wave offering of their gift, even all the wave offerings of the children of Israel. I have given them to you, and to your sons and to your daughters with you, as a portion forever. Everyone who is clean in your house shall eat of it.

Arminian

Arminian readers note that the wave offerings are shared with the whole priestly household, including daughters, if they are clean. God’s provision reaches the family.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the wider sharing as a lesser degree of holiness than the most holy portions. Both traditions generally see God’s care for the households of those who serve.

Numbers 18:12

12“I have given to you all the best of the oil, all the best of the vintage, and of the grain, the first fruits of them which they give to the LORD.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the best of the oil, wine and grain given to the priests as a sign that God’s servants receive the first and finest. Giving to God means giving the best.

Reformed

Reformed readers see first fruits as acknowledging that the whole harvest belongs to God. Readers in both traditions commonly see a lasting principle of giving God the first and best.

Numbers 18:13

13The first-ripe fruits of all that is in their land, which they bring to the LORD, shall be yours. Everyone who is clean in your house shall eat of it.

Arminian

Arminian readers note that the first-ripe fruits brought to the LORD become the priests’ portion. The people’s gratitude supports God’s servants.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the first fruits as a confession that God gives the land and its produce. Both traditions generally read the verse as joining worship and provision.

Numbers 18:14

14“Everything devoted in Israel shall be yours.

Arminian

Arminian readers see devoted things given to the priests as belonging wholly to God. What is set apart for him is not to be reclaimed (compare Leviticus 27:28).

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see devoted things as permanently God’s. Readers in both traditions commonly read the verse as part of Israel’s covenant system, with the exact scope of “devoted” varying by context.

Numbers 18:15

15Everything that opens the womb, of all flesh which they offer to the LORD, both of man and animal, shall be yours. Nevertheless, you shall surely redeem the firstborn of man, and you shall redeem the firstborn of unclean animals.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the firstborn as belonging to the LORD, with human firstborn and unclean animals redeemed (compare Exodus 13:13). Redemption is built into Israel’s worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the redemption of the firstborn as a reminder of the Passover and of God’s claim on his people. Many in both traditions see it as a picture of redemption that later Scripture develops, while generally keeping that connection modest.

Numbers 18:16

16You shall redeem those who are to be redeemed of them from a month old, according to your estimation, for five shekels of money, according to the shekel of the sanctuary, which weighs twenty gerahs.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the fixed price of five shekels as making redemption accessible and orderly (compare Numbers 3:47). The WEB footnotes give only approximate modern equivalents for the shekel and gerah.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the redemption price as part of God’s ordered provision for his people. Readers in both traditions generally focus on the principle of redemption rather than the exact value.

Numbers 18:17

17“But you shall not redeem the firstborn of a cow, or the firstborn of a sheep, or the firstborn of a goat. They are holy. You shall sprinkle their blood on the altar, and shall burn their fat for an offering made by fire, for a pleasant aroma to the LORD.

Arminian

Arminian readers note that clean firstborn animals are not redeemed but offered, with their blood and fat given to the LORD. They are holy and belong wholly to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the sacrifice of the clean firstborn as worship that pleases God, a pleasant aroma to him. Both traditions commonly see the sacrificial system as pointing to Christ’s once-for-all offering.

Numbers 18:18

18Their meat shall be yours, as the wave offering breast and as the right thigh, it shall be yours.

Arminian

Arminian readers see the meat of the firstborn given to the priests like the wave breast and right thigh. God provides for his servants from what is offered to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the priests’ share as God’s appointed provision. Readers in both traditions generally read the verse as continuing the theme of support for those who serve.

Numbers 18:19

19All the wave offerings of the holy things which the children of Israel offer to the LORD, I have given you and your sons and your daughters with you, as a portion forever. It is a covenant of salt forever before the LORD to you and to your offspring with you.”

Arminian

Arminian readers see the “covenant of salt forever” as underlining the permanence and reliability of God’s provision. The image of salt suggests something lasting (compare Leviticus 2:13).

Reformed

Reformed readers see God binding himself by covenant to provide for the priests (compare 2 Chronicles 13:5). The exact meaning of “covenant of salt” is debated, and readers in both traditions commonly take it as signifying an enduring commitment.

Numbers 18:20

20The LORD said to Aaron, “You shall have no inheritance in their land, neither shall you have any portion among them. I am your portion and your inheritance among the children of Israel.

Arminian

Arminian readers find great comfort in God’s words to Aaron, “I am your portion and your inheritance.” Having no land, the priest has God himself.

Reformed

Reformed readers see this as one of the most precious promises in the passage, echoed in Psalm 16:5 and Lamentations 3:24. Both traditions commonly apply it devotionally to all believers, whose deepest inheritance is God himself.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
