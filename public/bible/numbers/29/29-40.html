Passage

Numbers 29

Verses 29–40 · WEB

These verses finish the feast of booths (the sixth and seventh days), then add an eighth day, “a solemn assembly” (29:35; compare Lev 23:36), whose offerings return to the smaller scale of the first and tenth days of the month. 29:39 closes the calendar of Numbers 28–29: these fixed offerings are owed at the set feasts, apart from anything an Israelite chooses to give by vow or free will. 29:40 reports that Moses passed it all on to Israel. In Hebrew Bibles this verse is numbered 30:1, so Hebrew numbering of chapter 30 runs one verse ahead of English; the verse numbers here follow the WEB.

Numbers 29:29

29“ ‘On the sixth day: eight bulls, two rams, fourteen male lambs a year old without defect;

Arminian

The sixth day has eight bulls, still with two rams and fourteen lambs.

Arminian readers see that the feast keeps its fullness to the end, even as the count of bulls falls.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the pattern continuing exactly as commanded: eight bulls, still with two rams and fourteen lambs, all without defect.

Shared ground.

Numbers 29:30

30and their meal offering and their drink offerings for the bulls, for the rams, and for the lambs, according to their number, after the ordinance,

Arminian

The meal and drink offerings follow the established rule.

Arminian readers see the same willing obedience asked of the people on the sixth day as on the first.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same ordinance applied faithfully on the sixth day, with meal and drink offerings measured to the animals offered that day.

Shared ground.

Numbers 29:31

31and one male goat for a sin offering; in addition to the continual burnt offering, its meal offering, and the drink offerings of it.

Arminian

Again one male goat is offered for sin, alongside the continual offering with its meal and drink offerings.

Arminian readers see God’s mercy offered once more as the feast nears its end.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that atonement runs through every day of the feast, including this late day, alongside the continual offering.

Shared ground.

Numbers 29:32

32“ ‘On the seventh day: seven bulls, two rams, fourteen male lambs a year old without defect;

Arminian

On the seventh day seven bulls are offered, bringing the total to seventy for the feast. The text does not comment on the numbers.

Arminian readers see the completed week of offerings as a full offering of thanks.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the completed set as fulfilling God’s ordinance in full.

Shared ground: both traditions caution against building on symbolism the text does not explain.

Numbers 29:33

33and their meal offering and their drink offerings for the bulls, for the rams, and for the lambs, according to their number, after the ordinance,

Arminian

The seventh day’s meal and drink offerings follow the same rule.

Arminian readers see faithfulness carried through to the last day.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the pattern closing exactly as it began, “after the ordinance.” The seventh day’s grain and drink portions still follow the rule already given.

Shared ground.

Numbers 29:34

34and one male goat for a sin offering; in addition to the continual burnt offering, its meal offering, and its drink offering.

Arminian

The seventh day ends with the same sin offering and continual offering as every other day.

Arminian readers see the people’s need for grace continuing to the end of the feast.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that the continual offering frames the whole seven days. The last of those days still ends with a sin offering and the daily sacrifice.

Shared ground.

Numbers 29:35

35“ ‘On the eighth day you shall have a solemn assembly. You shall do no regular work;

Arminian

An eighth day follows as “a solemn assembly” with no regular work (Lev 23:36). After the exile Israel kept it “according to the ordinance” (Neh 8:18). John 7:37 refers to “the last and greatest day of the feast.”

Arminian readers see the final assembly as a last call to gather before the LORD.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that God appoints both the beginning and the end of the feast. The eighth day is his solemn assembly, not an addition Israel invents.

Shared ground.

Numbers 29:36

36but you shall offer a burnt offering, an offering made by fire, a pleasant aroma to the LORD: one bull, one ram, seven male lambs a year old without defect;

Arminian

The eighth day returns to one bull, one ram and seven lambs, the same scale as the first and tenth days of the month (29:2, 8).

Arminian readers see the people returning from the abundance of the feast to the ordinary rhythm of worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the eighth day as distinct from the seven, with its own offering set by God: one bull, one ram, and seven lambs, not the feast-week scale.

Shared ground.

Numbers 29:37

37their meal offering and their drink offerings for the bull, for the ram, and for the lambs, shall be according to their number, after the ordinance,

Arminian

The meal and drink offerings follow the rule again, now for a single bull and ram.

Arminian readers see that the same rule serves both the great days and the ordinary ones.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the consistency of God’s ordinance across every occasion, whether the day has many bulls or only one.

Shared ground.

Numbers 29:38

38and one male goat for a sin offering, in addition to the continual burnt offering, with its meal offering, and its drink offering.

Arminian

The last day ends like every other, with one male goat for sin and the continual offering.

Arminian readers see that the month that began with trumpets ends with mercy.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that from the first day to the last, Israel’s worship rests on sacrifice for sin.

Shared ground: both traditions see the whole round of offerings fulfilled in Christ, who offered himself once (Heb 10:1–4).

Numbers 29:39

39“ ‘You shall offer these to the LORD in your set feasts—in addition to your vows and your free will offerings—for your burnt offerings, your meal offerings, your drink offerings, and your peace offerings.’ ”

Arminian

This summary closes Numbers 28–29. These offerings are owed at the set feasts, “in addition to” what Israelites chose to give by vow or free will (compare Lev 23:37–38).

Arminian readers note the distinction between what God requires and what the people may freely give. Both matter to him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the fixed offerings as God’s appointed worship, with free gifts welcomed but not replacing what he has commanded.

Shared ground: the mention of vows leads directly into chapter 30.

Numbers 29:40

40Moses told the children of Israel according to all that the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

Moses passes on to Israel everything the LORD commanded. In Hebrew Bibles this verse is 30:1.

Arminian readers see Moses’ faithfulness as a mediator who delivers God’s word fully.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same: what Israel receives is “all that the LORD commanded Moses,” nothing more or less.

Shared ground.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
