Passage

Numbers 34

Verses 16–29 · WEB

The LORD names the men who will divide the land: Eleazar the priest and Joshua son of Nun, with one leader from each of the ten tribes still to receive land west of the Jordan. Caleb, who with Joshua had urged faith at Kadesh, represents Judah (compare Numbers 14:6–9, 24, 30). The other leaders are not mentioned elsewhere. Joshua 14:1 reports that Eleazar, Joshua, and the tribal heads carried out this distribution. Both traditions read the list as a sign of order and fairness in receiving God’s gift, and of his faithfulness to a new generation.

Numbers 34:16

16The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses about those who will divide the land. Leadership for the distribution is appointed.

Arminian readers note that God provides responsible leaders to carry out his purposes, and that their faithfulness matters.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God appoints the leaders himself. The distribution is under his authority. Even administration falls under his command.

Both traditions see the importance of trustworthy leadership in handling what God gives his people.

Numbers 34:17

17“These are the names of the men who shall divide the land to you for inheritance: Eleazar the priest, and Joshua the son of Nun.

Arminian

Eleazar the priest and Joshua son of Nun will divide the land. Priest and leader serve together.

Arminian readers note that Joshua, who trusted God at Kadesh, now receives the task of distributing the land he once urged the people to enter.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the priesthood and civil leadership work together under God. Joshua 14:1 records this arrangement carried out. Word and worship, government and priesthood, serve one purpose together.

Both traditions see God honoring faithfulness in the appointment of Joshua and Eleazar.

Numbers 34:18

18You shall take one prince of every tribe, to divide the land for inheritance.

Arminian

One leader from each tribe will help divide the land. Every tribe is represented.

Arminian readers note that shared responsibility protects fairness and gives each tribe a voice. The process models accountable leadership that serves the whole people.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God provides order and representation among his people. The distribution is not imposed by one man alone. Order among his people reflects God’s own orderly character.

Both traditions see wisdom in shared leadership under God’s direction.

Numbers 34:19

19These are the names of the men: Of the tribe of Judah, Caleb the son of Jephunneh.

Arminian

For Judah, Caleb son of Jephunneh. Caleb was one of the two spies who urged faith at Kadesh.

Arminian readers note that Caleb’s wholehearted following of God is rewarded with a place of honor in the distribution (compare Numbers 14:24).

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God keeps his promise to Caleb, preserving him through forty years to see the land. His faithfulness to his servant is evident.

Both traditions honor Caleb as an example of persevering faith.

Numbers 34:20

20Of the tribe of the children of Simeon, Shemuel the son of Ammihud.

Arminian

For Simeon, Shemuel son of Ammihud. He is not mentioned elsewhere.

Arminian readers note that the new generation’s leaders, though otherwise unknown, take up real responsibility in God’s work. Arminian readers see God entrusting his work to ordinary people who answer his call.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God uses unknown servants as well as famous ones. Each leader’s name is preserved in his word.

Both traditions take encouragement that faithful service matters even when it leaves little trace in history.

Numbers 34:21

21Of the tribe of Benjamin, Elidad the son of Chislon.

Arminian

For Benjamin, Elidad son of Chislon. He too is otherwise unknown.

Arminian readers note that every tribe, large or small, participates in receiving God’s gift. Benjamin, the tribe of Jacob’s youngest son, receives a leader with the same standing as Judah’s.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God’s order includes every tribe and leader. Benjamin’s place is secure in the distribution.

Both traditions see God’s care for each part of his people, a theme Christians apply to the many members of the church (compare 1 Corinthians 12:12–27).

Numbers 34:22

22Of the tribe of the children of Dan a prince, Bukki the son of Jogli.

Arminian

For Dan, Bukki son of Jogli. The name is not mentioned elsewhere.

Arminian readers note that Dan’s later struggle to possess its land (Judges 18) shows that receiving an allotment did not guarantee faithful settlement.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God assigned Dan its place, even though the tribe later moved. His gift was real, whatever the tribe’s response.

Both traditions draw a lesson about the need to take hold of what God gives.

Numbers 34:23

23Of the children of Joseph: of the tribe of the children of Manasseh a prince, Hanniel the son of Ephod.

Arminian

For Manasseh, Hanniel son of Ephod. The half-tribe west of the Jordan is represented.

Arminian readers note that Manasseh’s division across the Jordan shows God accommodating different needs among his people. Arminian readers see flexibility within God’s purposes for the needs of his people.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Joseph’s double portion through Manasseh and Ephraim reflects Jacob’s blessing (Genesis 48:5). God’s word to Jacob is honored generations later.

Both traditions see God’s promises to the patriarchs worked out in the distribution of the land.

Numbers 34:24

24Of the tribe of the children of Ephraim a prince, Kemuel the son of Shiphtan.

Arminian

For Ephraim, Kemuel son of Shiphtan. Ephraim would become a leading tribe in the north.

Arminian readers note that Ephraim’s later prominence carried both privilege and responsibility, including the danger of pride. Arminian readers note that Hosea later addresses Ephraim for its waywardness.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Jacob’s blessing gave Ephraim precedence over Manasseh (Genesis 48:19–20), and that God’s choice does not follow birth order.

Both traditions read the name as part of God’s unfolding plan for his people.

Numbers 34:25

25Of the tribe of the children of Zebulun a prince, Elizaphan the son of Parnach.

Arminian

For Zebulun, Elizaphan son of Parnach. The name is otherwise unknown as a leader of Zebulun.

Arminian readers note that each leader represents a family whose faithfulness in the land would matter for generations.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God remembered Zebulun, later named in Isaiah 9:1–2 among the places that would see a great light. His purposes for this tribe reached into the gospel age.

Both traditions see God’s purposes reaching far beyond the immediate distribution.

Numbers 34:26

26Of the tribe of the children of Issachar a prince, Paltiel the son of Azzan.

Arminian

For Issachar, Paltiel son of Azzan. The name appears only here.

Arminian readers note that the leaders of the new generation stand where their fathers failed, called to trust God in receiving the land.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God raised up a new generation of leaders, showing that his purposes do not depend on any one generation. Each generation must receive God’s promises afresh.

Both traditions draw hope from God’s faithfulness across generations.

Numbers 34:27

27Of the tribe of the children of Asher a prince, Ahihud the son of Shelomi.

Arminian

For Asher, Ahihud son of Shelomi. The name appears only here.

Arminian readers note that even tribes on the margins of later history were full participants in the inheritance. No tribe was too small or too distant to share in God’s gift.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God’s order includes every tribe. Asher’s place in the land was given by God, not won by its own strength.

Both traditions see the completeness of God’s provision for all his people.

Numbers 34:28

28Of the tribe of the children of Naphtali a prince, Pedahel the son of Ammihud.”

Arminian

For Naphtali, Pedahel son of Ammihud. The list of leaders is complete.

Arminian readers note that the list ends with every tribe represented and ready to receive the land. The completed list shows a people fully prepared to receive what God had promised.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Naphtali, like Zebulun, is later named in the promise of Isaiah 9:1–2, fulfilled in Jesus’ ministry in Galilee (Matthew 4:13–16).

Both traditions treat such canonical links with care, seeing God’s purposes for the tribes reaching into the gospel.

Numbers 34:29

29These are they whom the LORD commanded to divide the inheritance to the children of Israel in the land of Canaan.

Arminian

These are the men the LORD commanded to divide the inheritance in Canaan. The chapter closes with a summary.

Arminian readers note that faithful leadership in distributing the land would require integrity and fairness, qualities God expects of all who hold responsibility.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the inheritance is distributed at God’s command. The whole process rests on his authority and promise.

Both traditions read the verse as completing God’s preparation for Israel’s life in the land, the fulfillment of his word to Abraham.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
