Passage

Numbers 3

Verses 14–39 · WEB

Numbers 3:14–39 records the Levite census, counting every male a month old and up rather than men of fighting age (compare 1:3, 47–49). For each clan (Gershon, Kohath, and Merari) it gives the families, the total, where they camp around the tabernacle, their leader, and their duties. Moses, Aaron, and the priests camp on the east, at the entrance. Both traditions read the passage as God’s careful ordering of his dwelling among his people, with every family given a place and a task.

Numbers 3:14

14The LORD spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, saying,

Arminian

The Levite census is commanded in the wilderness of Sinai. Arminians note that God numbers those he calls, showing personal knowledge and care.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the census reflects God’s ordering of his people. Both traditions agree on the verse. The setting at Sinai ties the census to the covenant just given.

Numbers 3:15

15“Count the children of Levi by their fathers’ houses, by their families. You shall count every male from a month old and upward.”

Arminian

Every male a month old and up is counted, unlike the military census of men twenty and up. Arminians see that the Levites are counted for belonging to God, not for fighting strength.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same contrast and connect the one-month threshold with the redemption of firstborn (18:16). Both traditions agree that the census counts those claimed by God.

Numbers 3:16

16Moses counted them according to the LORD’s word, as he was commanded.

Arminian

Moses counted ‘according to the LORD’s word’. Arminians see Moses as a model of responsive obedience. His obedience matches the pattern of Exodus 39–40, where the tabernacle is built ‘as the LORD commanded’.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters likewise highlight the precise obedience. Both traditions note that the phrase recurs throughout this section and agree on its meaning.

Numbers 3:17

17These were the sons of Levi by their names: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.

Arminian

Gershon, Kohath, and Merari are Levi’s sons (Genesis 46:11; Exodus 6:16). Arminians see God keeping track of each branch of the family he calls.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the continuity with Genesis and Exodus genealogies. Both traditions read the list as rooting tabernacle service in the history of God’s covenant family.

Numbers 3:18

18These are the names of the sons of Gershon by their families: Libni and Shimei.

Arminian

Gershon’s sons are Libni and Shimei. Arminians note that even lesser-known names are recorded, which reflects God’s attention to every family.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the names record real households in Israel’s history. Both traditions read the verse as genealogy and agree on it.

Numbers 3:19

19The sons of Kohath by their families: Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel.

Arminian

Kohath’s sons are Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel. Arminians note that Amram is the father of Moses and Aaron (Exodus 6:20), so the leaders arise from within this clan.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same connection. Both traditions see the verse as setting up the Kohathites’ special duties with the most holy things and agree on its meaning.

Numbers 3:20

20The sons of Merari by their families: Mahli and Mushi. These are the families of the Levites according to their fathers’ houses.

Arminian

Merari’s sons are Mahli and Mushi. Arminians see God’s order extending to every family in the tribe. Even the smallest clan, with only two families, receives a named place in the record.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and note the closing summary of the families by fathers’ houses. Both traditions read the verse as genealogy and agree on it.

Numbers 3:21

21Of Gershon was the family of the Libnites, and the family of the Shimeites. These are the families of the Gershonites.

Arminian

The Libnites and Shimeites form the Gershonite families. Arminians note that each family has an identity within the larger tribe.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree on the structure of clan and family. Both traditions see the verse as part of the orderly record and agree on it.

Numbers 3:22

22Those who were counted of them, according to the number of all the males from a month old and upward, even those who were counted of them were seven thousand five hundred.

Arminian

The Gershonites number 7,500. Arminians see the large number as evidence of God’s blessing on the tribe he called to serve.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note that this total contributes to the Levite sum in verse 39 and agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:23

23The families of the Gershonites shall encamp behind the tabernacle westward.

Arminian

The Gershonites camp behind the tabernacle, to the west. Arminians see God placing his servants around his dwelling, with each family given a home in relation to his presence.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the Levites form an inner ring between the tabernacle and the other tribes (1:53). Both traditions agree that the arrangement guards holiness and centers the camp on God’s presence.

Numbers 3:24

24Eliasaph the son of Lael shall be the prince of the fathers’ house of the Gershonites.

Arminian

Eliasaph son of Lael leads the Gershonites. Arminians note that God provides leaders for each group so that service is orderly.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree on the value of appointed leadership. Both traditions read the verse as part of the record and agree on it.

Numbers 3:25

25The duty of the sons of Gershon in the Tent of Meeting shall be the tabernacle, the tent, its covering, the screen for the door of the Tent of Meeting,

Arminian

The Gershonites are responsible for the tabernacle, tent, coverings, and entrance screen. Arminians see that caring for fabrics and coverings is real service to God, no less valued than more visible tasks.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that every task assigned by God is honorable. Both traditions connect this with the varied gifts of the one body (1 Corinthians 12:14–25).

Numbers 3:26

26the hangings of the court, the screen for the door of the court which is by the tabernacle and around the altar, and its cords for all of its service.

Arminian

They also handle the court hangings, the court gate screen, and the cords. Arminians note that even the cords are named, which shows God’s care for the smallest parts of his service.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree on this attention to detail. Both traditions read the verse as completing the Gershonite duty list and agree on it.

Numbers 3:27

27Of Kohath was the family of the Amramites, the family of the Izharites, the family of the Hebronites, and the family of the Uzzielites. These are the families of the Kohathites.

Arminian

The Kohathite families are the Amramites, Izharites, Hebronites, and Uzzielites. Arminians note that the clan of Moses and Aaron receives no exemption from orderly service.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree on the structure and note the special responsibility that follows. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:28

28According to the number of all the males from a month old and upward, there were eight thousand six hundred keeping the requirements of the sanctuary.

Arminian

The Kohathites number 8,600, keeping the requirements of the sanctuary. Arminians note the figure as given. Some scholars propose that it was originally 8,300, which would make the clan totals match the 22,000 of verse 39, but this is a conjecture.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters note the same proposal and the WEB footnote at verse 39 about rounding. Both traditions treat the numerical question as a textual matter that does not affect the passage’s teaching.

Numbers 3:29

29The families of the sons of Kohath shall encamp on the south side of the tabernacle.

Arminian

The Kohathites camp on the south side. Arminians see the same pattern of each family having its God-given place. The south side places the Kohathites next to the tribes of Reuben, Simeon, and Gad (2:10–16).

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the camp layout as an ordered picture of a people gathered around God’s presence and agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:30

30The prince of the fathers’ house of the families of the Kohathites shall be Elizaphan the son of Uzziel.

Arminian

Elizaphan son of Uzziel leads the Kohathites. Arminians note that God raises leaders from various branches of a clan. He is probably the Elzaphan son of Uzziel who, with Mishael, carried out the bodies of Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10:4).

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note that Elizaphan’s family appears again in later lists of Levite leaders and agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:31

31Their duty shall be the ark, the table, the lamp stand, the altars, the vessels of the sanctuary with which they minister, the screen, and all its service.

Arminian

The Kohathites care for the ark, table, lampstand, altars, vessels, and screen. Arminians see the honor of handling the most holy things, along with the responsibility it brings.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and note that chapter 4 details how carefully these must be covered before carrying. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:32

32Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest shall be prince of the princes of the Levites, with the oversight of those who keep the requirements of the sanctuary.

Arminian

Eleazar son of Aaron is chief over the Levite leaders and oversees the sanctuary keepers. Arminians see accountable oversight as a gift to God’s people.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters note the ordered layers of responsibility under the priesthood. Both traditions read the verse as establishing supervision and agree on it.

Numbers 3:33

33Of Merari was the family of the Mahlites and the family of the Mushites. These are the families of Merari.

Arminian

The Mahlites and Mushites form the families of Merari. Arminians see God’s order reaching every family. Merari’s two families take responsibility for the tabernacle’s structural frame.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as part of the genealogical record and agree on it. Their listing completes the threefold division of Levi that shapes the rest of chapters 3–4.

Numbers 3:34

34Those who were counted of them, according to the number of all the males from a month old and upward, were six thousand two hundred.

Arminian

The Merarites number 6,200. Arminians note this as another evidence of God’s blessing on Levi’s families. Merari is the smallest of the three clans counted here.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note this total in relation to verse 39 and agree on the verse. The figure is given without comment, as is typical of census records in Numbers.

Numbers 3:35

35The prince of the fathers’ house of the families of Merari was Zuriel the son of Abihail. They shall encamp on the north side of the tabernacle.

Arminian

Zuriel son of Abihail leads Merari, and they camp on the north side. Arminians see each clan given leadership and a place.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as completing the circle of Levite camps around the tabernacle and agree on it.

Numbers 3:36

36The appointed duty of the sons of Merari shall be the tabernacle’s boards, its bars, its pillars, its sockets, all its instruments, all its service,

Arminian

The Merarites carry the boards, bars, pillars, sockets, and instruments. Arminians see that heavy, structural work is as much a part of worship as handling sacred vessels.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that God honors every kind of labor done in his service. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:37

37the pillars of the court around it, their sockets, their pins, and their cords.

Arminian

They also carry the court pillars, sockets, pins, and cords. Arminians note again that no item is too small to be named in God’s service.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as completing the Merarite duty list and agree on it. The pins and cords held the court together, so the Merarites’ work kept the whole structure standing.

Numbers 3:38

38Those who encamp before the tabernacle eastward, in front of the Tent of Meeting toward the sunrise, shall be Moses, with Aaron and his sons, keeping the requirements of the sanctuary for the duty of the children of Israel. The outsider who comes near shall be put to death.

Arminian

Moses, Aaron, and his sons camp on the east, before the entrance, guarding the sanctuary, and the outsider who comes near is to die. Arminians see the leaders positioned to protect access for the people’s good.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters note the strategic eastern position at the only entrance. Both traditions read the warning as showing God’s holiness and see open access fulfilled in Christ’s priesthood (Hebrews 4:14–16).

Numbers 3:39

39All who were counted of the Levites, whom Moses and Aaron counted at the commandment of the LORD, by their families, all the males from a month old and upward, were twenty-two thousand.

Arminian

The Levites total 22,000. Arminians note the WEB footnote: the clan figures add to 22,300, and the Hebrew text gives the rounded figure.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same footnote and the proposals for explaining the difference, including rounding and a possible copying change in verse 28. Both traditions agree that the difference is a textual matter, not a doctrinal one.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
