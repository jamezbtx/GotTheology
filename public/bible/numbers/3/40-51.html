Passage

Numbers 3

Verses 40–51 · WEB

Numbers 3:40–51 carries out the substitution announced in 3:12–13. Israel’s firstborn males are counted (22,273), the Levites are taken in their place, and the Levites’ livestock stands in for Israel’s firstborn livestock. The 273 firstborn who outnumber the Levites are redeemed at five shekels each, and the money goes to Aaron and his sons. Arminian and Reformed readers agree on what the passage depicts: redemption by substitution and payment, a pattern the New Testament fulfills in Christ (1 Peter 1:18–19).

Numbers 3:40

40The LORD said to Moses, “Count all the firstborn males of the children of Israel from a month old and upward, and take the number of their names.

Arminian

Israel’s firstborn males a month old and up are counted. Arminians see God’s attention to each one he claimed at Passover.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the count prepares for the exchange with the Levites. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:41

41You shall take the Levites for me—I am the LORD—instead of all the firstborn among the children of Israel; and the livestock of the Levites instead of all the firstborn among the livestock of the children of Israel.”

Arminian

The Levites, and their livestock, are taken instead of Israel’s firstborn, and God states, ‘I am the LORD.’ Arminians see God’s authority and grace in arranging the exchange.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and note the substitution extends even to livestock. Both traditions read the verse as one of the Old Testament’s clear patterns of substitution.

Numbers 3:42

42Moses counted, as the LORD commanded him, all the firstborn among the children of Israel.

Arminian

Moses counts as commanded. Arminians see another instance of faithful obedience. The count is the first step in carrying out the exchange God described in verses 12–13.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note the refrain of obedience and agree on the verse. The verse repeats the pattern of command and fulfillment that runs through Numbers 1–4.

Numbers 3:43

43All the firstborn males according to the number of names from a month old and upward, of those who were counted of them, were twenty-two thousand two hundred seventy-three.

Arminian

The firstborn males number 22,273. Arminians note the relatively low number for a large population, which interpreters have explained in various ways.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same question. Both traditions recognize that explanations differ, including counting only firstborn born after Exodus or other proposals, and treat the matter cautiously.

Numbers 3:44

44The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

A divine speech introduces the exchange. Arminians see God personally directing the act of redemption. The repeated speech formula marks the exchange as resting on God’s word alone.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions agree on the function of the verse. Verse 45 restates the substitution of verse 41 before the payment details follow.

Numbers 3:45

45“Take the Levites instead of all the firstborn among the children of Israel, and the livestock of the Levites instead of their livestock; and the Levites shall be mine. I am the LORD.

Arminian

‘The Levites shall be mine. I am the LORD.’ Arminians see God’s claim on his servants as a privilege and a calling to dedicated life.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that God’s claim rests on his sovereign right and redemptive act. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:46

46For the redemption of the two hundred seventy-three of the firstborn of the children of Israel who exceed the number of the Levites,

Arminian

The 273 firstborn exceeding the Levites must be redeemed. Arminians see that no firstborn is left unaccounted for, which shows God’s care for each person.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and see that redemption is required where no substitute is available. Both traditions read the verse as a picture of redemption and agree on it.

Numbers 3:47

47you shall take five shekels apiece for each one; according to the shekel of the sanctuary you shall take them (the shekel is twenty gerahs);

Arminian

Five shekels each, by the sanctuary shekel of twenty gerahs (compare 18:16). Arminians see a fixed, fair price set by God.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same price and the WEB footnotes on shekel and gerah weights. Both traditions see the payment pointing to redemption at a price, fulfilled in Christ’s precious blood (1 Peter 1:18–19).

Numbers 3:48

48and you shall give the money, with which their remainder is redeemed, to Aaron and to his sons.”

Arminian

The redemption money goes to Aaron and his sons. Arminians note that it supports the priestly ministry. Redemption money thus becomes part of God’s provision for those who serve at his sanctuary.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as connecting redemption with support of God’s service and agree on it.

Numbers 3:49

49Moses took the redemption money from those who exceeded the number of those who were redeemed by the Levites;

Arminian

Moses collects the redemption money from those exceeding the Levites. Arminians see the instructions carried out precisely.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The verse shows the instruction of verses 46–48 carried out exactly as given.

Numbers 3:50

50from the firstborn of the children of Israel he took the money, one thousand three hundred sixty-five shekels, according to the shekel of the sanctuary;

Arminian

The total is 1,365 shekels (273 × 5), about 13.65 kilograms according to the WEB footnote. Arminians note that the arithmetic is exact.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions see the precision as part of the record of faithful obedience and agree on the verse.

Numbers 3:51

51and Moses gave the redemption money to Aaron and to his sons, according to the LORD’s word, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

Moses gives the money to Aaron and his sons ‘according to the LORD’s word’. Arminians see faithful obedience completing God’s arrangement.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and see the closing refrain as stressing that the whole exchange followed God’s command. Both traditions agree on the verse.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
