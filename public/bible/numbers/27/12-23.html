Passage

Numbers 27

Verses 12–23 · WEB

Numbers 27:12–23 tells Moses to view the promised land from the Abarim mountains, because he will die outside it for his failure at Meribah (20:1–13). Moses responds not with complaint but with a prayer that the LORD appoint a shepherd for his people. God names Joshua, ‘a man in whom is the Spirit’, and Moses lays his hand on him and commissions him before Eleazar and the congregation. Both traditions see here faithful succession under God’s choice and a leader pointing ahead to the Good Shepherd.

Numbers 27:12

12The LORD said to Moses, “Go up into this mountain of Abarim, and see the land which I have given to the children of Israel.

Arminian

Moses is told to climb the Abarim mountains and see the land God has given Israel. Arminians see grace even in judgment: Moses is allowed to see the promise fulfilled for his people.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and note that Deuteronomy 32:49 and 34:1–4 name Mount Nebo in the Abarim range. Both traditions see God’s kindness in showing Moses the land and his faithfulness to the promise.

Numbers 27:13

13When you have seen it, you also shall be gathered to your people, as Aaron your brother was gathered;

Arminian

Moses will be ‘gathered to your people’, as Aaron was (20:22–29). Arminians see the phrase as a gentle way of describing death that points to continued existence among God’s people.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that the phrase is more than burial, since Moses is not buried with his ancestors. Both traditions see it as consistent with hope beyond death, while recognizing that the Old Testament reveals that hope gradually.

Numbers 27:14

14because in the strife of the congregation, you rebelled against my word in the wilderness of Zin, to honor me as holy at the waters before their eyes.” (These are the waters of Meribah of Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin.)

Arminian

Moses rebelled at Meribah by not honoring God as holy before the people (20:1–13). Arminians note that even a great leader is held accountable and that faithfulness over many years does not excuse a public failure.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree on the seriousness of the sin and note that God still treats Moses with mercy and honor. Both traditions agree that the verse teaches the holiness God requires of leaders.

Numbers 27:15

15Moses spoke to the LORD, saying,

Arminian

Moses speaks to the LORD. Arminians note that Moses responds to the news of his death with prayer, not bitterness. His first concern is for the people, not for himself.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and see Moses’ selflessness. Both traditions read the verse as introducing Moses’ intercession for the people and agree on its meaning.

Numbers 27:16

16“Let the LORD, the God of the spirits of all flesh, appoint a man over the congregation,

Arminian

Moses calls on ‘the God of the spirits of all flesh’ (compare 16:22) to appoint a leader. Arminians see Moses trusting God, who knows every heart, to choose rightly.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the title stresses God’s knowledge of and authority over every person. Both traditions agree that the choice of leader belongs to God.

Numbers 27:17

17who may go out before them, and who may come in before them, and who may lead them out, and who may bring them in, that the congregation of the LORD may not be as sheep which have no shepherd.”

Arminian

A leader is needed so that the people are not ‘as sheep which have no shepherd’. Arminians see Moses’ pastoral heart and note that Jesus uses the same image (Matthew 9:36).

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and see Christ as the Good Shepherd who fulfills this need (John 10:11). Both traditions read the verse as showing that God’s people need godly shepherds.

Numbers 27:18

18The LORD said to Moses, “Take Joshua the son of Nun, a man in whom is the Spirit, and lay your hand on him.

Arminian

God chooses Joshua, ‘a man in whom is the Spirit’, and tells Moses to lay his hand on him. Arminians see God equipping those he calls, with the Spirit as the source of fitness for leadership.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that God’s choice and the Spirit’s presence qualify Joshua. Both traditions note Deuteronomy 34:9 and the later practice of laying on hands in commissioning (Acts 6:6; 1 Timothy 4:14).

Numbers 27:19

19Set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation; and commission him in their sight.

Arminian

Joshua is set before Eleazar and the congregation and commissioned publicly. Arminians see the public commissioning as giving the people confidence in God’s chosen leader.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that public recognition confirms the appointment. Both traditions see a pattern for orderly, visible transitions of leadership.

Numbers 27:20

20You shall give authority to him, that all the congregation of the children of Israel may obey.

Arminian

Moses is to give Joshua authority so that the people will obey (the Hebrew may be read as ‘some of’ Moses’ authority). Arminians see authority as entrusted by God for the people’s good.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and note that the Hebrew can be read as ‘some of’ Moses’ authority, which would suggest Joshua does not fully replace Moses’ unique role. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 27:21

21He shall stand before Eleazar the priest, who shall inquire for him by the judgment of the Urim before the LORD. At his word they shall go out, and at his word they shall come in, both he and all the children of Israel with him, even all the congregation.”

Arminian

Joshua will stand before Eleazar, who will inquire for him by the Urim (Exodus 28:30). Arminians see leadership accountable to God’s guidance, sought through the means he appointed.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that civil leadership operates under God’s word through the priesthood. Both traditions note that the exact working of the Urim is not described in Scripture.

Numbers 27:22

22Moses did as the LORD commanded him. He took Joshua, and set him before Eleazar the priest and before all the congregation.

Arminian

Moses does as commanded and sets Joshua before Eleazar and the congregation. Arminians see Moses’ humble obedience in handing over leadership.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that Moses obeys without hesitation. Both traditions see Moses’ faithfulness to the end of his ministry.

Numbers 27:23

23He laid his hands on him and commissioned him, as the LORD spoke by Moses.

Arminian

Moses lays his hands on Joshua and commissions him. Arminians see a peaceful, God-directed succession that prepares Israel to enter the land.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions note that Joshua, whose name is the Hebrew form of ‘Jesus’, leads Israel into the land, while Hebrews 4:8 shows that the final rest comes through Christ.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
