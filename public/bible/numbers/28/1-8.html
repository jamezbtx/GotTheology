Passage

Numbers 28

Verses 1–8 · WEB

Numbers 28:1–8 begins the calendar of public offerings (chapters 28–29) for the generation about to enter the land. It opens with the foundation of the whole system: the continual burnt offering of a lamb each morning and evening, with its grain and drink offerings (compare Exodus 29:38–42). Arminian and Reformed readers agree that the daily offering expresses Israel’s continual dedication to God and his continual provision for approach, fulfilled in Christ’s once-for-all sacrifice (Hebrews 10:10–14).

Numbers 28:1

1The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses again. Arminians note that the instructions for worship in the land come from God, not human preference.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that worship is regulated by God’s command. Both traditions see the verse as introducing the calendar of offerings for the new generation.

Numbers 28:2

2“Command the children of Israel, and tell them, ‘See that you present my offering, my food for my offerings made by fire, as a pleasant aroma to me, in their due season.’

Arminian

Israel must present God’s offering, ‘my food’, at the proper times. Arminians see that God calls his people to faithful, regular worship as an expression of love.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and note that ‘food’ is figurative, since God has no need of food (Psalm 50:12–13). Both traditions read the verse as stressing faithful worship ‘in their due season’.

Numbers 28:3

3You shall tell them, ‘This is the offering made by fire which you shall offer to the LORD: male lambs a year old without defect, two day by day, for a continual burnt offering.

Arminian

Two year-old lambs without defect each day form the continual burnt offering. Arminians see daily dedication of the whole community to God.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and connect the spotless lambs with Christ, the Lamb without blemish (1 Peter 1:19). Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 28:4

4You shall offer the one lamb in the morning, and you shall offer the other lamb at evening,

Arminian

One lamb in the morning and one in the evening. Arminians see worship framing each day, from beginning to end. The pattern became the backbone of Israel’s daily temple worship.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions often connect the morning and evening offerings with daily prayer (Psalm 141:2) and agree on the verse.

Numbers 28:5

5with one tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a meal offering, mixed with the fourth part of a hin of beaten oil.

Arminian

A tenth of an ephah of flour with a quarter hin of oil (about 22 liters per ephah and 6.5 liters per hin, per the WEB footnotes). Arminians see the people’s daily bread offered back to God in gratitude.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the grain offering acknowledges God as provider. Both traditions agree on the verse. The flour and oil were common staples, so the offering drew on Israel’s ordinary food.

Numbers 28:6

6It is a continual burnt offering which was ordained in Mount Sinai for a pleasant aroma, an offering made by fire to the LORD.

Arminian

This continual offering was ordained at Sinai (Exodus 29:38–42). Arminians see continuity: the worship begun at Sinai is to continue in the land.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and stress that the new generation inherits the covenant worship of their parents. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 28:7

7Its drink offering shall be the fourth part of a hin for each lamb. You shall pour out a drink offering of strong drink to the LORD in the holy place.

Arminian

A quarter hin of strong drink is poured out for each lamb in the holy place. Arminians see the drink offering as an act of joyful dedication.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note that Paul uses the image of being ‘poured out’ like a drink offering for his own self-giving (Philippians 2:17; 2 Timothy 4:6).

Numbers 28:8

8The other lamb you shall offer at evening. As the meal offering of the morning, and as its drink offering, you shall offer it, an offering made by fire, for a pleasant aroma to the LORD.

Arminian

The evening lamb is offered like the morning one. Arminians see consistency and faithfulness in worship throughout the day.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as completing the daily pattern and agree on its meaning. The repetition of verse 4 frames the paragraph and underlines the twice-daily pattern.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
