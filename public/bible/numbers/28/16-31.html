Passage

Numbers 28

Verses 16–31 · WEB

Numbers 28:16–31 gives the offerings for Passover, the seven-day Feast of Unleavened Bread, and the Feast of Weeks (the day of firstfruits). Each festival has holy convocations with no regular work and the same set of burnt offerings, grain offerings, and a goat ‘to make atonement for you’, all added to the continual offering. Arminian and Reformed readers agree that these feasts recall God’s redemption and provision, and the New Testament links them to Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7) and to Pentecost (Acts 2:1).

Numbers 28:16

16“ ‘In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month, is the LORD’s Passover.

Arminian

Passover falls on the fourteenth day of the first month. Arminians see the feast as a yearly memorial of God’s redemption from Egypt.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and connect it with Christ our Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7). Both traditions agree on the verse. The date matches the original Passover in Exodus 12:6 and Leviticus 23:5.

Numbers 28:17

17On the fifteenth day of this month shall be a feast. Unleavened bread shall be eaten for seven days.

Arminian

A seven-day feast of unleavened bread begins on the fifteenth. Arminians see a call to holiness following redemption, since Paul applies the unleavened bread to sincerity and truth (1 Corinthians 5:8).

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree on the link between redemption and holy living. Both traditions agree on the verse. The feast follows Passover immediately, as in Exodus 12:15–20 and Leviticus 23:6.

Numbers 28:18

18In the first day shall be a holy convocation. You shall do no regular work,

Arminian

The first day is a holy convocation with no regular work. Arminians see rest and gathering as part of God’s gift to his people.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as setting the festival apart for worship and agree on it. The ban on regular work marks the day as belonging to the LORD.

Numbers 28:19

19but you shall offer an offering made by fire, a burnt offering to the LORD: two young bulls, one ram, and seven male lambs a year old. They shall be without defect,

Arminian

Two bulls, a ram, and seven lambs without defect are offered. Arminians see the same generous offering as at the new moon, showing the festival’s importance.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the offering matches the monthly pattern. Both traditions agree on the verse. The requirement that the animals be without defect repeats a standard found throughout the sacrificial law.

Numbers 28:20

20with their meal offering, fine flour mixed with oil. You shall offer three tenths for a bull, and two tenths for the ram.

Arminian

Three tenths of flour for each bull and two tenths for the ram. Arminians see the same careful proportions applied consistently.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. The grain proportions match those given for the new moon in verses 12–13.

Numbers 28:21

21You shall offer one tenth for every lamb of the seven lambs;

Arminian

One tenth for each of the seven lambs. Arminians see God’s attention to every part of the offering. The tenth for each lamb matches the daily offering in verse 5.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. The verse completes the grain offering that goes with the festival burnt offering.

Numbers 28:22

22and one male goat for a sin offering, to make atonement for you.

Arminian

A male goat is offered as a sin offering ‘to make atonement for you’. Arminians see that even festal joy includes humble recognition of sin and God’s provision for cleansing.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and see the atonement language as pointing to Christ’s sacrifice. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 28:23

23You shall offer these in addition to the burnt offering of the morning, which is for a continual burnt offering.

Arminian

These are offered in addition to the morning continual offering. Arminians see festival worship building on the daily foundation.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. The festival offerings never replace the continual offering, which remains the foundation of Israel’s worship.

Numbers 28:24

24In this way you shall offer daily, for seven days, the food of the offering made by fire, of a pleasant aroma to the LORD. It shall be offered in addition to the continual burnt offering and its drink offering.

Arminian

The same offerings are made daily for seven days. Arminians see sustained devotion throughout the feast. The repetition over seven days gives the whole week a festive and holy character.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions note the repeated phrase ‘pleasant aroma to the LORD’ and agree on the verse. The verse stresses that the daily continual offering still goes on underneath the festival offerings.

Numbers 28:25

25On the seventh day you shall have a holy convocation. You shall do no regular work.

Arminian

The seventh day is a holy convocation with no regular work. Arminians see the feast framed by rest and worship at both ends.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as closing the festival and agree on it. Holy convocations on both the first and last days frame the week, as in Leviticus 23:7–8.

Numbers 28:26

26“ ‘Also in the day of the first fruits, when you offer a new meal offering to the LORD in your feast of weeks, you shall have a holy convocation. You shall do no regular work;

Arminian

On the day of firstfruits, at the Feast of Weeks, there is a holy convocation and no regular work. Arminians see a celebration of God’s provision at harvest.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and note that this feast became known as Pentecost, when the Spirit was poured out (Acts 2:1). Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 28:27

27but you shall offer a burnt offering for a pleasant aroma to the LORD: two young bulls, one ram, seven male lambs a year old;

Arminian

Two bulls, a ram, and seven lambs are offered as a pleasant aroma. Arminians see generous worship in response to God’s bounty.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note that Leviticus 23:18–20 lists additional offerings for this feast and read the two lists as complementary.

Numbers 28:28

28and their meal offering, fine flour mixed with oil, three tenths for each bull, two tenths for the one ram,

Arminian

Three tenths of flour for each bull and two tenths for the ram. Arminians see the consistent proportions again. The Feast of Weeks uses the same grain proportions as Passover week.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. The repetition of the same pattern across festivals shows the calendar’s unity.

Numbers 28:29

29one tenth for every lamb of the seven lambs;

Arminian

One tenth for each of the seven lambs. Arminians see the careful completeness of the offering. The tenth for each lamb matches the daily and festival offerings elsewhere in the chapter.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. The verse completes the grain offering that goes with this festival’s burnt offering.

Numbers 28:30

30and one male goat, to make atonement for you.

Arminian

A male goat is offered ‘to make atonement for you’. Arminians see that thanksgiving at harvest is joined with confession and cleansing.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and see the pattern pointing to Christ’s atonement. Both traditions agree on the verse. The goat repeats the sin offering of verse 22, so every festival includes atonement.

Numbers 28:31

31Besides the continual burnt offering and its meal offering, you shall offer them and their drink offerings. See that they are without defect.

Arminian

These offerings are added to the continual offering and must be without defect. Arminians see that God deserves the best in every act of worship.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and connect the requirement with Christ, the spotless offering (Hebrews 9:14). Both traditions agree on the verse.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
