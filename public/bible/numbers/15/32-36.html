Passage

Numbers 15

Verses 32–36 · WEB

A brief narrative follows the law about high-handed sin: a man is found gathering sticks on the Sabbath, held until the LORD gives a ruling, and executed by the congregation outside the camp. The episode is best read soberly within Israel’s covenant setting, where Sabbath was a sign of the covenant (Exodus 31:13–17). The text does not state his motives, and both traditions avoid speculating beyond it.

Numbers 15:32

32While the children of Israel were in the wilderness, they found a man gathering sticks on the Sabbath day.

Arminian

A man is found gathering sticks on the Sabbath. Arminian readers see this narrative illustrating the preceding law. The text does not describe his motives in detail.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see a case placed right after the law on high-handed sin. Both traditions avoid speculation about his reasons. The Sabbath was a covenant sign, as Exodus 31:13–17 says.

Numbers 15:33

33Those who found him gathering sticks brought him to Moses and Aaron, and to all the congregation.

Arminian

He is brought to Moses, Aaron, and the congregation. Arminian readers see due process in the community. The case is handled publicly, not privately. No one acts alone.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see orderly procedure under God’s appointed leaders. Both traditions read the verse as shared ground. The case is brought before Moses, Aaron, and the whole congregation, not settled by private vengeance.

Numbers 15:34

34They put him in custody, because it had not been declared what should be done to him.

Arminian

He is held in custody because it was not yet declared what to do. Arminian readers see leaders waiting for God’s direction. They do not act on their own judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see dependence on revelation. Exodus 31:14 had already set a penalty, so interpreters discuss what was uncertain. Both traditions hedge on that point.

Numbers 15:35

35The LORD said to Moses, “The man shall surely be put to death. All the congregation shall stone him with stones outside of the camp.”

Arminian

The LORD declares the man must die by stoning outside the camp. Arminian readers see the gravity of breaking the covenant sign. The sentence comes from God, not human anger.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s holiness upheld in Israel’s covenant setting. Christians in both traditions read this as part of Israel’s civil law, not a penalty for today.

Numbers 15:36

36All the congregation brought him outside of the camp, and stoned him to death with stones, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

The congregation carries out the sentence as the LORD commanded. Arminian readers see sober obedience to a hard command. The narrative records it without elaboration.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the community acting under God’s direction. Both traditions read the episode soberly within its covenant context. The narrative records obedience without commentary, leaving readers to weigh the seriousness of the covenant sign.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
