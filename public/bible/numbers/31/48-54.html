Passage

Numbers 31

Verses 48–54 · WEB

The officers report that not one soldier is missing and bring the gold jewelry they had taken as an offering to the LORD, “to make atonement for our souls.” Moses and Eleazar receive the gold, which weighs 16,750 shekels (about 167.5 kilograms, according to the WEB footnote), and place it in the Tent of Meeting as a memorial for Israel before the LORD.

Numbers 31:48

48The officers who were over the thousands of the army, the captains of thousands, and the captains of hundreds, came near to Moses.

Arminian

The officers over thousands and hundreds come near to Moses. Arminian readers note that the initiative comes from the officers themselves, after Moses’ earlier anger with them (31:14). Both traditions agree that this begins a voluntary act of devotion.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the same officers rebuked in 31:14 now approach with an offering. Shared ground: the scene moves from rebuke to worship, showing leaders responding to God’s preservation with gratitude.

Numbers 31:49

49They said to Moses, “Your servants have taken the sum of the men of war who are under our command, and there lacks not one man of us.

Arminian

The officers report that not one soldier is missing. Arminian readers see God’s protection of his people in battle. Both traditions agree that the report moves the officers to thankfulness rather than pride.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the preservation of every soldier as a sign of God’s providential care. Shared ground: the officers attribute their safety to God. Their count echoes the census theme that runs through Numbers, now ending in thanksgiving rather than loss.

Numbers 31:50

50We have brought the LORD’s offering, what every man found: gold ornaments, armlets, bracelets, signet rings, earrings, and necklaces, to make atonement for our souls before the LORD.”

Arminian

The officers bring gold jewelry as the LORD’s offering, “to make atonement for our souls.” Arminian readers see humble recognition of their need before God. Both traditions note a possible connection with the ransom for those counted in a census (Exodus 30:12), though the text does not say so explicitly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the officers seeking atonement after numbering their men and handling the spoil of war. Shared ground: the verse expresses awareness that even victory leaves a need to approach God humbly, and the gift is offered voluntarily.

Numbers 31:51

51Moses and Eleazar the priest took their gold, even all worked jewels.

Arminian

Moses and Eleazar receive the gold and worked jewels. Arminian readers note that civil and priestly leaders accept the offering together. Both traditions agree that receiving it confirms its acceptance on God’s behalf.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the joint role of Moses and Eleazar, reflecting the order God established. Shared ground: the offering is received by those appointed to do so, not kept by the officers.

Numbers 31:52

52All the gold of the wave offering that they offered up to the LORD, of the captains of thousands, and of the captains of hundreds, was sixteen thousand seven hundred fifty shekels.

Arminian

The gold totals 16,750 shekels, about 167.5 kilograms according to the WEB footnote. Arminian readers note the generosity of the officers’ gift. Both traditions agree that the weight shows the gift was substantial and freely given.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the precise weight, consistent with the chapter’s careful accounting. The WEB footnote gives the modern equivalent. Shared ground: the officers’ offering is large, and it is presented as a wave offering to the LORD.

Numbers 31:53

53The men of war had taken booty, every man for himself.

Arminian

The ordinary soldiers had taken booty for themselves. Arminian readers note the contrast with the officers, who gave their gold to God. Both traditions agree that this verse distinguishes personal booty from the counted plunder of 31:32.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the soldiers’ private booty was permitted, while the officers chose to offer theirs. Shared ground: the narrative records both without condemning the soldiers, highlighting the officers’ voluntary devotion.

Numbers 31:54

54Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold of the captains of thousands and of hundreds, and brought it into the Tent of Meeting for a memorial for the children of Israel before the LORD.

Arminian

The gold is brought into the Tent of Meeting as a memorial for Israel before the LORD. Arminian readers see a lasting reminder of God’s protection and the people’s gratitude. Both traditions agree that memorials in Israel’s worship call God’s acts to remembrance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the memorial as testifying to God’s faithfulness in preserving his people. Shared ground: the chapter closes not with the battle but with worship, as the gold is placed before the LORD as a reminder.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
