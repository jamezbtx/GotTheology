Passage

Numbers 26

Verses 35–51 · WEB

The census concludes with Ephraim, Benjamin, Dan, Asher, and Naphtali, followed by the grand total in 26:51: 601,730 men. The tribal figures in 26:7–50 add up exactly to that total. It is 1,820 fewer than the 603,550 of the first census (1:46). Ephraim has declined, while Benjamin and Asher have grown. Dan has only one clan, Shuham, yet is the second largest tribe. Benjamin’s clans differ somewhat from the list in Gen 46:21. Asher’s entry names a daughter, Serah. As with the first census, interpreters discuss how such large totals should be understood, and readers in both traditions hold different views on that question. Both traditions read the near-equal total as a sign that God sustained his people’s strength through forty years of wilderness and judgment, and that the promise to Abraham did not fail.

Numbers 26:35

35These are the sons of Ephraim after their families: of Shuthelah, the family of the Shuthelahites; of Becher, the family of the Becherites; of Tahan, the family of the Tahanites.

Arminian

Ephraim’s clans come from Shuthelah, Becher, and Tahan.

Arminian readers note that Ephraim’s families are recorded for the inheritance.

Reformed

The clans of Ephraim are listed after Manasseh.

Reformed readers see God preserving Joseph’s second son’s line. Both traditions read the list as a record of preserved families.

Numbers 26:36

36These are the sons of Shuthelah: of Eran, the family of the Eranites.

Arminian

Shuthelah’s son Eran forms a further clan.

Arminian readers note the careful tracing of families, even a generation further down, in Ephraim’s line.

Reformed

The line of Shuthelah is extended.

Reformed readers see God’s care for each family line. Both traditions read the verse as part of the record.

Numbers 26:37

37These are the families of the sons of Ephraim according to those who were counted of them, thirty-two thousand five hundred. These are the sons of Joseph after their families.

Arminian

Ephraim numbers 32,500, down from 40,500 (1:33). The verse closes Joseph’s two tribes.

Arminian readers note that Ephraim, though blessed above Manasseh in Gen 48, is now smaller.

Reformed

Ephraim’s decline does not undo Jacob’s blessing, which concerned the tribe’s future.

Reformed readers stress that God’s purposes unfold over time and are not measured by a single count. Both traditions agree that the text records the decline without explanation.

Numbers 26:38

38The sons of Benjamin after their families: of Bela, the family of the Belaites; of Ashbel, the family of the Ashbelites; of Ahiram, the family of the Ahiramites;

Arminian

Benjamin’s clans begin with Bela, Ashbel, and Ahiram.

Arminian readers note the continuity from Jacob’s youngest son. The names keep Benjamin’s place in the tribal count.

Reformed

Benjamin’s families are preserved.

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness to Rachel’s line. Both traditions read the list as a record of preserved families.

Numbers 26:39

39of Shephupham, the family of the Shuphamites; of Hupham, the family of the Huphamites.

Arminian

The clans of Shephupham and Hupham follow. The WEB prints the clan name as “Shuphamites.”

Arminian readers note that the clan names differ somewhat from the list in Gen 46:21.

Reformed

Benjamin’s clans continue to be listed.

Reformed readers see God preserving the tribe’s families. Both traditions treat the differences from Genesis as normal features of genealogical records.

Numbers 26:40

40The sons of Bela were Ard and Naaman: the family of the Ardites; and of Naaman, the family of the Naamites.

Arminian

Ard and Naaman are listed here as sons of Bela, while Gen 46:21 lists them among Benjamin’s sons. Interpreters note that genealogies sometimes list grandsons with sons.

Arminian readers read the list as a family record shaped for the census, not a complete genealogy.

Reformed

The clans of Bela are traced further.

Reformed readers note that biblical genealogies are selective and serve particular purposes. Both traditions agree that the difference is in arrangement, not in the families recorded.

Numbers 26:41

41These are the sons of Benjamin after their families; and those who were counted of them were forty-five thousand six hundred.

Arminian

Benjamin numbers 45,600, up from 35,400 (1:37).

Arminian readers note the tribe’s growth, which the text records without explaining, and they leave the cause unstated.

Reformed

Benjamin’s increase is recorded for the inheritance.

Reformed readers see God’s blessing on the tribe. Both traditions agree that the text records the growth without explanation.

Numbers 26:42

42These are the sons of Dan after their families: of Shuham, the family of the Shuhamites. These are the families of Dan after their families.

Arminian

Dan has only one clan, Shuham. Gen 46:23 names Dan’s son as Hushim.

Arminian readers note that a single family line could grow into a large tribe.

Reformed

Dan’s one clan stands out in the list.

Reformed readers see God’s power to multiply a single line. Both traditions read the detail as part of the faithful record.

Numbers 26:43

43All the families of the Shuhamites, according to those who were counted of them, were sixty-four thousand four hundred.

Arminian

The Shuhamites number 64,400, up from 62,700 (1:39). Dan is the second largest tribe.

Arminian readers note how much God multiplied this one family.

Reformed

The large number from a single clan shows God’s blessing.

Reformed readers see the promise of many descendants at work. Both traditions agree that the figure displays God’s faithfulness to Abraham.

Numbers 26:44

44The sons of Asher after their families: of Imnah, the family of the Imnites; of Ishvi, the family of the Ishvites; of Beriah, the family of the Berites.

Arminian

Asher’s clans come from Imnah, Ishvi, and Beriah. Gen 46:17 also lists Ishvah, who does not appear here.

Arminian readers note the continuity with Jacob’s family.

Reformed

The clans of Asher are preserved.

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness across generations. Both traditions read the list as a record of preserved families.

Numbers 26:45

45Of the sons of Beriah: of Heber, the family of the Heberites; of Malchiel, the family of the Malchielites.

Arminian

Beriah’s sons Heber and Malchiel form clans, matching Gen 46:17.

Arminian readers note the close agreement with Genesis.

Reformed

The line of Beriah is extended.

Reformed readers see God’s care for each family line. Both traditions read the verse as part of the record.

Numbers 26:46

46The name of the daughter of Asher was Serah.

Arminian

“The name of the daughter of Asher was Serah.” She is also named in Gen 46:17. Why she is mentioned here is not explained.

Arminian readers note that a woman is named in a list otherwise made up of men.

Reformed

Serah’s name is preserved across the generations.

Reformed readers see God’s care for individuals in his people. Both traditions agree that the text does not explain her inclusion, and caution against speculation.

Numbers 26:47

47These are the families of the sons of Asher according to those who were counted of them, fifty-three thousand four hundred.

Arminian

Asher numbers 53,400, up from 41,500 (1:41).

Arminian readers note the tribe’s growth, recorded without explanation, and they do not invent a cause the text does not give.

Reformed

Asher’s increase is recorded for the inheritance.

Reformed readers see God’s blessing on the tribe. Both traditions agree that the text records the figure without explanation.

Numbers 26:48

48The sons of Naphtali after their families: of Jahzeel, the family of the Jahzeelites; of Guni, the family of the Gunites;

Arminian

Naphtali’s clans begin with Jahzeel and Guni, matching Gen 46:24.

Arminian readers note the close match with Genesis. The clan names have been preserved from the earlier list.

Reformed

Naphtali’s families are preserved.

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness to Jacob’s house. Both traditions read the list as showing continuity.

Numbers 26:49

49of Jezer, the family of the Jezerites; of Shillem, the family of the Shillemites.

Arminian

The clans of Jezer and Shillem complete Naphtali.

Arminian readers note that every clan is named, so the tribe’s full household is in view before the total is given.

Reformed

Naphtali’s four clans are listed.

Reformed readers stress God’s care for each family line. Both traditions read the verse as completing the list.

Numbers 26:50

50These are the families of Naphtali according to their families; and those who were counted of them were forty-five thousand four hundred.

Arminian

Naphtali numbers 45,400, down from 53,400 (1:43).

Arminian readers note the decline without speculating on its causes. The figure is simply given for the coming allotment.

Reformed

The total fixes Naphtali’s strength for the inheritance.

Reformed readers note that God provides land for the people he has preserved. Both traditions agree that the text does not explain the decrease.

Numbers 26:51

51These are those who were counted of the children of Israel, six hundred one thousand seven hundred thirty.

Arminian

The total is 601,730. It matches the sum of the tribal figures and is 1,820 fewer than the 603,550 of Num 1:46. Interpreters discuss how such large totals should be understood.

Arminian readers note that a whole generation had died in the wilderness, yet the nation’s strength was nearly the same. They see God’s faithfulness alongside the real cost of unbelief.

Reformed

The near-equal total shows that God’s judgment on one generation did not cancel his promise to the nation.

Reformed readers stress God’s covenant faithfulness in raising up a new generation to inherit the land. Both traditions agree that the total displays both God’s judgment and his faithfulness to Abraham.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
