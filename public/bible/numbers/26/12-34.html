Passage

Numbers 26

Verses 12–34 · WEB

The census continues through Simeon, Gad, Judah, Issachar, Zebulun, and Manasseh. Each tribe’s entry lists its clans and then its total. Most clan names correspond to the sons and grandsons of Jacob in Genesis 46, though some names differ in form, and a few names there are missing here. Compared with Numbers 1, Simeon shows by far the largest drop, from 59,300 to 22,200, and Manasseh the largest rise, from 32,200 to 52,700. The text does not explain the changes. Some interpreters connect Simeon’s decline with the plague at Peor, since Zimri was a Simeonite leader (25:14), but the text does not draw that link. Two notes point beyond the list: Er and Onan’s deaths (Genesis 38) and the daughters of Zelophehad, whose case is taken up in Numbers 27 and 36. Both traditions read the lists as a record of God’s preservation of Israel’s families for their inheritance.

Numbers 26:12

12The sons of Simeon after their families: of Nemuel, the family of the Nemuelites; of Jamin, the family of the Jaminites; of Jachin, the family of the Jachinites;

Arminian

Simeon’s clans begin with Nemuel, Jamin, and Jachin. Gen 46:10 lists Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar, and Shaul. Some names differ in form, and Ohad does not appear here.

Arminian readers note that the lists record real families who lived through real history.

Reformed

The clans of Simeon trace back to Jacob’s son.

Reformed readers see God preserving the tribe despite its history. Both traditions note that differences of form between genealogical lists are common and are not treated here as a problem for the text’s reliability.

Numbers 26:13

13of Zerah, the family of the Zerahites; of Shaul, the family of the Shaulites.

Arminian

The clans of Zerah and Shaul complete Simeon’s five clans.

Arminian readers note that every clan is named, even in a diminished tribe.

Reformed

Simeon still has its families and its place in Israel.

Reformed readers stress that God keeps the tribes for their inheritance. Both traditions read the verse as completing the tribe’s list.

Numbers 26:14

14These are the families of the Simeonites, twenty-two thousand two hundred.

Arminian

Simeon numbers 22,200, down from 59,300 in Num 1:23, a drop of 37,100. The text gives no reason. Some interpreters suggest a link with the plague at Peor and Zimri the Simeonite, but this is an inference.

Arminian readers see the figure as a sober reminder that tribes as well as individuals can be diminished, while noting that the cause is not stated.

Reformed

The steep decline stands out in the list.

Reformed readers note that God still preserved Simeon as a tribe in Israel. Both traditions agree that the cause of the drop should be stated only as tentatively as the text allows.

Numbers 26:15

15The sons of Gad after their families: of Zephon, the family of the Zephonites; of Haggi, the family of the Haggites; of Shuni, the family of the Shunites;

Arminian

Gad’s clans begin with Zephon, Haggi, and Shuni. These correspond to Ziphion, Haggi, and Shuni in Gen 46:16.

Arminian readers note the continuity of Gad’s families from Jacob’s time.

Reformed

The clans of Gad are traced to Jacob’s son.

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness across the generations. Both traditions read the list as a record of preserved families.

Numbers 26:16

16of Ozni, the family of the Oznites; of Eri, the family of the Erites;

Arminian

The clans of Ozni and Eri follow. Ozni appears to correspond to Ezbon in Gen 46:16.

Arminian readers note that the clans persist through the wilderness.

Reformed

Gad’s clans continue to be listed.

Reformed readers see God preserving the tribe’s families. Both traditions agree that the names connect the census to the patriarchal record.

Numbers 26:17

17of Arod, the family of the Arodites; of Areli, the family of the Arelites.

Arminian

The clans of Arod and Areli complete Gad’s seven clans.

Arminian readers note that every clan has its place, and that the list does not treat any of Gad’s families as an afterthought.

Reformed

Gad’s seven clans are preserved.

Reformed readers stress God’s faithfulness to each family line. Both traditions read the verse as completing the list.

Numbers 26:18

18These are the families of the sons of Gad according to those who were counted of them, forty thousand and five hundred.

Arminian

Gad numbers 40,500, down from 45,650 (1:25).

Arminian readers note that the change is recorded without explanation, and they leave the reason unstated, as the text does.

Reformed

The total fixes Gad’s strength for the inheritance.

Reformed readers note that God provides land according to the people he has preserved. Both traditions agree that the text does not explain the decrease.

Numbers 26:19

19The sons of Judah: Er and Onan. Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan.

Arminian

Judah’s sons Er and Onan “died in the land of Canaan” (Genesis 38). Their deaths are remembered even in a census list.

Arminian readers see a reminder that God judges sin even within the royal tribe.

Reformed

The note recalls God’s judgment on Er and Onan.

Reformed readers see God preserving Judah’s line despite sin and death, through Shelah and especially Perez. Both traditions agree that Judah’s line continued by God’s providence through troubled circumstances.

Numbers 26:20

20The sons of Judah after their families were: of Shelah, the family of the Shelanites; of Perez, the family of the Perezites; of Zerah, the family of the Zerahites.

Arminian

Judah’s clans come from Shelah, Perez, and Zerah.

Arminian readers note that the line continued through the children named in Genesis 38.

Reformed

Perez becomes the main line of Judah, through which David will later come (Ruth 4:18–22).

Reformed readers see God’s purpose for the royal line quietly preserved in the list. Both traditions read the clans of Judah with an eye to its later role.

Numbers 26:21

21The sons of Perez were: of Hezron, the family of the Hezronites; of Hamul, the family of the Hamulites.

Arminian

Perez’s sons Hezron and Hamul also form clans, as in Gen 46:12.

Arminian readers note that the line of Perez is traced further than the others.

Reformed

The extended line of Perez points ahead to David’s genealogy.

Reformed readers see God’s purpose through Judah moving forward. Both traditions agree that the detail matters for the later history of the tribe.

Numbers 26:22

22These are the families of Judah according to those who were counted of them, seventy-six thousand five hundred.

Arminian

Judah numbers 76,500, up from 74,600 (1:27). It remains the largest tribe.

Arminian readers note that Judah keeps its leading strength into the new generation.

Reformed

Judah’s growth fits its leading role (Gen 49:8–10).

Reformed readers often see God’s purpose for the royal tribe at work here. Both traditions read Judah’s size as significant for its later leadership.

Numbers 26:23

23The sons of Issachar after their families: of Tola, the family of the Tolaites; of Puvah, the family of the Punites;

Arminian

Issachar’s clans begin with Tola and Puvah. The WEB prints the clan name as “Punites.”

Arminian readers note that the clans trace back to the sons named in Gen 46:13.

Reformed

Issachar’s families are preserved from Jacob’s time.

Reformed readers see continuity in God’s people. Both traditions read the list as a record of preserved families.

Numbers 26:24

24of Jashub, the family of the Jashubites; of Shimron, the family of the Shimronites.

Arminian

The clans of Jashub and Shimron complete Issachar. Jashub appears to correspond to Iob in Gen 46:13.

Arminian readers note that each clan is named.

Reformed

Issachar’s four clans are listed.

Reformed readers stress God’s care for each family line. Both traditions read the verse as completing the list.

Numbers 26:25

25These are the families of Issachar according to those who were counted of them, sixty-four thousand three hundred.

Arminian

Issachar numbers 64,300, up from 54,400 (1:29).

Arminian readers note the growth without speculating on its causes. The figure simply stands as the tribe’s strength for the coming allotment.

Reformed

Issachar’s increase is recorded for the inheritance.

Reformed readers see God’s blessing in the growth of the tribe. Both traditions agree that the text records the figure without explanation.

Numbers 26:26

26The sons of Zebulun after their families: of Sered, the family of the Seredites; of Elon, the family of the Elonites; of Jahleel, the family of the Jahleelites.

Arminian

Zebulun’s clans are Sered, Elon, and Jahleel, matching Gen 46:14.

Arminian readers note the close match with the Genesis list.

Reformed

Zebulun’s clans are preserved exactly as named in Genesis.

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness to Jacob’s house. Both traditions read the list as showing continuity.

Numbers 26:27

27These are the families of the Zebulunites according to those who were counted of them, sixty thousand five hundred.

Arminian

Zebulun numbers 60,500, up from 57,400 (1:31).

Arminian readers note the tribe’s modest growth, recorded with the same care as the larger changes, and they do not invent a cause the text does not give.

Reformed

The total is set for the coming inheritance.

Reformed readers see God’s provision for the tribe. Both traditions read the verse as a simple record of strength.

Numbers 26:28

28The sons of Joseph after their families: Manasseh and Ephraim.

Arminian

Joseph’s sons are named as Manasseh and Ephraim. Manasseh is listed first here, while Ephraim was listed first in Num 1:32–35.

Arminian readers note that Manasseh, Joseph’s firstborn (Gen 41:51–52), now also has the larger total.

Reformed

The two half-tribes of Joseph are counted separately, as in Numbers 1.

Reformed readers recall that Jacob placed Ephraim ahead of Manasseh (Gen 48:14–20), a blessing concerned with the tribes’ future rather than a single count. Both traditions note that the text does not explain the change of order.

Numbers 26:29

29The sons of Manasseh: of Machir, the family of the Machirites; and Machir became the father of Gilead; of Gilead, the family of the Gileadites.

Arminian

Manasseh’s line runs through Machir and Gilead.

Arminian readers note that the clans of Manasseh are traced through several generations.

Reformed

The line of Machir and Gilead will become important in the settlement east of the Jordan (Numbers 32).

Reformed readers see God’s purpose shaping the future of the tribe. Both traditions read the genealogy as preparing for later events.

Numbers 26:30

30These are the sons of Gilead: of Iezer, the family of the Iezerites; of Helek, the family of the Helekites;

Arminian

Gilead’s sons include Iezer and Helek, each forming a clan.

Arminian readers note the careful listing of families, so that Gilead’s descendants have a named share in Manasseh’s inheritance.

Reformed

The clans of Gilead are preserved.

Reformed readers stress God’s care for each family. Both traditions read the list as a record for the inheritance.

Numbers 26:31

31and Asriel, the family of the Asrielites; and Shechem, the family of the Shechemites;

Arminian

Asriel and Shechem form further clans.

Arminian readers note that each family is recognized by name, so the inheritance record stays tied to particular houses.

Reformed

The clans of Manasseh continue to be listed.

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness to the tribe. Both traditions read the verse as part of the inheritance record.

Numbers 26:32

32and Shemida, the family of the Shemidaites; and Hepher, the family of the Hepherites.

Arminian

Shemida and Hepher complete the clans of Gilead.

Arminian readers note that Hepher’s name leads to the case of Zelophehad.

Reformed

Hepher’s clan prepares for the next verse.

Reformed readers see the list setting up a question about inheritance. Both traditions read the verse as a transition.

Numbers 26:33

33Zelophehad the son of Hepher had no sons, but daughters: and the names of the daughters of Zelophehad were Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah.

Arminian

Zelophehad son of Hepher “had no sons, but daughters,” and the five daughters are named: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. Their case comes before Moses in Numbers 27 and is taken up again in Numbers 36.

Arminian readers note that women are named in the census record because their inheritance will matter.

Reformed

Naming the daughters prepares for God’s ruling on inheritance when there is no son.

Reformed readers see God’s law providing for families in unusual circumstances. Both traditions agree that the verse shows God’s care that no family in Israel lose its share in the land.

Numbers 26:34

34These are the families of Manasseh. Those who were counted of them were fifty-two thousand seven hundred.

Arminian

Manasseh numbers 52,700, up from 32,200 (1:35), the largest increase of any tribe.

Arminian readers note the growth without speculating on its causes.

Reformed

Manasseh’s strong growth is recorded for the inheritance.

Reformed readers see God’s blessing on Joseph’s line (compare Gen 49:22). Both traditions agree that the text does not explain the increase.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
