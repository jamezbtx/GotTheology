Passage

Numbers 2

Verses 1–16 · WEB

Numbers 2 sets out the arrangement of Israel’s camp around the Tent of Meeting. Three tribes camp on each side under the standard of a leading tribe, at a distance from the Tent. This section gives the eastern camp under Judah, which sets out first, and the southern camp under Reuben, which sets out second. Leaders and totals repeat those in chapter 1, and the subtotals add up exactly. Both traditions read the chapter as showing God dwelling at the center of his people. They also agree that it shows each tribe has an assigned place, and that the camp is organized for both rest and travel. Neither tradition treats the tribal order as a ranking of spiritual worth.

Numbers 2:1

1The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron together. The camp arrangement is entrusted to both leaders.

Arminian readers note that God gives clear instructions so the people can obey them.

Reformed

God himself designs the shape of the camp.

Reformed readers stress that God orders his people’s common life by his word. Both traditions agree that the arrangement is divine instruction, not human planning.

Numbers 2:2

2“The children of Israel shall encamp every man by his own standard, with the banners of their fathers’ houses. They shall encamp around the Tent of Meeting at a distance from it.

Arminian

Every man camps by his standard, under his fathers’ house banners, around the Tent “at a distance from it.” The Tent stands at the center, with a space kept between it and the tribes.

Arminian readers see both God’s nearness and his holiness in the layout. He dwells among them, but they must respect the boundary he sets.

Reformed

God’s presence at the center and the distance kept from it express both fellowship and holiness.

Reformed readers stress God’s holiness and the need for mediation, with the Levites camped nearest the Tent (1:53). Both traditions agree that the camp pictures a holy God dwelling among his people.

Numbers 2:3

3“Those who encamp on the east side toward the sunrise shall be of the standard of the camp of Judah, according to their divisions. The prince of the children of Judah shall be Nahshon the son of Amminadab.

Arminian

Judah camps on the east, “toward the sunrise,” the side facing the Tent’s entrance. Nahshon son of Amminadab is its prince.

Arminian readers note Judah’s place of honor and responsibility at the front of the camp.

Reformed

Judah’s position at the entrance fits its leading role promised in Gen 49:8–10.

Reformed readers often see God’s purpose for the royal tribe at work here. Both traditions read Judah’s eastern place as significant for its later leadership.

Numbers 2:4

4His division, and those who were counted of them, were seventy-four thousand six hundred.

Arminian

Judah’s division numbers 74,600, matching 1:27.

Arminian readers note that the camp is built from the same count already made, with nothing added or lost.

Reformed

The figure repeats the census result exactly.

Reformed readers see the census and camp as one ordered work. Both traditions agree that the repetition shows the record’s consistency.

Numbers 2:5

5“Those who encamp next to him shall be the tribe of Issachar. The prince of the children of Issachar shall be Nethanel the son of Zuar.

Arminian

Issachar camps next to Judah, led by Nethanel son of Zuar.

Arminian readers note that tribes are placed in relationship with one another, not left to themselves.

Reformed

Issachar is assigned to Judah’s camp.

Reformed readers stress that God places each tribe within a larger fellowship. Both traditions agree that the tribes serve together under one standard.

Numbers 2:6

6His division, and those who were counted of it, were fifty-four thousand four hundred.

Arminian

Issachar’s division numbers 54,400, matching 1:29.

Arminian readers see the count carried faithfully from census to camp.

Reformed

The figure agrees with chapter 1.

Reformed readers note the careful consistency of the record. Both traditions read the repeated figure as part of an orderly account.

Numbers 2:7

7“The tribe of Zebulun: the prince of the children of Zebulun shall be Eliab the son of Helon.

Arminian

Zebulun completes Judah’s camp, led by Eliab son of Helon.

Arminian readers note that the three eastern tribes are all sons of Leah.

Reformed

Zebulun joins Judah and Issachar, keeping Leah’s sons together.

Reformed readers see family lines respected in God’s arrangement. Both traditions agree that the grouping follows Genesis.

Numbers 2:8

8His division, and those who were counted of it, were fifty-seven thousand four hundred.

Arminian

Zebulun’s division numbers 57,400, matching 1:31.

Arminian readers read the figure as completing the strength of the leading camp.

Reformed

The figure again matches the census.

Reformed readers see the precision as part of God’s ordering. Both traditions read it as faithful record-keeping.

Numbers 2:9

9“All who were counted of the camp of Judah were one hundred eighty-six thousand four hundred, according to their divisions. They shall set out first.

Arminian

Judah’s camp totals 186,400, the largest of the four. It sets out first.

Arminian readers note that the people led by Judah go ahead of the rest. Numbers 10:14 records this order being followed.

Reformed

Judah leads the march, as later history and prophecy will develop.

Reformed readers see God’s choice of Judah given concrete form. Both traditions agree that the first place on the march suits Judah’s role, and the subtotal agrees with the three tribal figures.

Numbers 2:10

10“On the south side shall be the standard of the camp of Reuben according to their divisions. The prince of the children of Reuben shall be Elizur the son of Shedeur.

Arminian

Reuben leads the southern camp, with Elizur son of Shedeur as prince.

Arminian readers note that Reuben, though firstborn, does not lead the whole camp but still holds an honored place.

Reformed

Reuben heads a camp of its own while Judah leads overall.

Reformed readers see God’s purposes reordering birthright as in Genesis 49. Both traditions agree that Reuben keeps a real place among the tribes.

Numbers 2:11

11His division, and those who were counted of it, were forty-six thousand five hundred.

Arminian

Reuben’s division numbers 46,500, matching 1:21. Reuben heads the southern camp, with Simeon and Gad under his standard.

Arminian readers see the census carried into the camp unchanged, so the arrangement rests on the count already taken.

Reformed

The figure agrees with chapter 1.

Reformed readers note the consistency of the record. Both traditions read the numbers as one coherent account.

Numbers 2:12

12“Those who encamp next to him shall be the tribe of Simeon. The prince of the children of Simeon shall be Shelumiel the son of Zurishaddai.

Arminian

Simeon camps next to Reuben, led by Shelumiel son of Zurishaddai.

Arminian readers note that Leah’s second son joins her firstborn in the southern camp.

Reformed

Simeon is placed with Reuben.

Reformed readers see family relationships kept in God’s arrangement. Both traditions read the grouping as orderly rather than symbolic of rank.

Numbers 2:13

13His division, and those who were counted of them, were fifty-nine thousand three hundred.

Arminian

Simeon’s division numbers 59,300, matching 1:23.

Arminian readers read the figure as the tribe’s strength at this point in the journey.

Reformed

The figure repeats the census total.

Reformed readers note the faithful repetition. Both traditions agree that the record is consistent.

Numbers 2:14

14“The tribe of Gad: the prince of the children of Gad shall be Eliasaph the son of Reuel.

Arminian

Gad completes Reuben’s camp. The WEB names its prince’s father Reuel here and Deuel in 1:14. Ancient manuscripts and versions differ over the name.

Arminian readers treat this as a textual detail with no doctrinal weight.

Reformed

Gad, a son of Leah’s handmaid Zilpah, joins Leah’s sons Reuben and Simeon.

Reformed readers likewise treat the name difference as a matter for textual study. Both traditions agree that the same leader is meant.

Numbers 2:15

15His division, and those who were counted of them, were forty-five thousand six hundred fifty.

Arminian

Gad’s division numbers 45,650, matching 1:25. Gad completes the southern camp under Reuben’s standard.

Arminian readers see the count kept exactly as recorded, with no rounding or adjustment when the tribes are placed.

Reformed

The figure agrees with the census.

Reformed readers note the record’s consistency. Both traditions read the repetition as careful preservation of the count.

Numbers 2:16

16“All who were counted of the camp of Reuben were one hundred fifty-one thousand four hundred fifty, according to their armies. They shall set out second.

Arminian

Reuben’s camp totals 151,450 and sets out second. The text says “according to their armies,” using a word closely related to the “divisions” of other verses.

Arminian readers note that each camp knows its turn in the march.

Reformed

The second camp’s total agrees with its three tribal figures.

Reformed readers see God’s orderly design for the journey. Both traditions agree that the marching order is set by God for the good of the whole people.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
