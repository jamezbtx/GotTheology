Passage

Numbers 2

Verses 17–34 · WEB

The Tent of Meeting and the Levites move in the middle of the camps. The western camp under Ephraim sets out third, and the northern camp under Dan sets out last. The four camp totals add up to 603,550, the same grand total as 1:46, and the Levites are again excluded. The chapter ends with Israel doing all that the LORD commanded. Numbers 10 later describes the march itself, including how the Levite clans divide the carrying of the tabernacle. Both traditions read the arrangement as God at the center of a people ordered by his word. Both also see this picture of God dwelling among his people carried forward in the New Testament (John 1:14; Rev 21:3).

Numbers 2:17

17“Then the Tent of Meeting shall set out, with the camp of the Levites in the middle of the camps. As they encamp, so shall they set out, every man in his place, by their standards.

Arminian

The Tent of Meeting, with the Levites’ camp, sets out “in the middle of the camps.” The order of the camp at rest is kept on the march: “As they encamp, so shall they set out.”

Arminian readers see God’s presence going with his people throughout the journey, not only at the stopping places.

Reformed

God’s dwelling remains at the center of the people whether they rest or travel.

Reformed readers stress that God’s presence orders the whole life of his people. Both traditions agree that the journey is undertaken with God among them. Numbers 10 gives further details of how the tabernacle is carried.

Numbers 2:18

18“On the west side shall be the standard of the camp of Ephraim according to their divisions. The prince of the children of Ephraim shall be Elishama the son of Ammihud.

Arminian

Ephraim leads the western camp, with Elishama son of Ammihud as prince.

Arminian readers note that the younger son of Joseph heads the camp, following Jacob’s blessing (Gen 48:19–20).

Reformed

Ephraim’s leadership reflects the reversal of birth order in Jacob’s blessing.

Reformed readers see God’s purposes working through unexpected choices. Both traditions agree that the arrangement follows Genesis.

Numbers 2:19

19His division, and those who were counted of them, were forty thousand five hundred.

Arminian

Ephraim’s division numbers 40,500, matching 1:33. Ephraim heads the western camp, with Manasseh and Benjamin under his standard.

Arminian readers see the census carried exactly into the camp, so Joseph’s house takes its appointed place by the same figures already given.

Reformed

The figure agrees with chapter 1.

Reformed readers note the consistency of the record. Both traditions read the figures as one coherent account.

Numbers 2:20

20“Next to him shall be the tribe of Manasseh. The prince of the children of Manasseh shall be Gamaliel the son of Pedahzur.

Arminian

Manasseh camps next to Ephraim, led by Gamaliel son of Pedahzur.

Arminian readers note that Joseph’s two sons stand side by side.

Reformed

Manasseh joins Ephraim in the western camp.

Reformed readers see the double portion of Joseph kept together. Both traditions agree that the grouping honors the family of Rachel.

Numbers 2:21

21His division, and those who were counted of them, were thirty-two thousand two hundred.

Arminian

Manasseh’s division numbers 32,200, matching 1:35.

Arminian readers note that the smallest tribe has its secure place in the camp.

Reformed

The figure repeats the census.

Reformed readers stress that size does not decide place in God’s ordering. Both traditions agree that every tribe is included.

Numbers 2:22

22“The tribe of Benjamin: the prince of the children of Benjamin shall be Abidan the son of Gideoni.

Arminian

Benjamin completes the western camp, led by Abidan son of Gideoni.

Arminian readers note that all of Rachel’s descendants camp together on the west.

Reformed

Benjamin joins Joseph’s sons.

Reformed readers see family lines preserved in God’s design. Both traditions read the grouping as rooted in Jacob’s family.

Numbers 2:23

23His army, and those who were counted of them, were thirty-five thousand four hundred.

Arminian

Benjamin’s army numbers 35,400, matching 1:37. The WEB says “His army” here rather than “His division.”

Arminian readers see the count kept faithfully.

Reformed

The figure agrees with the census.

Reformed readers note the consistency of the record. Both traditions read the change of wording as having no bearing on the sense.

Numbers 2:24

24“All who were counted of the camp of Ephraim were one hundred eight thousand one hundred, according to their divisions. They shall set out third.

Arminian

Ephraim’s camp totals 108,100, the smallest of the four, and sets out third.

Arminian readers note that a smaller camp has its own appointed place in the march.

Reformed

The subtotal agrees with its three tribes.

Reformed readers see God’s order extending to every part of the march. Both traditions agree that each camp’s place is assigned by God.

Numbers 2:25

25“On the north side shall be the standard of the camp of Dan according to their divisions. The prince of the children of Dan shall be Ahiezer the son of Ammishaddai.

Arminian

Dan leads the northern camp, with Ahiezer son of Ammishaddai as prince.

Arminian readers note that the sons of the handmaids receive a full camp and a leading tribe.

Reformed

Dan heads a camp of its own.

Reformed readers see God giving each part of the family a place of responsibility. Both traditions agree that the arrangement includes all the tribes on equal terms.

Numbers 2:26

26His division, and those who were counted of them, were sixty-two thousand seven hundred.

Arminian

Dan’s division numbers 62,700, matching 1:39. Dan heads the northern camp and is the largest of the four camp totals in this arrangement.

Arminian readers see the count carried into the camp unchanged, a large division given its place like the smaller ones.

Reformed

The figure agrees with chapter 1.

Reformed readers note the consistency of the record. Both traditions read the totals as one ordered account.

Numbers 2:27

27“Those who encamp next to him shall be the tribe of Asher. The prince of the children of Asher shall be Pagiel the son of Ochran.

Arminian

Asher camps next to Dan, led by Pagiel son of Ochran.

Arminian readers note that Asher is joined to Dan in the shared task of guarding the rear.

Reformed

Asher is assigned to the northern camp.

Reformed readers see God placing each tribe with others for common service. Both traditions agree that the tribes serve together.

Numbers 2:28

28His division, and those who were counted of them, were forty-one thousand five hundred.

Arminian

Asher’s division numbers 41,500, matching 1:41. Asher stands with Dan and Naphtali on the north.

Arminian readers see the record kept exactly, tribe by tribe, as the last camps are filled in.

Reformed

The figure repeats the census.

Reformed readers note the faithful repetition. Both traditions read it as careful record-keeping.

Numbers 2:29

29“The tribe of Naphtali: the prince of the children of Naphtali shall be Ahira the son of Enan.

Arminian

Naphtali completes the northern camp, led by Ahira son of Enan.

Arminian readers note that the last tribe named still has a full place among the people.

Reformed

Naphtali joins Dan and Asher.

Reformed readers see the four handmaid tribes divided between the camps of Reuben and Dan. Both traditions read the arrangement as God’s ordering of the whole family.

Numbers 2:30

30His division, and those who were counted of them, were fifty-three thousand four hundred.

Arminian

Naphtali’s division numbers 53,400, matching 1:43. Naphtali completes the northern camp and so closes the tribal placement around the Tent.

Arminian readers see the last tribal figure carried into the camp, completing a record that has not been altered in transit.

Reformed

The figure agrees with the census.

Reformed readers note the completed consistency of the two lists. Both traditions agree that the record holds together.

Numbers 2:31

31“All who were counted of the camp of Dan were one hundred fifty-seven thousand six hundred. They shall set out last by their standards.”

Arminian

Dan’s camp totals 157,600 and sets out “last by their standards.” Numbers 10:25 calls it the rear guard of all the camps.

Arminian readers note the protective role entrusted to the last camp.

Reformed

The subtotal agrees with its three tribes, and Dan guards the rear.

Reformed readers see God arranging protection for his people on every side. Both traditions read the last place as a responsibility, not a demotion.

Numbers 2:32

32These are those who were counted of the children of Israel by their fathers’ houses. All who were counted of the camps according to their armies were six hundred three thousand five hundred fifty.

Arminian

The grand total of the camps is 603,550, matching 1:46. Interpreters discuss how such large totals should be understood.

Arminian readers see the whole people accounted for and prepared to move toward the land God promised.

Reformed

The four camps add up exactly to the census total.

Reformed readers see God’s faithfulness in multiplying and ordering Abraham’s descendants. Both traditions agree that the summary closes the account of a complete and ordered people.

Numbers 2:33

33But the Levites were not counted among the children of Israel, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

Again the Levites are not counted among Israel, “as the LORD commanded Moses.”

Arminian readers note that their distinct role, set out in 1:47–53, is maintained in the camp.

Reformed

The repeated exclusion stresses that Levi belongs to the sanctuary.

Reformed readers see God guarding the order of worship. Both traditions agree that the Levites’ separate place is for the people’s good.

Numbers 2:34

34Thus the children of Israel did. According to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so they encamped by their standards, and so they set out, everyone by their families, according to their fathers’ houses.

Arminian

The chapter ends, “Thus the children of Israel did.” They camp and set out as commanded, each by family and fathers’ house.

Arminian readers note that the people’s obedience at this point is genuine and gives an example of faithful response to God’s word.

Reformed

The account closes with complete compliance with God’s command.

Reformed readers see God’s word forming his people into an ordered community. Both traditions read the verse as a picture of the faithful obedience Numbers will soon contrast with rebellion.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
