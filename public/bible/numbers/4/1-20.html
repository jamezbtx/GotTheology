Passage

Numbers 4

Verses 1–20 · WEB

Numbers 4:1–20 begins a second Levite census, this time of men aged thirty to fifty fit for the work of the Tent of Meeting, starting with Kohath. When the camp moves, the priests must first wrap the ark, table, lampstand, altars, and vessels in their coverings. Only then may the Kohathites carry them, without touching or seeing the holy things, lest they die. Both traditions see here God’s holiness and his merciful provision so that those who serve near him may live.

Numbers 4:1

1The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

Arminian

God speaks to Moses and Aaron together. Arminians note that both leaders are accountable for carrying out these instructions.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree on the shared responsibility. Both traditions agree on the verse. Aaron is addressed here because the priests must prepare the holy things before the Levites can carry them.

Numbers 4:2

2“Take a census of the sons of Kohath from among the sons of Levi, by their families, by their fathers’ houses,

Arminian

A census of the Kohathites is taken by families and fathers’ houses. Arminians see God appointing workers by name and family.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that the census organizes service. Both traditions note that the Kohathites come first because of their duties with the most holy things.

Numbers 4:3

3from thirty years old and upward even until fifty years old, all who enter into the service to do the work in the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

Men from thirty to fifty enter the service. Arminians see the age range as fitting mature, capable workers to demanding tasks.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note that 8:24 gives twenty-five, and interpreters explain the difference in various ways, such as a training period or different tasks.

Numbers 4:4

4“This is the service of the sons of Kohath in the Tent of Meeting, regarding the most holy things.

Arminian

The Kohathites serve ‘regarding the most holy things’. Arminians see the honor and the gravity of their calling. The phrase ‘most holy things’ sets the Kohathites’ task apart from the Gershonites’ and Merarites’ work.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as introducing the careful procedures that follow and agree on it.

Numbers 4:5

5When the camp moves forward, Aaron shall go in with his sons; and they shall take down the veil of the screen, cover the ark of the Testimony with it,

Arminian

Aaron and his sons cover the ark with the veil when the camp moves. Arminians see the priests shielding the holiest object so that others may serve safely.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and note that the veil that hid the ark in the tabernacle now covers it on the move. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 4:6

6put a covering of sealskin on it, spread a blue cloth over it, and put in its poles.

Arminian

A covering of ‘sealskin’ and a blue cloth go over the ark, and the poles are inserted. Arminians note that the exact meaning of the Hebrew word rendered ‘sealskin’ is uncertain.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same uncertainty and that the ark alone has the blue cloth on the outside. Both traditions avoid building doctrine on these details.

Numbers 4:7

7“On the table of show bread they shall spread a blue cloth, and put on it the dishes, the spoons, the bowls, and the cups with which to pour out; and the continual bread shall be on it.

Arminian

Blue cloth covers the table of showbread, its vessels, and the continual bread. Arminians see the ‘continual bread’ as a sign of God’s ongoing provision and fellowship.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and note that the bread remained on the table even in transit. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 4:8

8They shall spread on them a scarlet cloth, and cover it with a covering of sealskin, and shall put in its poles.

Arminian

A scarlet cloth and sealskin covering are added, and the poles are inserted. Arminians see careful, layered protection of the holy things.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as part of the procedure and agree on it. The table has three layers in all, blue, scarlet, and sealskin, which marks its high degree of holiness.

Numbers 4:9

9“They shall take a blue cloth and cover the lamp stand of the light, its lamps, its snuffers, its snuff dishes, and all its oil vessels, with which they minister to it.

Arminian

Blue cloth covers the lampstand, lamps, snuffers, and oil vessels. Arminians see care even for the tools of ministry. The lampstand gave the light in the Holy Place, so its care mattered for daily worship.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as part of the procedure and agree on it. The detailed list shows that the smallest tools used in God’s service were treated as holy.

Numbers 4:10

10They shall put it and all its vessels within a covering of sealskin, and shall put it on the frame.

Arminian

The lampstand and vessels go into a sealskin covering on a carrying frame. Arminians note the practical provision for transport.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. The carrying frame let the Kohathites move the lampstand without touching it directly.

Numbers 4:11

11“On the golden altar they shall spread a blue cloth, and cover it with a covering of sealskin, and shall put in its poles.

Arminian

Blue cloth and sealskin cover the golden altar, and the poles are inserted. Arminians see the incense altar, linked with prayer, guarded with the same care.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note the connection of incense with prayer elsewhere in Scripture (Psalm 141:2) and agree on the verse.

Numbers 4:12

12“They shall take all the vessels of ministry with which they minister in the sanctuary, and put them in a blue cloth, cover them with a covering of sealskin, and shall put them on the frame.

Arminian

The vessels of ministry are wrapped in blue cloth and sealskin and placed on a frame. Arminians see orderly care for every utensil.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. The pattern of blue cloth, then sealskin, repeats for the lesser vessels too.

Numbers 4:13

13“They shall take away the ashes from the altar, and spread a purple cloth on it.

Arminian

The ashes are removed from the altar and a purple cloth is spread on it. Arminians note the careful cleaning before transport.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. Clearing the ashes first shows how the altar was prepared respectfully for travel.

Numbers 4:14

14They shall put on it all its vessels with which they minister about it, the fire pans, the meat hooks, the shovels, and the basins—all the vessels of the altar; and they shall spread on it a covering of sealskin, and put in its poles.

Arminian

The altar’s fire pans, hooks, shovels, and basins go on it under a sealskin covering, and the poles are inserted. Arminians see the same careful pattern for the altar of sacrifice.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as completing the altar procedure and agree on it. The bronze altar receives purple rather than blue, a detail the text records without explaining.

Numbers 4:15

15“When Aaron and his sons have finished covering the sanctuary and all the furniture of the sanctuary, as the camp moves forward; after that, the sons of Kohath shall come to carry it; but they shall not touch the sanctuary, lest they die. The sons of Kohath shall carry these things belonging to the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

Only after the priests finish covering may the Kohathites carry, and they must not touch the holy things ‘lest they die’. Arminians see a merciful safeguard: God’s instructions protect those who serve.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and connect the warning with the death of Uzzah (2 Samuel 6:6–7). Both traditions read the verse as teaching both God’s holiness and his protective care.

Numbers 4:16

16“The duty of Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest shall be the oil for the light, the sweet incense, the continual meal offering, and the anointing oil, the requirements of all the tabernacle, and of all that is in it, the sanctuary, and its furnishings.”

Arminian

Eleazar oversees the oil, incense, continual meal offering, anointing oil, and the whole tabernacle. Arminians see accountable leadership over the most sensitive items.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and note the priest’s supervisory role. Both traditions agree on the verse. Eleazar’s oversight shows that the priests kept responsibility for the holiest items even while the Levites carried them.

Numbers 4:17

17The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

Arminian

A divine speech to Moses and Aaron follows. Arminians see God adding a specific protective concern. Repeating the address to both leaders underlines how important this safeguard is.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions agree on the function of the verse. Verses 18–20 explain that the purpose of the preceding procedures is to protect the Kohathites’ lives.

Numbers 4:18

18“Don’t cut off the tribe of the families of the Kohathites from among the Levites;

Arminian

‘Don’t cut off’ the Kohathites. Arminians see God’s concern that his servants not perish through careless handling of holy things.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that the instruction expresses God’s care for the Kohathites. Both traditions read the verse as merciful and agree on it.

Numbers 4:19

19but do this to them, that they may live, and not die, when they approach the most holy things: Aaron and his sons shall go in and appoint everyone to his service and to his burden;

Arminian

‘That they may live, and not die’: the priests appoint each Kohathite to his task. Arminians see God providing a way for his people to serve near his holiness and live.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and see God’s grace in providing mediation. Both traditions connect the pattern with Christ, through whom believers draw near without fear (Hebrews 10:19–22).

Numbers 4:20

20but they shall not go in to see the sanctuary even for a moment, lest they die.”

Arminian

They must not go in to see the holy things ‘even for a moment’. Arminians see the gravity of God’s holiness and the need for reverence.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and some connect the verse with 1 Samuel 6:19. Both traditions read it as teaching reverence and agree on its meaning.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
