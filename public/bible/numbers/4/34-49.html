Passage

Numbers 4

Verses 34–49 · WEB

Numbers 4:34–49 reports the results of the census of working Levites aged thirty to fifty: Kohathites 2,750, Gershonites 2,630, and Merarites 3,200, for a total of 8,580. A refrain repeats that each was counted ‘according to the commandment of the LORD by Moses’, each ‘according to his service and according to his burden’. Both traditions read the passage as a record of careful obedience, in which God knows and appoints each servant to his work.

Numbers 4:34

34Moses and Aaron and the princes of the congregation counted the sons of the Kohathites by their families, and by their fathers’ houses,

Arminian

Moses, Aaron, and the congregation’s leaders count the Kohathites. Arminians see shared leadership in carrying out God’s command.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The princes’ involvement shows that the whole congregation’s leadership took part in obeying God’s instructions.

Numbers 4:35

35from thirty years old and upward even to fifty years old, everyone who entered into the service for work in the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

Those from thirty to fifty who entered service are counted. Arminians note the faithful application of the standard. The report follows the command of verses 2–3 closely.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The wording echoes verse 3, so the fulfillment matches the command.

Numbers 4:36

36Those who were counted of them by their families were two thousand seven hundred fifty.

Arminian

The Kohathites number 2,750. Arminians note this as the record of God’s workers. The Kohathites who carried the most holy things were a relatively small working group.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions note that this contributes to the total in verse 48 and agree on the verse. The census thus records those ready for the most sensitive tabernacle service.

Numbers 4:37

37These are those who were counted of the families of the Kohathites, all who served in the Tent of Meeting, whom Moses and Aaron counted according to the commandment of the LORD by Moses.

Arminian

They were counted ‘according to the commandment of the LORD by Moses’. Arminians see obedience emphasized again. The phrase ‘by Moses’ marks Moses as God’s mediator for the instruction.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the refrain as stressing faithful obedience and agree on it. The refrain echoes the obedience language that closes Exodus’s tabernacle account.

Numbers 4:38

38Those who were counted of the sons of Gershon, by their families, and by their fathers’ houses,

Arminian

The Gershonites are counted by families and fathers’ houses. Arminians see the same careful process. The report of the Gershonite census follows the same pattern as the Kohathite report.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The parallel structure of verses 34–45 highlights consistent obedience across the clans.

Numbers 4:39

39from thirty years old and upward even to fifty years old—everyone who entered into the service for work in the Tent of Meeting,

Arminian

Those from thirty to fifty who entered service are counted. Arminians note the consistent standard. The same age range applies across all three working clans.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The repeated wording keeps the reports in step with the original command.

Numbers 4:40

40even those who were counted of them, by their families, by their fathers’ houses, were two thousand six hundred thirty.

Arminian

The Gershonites number 2,630. Arminians note the record of faithful workers. The Gershonites who carried the fabrics numbered slightly fewer than the Kohathites.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions note this total in relation to verse 48 and agree on the verse. The figure is recorded plainly, without commentary.

Numbers 4:41

41These are those who were counted of the families of the sons of Gershon, all who served in the Tent of Meeting, whom Moses and Aaron counted according to the commandment of the LORD.

Arminian

These Gershonites were counted by Moses and Aaron at the LORD’s command. Arminians see obedience completing each step. Each clan report closes the same way, emphasizing that the count followed God’s word.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the refrain as stressing obedience and agree on it. The repeated phrasing shows that Moses and Aaron carried out every part of the command.

Numbers 4:42

42Those who were counted of the families of the sons of Merari, by their families, by their fathers’ houses,

Arminian

The Merarites are counted by families and fathers’ houses. Arminians see the same careful process. The Merarite report completes the three-part pattern of verses 34–45.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The parallel structure underscores the uniform obedience of the census.

Numbers 4:43

43from thirty years old and upward even to fifty years old—everyone who entered into the service for work in the Tent of Meeting,

Arminian

Those from thirty to fifty who entered service are counted. Arminians note the consistent standard. The same age range is applied once more.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The repetition is characteristic of priestly records that stress exact compliance.

Numbers 4:44

44even those who were counted of them by their families, were three thousand two hundred.

Arminian

The Merarites number 3,200. Arminians note the largest working group carried the heaviest loads. This reflects the Merarites’ responsibility for the heaviest structural pieces.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same observation, offered as a reasonable inference rather than a stated reason. Both traditions agree on the verse.

Numbers 4:45

45These are those who were counted of the families of the sons of Merari, whom Moses and Aaron counted according to the commandment of the LORD by Moses.

Arminian

These Merarites were counted at the LORD’s command by Moses. Arminians see obedience emphasized again. The closing formula is the same as for the other clans.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the refrain as stressing obedience and agree on it. The final words, ‘by Moses’, again mark Moses as the channel of God’s instruction.

Numbers 4:46

46All those who were counted of the Levites whom Moses and Aaron and the princes of Israel counted, by their families and by their fathers’ houses,

Arminian

All the Levites counted by Moses, Aaron, and the leaders by families and houses. Arminians see a complete and orderly accounting.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The summary brings together all three clan reports into one total.

Numbers 4:47

47from thirty years old and upward even to fifty years old, everyone who entered in to do the work of service and the work of bearing burdens in the Tent of Meeting,

Arminian

Those from thirty to fifty doing service and carrying burdens. Arminians see work and burden-bearing honored together. The summary repeats both the age range and the type of service.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The double description, work and burden-bearing, sums up the Levites’ role.

Numbers 4:48

48even those who were counted of them, were eight thousand five hundred eighty.

Arminian

The total is 8,580, which matches the clan figures (2,750 + 2,630 + 3,200). Arminians note the exact accounting. This total represents the Levites’ working force, distinct from the 22,000 counted from one month old in chapter 3.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions note that, unlike 3:39, this total matches its parts, and they agree on the verse.

Numbers 4:49

49According to the commandment of the LORD they were counted by Moses, everyone according to his service and according to his burden. Thus they were counted by him, as the LORD commanded Moses.

Arminian

Each was counted ‘according to his service and according to his burden’, as the LORD commanded. Arminians see God appointing each person to a specific task, a dignity for every servant.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that God assigns each servant his work. Both traditions connect this with the varied gifts and callings in Christ’s body (Romans 12:4–8) and agree on the verse.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
