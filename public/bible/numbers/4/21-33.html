Passage

Numbers 4

Verses 21–33 · WEB

Numbers 4:21–33 gives the census instructions and duties for the Gershonites and Merarites. The Gershonites carry the fabrics (curtains, coverings, screens, and hangings), and the Merarites carry the frame (boards, bars, pillars, sockets, pins, and cords). Both clans work under Ithamar son of Aaron. Arminian and Reformed readers agree that the passage shows God valuing every kind of service and assigning it in an orderly way under appointed oversight.

Numbers 4:21

21The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

A divine speech introduces the Gershonite census. Arminians see God continuing to order every part of the service. The Gershonites’ duties come next, after the Kohathites’ more dangerous work has been arranged.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions agree on the function of the verse. The same formula introduced the Kohathite instructions in verse 1, which ties the sections together.

Numbers 4:22

22“Take a census of the sons of Gershon also, by their fathers’ houses, by their families;

Arminian

The Gershonites are counted by fathers’ houses and families. Arminians see each family included in God’s plan. The wording repeats verse 2 so that every clan is organized the same way.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. Counting by fathers’ houses reflects Israel’s family-based social structure.

Numbers 4:23

23you shall count them from thirty years old and upward until fifty years old: all who enter in to wait on the service, to do the work in the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

Men from thirty to fifty who serve in the Tent of Meeting are counted. Arminians see the same standard applied consistently to each clan.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The phrase ‘to wait on the service’ may carry a military image of serving in ranks, though interpreters differ on how much to make of it.

Numbers 4:24

24“This is the service of the families of the Gershonites, in serving and in bearing burdens:

Arminian

The Gershonites’ service is ‘in serving and in bearing burdens’. Arminians see faithful labor, even carrying loads, as service to God.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that ordinary labor offered in God’s service honors him. Both traditions agree on the verse. ‘Bearing burdens’ shows that physical labor was a recognized form of sacred duty.

Numbers 4:25

25they shall carry the curtains of the tabernacle and the Tent of Meeting, its covering, the covering of sealskin that is on it, the screen for the door of the Tent of Meeting,

Arminian

They carry the curtains, tent, coverings, and entrance screen. Arminians see trusted responsibility for the tabernacle’s fabrics.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. These fabrics were the visible coverings, so the Gershonites handled what onlookers would see of the tabernacle.

Numbers 4:26

26the hangings of the court, the screen for the door of the gate of the court which is by the tabernacle and around the altar, their cords, and all the instruments of their service, and whatever shall be done with them. They shall serve in there.

Arminian

They also carry the court hangings, gate screen, cords, and instruments, and ‘serve in there’. Arminians see thorough, faithful service.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions read the verse as completing the Gershonite list and agree on it. The list repeats 3:25–26 almost exactly, which confirms that the Gershonite duties were consistent.

Numbers 4:27

27At the commandment of Aaron and his sons shall be all the service of the sons of the Gershonites, in all their burden and in all their service; and you shall appoint their duty to them in all their responsibilities.

Arminian

Their work is under the direction of Aaron and his sons. Arminians see willing service under appointed leadership. The priests’ direction ensured that the Levites knew exactly what to carry.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the verse establishes ordered authority. Both traditions agree on its meaning. The priests appointed the tasks, so no Levite needed to guess his duty.

Numbers 4:28

28This is the service of the families of the sons of the Gershonites in the Tent of Meeting. Their duty shall be under the hand of Ithamar the son of Aaron the priest.

Arminian

The Gershonites serve under Ithamar son of Aaron. Arminians see accountable oversight provided for every group. Ithamar’s oversight spreads responsibility among Aaron’s surviving sons.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions note that Ithamar also oversaw the tabernacle accounts (Exodus 38:21) and agree on the verse.

Numbers 4:29

29“As for the sons of Merari, you shall count them by their families, by their fathers’ houses;

Arminian

The Merarites are counted by families and fathers’ houses. Arminians see God’s care extending to each clan. The Merarites are counted on the same basis as the other clans.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on it. The verse repeats the structure of verses 2 and 22 so that the census is uniform.

Numbers 4:30

30you shall count them from thirty years old and upward even to fifty years old—everyone who enters on the service, to do the work of the Tent of Meeting.

Arminian

Men from thirty to fifty who serve are counted. Arminians note the same consistent standard. The age range from thirty to fifty matches that of the other two clans.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Both traditions agree on the verse. The uniform standard ensures that the heaviest work is done by mature men.

Numbers 4:31

31This is the duty of their burden, according to all their service in the Tent of Meeting: the tabernacle’s boards, its bars, its pillars, its sockets,

Arminian

The Merarites carry the boards, bars, pillars, and sockets. Arminians see heavy work honored as part of worship. The Merarites’ loads were the heaviest of the tabernacle’s parts.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note that 7:8 later provides wagons for this heavy load and agree on the verse.

Numbers 4:32

32the pillars of the court around it, their sockets, their pins, their cords, with all their instruments, and with all their service. You shall appoint the instruments of the duty of their burden to them by name.

Arminian

The court pillars, sockets, pins, and cords are assigned ‘by name’. Arminians see personal responsibility for specific items.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that assigning items by name promotes accountability. Both traditions agree on the verse. Naming the items prevented confusion or loss during the frequent moves in the wilderness.

Numbers 4:33

33This is the service of the families of the sons of Merari, according to all their service in the Tent of Meeting, under the hand of Ithamar the son of Aaron the priest.”

Arminian

The Merarites serve under Ithamar. Arminians see the same accountable oversight. Gershon and Merari both work under Ithamar, while Eleazar oversees the Kohathites’ holy things (4:16).

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions read the verse as closing the instructions and agree on it. The repeated phrase ‘under the hand of Ithamar’ links verse 28 and verse 33.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
