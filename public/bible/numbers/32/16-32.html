Passage

Numbers 32

Verses 16–32 · WEB

The two tribes propose a compromise: they will build cities and sheepfolds for their families and flocks, but their fighting men will cross the Jordan ahead of Israel and not return until every tribe has received its inheritance. Moses accepts on that condition, warning, “be sure your sin will find you out” if they fail. He passes the agreement on to Eleazar, Joshua, and the tribal heads, and the tribes repeat their pledge.

Numbers 32:16

16They came near to him, and said, “We will build sheepfolds here for our livestock, and cities for our little ones;

Arminian

The tribes propose to build sheepfolds and cities for their families. Arminian readers see a constructive response to Moses’ rebuke. Both traditions agree that they now propose a compromise rather than insisting on their first request.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the tribes listen to correction and adjust their plan. Shared ground: their proposal shows a willingness to honor their obligation to Israel while still providing for their families.

Numbers 32:17

17but we ourselves will be ready armed to go before the children of Israel, until we have brought them to their place. Our little ones shall dwell in the fortified cities because of the inhabitants of the land.

Arminian

They pledge to go armed ahead of Israel until the other tribes are settled, while their families stay in fortified cities. Arminian readers see a genuine commitment. Both traditions agree that the pledge addresses Moses’ concern about discouraging Israel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise to go before Israel as an act of solidarity. Shared ground: the tribes offer to bear an even greater burden, leading the way into Canaan, while protecting their families in the east.

Numbers 32:18

18We will not return to our houses until the children of Israel have all received their inheritance.

Arminian

They will not return home until every tribe has received its inheritance. Arminian readers see a promise of perseverance in shared duty. Both traditions agree that this pledge was later kept (Joshua 22:1–4).

Reformed

Reformed readers see the commitment fulfilled under Joshua, demonstrating faithfulness to their word. Shared ground: the tribes bind themselves to the welfare of their brothers before their own rest. Their own rest waits on their brothers’ rest.

Numbers 32:19

19For we will not inherit with them on the other side of the Jordan and beyond, because our inheritance has come to us on this side of the Jordan eastward.”

Arminian

They will not inherit west of the Jordan, because their inheritance is on the east. Arminian readers note that they accept the trade-off clearly. Both traditions agree that the arrangement is stated openly to avoid later disputes.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the tribes renounce any claim west of the Jordan. Shared ground: the clarity of the agreement helps preserve unity, even as the eastern settlement creates a geographical separation that later texts address (Joshua 22:10–34).

Numbers 32:20

20Moses said to them: “If you will do this thing, if you will arm yourselves to go before the LORD to the war,

Arminian

Moses responds with a conditional promise: if they arm themselves to go before the LORD to war. Arminian readers see the agreement depending on their faithful action. Both traditions agree that the condition is essential to the arrangement.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Moses frames the agreement as service “before the LORD,” not merely before Israel. Shared ground: the conditional structure makes clear that the land east of the Jordan depends on keeping their pledge.

Numbers 32:21

21and every one of your armed men will pass over the Jordan before the LORD until he has driven out his enemies from before him,

Arminian

Every armed man must cross the Jordan until God drives out his enemies. Arminian readers note that the conquest is God’s work carried out through human obedience. Both traditions agree that the phrase “before the LORD” stresses divine leadership.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God as the one who drives out the enemies, with Israel as his instrument. Shared ground: the tribes’ participation is real, but the victory belongs to the LORD.

Numbers 32:22

22and the land is subdued before the LORD; then afterward you shall return, and be clear of obligation to the LORD and to Israel. Then this land shall be your possession before the LORD.

Arminian

Once the land is subdued, they may return and be free of obligation, and the land will be theirs. Arminian readers see a fair and generous outcome. Both traditions agree that fulfilling the obligation secures both their inheritance and their standing.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise of being “clear of obligation to the LORD and to Israel” as a reminder that obligations run in both directions. Shared ground: faithfulness to the agreement brings rest and secure possession.

Numbers 32:23

23“But if you will not do so, behold, you have sinned against the LORD; and be sure your sin will find you out.

Arminian

If they fail, they will have sinned against the LORD, and “be sure your sin will find you out.” Arminian readers see personal accountability. Both traditions agree that the warning means their failure would bring consequences, not merely that it would be discovered.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the warning as a statement of God’s moral order: sin brings its own exposure and judgment. Shared ground: the proverb has a wider application, but here it refers specifically to breaking the pledge to fight alongside Israel.

Numbers 32:24

24Build cities for your little ones, and folds for your sheep; and do that which has proceeded out of your mouth.”

Arminian

Moses tells them to build cities and folds and to do what they have promised. Arminian readers note the emphasis on keeping one’s word. Both traditions agree that Moses’ instruction reverses their order, mentioning children before sheep.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Moses’ wording puts the little ones first, perhaps a gentle correction of priorities, though this is an inference. Shared ground: the tribes must fulfill what “has proceeded out of your mouth.”

Numbers 32:25

25The children of Gad and the children of Reuben spoke to Moses, saying, “Your servants will do as my lord commands.

Arminian

The tribes reply that they will do as Moses commands. Arminian readers see willing submission to Moses’ authority. Both traditions agree that the response shows a change from their first request.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the tribes accepting Moses’ terms as God’s appointed leader. Shared ground: their reply reflects humility and commitment, resolving the tension raised in 32:6–15. Their language of “your servants” and “my lord” shows respect for Moses’ role.

Numbers 32:26

26Our little ones, our wives, our flocks, and all our livestock shall be there in the cities of Gilead;

Arminian

Their families and flocks will remain in the cities of Gilead. Arminian readers note the practical arrangements for those who stay behind. Both traditions agree that the families’ safety is considered.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the plan provides for both family and community duty. Shared ground: the tribes do not neglect their households while fulfilling their obligation to Israel. Wives are named alongside children, so the whole household is in view.

Numbers 32:27

27but your servants will pass over, every man who is armed for war, before the LORD to battle, as my lord says.”

Arminian

Every armed man will cross over before the LORD to battle, as Moses said. Arminian readers see a full commitment, with no partial participation. Both traditions agree that the phrase “before the LORD” again places the campaign under God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the tribes echoing Moses’ own terms, signaling agreement. Shared ground: the soldiers will go before the LORD, and the repetition reinforces the solemnity of the commitment. No armed man is excused from crossing over.

Numbers 32:28

28So Moses commanded concerning them to Eleazar the priest, and to Joshua the son of Nun, and to the heads of the fathers’ households of the tribes of the children of Israel.

Arminian

Moses commands Eleazar, Joshua, and the tribal heads concerning the agreement. Arminian readers note that the arrangement must outlive Moses. Both traditions agree that involving Joshua ensures continuity after Moses’ death.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Moses passing the responsibility to the next leaders, consistent with Joshua’s commissioning (27:18–23). Shared ground: the agreement is made public and binding on Israel’s future leadership. Moses will not cross the Jordan himself (27:12–14).

Numbers 32:29

29Moses said to them, “If the children of Gad and the children of Reuben will pass with you over the Jordan, every man who is armed to battle before the LORD, and the land is subdued before you, then you shall give them the land of Gilead for a possession;

Arminian

If Gad and Reuben cross over armed and the land is subdued, they are to receive Gilead. Arminian readers note the clear condition. Both traditions agree that the terms are restated for the leaders who will enforce them.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the legal precision of the restatement. Shared ground: the leaders must give the land only when the condition is met, ensuring fairness to all tribes. Conditions protect both the eastern tribes and their brothers.

Numbers 32:30

30but if they will not pass over with you armed, they shall have possessions among you in the land of Canaan.”

Arminian

If they do not cross armed, they will receive land in Canaan instead. Arminian readers see the alternative as protecting Israel’s unity. Both traditions agree that the arrangement prevents the eastern tribes from avoiding the shared task.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the alternative as ensuring that every tribe participates in the conquest. Shared ground: the agreement guards against division, and both outcomes keep the tribes bound to Israel’s mission.

Numbers 32:31

31The children of Gad and the children of Reuben answered, saying, “As the LORD has said to your servants, so will we do.

Arminian

The tribes answer, “As the LORD has said to your servants, so will we do.” Arminian readers see willing acceptance of God’s word through Moses. Both traditions agree that they recognize Moses’ terms as carrying divine authority.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that the tribes attribute the terms to the LORD. Shared ground: their response treats the agreement as obedience to God, not merely a deal with Moses. Their answer echoes the language of covenant obedience found elsewhere in the Pentateuch.

Numbers 32:32

32We will pass over armed before the LORD into the land of Canaan, and the possession of our inheritance shall remain with us beyond the Jordan.”

Arminian

They pledge to cross armed into Canaan while their inheritance remains east of the Jordan. Arminian readers see a final, clear commitment. Both traditions agree that the pledge sets the stage for the settlement recorded in the next section.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the pledge as fulfilled later in Joshua 4:12, where Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh cross armed before Israel. Shared ground: the agreement is concluded, and the narrative turns to the land itself.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
