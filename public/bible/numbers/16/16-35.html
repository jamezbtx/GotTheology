Passage

Numbers 16

Verses 16–35 · WEB

The test takes place at the Tent of Meeting. The LORD’s glory appears, Moses and Aaron intercede for the congregation, the people are told to separate from the rebels’ tents, the ground swallows Dathan, Abiram, and Korah’s household, and fire consumes the 250 men offering incense. Both traditions see God vindicating his chosen servants and judging rebellion, while Moses and Aaron model intercession.

Numbers 16:16

16Moses said to Korah, “You and all your company go before the LORD, you, and they, and Aaron, tomorrow.

Arminian

Moses tells Korah and his company to appear before the LORD tomorrow with Aaron. Arminian readers see the test set before God. Human dispute is referred to divine judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s presence as the place of decision. Both traditions read the verse as shared ground. Aaron stands among them, so God’s verdict will be visible to everyone present.

Numbers 16:17

17Each man take his censer and put incense on it, and each man bring before the LORD his censer, two hundred fifty censers; you also, and Aaron, each with his censer.”

Arminian

Each man brings his censer, 250 in all, with Aaron also. Arminian readers see all claimants given an equal test. God will choose. Aaron participates on equal footing.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the fairness of the procedure. Both traditions share this reading. Every claimant brings the same offering, so the outcome will clearly rest on God’s choice.

Numbers 16:18

18They each took his censer, and put fire in it, and laid incense on it, and stood at the door of the Tent of Meeting with Moses and Aaron.

Arminian

They take censers with fire and incense and stand at the tent door. Arminian readers see the rebels presuming to act as priests. They approach God on their own terms.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see unauthorized worship. Both traditions read the verse as shared ground. Offering incense was a priestly act, so their action itself expresses the claim they are making.

Numbers 16:19

19Korah assembled all the congregation opposite them to the door of the Tent of Meeting. The LORD’s glory appeared to all the congregation.

Arminian

Korah gathers the congregation, and the LORD’s glory appears. Arminian readers see Korah rallying the people. God’s presence now intervenes. The congregation sides with Korah.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s glory revealed in judgment. Both traditions share this reading. The appearance of the LORD’s glory signals that God himself will now judge the dispute.

Numbers 16:20

20The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron. Arminian readers see God addressing his servants directly. A decision is coming. Moses and Aaron are addressed together.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see divine initiative in the crisis. Both traditions read the verse as a transition. God speaks to both of the leaders whose authority had been challenged.

Numbers 16:21

21“Separate yourselves from among this congregation, that I may consume them in a moment!”

Arminian

God tells them to separate so he may consume the congregation. Arminian readers see the seriousness of corporate rebellion. The people have joined Korah. The whole congregation is at risk.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s holiness confronting sin. Both traditions read the threat in light of the intercession that follows. The threat shows how widely the rebellion had spread among the people.

Numbers 16:22

22They fell on their faces, and said, “God, the God of the spirits of all flesh, shall one man sin, and will you be angry with all the congregation?”

Arminian

Moses and Aaron fall down and plead: shall one man sin and you be angry with all? Arminian readers see intercession for the people. God’s servants pray even for those who opposed them.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see intercession appealing to God as God of the spirits of all flesh. Both traditions see this as a model of mediating prayer.

Numbers 16:23

23The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses. Arminian readers see God responding to intercession. The judgment is narrowed. God responds to the plea of verse 22. Prayer matters.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God answering prayer. Both traditions read the verse as shared ground. The command that follows narrows the judgment to the leaders of the rebellion.

Numbers 16:24

24“Speak to the congregation, saying, ‘Get away from around the tent of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram!’ ”

Arminian

The congregation must move away from the tents of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram. Arminian readers see God giving the people a chance to separate from rebellion. A choice is offered.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God distinguishing between the guilty and the rest. Both traditions share this reading. Separating from the rebels’ tents becomes the way for others to escape judgment.

Numbers 16:25

25Moses rose up and went to Dathan and Abiram; and the elders of Israel followed him.

Arminian

Moses goes to Dathan and Abiram, followed by the elders. Arminian readers see Moses going to those who refused to come. He pursues them one last time.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see leadership acting with the elders’ support. Both traditions read the verse as shared ground. Moses goes in person to those who would not come, accompanied by Israel’s elders.

Numbers 16:26

26He spoke to the congregation, saying, “Depart, please, from the tents of these wicked men, and touch nothing of theirs, lest you be consumed in all their sins!”

Arminian

Moses urges the people to depart from the wicked men’s tents and touch nothing. Arminian readers see separation from sin as necessary to avoid sharing its judgment. Association has consequences.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see a call to holiness echoed in 2 Corinthians 6:17. Both traditions share this reading. Touching nothing of theirs marks a clean break from their sin.

Numbers 16:27

27So they went away from the tent of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram, on every side. Dathan and Abiram came out, and stood at the door of their tents with their wives, their sons, and their little ones.

Arminian

The people withdraw, and Dathan and Abiram stand at their tents with their families. Arminian readers see the people choosing to separate. The rebels remain defiant.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the scene set for judgment. Both traditions read the verse soberly. The households stand with the rebels at their tent doors, which heightens the sorrow of the judgment that follows.

Numbers 16:28

28Moses said, “Hereby you shall know that the LORD has sent me to do all these works; for they are not from my own mind.

Arminian

Moses says this will prove the LORD sent him. Arminian readers see Moses submitting his authority to God’s verdict. He claims no self-made mission. His authority rests on God’s sending.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God vindicating his messenger. Both traditions share this reading. Moses insists that his works come from God, not from personal ambition, and lets God confirm it.

Numbers 16:29

29If these men die the common death of all men, or if they experience what all men experience, then the LORD hasn’t sent me.

Arminian

If they die a normal death, the LORD has not sent Moses. Arminian readers see a public test. Moses stakes everything on God’s action. The test is public and clear.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the clarity of the sign. Both traditions read the verse as shared ground. An ordinary death would disprove Moses’ claim; only a unique act of God will confirm it.

Numbers 16:30

30But if the LORD makes a new thing, and the ground opens its mouth, and swallows them up with all that belong to them, and they go down alive into Sheol, then you shall understand that these men have despised the LORD.”

Arminian

If the ground swallows them into Sheol, they have despised the LORD. Arminian readers see a unique judgment confirming God’s choice. The WEB footnote explains Sheol as the place of the dead.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see divine judgment revealing the truth. Both traditions share this reading. The new thing God creates will show who has despised the LORD.

Numbers 16:31

31As he finished speaking all these words, the ground that was under them split apart.

Arminian

As Moses finishes, the ground splits. Arminian readers see God answering immediately. The word is confirmed. There is no delay between word and fulfillment. God acts.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s power over creation. Both traditions read the verse as shared ground. The immediacy of the event confirms that Moses spoke for God.

Numbers 16:32

32The earth opened its mouth and swallowed them up with their households, all of Korah’s men, and all their goods.

Arminian

The earth swallows them with their households and goods. Arminian readers feel the severity of the judgment. Numbers 26:11 notes that Korah’s sons did not die.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the gravity of rebellion against God. The later sons of Korah appear in the Psalms. Both traditions share this reading and avoid speculation beyond the text.

Numbers 16:33

33So they, and all that belonged to them went down alive into Sheol. The earth closed on them, and they perished from among the assembly.

Arminian

They go down alive into Sheol, and the earth closes. Arminian readers see judgment final and public. They perish from the assembly. The judgment is complete and public.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the finality of the judgment. Both traditions read the verse soberly. They perish from among the assembly, removed from the community they tried to lead.

Numbers 16:34

34All Israel that were around them fled at their cry; for they said, “Lest the earth swallow us up!”

Arminian

Israel flees in fear of being swallowed. Arminian readers see terror at God’s judgment. The people recognize their danger. Fear spreads through the camp. Terror grips them.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see fear of God’s holiness. Both traditions share this reading. The people’s flight shows they recognize the judgment as God’s and fear for themselves.

Numbers 16:35

35Fire came out from the LORD, and devoured the two hundred fifty men who offered the incense.

Arminian

Fire from the LORD consumes the 250 who offered incense. Arminian readers see judgment on those who presumed to act as priests. God defends his appointed order.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see unauthorized worship judged, recalling Nadab and Abihu in Leviticus 10. Both traditions share this reading. Presumptuous worship meets the same holy fire that consumed Nadab and Abihu.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
