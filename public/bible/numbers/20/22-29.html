Passage

Numbers 20

Verses 22–29 · WEB

At Mount Hor God announces that Aaron will die without entering the land because of Meribah. Moses takes Aaron and Eleazar up the mountain before the congregation, transfers Aaron’s garments to Eleazar, and Aaron dies; Israel mourns thirty days. Both traditions see God’s word fulfilled and the priesthood continuing, and both note Hebrews 7, which contrasts priests ended by death with Christ’s permanent priesthood.

Numbers 20:22

22They traveled from Kadesh, and the children of Israel, even the whole congregation, came to Mount Hor.

Arminian

The whole congregation travels from Kadesh to Mount Hor. Arminian readers see the journey continuing toward the land despite the setbacks of the chapter. Loss does not halt the march.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God leading his people onward. Both traditions agree that the move sets the scene for another significant loss. The congregation moves together under God’s direction.

Numbers 20:23

23The LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron in Mount Hor, by the border of the land of Edom, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron at Mount Hor near Edom’s border. Arminian readers see God addressing both leaders together, as he did at Meribah.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God speaking to those he had appointed. Both traditions agree the location and audience prepare for the announcement about Aaron. The setting is solemn and deliberate.

Numbers 20:24

24“Aaron shall be gathered to his people; for he shall not enter into the land which I have given to the children of Israel, because you rebelled against my word at the waters of Meribah.

Arminian

Aaron will be gathered to his people and not enter the land because they rebelled against God’s word at Meribah. Arminian readers see the consequence announced at 20:12 now coming to pass.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s word fulfilled exactly. Both traditions agree that the text here names the sin as rebellion against God’s word, confirming the earlier explanation.

Numbers 20:25

25Take Aaron and Eleazar his son, and bring them up to Mount Hor;

Arminian

Moses is to take Aaron and Eleazar up Mount Hor. Arminian readers see God arranging an orderly transition while Aaron is still alive. Father and son go up together.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God providing for continuity in the priesthood. Both traditions agree that the ascent is a solemn act of obedience for all three.

Numbers 20:26

26and strip Aaron of his garments, and put them on Eleazar his son. Aaron shall be gathered, and shall die there.”

Arminian

Aaron’s garments are to be placed on Eleazar, and Aaron will die there. Arminian readers see the priestly office passing from father to son in God’s appointed way.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the office continuing even as its holder dies. Both traditions often note Hebrews 7:23–25, which contrasts priests prevented by death from continuing with Christ’s permanent priesthood.

Numbers 20:27

27Moses did as the LORD commanded. They went up onto Mount Hor in the sight of all the congregation.

Arminian

Moses does as commanded, and they climb the mountain in the sight of the congregation. Arminian readers see public obedience as the community watches the transition.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see Moses carrying out a painful command faithfully. Both traditions agree that the congregation’s presence makes the transfer visible and recognized. Obedience here is costly and personal.

Numbers 20:28

28Moses stripped Aaron of his garments, and put them on Eleazar his son. Aaron died there on the top of the mountain, and Moses and Eleazar came down from the mountain.

Arminian

Moses transfers the garments to Eleazar; Aaron dies on the mountain, and Moses and Eleazar come down. Arminian readers see the end of Aaron’s long service and the beginning of Eleazar’s.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s word fulfilled in Aaron’s death and Eleazar’s succession. Both traditions agree the brief account is marked by dignity and restraint.

Numbers 20:29

29When all the congregation saw that Aaron was dead, they wept for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel.

Arminian

The whole house of Israel weeps for Aaron thirty days. Arminian readers see the community honoring a leader whose failures did not erase his years of faithful service.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see genuine grief for God’s servant. Both traditions agree that mourning is a fitting response to loss, and that God’s purposes continue beyond any single leader.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
