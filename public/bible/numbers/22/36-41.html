Passage

Numbers 22

Verses 36–41 · WEB

Balak meets Balaam at the border of Moab, reproaches him for delaying, and promises him honor, but Balaam replies that he can speak only the word God puts in his mouth. Balak offers sacrifices and takes Balaam to a high place from which he can see part of Israel, setting the stage for the oracles. Both traditions see God’s control over a pagan seer and a hostile king, with broad shared ground.

Numbers 22:36

36When Balak heard that Balaam had come, he went out to meet him to the City of Moab, which is on the border of the Arnon, which is in the utmost part of the border.

Arminian

Balak goes out to meet Balaam at the border of Moab. Arminian readers note Balak’s eagerness.

The “City of Moab” may be Ar or another site; the identification is uncertain.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Balak’s urgency. He goes to the edge of his territory to receive the seer he has paid for.

The Arnon border, already important in chapter 21, frames the meeting.

Numbers 22:37

37Balak said to Balaam, “Didn’t I earnestly send for you to summon you? Why didn’t you come to me? Am I not able indeed to promote you to honor?”

Arminian

Balak asks why Balaam delayed and whether he can’t promote him. Arminian readers note that Balak still thinks in terms of reward.

He does not understand that Balaam is under another’s authority.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Balak’s outlook: honor and money control outcomes.

The story will show that God’s word cannot be bought. Balak can promote Balaam, but he cannot change what God has said about Israel.

Numbers 22:38

38Balaam said to Balak, “Behold, I have come to you. Have I now any power at all to speak anything? I will speak the word that God puts in my mouth.”

Arminian

Balaam says he can only speak what God puts in his mouth. Arminian readers note that Balaam acknowledges his limits.

This echoes God’s condition in verses 20 and 35.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sovereignty over Balaam’s speech. God puts the word in his mouth, language used of true prophets elsewhere (Deut 18:18; Jer 1:9).

Balaam has no power of his own to curse or bless.

Numbers 22:39

39Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kiriath Huzoth.

Arminian

Balaam goes with Balak to Kiriath Huzoth. Arminian readers note that the location is unknown.

The brief travel note moves the story toward the oracles of chapters 23–24.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the scene being set for God’s word to be spoken.

The place name occurs only here, so no attempt is made to locate it.

Numbers 22:40

40Balak sacrificed cattle and sheep, and sent to Balaam, and to the princes who were with him.

Arminian

Balak offers sacrifices and sends portions to Balaam and the princes. Arminian readers note the religious preparations.

Balak hopes to secure divine favor through ritual.

Reformed

Reformed readers see pagan worship attempting to secure favor from the divine.

The sacrifices cannot change God’s word. Balak treats religion as a means to his ends, while God has already declared Israel blessed (v. 12).

Numbers 22:41

41In the morning, Balak took Balaam, and brought him up into the high places of Baal; and he saw from there part of the people.

Arminian

Balak takes Balaam to the high places of Baal, where he sees part of Israel. Arminian readers note that the Hebrew may be a place name, Bamoth Baal (cf. Josh 13:17).

Balak hopes the view will aid the curse.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the stage set for blessing instead of cursing.

Balak shows Balaam only part of Israel, perhaps hoping a smaller view will make cursing easier. God will use the occasion for his own word in chapter 23.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
