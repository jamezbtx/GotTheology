Passage

Genesis 27

Verses 1–29 · ESV · Voices

Isaac, old and nearly blind, means to bless Esau. Rebekah and Jacob deceive him, and Jacob receives the blessing the Lord had already assigned—through sinful means God does not author as a lie.

Genesis 27:1

1When Isaac was old and his eyes were dim so that he could not see, he called Esau his older son and said to him, “My son”; and he answered, “Here I am.”

Arminian

Isaac is old, eyes dim, and he calls Esau “his older son.” Favoritism chooses the audience before the blessing is spoken.

Classical Arminians hear a real, responsible preference. Blindness does not cancel agency: the father summons the son he loves, not the son the oracle named (25:23).

Reformed

Age and dim sight set the scene, yet the first move is Isaac’s call to Esau. The household already leans (25:28).

Reformed readers remember that God’s purpose according to election stands behind this mess. Providence will overrule the father’s favoring without making that favoring holy.

Genesis 27:2

2He said, “Behold, I am old; I do not know the day of my death.

Arminian

“I do not know the day of my death” is honest frailty, then used to hurry a blessing. Mortality can sober a man or scramble him.

Arminian pastoral note: Isaac still chooses whom to bless. Uncertainty about the day is not an excuse to ignore the Lord’s earlier word.

Reformed

The patriarch feels death near and moves to settle the blessing on his own terms.

Reformed accent: even a fading saint can act against the oracle. God’s purpose for Jacob does not wait on Isaac’s accurate timetable.

Genesis 27:3

3Now then, take your weapons, your quiver and your bow, and go out to the field and hunt game for me,

Arminian

Quiver, bow, field, game—Isaac sends Esau into the work he loves. The errand is a real assignment Esau can fulfill.

Classical Arminians see ordinary vocation recruited into a biased plan. Hunting is not the sin; using it to lock in favor is.

Reformed

The hunt is means. Isaac will eat what Esau brings and then bless.

Reformed note: providence can let the older son go to the field so the younger can enter the tent. God is not the author of the scheme that will fill the gap.

Genesis 27:4

4and prepare for me delicious food, such as I love, and bring it to me so that I may eat, that my soul may bless you before I die.”

Arminian

“Delicious food, such as I love” joins appetite to blessing. Isaac’s love of savory game (25:28) still steers his soul.

Arminian warning: holy speech can be yoked to a favorite’s plate. The blessing he intends is a genuine act he is responsible to aim at the right son.

Reformed

Soul-blessing is covenantal, not a mood. Isaac tries to channel the promise toward Esau by a meal.

Reformed readers hear a father’s will leaning against 25:23. The Lord will let the words fall on Jacob without endorsing the appetite as theology.

Genesis 27:5

5Now Rebekah was listening when Isaac spoke to his son Esau. So when Esau went to the field to hunt for game and bring it,

Arminian

Rebekah is listening. Overhearing is not yet the crime, but it becomes fuel for a plot.

Classical Arminians name her as a moral agent: she hears, she will command. Election of Jacob does not license turning news into fraud.

Reformed

The narrator lets us see what Isaac did not: Rebekah hears the plan.

Reformed accent: God is not surprised. What she overhears will be used in a lie He does not condone, while His purpose according to election still stands.

Genesis 27:6

6Rebekah said to her son Jacob, “I heard your father speak to your brother Esau,

Arminian

She reports to “her son Jacob,” matching Isaac’s “his older son.” The split household now speaks in sides.

Arminian reading: Rebekah chooses a confidant and will recruit him. Grace does not erase her responsibility for what she does with the news.

Reformed

The mother’s address already picks a side.

Reformed note: the oracle named Jacob, yet this verse shows a woman taking matters into her own hands. Providence can employ a report without baptizing the coming deceit.

Genesis 27:7

7‘Bring me game and prepare for me delicious food, that I may eat it and bless you before the LORD before I die.’

Arminian

She quotes Isaac, adding “before the LORD.” The blessing is treated as worship even while she plans to steal it.

Classical Arminians hear the danger: naming the LORD over a scheme does not make the scheme holy.

Reformed

“Before the LORD” tells the truth about the blessing’s weight.

Reformed comfort: the Name Isaac invoked is bigger than Isaac’s intent. God will own His promise without owning Rebekah’s method.

Genesis 27:8

8Now therefore, my son, obey my voice as I command you.

Arminian

“Obey my voice as I command you.” Maternal authority is pressed into fraud.

Arminian pastoral: a child can refuse a sinful command. Jacob will still have to choose; Rebekah is responsible for issuing the order.

Reformed

Command language fills the tent. This is not God’s voice.

Reformed readers distinguish 25:23 from a mother’s plot. Providence overrules; it does not speak this command.

Genesis 27:9

9Go to the flock and bring me two good young goats, so that I may prepare from them delicious food for your father, such as he loves.

Arminian

Two goats from the flock will impersonate wild game. The plan is concrete, skilled, and hers.

Arminians refuse to call this faith. Trust in the oracle would wait on God; this is cooking a lie.

Reformed

She will manufacture the meal Isaac tied to the blessing.

Reformed accent: the goats are not the Lord’s appointed means. He can still give Jacob the blessing without having required a counterfeit stew.

Genesis 27:10

10And you shall bring it to your father to eat, so that he may bless you before he dies.”

Arminian

The aim is stated: so that he may bless you before he dies. Ends are named; means are fraud.

Arminian warning: a true promise must not be seized by deceit. Election is not cover for getting there first.

Reformed

She aims Jacob at the blessing Isaac meant for Esau.

Reformed reading: God’s purpose will land on Jacob, yet this sentence is still a human plot. The Lord does not need her timetable to keep 25:23.

Genesis 27:11

11But Jacob said to Rebekah his mother, “Behold, my brother Esau is a hairy man, and I am a smooth man.

Arminian

Jacob’s first objection is tactical: Esau is hairy, I am smooth. He fears detection more than deceit.

Classical Arminians hear a responsible pause that does not yet become repentance. He could have refused the whole scheme.

Reformed

The younger son names the risk, not the sin.

Reformed note: even the elect line can think like a trickster. God’s choice of Jacob is not Jacob’s virtue on display here.

Genesis 27:12

12Perhaps my father will feel me, and I shall seem to be mocking him and bring a curse upon myself and not a blessing.”

Arminian

He dreads a curse if his father feels him. Conscience flickers, then calculates.

Arminian pastoral: fear of being caught is not love of truth. He still stands at a live fork—honesty that trusts God, or blessing by fraud.

Reformed

Curse versus blessing is covenant language, now treated as a gamble.

Reformed accent: Jacob cannot earn what God promised, and he also cannot steal it as if the Lord were boxed in. The fear is real; so is the unbelief inside it.

Genesis 27:13

13His mother said to him, “Let your curse be on me, my son; only obey my voice, and go, bring them to me.”

Arminian

“Let your curse be on me”—Rebekah absorbs the risk to keep the plot moving.

Classical Arminians do not praise this as sacrifice. Taking a curse to enable a lie is still sin; a mother cannot transfer guilt by fiat.

Reformed

She offers to bear the curse so Jacob will obey.

Reformed readers hear reckless love, not atonement. God will neither need her oath nor treat the lie as her gift to providence.

Genesis 27:14

14So he went and took them and brought them to his mother, and his mother prepared delicious food, such as his father loved.

Arminian

He went, took, brought; she prepared. The verbs are ordinary and guilty.

Arminian reading: Jacob’s feet ratify the scheme. Agency is not erased by her cooking.

Reformed

The meal is prepared “such as his father loved.”

Reformed note: providence can let savory food be made without condoning the hands that make it. The blessing’s certainty does not cleanse the kitchen.

Genesis 27:15

15Then Rebekah took the best garments of Esau her older son, which were with her in the house, and put them on Jacob her younger son.

Arminian

Esau’s best garments go on Jacob. Identity is borrowed, not received.

Arminians name the theft of a brother’s honor as a real choice, not a necessary step in election.

Reformed

Clothes become a mask.

Reformed accent: God had already named Jacob; the wardrobe cannot improve the oracle. Providence will overrule Isaac’s eyes without endorsing the costume.

Genesis 27:16

16And the skins of the young goats she put on his hands and on the smooth part of his neck.

Arminian

Goatskins on hands and neck complete the impersonation. The body is falsified.

Classical Arminians refuse the excuse that God wanted Jacob blessed, so the skins were required. They were not.

Reformed

Touch will be deceived because skin was faked.

Reformed honesty: the Lord can use a blind father’s hands without authoring the goatskins. Means He hates can still fail to overturn a purpose He loves.

Genesis 27:17

17And she put the delicious food and the bread, which she had prepared, into the hand of her son Jacob.

Arminian

Food and bread are placed in Jacob’s hand—he must carry the lie in.

Arminian pastoral: the last step is still his. Grace that chose Jacob does not force this tray into Isaac’s room.

Reformed

The younger son is now equipped.

Reformed readers watch providence permit the entrance. Permission is not approval; the God of 25:23 will keep His word without signing this menu.

Genesis 27:18

18So he went in to his father and said, “My father.” And he said, “Here I am. Who are you, my son?”

Arminian

“My father.” “Who are you, my son?” The question is a mercy—an opening to tell the truth.

Classical Arminians hear a live chance Jacob can still take. He is not a puppet walking a script.

Reformed

Isaac’s uncertainty is already a crack in the plan.

Reformed note: God can unsettle the deceiver with a simple question. The father’s “Who are you?” does not make God the author of the answer that follows.

Genesis 27:19

19Jacob said to his father, “I am Esau your firstborn. I have done as you told me; now sit up and eat of my game, that your soul may bless me.”

Arminian

“I am Esau your firstborn.” The lie is plain, plus a fake obedience. He claims the blessing under a stolen name.

Arminian warning: this is fraud, not faith. Grace does not erase the responsibility of that sentence.

Reformed

He takes Esau’s name and Esau’s errand in one breath.

Reformed accent: Jacob does not earn the blessing here; he sins toward it. God’s purpose stands in spite of the lie, not because the lie was needed.

Genesis 27:20

20But Isaac said to his son, “How is it that you have found it so quickly, my son?” He answered, “Because the LORD your God granted me success.”

Arminian

Pressed on speed, Jacob drags the LORD’s name into the lie. Pious speech now covers deceit.

Classical Arminians hear the worst turn yet. Using the Lord’s name as camouflage is the abuse this chapter warns against: election as cover for fraud.

Reformed

He attributes the fraud’s timing to YHWH.

Reformed grief: providence is real, and taking God’s name in vain is still sin. The Lord may have allowed a quick return; He did not authorize this testimony.

Genesis 27:21

21Then Isaac said to Jacob, “Please come near, that I may feel you, my son, to know whether you are really my son Esau or not.”

Arminian

Isaac asks to feel him—doubt seeking certainty. The father is not merely a dupe; he tests.

Arminian reading: both men remain agents. Isaac’s caution is a real attempt at discernment; Jacob can still confess.

Reformed

Touch is summoned because the voice has already raised suspicion.

Reformed note: God does not owe Isaac a miracle of recognition. He may let dim eyes fail while still judging the lie.

Genesis 27:22

22So Jacob went near to Isaac his father, who felt him and said, “The voice is Jacob’s voice, but the hands are the hands of Esau.”

Arminian

“The voice is Jacob’s… the hands are Esau’s.” Isaac almost names the truth.

Classical Arminians honor this flicker of discernment. He could have stopped; he will talk himself past his own ears.

Reformed

Voice and hands disagree.

Reformed readers hear a man torn between senses, not a God who needed confusion. Providence can withhold clarity without authoring the costume that confused him.

Genesis 27:23

23And he did not recognize him, because his hands were hairy like his brother Esau’s hands. So he blessed him.

Arminian

He does not recognize him, “so he blessed him.” Recognition fails; blessing proceeds.

Arminian pastoral: a mistaken blessing is still Isaac’s act, and Jacob’s disguise is still Jacob’s guilt. God is not the one who put hair on those hands.

Reformed

Hebrews 11:20 will later say Isaac blessed by faith—faith in God’s future, not faith in a trick.

Reformed accent: the blessing lands on the elect son through a father’s erring senses. Purpose stands; the deceit is not thereby made righteous.

Genesis 27:24

24He said, “Are you really my son Esau?” He answered, “I am.”

Arminian

“Are you really my son Esau?” “I am.” A second chance, a second lie.

Classical Arminians refuse to soften the word. Direct denial is a responsible choice, not a slip of nerves.

Reformed

Isaac asks again; Jacob doubles down.

Reformed note: confirmation by a lie does not create the blessing’s validity. God had already chosen; this “I am” is Jacob’s sin, not Jacob’s title-deed.

Genesis 27:25

25Then he said, “Bring it near to me, that I may eat of my son’s game and bless you.” So he brought it near to him, and he ate; and he brought him wine, and he drank.

Arminian

Eat, drink, then bless—Isaac binds the meal to the word. Jacob serves wine as part of the ruse.

Arminian reading: table fellowship is being counterfeited. Sharing food under a false name is still his act.

Reformed

The patriarch eats “his son’s game” that is not Esau’s.

Reformed accent: the meal proceeds under a misnomer, yet the God who owns the blessing is not fooled. He will honor His purpose without honoring the menu’s pretense.

Genesis 27:26

26Then his father Isaac said to him, “Come near and kiss me, my son.”

Arminian

“Come near and kiss me.” Affection is requested and will be used as cover.

Classical Arminians see how love-tokens can be recruited to a lie. Jacob can still refuse the kiss.

Reformed

Nearness and a kiss precede the poem of blessing.

Reformed readers do not treat the kiss as God’s idea. Providence may let a father embrace the wrong face; He does not command the fraud that made the face feel right.

Genesis 27:27

27So he came near and kissed him. And Isaac smelled the smell of his garments and blessed him and said, “See, the smell of my son is as the smell of a field that the LORD has blessed!

Arminian

Smell of garments, then blessing: “a field that the LORD has blessed.” Isaac reads Esau’s clothes as the Lord’s field.

Arminian note: sensory piety can misread. The LORD does bless the land; He did not perfume this disguise.

Reformed

The blessing begins with smell and ascribes it to the LORD.

Reformed comfort: the field-language is bigger than Esau’s cloak. God can speak true plenty over Jacob even when the father’s nose is mistaken.

Genesis 27:28

28May God give you of the dew of heaven and of the fatness of the earth and plenty of grain and wine.

Arminian

Dew, fatness, grain, and wine—ordinary gifts named as God’s giving. The content is generous and public.

Classical Arminians hear real petition: “May God give.” Isaac is asking, not magically creating; the words still pass through a deceived mouth.

Reformed

The blessing’s substance is God-given abundance.

Reformed accent: these goods belong to the promise-line. The Lord can grant dew and fatness to Jacob because He had already chosen him—not because Jacob earned grain by a costume.

Genesis 27:29

29Let peoples serve you, and nations bow down to you. Be lord over your brothers, and may your mother’s sons bow down to you. Cursed be everyone who curses you, and blessed be everyone who blesses you!”

Arminian

Peoples, nations, lordship over brothers, curse and blessing echoing 12:3. The words are Abraham-sized.

Arminian warning: Jacob has not merited this by deceit. The echo of Abram’s call is gift; using fraud to wear it is still sin.

Reformed

Nations bowing and the 12:3 formula show this is the covenant blessing, not a private wish.

Reformed confession: God’s purpose according to election stands. The Lord overrules Isaac’s intended target without making the lie the engine of the promise.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the stolen blessing and the word that still stands—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Genesis 27:1–29.

Arminian Notable voices

“Those that are blessed of God are kissed with the kisses of his mouth, and they do by love and loyalty kiss the son, Psa 2:12. 2. Upon occasion of the sweet smell of his garments he said, See the smell of my son is as the smell of a field which the Lord hath blessed—That is, like that of the most fragrant flowers and spices. Three things Jacob is here blessed with, Plenty, Ge 27:28.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Gen 27:27)

Reformed Notable voices

“The allegory of Ambrose on this passage is not displeasing to me. Jacob, the younger brother, is blessed under the person of the elder; the garments which were borrowed from his brother breathe an odour grateful and pleasant to his father. In the same manner we are blessed, as Ambrose teaches, when, in the name of Christ, we enter the presence of our Heavenly Father: we receive from him the robe of righteousness, which, by its odour, procures his favor; in short, we are thus blessed when we are put in his place.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Genesis (on Gen 27:27)
