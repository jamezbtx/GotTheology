Passage

Genesis 14

Verses 13–16 · ESV · Voices

News reaches Abram the Hebrew at Mamre. He musters 318 trained men born in his house, allies with Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre, pursues as far as Dan and Hobah, and brings Lot back with the people and the goods.

Genesis 14:13

13Then one who had escaped came and told Abram the Hebrew, who was living by the oaks of Mamre the Amorite, brother of Eshcol and of Aner. These were allies of Abram.

Arminian

An escapee tells Abram. The title “the Hebrew” marks him as a sojourner with a people-name; he lives by Mamre’s oaks among Amorite allies. News is a summons: he can ride or stay.

Classical Arminians hear responsible kinship. Grace has placed him in peace at Mamre; the message now asks for costly love. Allies are real partners, not props. Abram must decide whether the escaped word will become a rescue.

Reformed

Providence supplies a messenger and a title: Abram the Hebrew, rooted at Mamre with named allies. God has already woven Gentile friendship around the heir of promise.

Reformed accent: the report is means. The Lord who will give victory first gives news, a place, and confederates. Abram’s household is being called into a work God has prepared.

Genesis 14:14

14When Abram heard that his kinsman had been taken captive, he led forth his trained men, born in his house, 318 of them, and went in pursuit as far as Dan.

Arminian

Hearing produces leading forth. Kinship, not empire, moves him. Three hundred eighteen trained men born in his house is a catechized household willing to go—small against four kings, large enough to obey.

Arminian pastoral: this is grace-enabled courage, not bloodlust. Abram could have counted Lot’s Sodom choice as deserved. He pursues instead. Love of kinsman is a real act; the road to Dan is chosen mercy.

Reformed

The heir of promise arms a household army. “Born in his house” signals a people God has already given him. Three hundred eighteen against a flushed coalition is weakness God intends to use.

Reformed comfort: pursuit as far as Dan is faith’s fruit under sovereignty. The Lord who promised land now lets Abram taste a prelude of deliverance—Lot retrieved because God keeps the family of the promise.

Genesis 14:15

15And he divided his forces against them by night, he and his servants, and defeated them and pursued them to Hobah, north of Damascus.

Arminian

Night, division of forces, pursuit to Hobah: tactics are human and skillful. Servants fight with him. Defeat of the kings is not magic that erases planning; it is courage God assists.

Classical Arminians honor means. Abram uses darkness and surprise; he is responsible for the campaign’s conduct. Shared ground: the victory is the Lord’s kindness through a faithful household, not Abram’s right to plunder.

Reformed

Night attack and pursuit north of Damascus display more than guerrilla luck. God grants the strategy success against a larger, recent-victorious army.

Reformed reading: dividing forces is the method; the outcome is gift. The same Lord who called Abram from Haran now scatters kings from Hobah so that a kinsman can come home.

Genesis 14:16

16Then he brought back all the possessions, and also brought back his kinsman Lot with his possessions, and the women and the people.

Arminian

He brings back all—goods, Lot, Lot’s things, women, and people. Rescue is thorough. Charity aimed at a nephew overflows to captives Abram did not owe.

Arminian hope: grace-enabled love restores more than it was asked. Lot is truly returned; he remains a responsible man who will still have to choose how close to Sodom he lives. Shared confession: Abram’s sword served kinship, not a throne.

Reformed

Complete restoration is the Lord’s signature on the raid. Possessions, kinsman, women, people—nothing the coalition took is left as their boast.

Reformed assurance: God can empty Sodom and fill Abram’s hands in the same week, then soon teach him to empty those hands again. Lot’s return is mercy to the promised household and a stay of judgment for the people of the plain.

Key contrast Both praise the rescue as kinship under God, not imperial sport. Arminians typically stress Abram’s grace-enabled choice to pursue and Lot’s restored agency; Reformed readers typically stress that a small household army prevails because providence keeps the promise-line and retrieves its endangered kin.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Abram the Hebrew pursuing and recovering Lot—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Genesis 14:13–16.

Arminian Notable voices

“He armed his trained servants, born in his house—To the number of three hundred and eighteen: a great family, but a small army; about as many as Gideon's that routed the Midianites, Jud 7:7. He drew out his trained servants, or his catechized servants; not only instructed in the art of war, but instructed in the principles of religion; for Abram commanded his household to keep the way of the Lord.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Gen 14:14)

Reformed Notable voices

“But here various questions arise; as, whether it was lawful for Abram, a private person, to arm his family against kings, and to undertake a public war. I do not, however, doubt, that as he went to the war endued with the power of the Spirit, so also he was guarded by a heavenly command, that he did not transgress the bounds of his vocation. A similar prelude of what was to follow, we read in the case of Moses, when he slew the Egyptian, before he openly presented himself as the avenger and deliverer of his nation.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Genesis (on Gen 14:13)

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
