Passage

Genesis 46

Verses 28–34 · ESV · Voices

Judah is sent ahead to Joseph to show the way to Goshen. Joseph appears, falls on Israel’s neck, and weeps a good while; Israel says he can now die, having seen Joseph’s face. Joseph then coaches the brothers to tell Pharaoh they are shepherds and livestock keepers, so they may dwell in Goshen—every shepherd being an abomination to the Egyptians.

Genesis 46:28

28He had sent Judah ahead of him to Joseph to show the way before him in Goshen, and they came into the land of Goshen.

Arminian

Judah is sent ahead to Joseph to show the way to Goshen; they arrive. The brother who pledged himself now leads the last miles.

Responsible going-down still uses a human guide. Judah’s errand is real service, not a puppet’s walk.

Reformed

Judah ahead, then Goshen. The path Joseph named in chapter 45 is the path God now completes.

Providence uses Judah’s lead to land the family where preservation will happen. Arrival is the Lord’s steering, not luck.

Genesis 46:29

29Then Joseph prepared his chariot and went up to meet Israel his father in Goshen. He presented himself to him and fell on his neck and wept on his neck a good while.

Arminian

Joseph readies his chariot, meets Israel in Goshen, presents himself, and weeps on his neck a good while. Reunion is unhurried grief and joy.

Love’s tears are a free, human response. God has kept both men; they still must embrace. Enabling grace does not skip the neck and the weeping.

Reformed

The governor’s chariot serves a son’s reunion. Presentation and long weeping are the fruit of years God governed.

Covenant mercy takes time on a neck. The God who went down with Jacob now lets Joseph’s arms do what the night vision promised in another key.

Genesis 46:30

30Israel said to Joseph, “Now let me die, since I have seen your face and know that you are still alive.”

Arminian

“Now let me die, since I have seen your face and know that you are still alive.” Sight of Joseph is enough; the fear can rest.

Jacob’s words are honest surrender, not despair. He receives what God sincerely gave: the living son. Peace to die is gift, still spoken in his own mouth.

Reformed

Israel’s death-wish is contentment, not nihilism. Seeing Joseph’s face is seeing God’s keeping.

The night word about Joseph’s hand closing his eyes is already in view. Providence has brought the father to the son who will bury him.

Genesis 46:31

31Joseph said to his brothers and to his father’s household, “I will go up and tell Pharaoh and will say to him, ‘My brothers and my father’s household, who were in the land of Canaan, have come to me.

Arminian

Joseph will go up and tell Pharaoh that the Canaan household has come. He does not hide the family; he prepares a true report.

Responsible speech before power. Enabling wisdom still requires Joseph to go, tell, and not freelance a false identity.

Reformed

Joseph scripts the audience with Pharaoh. The court is a tool in God’s preservation.

Covenant care includes politics. The God who set Joseph next to Pharaoh now uses that office to house Israel.

Genesis 46:32

32And the men are shepherds, for they have been keepers of livestock, and they have brought their flocks and their herds and all that they have.’

Arminian

They are shepherds and livestock keepers; flocks, herds, and all they have have come. The occupation is truth, not a costume.

Honest vocation before Pharaoh. They need not pretend to be Egyptians; grace allows them to remain who they are.

Reformed

Shepherd identity is stated twice. God preserved a livestock people, not a court people.

Providence will turn that very trade into Goshen’s door. What they have always been becomes the means of distinct dwelling.

Genesis 46:33

33When Pharaoh calls you and says, ‘What is your occupation?’

Arminian

Pharaoh will ask, “What is your occupation?” Joseph anticipates a real question they must answer.

The brothers remain responsible for their words. Coaching is help, not a spell; they will still have to speak.

Reformed

The question is foreseen because God has placed Joseph inside Pharaoh’s habits.

Even the interview sits under providence. The Lord who steers the family also steers the conversation that will locate them.

Genesis 46:34

34you shall say, ‘Your servants have been keepers of livestock from our youth even until now, both we and our fathers,’ in order that you may dwell in the land of Goshen, for every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians.”

Arminian

Say they have been keepers of livestock from youth, they and their fathers, so they may dwell in Goshen—for every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians.

Truth that also protects: Goshen is pasture and a buffer. Responsible prudence uses Egypt’s distaste without lying. God can house them in the open without swallowing their calling.

Reformed

Goshen is the aim: dwell there because shepherds are an abomination to Egyptians. Social contempt becomes geographic mercy.

Providence turns Egyptian prejudice into a separate land for the covenant family. God keeps them distinct for preservation and for a later bringing-up.

Key contrast Both see Goshen as the fitting home. Arminians typically stress honest, prudent speech the brothers must actually give; Reformed readers typically stress that God uses Egypt’s scorn of shepherds to settle Israel apart for keeping and future exodus.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Joseph weeping on Israel’s neck, and Goshen for shepherds—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Genesis 46:28–34.

Arminian Notable voices

“Now let me die—Not but that it was farther desirable to live with Joseph, and to see his honour and usefulness; but he had so much satisfaction in this first meeting, that he thought it too much to desire or expect any more in this world.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Gen 46:30)

Reformed Notable voices

“After Joseph had gone forth to meet his father for the purpose of doing him honor, he also provides what will be useful for him. On this account he advises his father to make it known to Pharaoh that he and his sons were keepers of cattle. For, since the Egyptians held this occupation in abhorrence, he infers that Pharaoh will grant them a dwelling in the land of Goshen.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Genesis (on Gen 46:31)
