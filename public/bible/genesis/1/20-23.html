Passage

Genesis 1

Verses 20–23 · ESV

Day five fills the waters and skies with living swarms. Verses 20–23 introduce blessing for the first time—“Be fruitful and multiply”—spoken to sea creatures and birds according to their kinds. Life is not an accident of the elements; God creates it, names it good, and sends it forth with His benediction.

Genesis 1:20

20And God said, “Let the waters swarm with swarms of living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth across the expanse of the heavens.”

Arminian

God said, “Let the waters swarm with swarms of living creatures, and let birds fly….” Life fills seas and sky by speech. Swarm and flight are commanded abundance.

Arminian pastoral weight: God likes living things. The word that made space now fills it; generosity, not scarcity, is creation’s first note over the waters.

Reformed

Waters are told to swarm with living creatures, and birds to fly across the expanse. Lifeless elements receive living offspring by command.

Reformed accent: affinity of water and air does not explain the miracle. He who made the world from nothing may bring birds from waters; the word is heard, and the seas teem.

Genesis 1:21

21So God created the great sea creatures and every living creature that moves, with which the waters swarm, according to their kinds, and every winged bird according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

Arminian

God created the great sea creatures and every moving living thing according to kinds, and every winged bird according to its kind—and saw that it was good. Even the vast and fearsome are His good work.

Classical Arminians confess kinds and goodness together. Great creatures are not rival myths; they are created, sorted, and approved. The world’s wildness is not outside the Father’s art.

Reformed

“Created” reaches even formed life from the waters: great sea creatures, swarms, birds, each according to kind, all called good.

Reformed reading: kinds are God’s stable order, and the verb still names His work when He shapes life from the given mass. Nothing that swims or flies is self-originated.

Genesis 1:22

22And God blessed them, saying, “Be fruitful and multiply and fill the waters in the seas, and let birds multiply on the earth.”

Arminian

God blessed them, saying, “Be fruitful and multiply and fill….” Blessing is spoken enablement: fruitfulness is gift before it is instinct.

Arminian emphasis: the first blessing in Scripture is life’s increase under God’s word. It is not a bare wish; it truly helps creatures do what they could not give themselves—while remaining a benediction, a kindness addressed to them.

Reformed

God blessed them by commanding increase. Blessing here is not human-style prayer; by His purpose He effects what He says.

Reformed comfort: fecundity is infused by the word and rooted in their nature thereafter. The seas fill and birds multiply because the benediction took hold—governance as gift that works.

Genesis 1:23

23And there was evening and there was morning, the fifth day.

Arminian

Evening and morning, the fifth day. Waters and heavens now host blessed life, still awaiting land animals and humankind.

Classical Arminians receive the pause as wonder. The God who blesses swarms will soon bless image-bearers; the week teaches us to see life as given, not seized.

Reformed

The fifth day closes on God’s clock, after blessing has already gone out to sea and sky.

Reformed note: blessing and the day’s bound belong together. Increase is real, and so is the Lord’s timing; history does not run ahead of His “evening and morning.” The word that fills also measures the hours.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

From the tradition

Voices from both camps

Historic words from Arminian and Reformed teachers—so you hear each side in its own voice, not a caricature.

Arminian Notable voices

“I ascribe to grace the commencement, the continuance and the consummation of all good… The controversy… relates solely to the mode of operation, whether it be irresistible or not… I believe, according to the scriptures, that many persons resist the Holy Spirit and reject the grace that is offered.”
Jacobus Arminius · Declaration of Sentiments (1608)
“The grace or love of God, whence cometh our salvation, is FREE IN ALL, and FREE FOR ALL.”
John Wesley · Sermon 128, “Free Grace” (1739)
“That this grace of God is the beginning, continuance, and accomplishment of all good… But as respects the mode of the operation of this grace, it is not irresistible, inasmuch as it is written concerning many, that they have resisted the Holy Spirit.”
The Remonstrance · Article IV (1610)

Reformed Notable voices

“We call predestination God’s eternal decree, by which he determined with himself what he willed to become of each man. For all are not created in equal condition; rather, eternal life is foreordained for some, eternal damnation for others.”
John Calvin · Institutes of the Christian Religion 3.21.5
“I believe the doctrine of election, because I am quite certain that, if God had not chosen me, I should never have chosen Him; and I am sure He chose me before I was born, or else He never would have chosen me afterwards.”
Charles H. Spurgeon · “A Defense of Calvinism”
“But now that election operates according to grace, there is hope even for the most wretched. If work and reward were the standard of admission into the kingdom of heaven, its gates would be opened for no one.”
Herman Bavinck · Reformed Dogmatics, vol. 2

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
