Passage

Genesis 1

Verses 9–13 · ESV · Voices

Day three gathers the waters so dry land appears, then fills that land with vegetation according to kinds. Verses 9–13 give Earth and Seas their names, pronounce the land good, and let the earth sprout seed-bearing plants and fruit trees—still before the sun is mentioned. Habitable earth and fruitfulness come by the same creating word.

Genesis 1:9

9And God said, “Let the waters under the heavens be gathered together into one place, and let the dry land appear.” And it was so.

Arminian

God said, “Let the waters under the heavens be gathered… and let the dry land appear.” And it was so. Habitable earth arrives by command, a stage prepared for plants, animals, and finally image-bearers.

Classical Arminians hear provision before inhabitants. God freely makes a home; the gathering of waters is kindness, not a reluctant concession to chaos.

Reformed

Waters gather to one place and dry land appears because God said so. The sea’s bounds are His.

Reformed confession: we dwell on dry ground by command. The elements do not graciously recede on their own; paternal government holds the waters so that a world may be inhabited.

Genesis 1:10

10God called the dry land Earth, and the waters that were gathered together he called Seas. And God saw that it was good.

Arminian

God called dry land Earth and the gathered waters Seas, and saw that it was good. Names and a verdict: the split world is not a mistake.

Arminian pastoral reading: Earth and Seas are gifts with names, judged good before anyone farms or sails. Gratitude is the fitting first theology of geography.

Reformed

Naming Earth and Seas is further lordship; “good” is God’s own stamp on the arrangement of dry ground and gathered water.

Reformed comfort: the world’s map is not self-made. What God names and approves stands under His care—seas gathered, land given—so creaturely life will have a place.

Genesis 1:11

11And God said, “Let the earth sprout vegetation, plants yielding seed, and fruit trees bearing fruit in which is their seed, each according to its kind, on the earth.” And it was so.

Arminian

God said, “Let the earth sprout vegetation… each according to its kind.” Seed and fruit are in the plants from the start; kinds are His idea, not later human sorting only.

Classical Arminians affirm both the earth’s real fruitfulness and its dependence. The soil brings forth because God enables it; kinds are a generous stability, not a prison.

Reformed

The barren earth is told to sprout, with seed according to kind. Fruitfulness is word-given before sun or farmer.

Reformed accent: the earth had no germinating principle until God opened His mouth. What we now call nature’s law is the lingering force of that once-spoken command.

Genesis 1:12

12The earth brought forth vegetation, plants yielding seed according to their own kinds, and trees bearing fruit in which is their seed, each according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

Arminian

The earth actually brought forth vegetation according to kinds, and God saw that it was good. Command becomes harvest; kinds hold.

Arminian emphasis: secondary causes are real—the earth “brought forth”—under the God who spoke. Creaturely agency is not a threat to the Creator; it is His intended means.

Reformed

The earth brought forth as ordered: plants and trees according to their kinds, and God judged it good.

Reformed note: kinds are stable because God made them so. The earth’s obedience displays effectual speech; what He specified, the ground produced, and He called that yield good.

Genesis 1:13

13And there was evening and there was morning, the third day.

Arminian

Evening and morning, the third day. Land, seas, and a green world now stand, still waiting for lights, animals, and humankind.

Classical Arminians receive the day’s close as God’s pacing of gifts. The week is not rushed; fruitfulness is in place before those who will eat it exist.

Reformed

The third day ends as the first two did—by God’s marking of evening and morning. Land and green life now sit inside a counted day.

Reformed instinct: time remains His. The vegetated earth sits inside a week God counts, a reminder that history and harvest unfold on His schedule, not on an independent clock.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on dry land, seas, and the earth made fruitful—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Genesis 1:9–13.

Arminian Notable voices

“The third day's work is related in these verses; the forming the sea and the dry land, and making the earth fruitful. Hitherto the power of the Creator had been employed about the upper part of the visible world; now he descends to this lower world, designed for the children of men, both for their habitation, and their maintenance. How the earth was prepared to be a habitation for man by the gathering of the waters together, and making the dry land appear.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Gen 1:9)

Reformed Notable voices

“Let the earth bring forth grass Hitherto the earth was naked and barren, now the Lord fructifies it by his word. For though it was already destined to bring forth fruit, yet till new virtue proceeded from the mouth of God, it must remain dry and empty. For neither was it naturally fit to produce anything, nor had it a germinating principle from any other source, till the mouth of the Lord was opened.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Genesis (on Gen 1:11)
