Passage

Genesis 22

Verses 20–24 · ESV · Voices

After the Akedah, news reaches Abraham: Milcah has borne children to his brother Nahor. The genealogy names eight sons, including Bethuel, who fathered Rebekah, then four more by the concubine Reumah—quietly stocking the family line from which Isaac’s wife will come.

Genesis 22:20

20Now after these things it was told to Abraham, “Behold, Milcah also has borne children to your brother Nahor:

Arminian

“After these things” again: the mountain yields to a family report. Abraham hears that Nahor’s house has children too.

Classical Arminians hear providence in ordinary news. Grace that provided a ram also prepares a future wife for Isaac through relatives Abraham did not arrange today.

Reformed

The same narrative hinge—“after these things”—now opens a genealogy. God informs Abraham of Nahor’s fruitfulness.

Reformed framing: the oath’s “multiply” begins to show on the Haran side of the family. Nothing here is filler; the line of Rebekah is being named.

Genesis 22:21

21Uz his firstborn, Buz his brother, Kemuel the father of Aram,

Arminian

Names accumulate: Uz, Buz, Kemuel father of Aram. The promise-people’s story includes cousins offstage.

Arminian reading: God works in more households than the one we are watching. Attention to these names is a form of trust that no branch is wasted.

Reformed

Firstborn and brothers are listed with care. Aram is already a father’s title in the third name.

Reformed note: Scripture’s memory is covenantal. Even the collateral line is kept because the God of Abraham is ordering generations.

Genesis 22:22

22Chesed, Hazo, Pildash, Jidlaph, and Bethuel.”

Arminian

The list ends on Bethuel—the name that will matter for chapter 24. Most of these sons never reenter the spotlight.

Arminian pastoral take: faithfulness includes unnamed obedience in other houses. Grace can hide a key person inside a list.

Reformed

Bethuel is planted at the end of Milcah’s eight. The narrator is aiming at a daughter not yet mentioned.

Reformed providence: God stocks the family from which He will call Rebekah. The Akedah’s surviving son will not lack a chosen wife.

Genesis 22:23

23(Bethuel fathered Rebekah.) These eight Milcah bore to Nahor, Abraham’s brother.

Arminian

Parenthesis as gospel hint: Bethuel fathered Rebekah. Isaac’s future is already in the report Abraham hears.

Classical Arminians rejoice that after the knife is stayed, life is already being prepared. Hearers are invited to trust God’s timing in family stories they cannot yet see.

Reformed

The narrator cannot wait for chapter 24. Rebekah is named so readers know the oath is already threading the next marriage.

Reformed comfort: God provides not only a ram but a lineage. Eight born to Milcah include the mother of Israel’s next generation.

Key contrast Both see Rebekah named on purpose after Moriah. Arminians typically stress grace quietly preparing the next obedient household; Reformed readers typically stress God’s sovereign stocking of the line He has sworn to bless.

Genesis 22:24

24Moreover, his concubine, whose name was Reumah, bore Tebah, Gaham, Tahash, and Maacah.

Arminian

Reumah’s four sons close the chapter without romance. The family is larger than the covenant’s inner circle.

Arminian caution: not every named person carries the promise, yet all are under God’s world. The story returns us from the mount to the wide human household.

Reformed

A concubine’s sons are not erased from the record. The chapter that opened with “your only son” ends with many names.

Reformed close: election does not require amnesia about other branches. God has kept Isaac, named Rebekah, and still tells the truth about Nahor’s whole house.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Nahor’s sons, and Rebekah named in the family news—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Genesis 22:20–24.

Arminian Notable voices

“This is recorded here, To show that tho' Abraham saw his own family highly dignified with peculiar privileges, yet he did not look with contempt upon his relations, but was glad to hear of the increase and prosperity of their families. To make way for the following story of the marriage of Isaac to Rebekah, a daughter of this family.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Gen 22:20)

Reformed Notable voices

“Moses repeats, that Abraham, after having passed through this severe and incredible temptation, had a quiet abode in Beersheba. This narration is inserted, together with what follows concerning the increase of Abraham's kindred, for the purpose of showing that the holy man, when he had been brought up again from the abyss of death, was made happy, in more ways than one.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Genesis (on Gen 22:19)
