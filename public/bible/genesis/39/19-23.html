Passage

Genesis 39

Verses 19–23 · ESV · Voices

Potiphar’s anger sends Joseph to the king’s prison. The LORD is with him there too: steadfast love, favor with the keeper, charge of the prisoners, and success the LORD makes.

Genesis 39:19

19As soon as his master heard the words that his wife spoke to him, “This is the way your servant treated me,” his anger was kindled.

Arminian

He hears her words; anger kindles. No cross-examination is recorded. Arminian reading: masters remain accountable for rage that skips the truth. Joseph’s integrity does not control Potiphar’s verdict.

Reformed

Anger kindled by a lie still moves the story God is writing. Reformed note: Potiphar’s wrath is real and unjust; it is not outside providence. The next room is a prison the LORD already occupies.

Genesis 39:20

20And Joseph’s master took him and put him into the prison, the place where the king’s prisoners were confined, and he was there in prison.

Arminian

Taken, put, confined: he was there. The innocent suffer. Classical Arminians refuse to call the injustice good. Joseph must now live faithfully in a place he did not earn.

Reformed

The king’s prison is not a random hole. Reformed readers already see cupbearer and baker on this hallway. “He was there” is location under the God who was with him in the house.

Genesis 39:21

21But the LORD was with Joseph and showed him steadfast love and gave him favor in the sight of the keeper of the prison.

Arminian

The same refrain, now in a cell: the LORD was with Joseph. Steadfast love and favor are given. Arminians hear mercy that does not wait for release. You can receive God’s hesed inside a wrong sentence.

Reformed

Hesed in prison is covenant love, not a mood. Reformed confession: presence is not proved by promotion only. The LORD gives favor with the keeper as surely as He gave it with Potiphar.

Key contrast Both cling to “the LORD was with Joseph” in prison. Arminians typically stress mercy sincerely given in suffering, to be received by faith; Reformed readers typically stress sovereign hesed that does not leave the man God is sending toward Egypt’s throne.

Genesis 39:22

22And the keeper of the prison put Joseph in charge of all the prisoners who were in the prison. Whatever was done there, he was the one who did it.

Arminian

Charge of all, again. Trust repeats in a harder house. Arminian pastoral: faithfulness in the small overseeing is still required. Presence does not cancel the work.

Reformed

The pattern of verses 4–6 returns behind bars. Reformed accent: God is rehearsing Joseph’s future administration. Whatever was done there passed through the hands the LORD was training.

Genesis 39:23

23The keeper of the prison paid no attention to anything that was in Joseph’s charge, because the LORD was with him. And whatever he did, the LORD made it succeed.

Arminian

The keeper’s ease has the same cause as Potiphar’s: the LORD was with him. Arminians end where the chapter began—success as the LORD’s doing, and Joseph still doing the work. Prison has not expelled God.

Reformed

Inclusio: the LORD made it succeed. Reformed comfort: house and dungeon rhyme because God has not changed. The path from pit to prison is still the path of the God who will keep His promise to Abraham through this man.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on prison, steadfast love, and the LORD making Joseph prosper—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Genesis 39:19–23.

Arminian Notable voices

“God despiseth not his prisoners, Psa 69:33. No gates nor bars can shut out his gracious presence from his people. God gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison—God can raise up friends for his people even where they little expect them.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Gen 39:21)

Reformed Notable voices

“It appears, from the testimony of the Psalmist just cited, that Joseph's extreme sufferings were not immediately alleviated. The Lord purposely suffered him to be reduced to extremity, that he might bring him back as from the grave. We know that as the light of the sun is most clearly seen when we are looking from a dark place; so, in the darkness of our miseries, the grace of God shines more brightly when, beyond expectation, he succors us.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Genesis (on Gen 39:21)
