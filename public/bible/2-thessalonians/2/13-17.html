Passage

2 Thessalonians 2

Verses 13–17 · WEB

In contrast to those who reject truth, Paul thanks God for the beloved church’s election to salvation through the Spirit and faith. Gospel calling leads toward Christ’s glory, so the believers must hold fast to apostolic teaching. Both traditions affirm divine initiative and necessary faith, while differing over election and perseverance. The paragraph closes in prayer for gracious comfort and practical strength.

2 Thessalonians 2:13

13But we are bound to always give thanks to God for you, brothers loved by the Lord, because God chose you from the beginning for salvation through sanctification of the Spirit and belief in the truth,

Arminian

God’s loving choice brings salvation through the Spirit’s sanctifying work and belief in truth. Classical Arminian readings connect election with foreknown faith in Christ; corporate readings emphasize God’s choice of a believing people. None makes faith a meritorious payment.

Reformed

Reformed readers understand this choice as God’s unconditional election, realized through the Spirit’s work and faith. Belief and holiness are appointed means of salvation, not optional additions to an election detached from Christ.

Key contrast Both affirm God’s initiative, the Spirit’s sanctifying work, and the necessity of faith. Classical Arminian readings relate election to foreknown faith, with corporate readings stressing the believing people in Christ; Reformed readings ground faith in unconditional election.

2 Thessalonians 2:14

14to which he called you through our Good News, for the obtaining of the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Arminian

God calls through the gospel to share Christ’s glory. Arminian readers see a gracious summons genuinely received through faith, giving the church a reason to proclaim rather than presume.

Reformed

Reformed readers connect this call with God’s effective work bringing his chosen people to faith through the gospel. Both traditions regard proclamation, not speculation about hidden decrees, as the church’s task.

Key contrast Both affirm that God calls through the gospel. Arminian readers understand the saving summons as resistible; Reformed readers distinguish the outward summons from the effectual call that brings the elect to faith.

2 Thessalonians 2:15

15So then, brothers, stand firm and hold the traditions which you were taught by us, whether by word or by letter.

Arminian

The call to stand firm requires continuing allegiance to apostolic teaching. Arminian readers see a genuine responsibility to persevere in grace rather than assuming that past belief makes later faithfulness unnecessary.

Reformed

Reformed readers understand this command as a means God uses to preserve believers. Confidence in election strengthens rather than cancels the duty to hold fast to apostolic teaching.

Key contrast Both require persevering faithfulness to apostolic teaching. Arminian readers regard final abandonment of faith as a real danger; Reformed readers see the exhortation as a means by which God secures the perseverance of his people.

2 Thessalonians 2:16

16Now our Lord Jesus Christ himself, and God our Father, who loved us and gave us eternal comfort and good hope through grace,

Arminian

The Father and Christ have given lasting comfort and hope through grace. Arminian readers locate encouragement in prior divine love, not in the strength of their own resolve.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s love and gracious giving as the foundation of assurance. Hope rests in the giver’s goodness rather than in human achievement.

2 Thessalonians 2:17

17comfort your hearts and establish you in every good work and word.

Arminian

Paul asks God to steady hearts for good speech and action. Arminian readers see sustaining grace enabling practical obedience as believers continue trusting him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see divine strengthening producing real human obedience. Both traditions connect consolation with holiness rather than making comfort an escape from responsibility.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
