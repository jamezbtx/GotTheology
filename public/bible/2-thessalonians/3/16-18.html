Passage

2 Thessalonians 3

Verses 16–18 · WEB

Following firm correction, Paul closes with prayer for the Lord’s peace, a handwritten sign authenticating the letter, and a blessing of grace for all. These words answer the concern about misleading messages and keep discipline within pastoral care. Both traditions locate the church’s peace and continued life in Christ’s presence and grace.

2 Thessalonians 3:16

16Now may the Lord of peace himself give you peace at all times in all ways. The Lord be with you all.

Arminian

After correction Paul prays for peace for the whole church. Arminian readers see the Lord’s presence as necessary for reconciliation, not merely the absence of outward conflict.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise trace enduring peace to the Lord himself. His presence among all the believers is sought even while the community addresses disobedience.

2 Thessalonians 3:17

17I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand, which is the sign in every letter. This is how I write.

Arminian

Paul’s handwritten greeting authenticates the letter, answering concern about misleading communications. Arminian readers see a practical safeguard that supports discernment rather than credulity.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise value the apostolic authentication of this message. The church’s confidence belongs to trustworthy teaching, not to every claim made in Paul’s name.

2 Thessalonians 3:18

18The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.

Arminian

The letter ends with grace for all, including those needing correction. Arminian readers see every step of faithful response depending on the generous help of Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise hear dependence on Christ’s grace from beginning to end. Firm discipline and final blessing belong together in care for the whole church.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
