Passage

2 Thessalonians 3

Verses 6–15 · WEB

Paul addresses persistent disorder, especially unwillingness to work and disruptive meddling, by recalling the missionaries’ example and instructing corrective withdrawal. He distinguishes refusal to work from inability by speaking of unwillingness, and he preserves the right to ministry support. Both traditions should hold responsibility, continued generosity, and restorative brotherly admonition together without presuming an unstated cause for the disorder.

2 Thessalonians 3:6

6Now we command you, brothers, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you withdraw yourselves from every brother who walks in rebellion and not after the tradition which they received from us.

Arminian

Withdrawal addresses a brother’s persistent disorder contrary to apostolic instruction. Arminian readers see corrective discipline serving repentance, not license to abandon compassion or presume final condemnation.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise treat discipline as a serious means of correction within the community. The command concerns defiant conduct, not a warrant to exclude everyone who lacks paid employment.

2 Thessalonians 3:7

7For you know how you ought to imitate us. For we didn’t behave ourselves rebelliously among you,

Arminian

Paul’s example gives practical shape to his instruction. Arminian readers emphasize a life believers can imitate through grace rather than teaching that excuses irresponsible conduct.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise join doctrine to visible patterns of faithfulness. Apostolic authority is exercised through service and example, not simply by issuing commands.

2 Thessalonians 3:8

8neither did we eat bread from anyone’s hand without paying for it, but in labor and travail worked night and day, that we might not burden any of you.

Arminian

The missionaries worked hard rather than unnecessarily burdening their hosts. Arminian readers see costly love that protects others’ resources and gives credibility to instruction.

Reformed

Reformed readers also commend this willing labor as care for the church. Paul’s particular choice is an example of service, not a claim that receiving support is always wrong.

2 Thessalonians 3:9

9This was not because we don’t have the right, but to make ourselves an example to you, that you should imitate us.

Arminian

Paul explicitly preserves the right to support while choosing to model diligence. Arminian readers see Christian freedom used for others’ good, not a universal ban on supported ministry.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise distinguish a legitimate right from its voluntary relinquishment. The example commends self-denial without turning one missionary practice into an absolute rule.

2 Thessalonians 3:10

10For even when we were with you, we commanded you this: “If anyone is not willing to work, don’t let him eat.”

Arminian

The command concerns unwillingness to work, not inability through illness, disability, or lack of opportunity. Arminian readers see responsible effort and compassionate support as compatible obligations.

Reformed

Reformed readers make the same distinction between refusal and inability. The verse challenges willful dependence; it does not authorize withholding care from those who cannot provide for themselves.

2 Thessalonians 3:11

11For we hear of some who walk among you in rebellion, who don’t work at all, but are busybodies.

Arminian

The disorder includes meddling in place of productive labor. Arminian readers see a call to use time for service rather than burdening and disrupting neighbors.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise note that the concern is socially harmful conduct, not merely an employment category. Community life requires diligence without intrusive interference in others’ affairs.

2 Thessalonians 3:12

12Now those who are that way, we command and exhort in the Lord Jesus Christ, that they work with quietness and eat their own bread.

Arminian

The remedy is quiet, responsible work under Christ’s authority. Arminian readers see repentance taking a concrete form, enabling people to contribute rather than exploit generosity.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise connect gospel obedience with ordinary labor and peaceable conduct. The instruction seeks restored responsibility rather than humiliation for its own sake.

2 Thessalonians 3:13

13But you, brothers, don’t be weary in doing what is right.

Arminian

Others’ misuse of generosity must not make the faithful weary of doing good. Arminian readers encourage sustained love while maintaining the boundaries Paul has just required.

Reformed

Reformed readers also urge perseverance in good works amid disappointment. Correcting abuse should preserve genuine charity, not produce suspicion toward every person in need.

2 Thessalonians 3:14

14If any man doesn’t obey our word in this letter, note that man and have no company with him, to the end that he may be ashamed.

Arminian

The social boundary is intended to awaken an unresponsive person to the seriousness of disobedience. Arminian readers see a restorative warning, not a declaration that repentance is impossible.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise view this discipline as corrective rather than vindictive. The letter’s authority warrants a response, while the next verse limits how the offender is treated.

2 Thessalonians 3:15

15Don’t count him as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother.

Arminian

Paul explicitly forbids treating the offender as an enemy. Arminian readers see brotherly admonition holding accountability and hope of restoration together.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise insist that discipline retain familial concern. Neither doctrinal seriousness nor community boundaries justify cruelty or contempt.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
