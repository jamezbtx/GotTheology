Passage

Jonah 3

Verses 1–4 · WEB

The LORD’s word comes to Jonah a second time with the same summons. This time Jonah obeys and goes to Nineveh, described as an exceedingly great city of three days’ journey. A day into the city he proclaims, “In forty days, Nineveh will be overthrown!”

Jonah 3:1

1The LORD’s word came to Jonah the second time, saying,

Arminian

The LORD’s word came to Jonah the second time. Arminian readers stress God’s patience: the disobedient prophet is not discarded but commissioned again. Restoration after failure is a mark of God’s grace toward his servants, as later with Peter.

Reformed

Reformed readers also see grace in the second call. God’s purpose for Nineveh has not changed, and neither has his choice of messenger. God’s calling is not withdrawn because of the servant’s failure, which reflects the steadfastness of his purpose.

Jonah 3:2

2“Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and preach to it the message that I give you.”

Arminian

Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city repeats the first call. Now Jonah is to preach the message that I give you. Arminian readers note the emphasis on faithfully delivering God’s word rather than the prophet’s own preferences.

Reformed

Reformed readers stress that the message is God’s own word, not Jonah’s. The prophet is a herald who speaks what he is given. The power to change Nineveh lies in the word God supplies, not in the messenger.

Jonah 3:3

3So Jonah arose, and went to Nineveh, according to the LORD’s word. Now Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, three days’ journey across.

Arminian

So Jonah arose and went, according to the LORD’s word. The obedience is real, though chapter 4 will show his heart remains unconverted to God’s mercy. An exceedingly great city, three days’ journey across stresses the size of the task. Readers debate whether this means its diameter, its wider district, or the time needed to visit it.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that outward obedience can coexist with an inner reluctance that God must still address. The phrase about Nineveh’s size, literally great to God, is sometimes read as meaning great in God’s sight. That foreshadows 4:11, where God cares for the great city.

Jonah 3:4

4Jonah began to enter into the city a day’s journey, and he cried out, and said, “In forty days, Nineveh will be overthrown!”

Arminian

Jonah’s message is short: In forty days, Nineveh will be overthrown! It contains no explicit call to repent and no offer of mercy. Arminian readers often see an implied condition here, in line with Jeremiah 18:7–8: when God announces disaster on a nation that then turns from evil, he relents.

Reformed

Reformed readers also generally see the announcement as a warning that carries an implied condition, not an unconditional decree of destruction. God’s threats are means by which he calls people to repentance. The word overthrown can also mean turned or changed, which some commentators see as an ironic double meaning.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
