Passage

Daniel 2

Verses 31–45 · ESV · Voices

Daniel tells the forgotten dream: a great image of gold, silver, bronze, iron, and mixed feet, struck by a stone cut without hands. The image becomes chaff; the stone becomes a mountain filling the earth. Interpretation: Nebuchadnezzar is the head of gold; three kingdoms follow; in their days the God of heaven sets up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed. Historic Christian reading: the stone is Christ’s kingdom.

Daniel 2:31

31You saw, O king, and behold, a great image. This image, mighty and of exceeding brightness, stood before you, and its appearance was frightening.

Arminian

A great image, mighty, bright, frightening. Wesley will read four monarchies and then Christ’s kingdom. The terror is the king’s, not a cue for a culture-war on empire.

Arminian sight: a real vision of real kingdoms. The Most High who changes times (2:21) now shows their shape. Human empires stand and frighten; they are still creatures under the stone that is coming.

Reformed

Calvin: not with natural eyes; a specimen of revelation. Interpreters of moderate judgment explain it of four monarchies—Babylonian, Persian, Macedonian, Roman. God does not regard agreement among them, but the state of the world till Christ. One image because one world-course, not because the empires were one soul.

Reformed sight: the fright is God’s sting still working. Shared: a frightening image. The metals will be named; the stone will not be a millennial chart-fight.

Daniel 2:32

32The head of this image was of fine gold, its chest and arms of silver, its middle and thighs of bronze,

Arminian

Head gold, chest and arms silver, middle and thighs bronze. Wesley: he was the head of gold because of vast riches, long standing, fortunate to the last. Inferior kingdoms follow in time and peace, not a slogan about “civilization.”

Arminian metals: successive, creaturely realms. Gold is first, not final. The God of heaven gave the head (v. 37); the rest will arise by the same rule, with real armies and real years.

Reformed

Calvin: gold by comparison, because the world grows worse as it ages. We must not look at one man’s character (Cyrus’s valor) but the continued state of the empire. Experience shows degeneration. Shared names: Babylon, then Medes and Persians, then Greece.

Reformed metals: the decline is inside providence, not luck. Shared with Wesley: gold, silver, bronze as those three. Rome waits in the iron.

Daniel 2:33

33its legs of iron, its feet partly of iron and partly of clay.

Arminian

Legs iron; feet iron and clay. Wesley: the Roman kingdom, strong as iron, later divided, partly strong and partly weak. Division is history, not a modern map to fight over.

Arminian feet: a real mixture. Strength and brittleness together under the Most High. Human politics mix and fail; the stone will not need them to hold.

Reformed

Calvin: iron fits Roman cruelty, ambition, avarice—without a culture-war on “Rome” as a present cause. Clay in the feet: the ruin when God dispersed that monarchy; it fell by itself according to this word, broken up by Christ.

Reformed feet: even the mix is foretold. Shared: iron and clay. The strike lands here (v. 34).

Daniel 2:34

34As you looked, a stone was cut out by no human hand, and it struck the image on its feet of iron and clay, and broke them in pieces.

Arminian

A stone cut out by no human hand strikes the feet and breaks them. Wesley: different rise of Christ from all other; conception by the Holy Ghost; a stone that grinds to powder those it falls on.

Arminian stone: both camps confess this is Christ and His kingdom—not a rapture timetable, not an invented millennial chart as Arminian versus Reformed. Cut without hands: the kingdom is God’s doing. It still meets real feet: Rome’s mix, real history.

Reformed

Calvin: why Christ breaks the image if by Him kings reign—empires are broken accidentally, when pride will not bow. If kings exercise office honestly, Christ’s kingdom is not contrary to their power. Untamable pride feels the iron scepter. Cut without hands: origin not human.

Reformed stone: already the eternal wisdom, already supreme before the open advent. Shared christology: the stone is Christ. Do not recast Wesley and Calvin as a culture fight about millennial charts.

Daniel 2:35

35Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver, and the gold, all together were broken in pieces, and became like the chaff of the summer threshing floors; and the wind carried them away, so that not a trace of them could be found. But the stone that struck the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.

Arminian

All metals chaff; wind carries them; no trace; the stone becomes a mountain filling the earth. Wesley: a growing stone even to a mountain, and therefore will fill the earth. Small beginning of Christ’s visible kingdom.

Arminian mountain: growth that fills. The gospel sincerely runs; nations may receive or grind. Filling the earth is Christ’s promise, not a political program and not a date-chart.

Reformed

Calvin: the image perishes as Christ takes what was splendid. The fashion of this world passes; this vision marks the four until renovation in Christ. The mountain is the kingdom, not a human pile.

Reformed mountain: effectual filling, not a bare offer that may leave the earth empty. Shared: chaff for the metals, a mountain for the stone. Both mean Christ’s kingdom, forever (v. 44).

Daniel 2:36

36This was the dream. Now we will tell the king its interpretation.

Arminian

This was the dream; we will tell the interpretation. Wesley: by this word “we” appears Daniel’s piety and modesty, or he declares that he and his companions had begged this skill from God, and therefore he did not arrogate it to himself.

Arminian we: the prayers of 2:18 still sit in the room. Gift shared, not a solo genius. Interpretation is mercy for a king, not a weapon.

Reformed

Calvin: he now records the dream and will not touch interpretation without the matter itself. What follows confirms the four monarchies and the stone.

Reformed we: even the modest plural is inside the revelation already given. Shared: dream first, then meaning. The God of heaven is about to be named as Giver.

Daniel 2:37

37You, O king, the king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom, the power, and the might, and the glory,

Arminian

King of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom, the power, the might, and the glory. Wesley: He hath given thee absolute dominion of all creatures within the bounds of thy vast kingdom. The giving matches 2:21.

Arminian given: the Most High rules—this gift is real. It does not empty Nebuchadnezzar of agency or of blame for the death-decree. He is king of kings under a higher King. Match the hub: a King who governs, provides, and judges, leaving creaturely rule as a real second cause.

Reformed

Calvin: the God of heaven has given—not valor, counsel, fortune, or luck, as 1:2 already taught. He appoints kings and raises them from the rest of mankind as He pleases. Government remains with the one God.

Reformed given: effectual appointment, not a bare permission after Babylon rose. The power, might, and glory are His loan. Match the hub: nothing happens but what He has knowingly and willingly decreed, yet He is not the author of the king’s fury as fury.

Key contrast Both confess the Most High rules: the God of heaven has given the kingdom (2:37), as He changes times and kings (2:21). Arminians typically hear real imperial agency under that gift. Reformed readers typically hear effectual appointment, not a bare permission (Calvin).

Daniel 2:38

38and into whose hand he has given, wherever they dwell, the children of man, the beasts of the field, and the birds of the heavens, making you rule over them all—you are the head of gold.

Arminian

Into his hand: mankind, beasts, birds; he is the head of gold. Wesley: first in order, as the head is before the other parts; the vision began in him and descended; gold for riches, length, and flourishing.

Arminian head: a named man, a named gift. Dominion over creatures is Genesis language under exile, not a culture-war on “dominion.” The head is gold and mortal.

Reformed

Calvin: wherever they dwell—the grant is wide and still a grant. Gold names the first monarchy, not a metal to hoard as theology.

Reformed head: the hand that holds beasts is the hand God filled. Shared with Wesley: you are the head of gold. Inferior kingdoms will not make this untrue; they will make it second.

Daniel 2:39

39Another kingdom inferior to you shall arise after you, and yet a third kingdom of bronze, which shall rule over all the earth.

Arminian

Another inferior; then a third of bronze over all the earth. Wesley: Medes and Persians, inferior in time, not half so long in prosperity, yet rich and large; third the Grecian under Alexander, coarser, marching even to the Indies, said to conquer the world.

Arminian succession: real second and third kingdoms. Inferior does not mean unreal. The God who removes kings (2:21) does it through Medes, Persians, and Greeks who actually fight.

Reformed

Calvin: Medes and Persians worse in morals than the first; Macedonian bronze for Alexander’s cruelty and his successors’ blood, family exterminated. Do not restrict the third to one man’s politeness.

Reformed succession: each rise is appointment, each fall judgment. Shared names: Persia, then Greece. No invented fourth before Rome.

Daniel 2:40

40And there shall be a fourth kingdom, strong as iron, because iron breaks to pieces and shatters all things. And like iron that crushes, it shall break and crush all these.

Arminian

A fourth, strong as iron, breaking and crushing all. Wesley: the kingdom of the Romans, to last not only to Christ’s first coming, but under antichrist, to his second coming; it broke in pieces all other kingdoms till the stone fell upon it.

Arminian iron: historic Rome, as Wesley and Calvin both read. Wesley extends that iron under antichrist toward the second coming; that is his note, not a modern rapture chart, and not the Arminian-versus-Reformed fork. Shared christology still waits on the stone (vv. 44–45).

Reformed

Calvin: iron for Roman tyranny; the world worse as it aged. The fourth is Rome until Christ openly breaks the image. He does not recast the metals as a millennial scheme against Wesley; he reads the advent as already the stone’s strike.

Reformed iron: Rome’s crush is real and under decree. Shared: fourth = Rome in the classic Christian reading. Do not invent an Arminian premill versus Reformed amill fight here; stay with these two voices.

Daniel 2:41

41And as you saw the feet and toes, partly of potter's clay and partly of iron, it shall be a divided kingdom, but some of the firmness of iron shall be in it, just as you saw iron mixed with the soft clay.

Arminian

Feet and toes, clay and iron: a divided kingdom, some firmness remaining. Wesley: partly strong, partly weak; divided by civil wars and by provinces casting off the yoke, into ten kingdoms or toes.

Arminian divided: mixture as history. Ten toes in Wesley are his reading of later fracture, not a license for date-setting or culture maps. Brittleness is still under the God who sets up kings.

Reformed

Calvin: clay points to ruin without needing a chart of ten modern states. The monarchy mixed and did not last as gold had lasted.

Reformed divided: providence includes the mix. Shared: iron and clay together. The stone hits the feet, not a newspaper.

Daniel 2:42

42And as the toes of the feet were partly iron and partly clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong and partly brittle.

Arminian

Toes partly iron, partly clay: partly strong, partly brittle. Wesley: plain in the civil wars of the Romans, and the falling off of some countries, especially toward the end.

Arminian brittle: real weakness in a real empire. Strong and weak together is not a parable about present parties.

Reformed

Calvin: the feet’s clay is why it could fall without a fifth metal. Christ is the cause the image does not stand.

Reformed brittle: appointed fracture. Shared: partly strong, partly brittle. Marriage-mix follows.

Daniel 2:43

43As you saw the iron mixed with soft clay, so they will mix with one another in marriage, but they will not hold together, just as iron does not mix with clay.

Arminian

They mix in marriage but will not hold, as iron does not mix with clay. Wesley: by marriage, but they shall never knit well, because ambition is stronger than affinity.

Arminian mix: human schemes to glue a divided realm. Agency under rule—alliances are real and fail. Not a sermon on modern weddings or politics.

Reformed

Calvin: they try to unite what God has shown will not cohere. Ambition and policy cannot reverse the vision.

Reformed mix: even the marriages sit inside the decree that they will not hold. Shared: iron does not mix with clay. The next verse is the kingdom that does hold.

Daniel 2:44

44And in the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed, nor shall the kingdom be left to another people. It shall break in pieces all these kingdoms and bring them to an end, and it shall stand forever,

Arminian

In the days of those kings the God of heaven sets up a kingdom never destroyed, not left to another people; it breaks the others and stands forever. Wesley: while the iron kingdom stood, for Christ was born in the reign of Augustus. Not bounded as worldly empires; truly universal; for ever.

Arminian kingdom: both camps confess this is Christ’s. Set up in those kings’ days—first advent under Rome—not a rapture-versus-amillennial culture fight. The kingdom sincerely gathers; it is not a human handoff. Forever means forever, not a millennial chart Wesley and Calvin did not fight as Arminian versus Reformed.

Reformed

Calvin: God of heaven sets it up—the same Giver as v. 37. Christ already possessed supreme power before He appeared; at the advent He overthrows these monarchies. If kings serve honestly, His kingdom is not contrary to their power; pride finds the scepter. It shall stand forever: not left to another people.

Reformed kingdom: effectual, divine, unending. Shared christology with Wesley: born under Rome, kingdom forever, breaks the image. Difference of accent on how far they trace Rome’s iron is not a camp millennial war.

Daniel 2:45

45just as you saw that a stone was cut from a mountain by no human hand, and that it broke in pieces the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver, and the gold. A great God has made known to the king what shall be after this. The dream is certain, and its interpretation sure.”

Arminian

Stone cut from a mountain by no human hand; it broke the metals. A great God has made known what shall be; the dream is certain, the interpretation sure. Wesley: small beginning of Christ’s visible kingdom; conception by the Holy Ghost as to His two natures; a stone that grinds to powder; a growing stone to a mountain filling the earth.

Arminian sure: certain because God spoke, not because Daniel was clever (2:30). The stone is Christ; filling the earth is the gospel’s reach. Hearers remain responsible under that sure word. No invented egalitarian politics, and no date-chart.

Reformed

Calvin: cut without hands—the origin of Christ’s kingdom is not human. A great God has made it known: the same God who reveals secrets (2:28). Certain and sure, so the king cannot treat it as a Magi’s guess.

Reformed sure: the interpretation stands because God decreed the history it names. Shared with Wesley: no human hand, Christ’s kingdom, the earth filled. The next range is the king’s homage, not a millennial appendix.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on four kingdoms and a stone cut without hands that becomes a mountain—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Daniel 2:31–45, agreed that the stone is Christ’s kingdom.

Arminian Notable voices

“In the days of these kings—while the iron kingdom stood, for Christ was born in the reign of Augustus Caesar. And this kingdom is not bounded by any limits, as worldly empires are, but is truly universal. And it shall be for ever, never destroyed or given to others, as the rest were. And the gold—this denotes the small beginning of Christ’s visible kingdom, and the different rise of Christ from all other; his conception by the Holy Ghost, without father and mother, respectively as to his two natures. This stone, falling from the mountain, brake the image in pieces; for Christ is a stone that grinds to powder those it falls on: and he is a growing stone even to a mountain, and therefore will fill the earth.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Dan 2:44–45)

Reformed Notable voices

“God wished, under this figure, to represent the future condition of the world till the advent of Christ. Earthly empires are swallowed and broken up by Christ accidentally, as they say. If kings exercise their office honestly, Christ’s kingdom is not contrary to their power. Whence then does it happen that Christ strikes kings with an iron scepter? Because their pride is untamable. Before Christ appeared, he already possessed supreme power, both in heaven and earth, and thus broke up and annihilated the pride and violence of all men. The stone was cut out of the mountain without hands, because the origin of Christ’s kingdom is not human.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Dan 2:31–45)
