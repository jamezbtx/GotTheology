Passage

Daniel 2

Verses 17–30 · ESV · Voices

Daniel tells Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah to seek mercy from the God of heaven. The mystery is revealed in a night vision; Daniel blesses God, who changes times and kings and gives wisdom. He asks Arioch to spare the wise men, is brought before the king, and confesses that no court guild can show the mystery—there is a God in heaven who reveals it, not because of Daniel’s surplus wisdom.

Daniel 2:17

17Then Daniel went to his house and made the matter known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions,

Arminian

Daniel goes home and makes the matter known to his three companions. Wesley’s later note will mark piety and modesty in “we.” The house is a place of counsel, not a slogan.

Arminian company: he does not hoard the crisis. Real speech among four men under the Most High. Grace that gave learning in 1:17 now meets a shared hour. No Pelagian leftover—they already live by gift—and no fatalism that skips the telling.

Reformed

Calvin: his object was to implore God’s grace; confidence added, since he perceived a double punishment if he disappointed the king. He expected what he obtained—the dream revealed. He urges companions to implore mercy unitedly. They were not rivals; they prayed not for private advantage.

Reformed company: the gathering is already inside expectation God will give. Shared: the four are told. Prayer is next, not panic as a method.

Daniel 2:18

18and told them to seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his companions might not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

Arminian

Seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, that they might not be destroyed with the rest. Wesley: magicians confessed revelation must come from God; Daniel by prudence and piety saved lives. Mercy is asked, not a prosperity bargain.

Arminian mercy: real petition. The God of heaven may grant or withhold; they ask anyway. Agency under rule—four men on their faces, a decree still out. Match the hub: a King who governs and provides, leaving creaturely asking as a real second cause. They do not treat the Magi’s death as indifferent; they plead to live.

Reformed

Calvin: they desired mercy from God. Whenever we fly to God, our senses ought to be turned toward His mercy, for His good will reconciles Him to us. Some think they wished to snatch the Magi also from death; they wished all persons safe, yet clearly separate themselves. James: those who pray with difference are unworthy of being heard; Daniel prayed with true faith, life in God’s hands.

Reformed mercy: the asking is effectual because God had already purposed to reveal. Shared: they seek mercy. The vision of v. 19 is gift answering prayer, not a technique.

Daniel 2:19

19Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

Arminian

The mystery revealed in a night vision; Daniel blessed the God of heaven. Wesley will soon attribute times and kings to this God, not to chance. Blessing comes before the court speech.

Arminian revealed: they asked; God gave. Revelation is mercy, not wages. The night is still a night; Daniel still blesses. Faith that sought does not become fatalism when the gift arrives, nor a claim that prayer bent an unwilling God.

Reformed

Calvin: God showed Himself propitious; Daniel prayed as if he had already obtained. He is clearly persuaded God did not vainly harass the king but was preparing a remarkable judgment. God never closes His ears when rightly invoked; the secret made known in a vision by night.

Reformed revealed: the vision is the God of heaven’s act, including the prayers as means. Shared: mystery given, then blessing. Thanksgiving is the fit first use.

Daniel 2:20

20Daniel answered and said: “Blessed be the name of God forever and ever, to whom belong wisdom and might.

Arminian

Blessed be the name of God forever; to Him belong wisdom and might. Wesley: God only, that made all by His power, doth rule and over-rule all by His providence. Heathen chance is refused without a culture-war on “nature.”

Arminian belonging: wisdom and might are God’s before they are anyone’s. Daniel will still speak in court. Doxology does not erase the four who asked; it names the Giver.

Reformed

Calvin: he thanks God after the dream was made known, snatched from death beyond expectation. Whose is the wisdom and the strength—to God alone. Scripture distinguishes the true God by two things that must not be torn: He foresees all, and He governs, allowing nothing to happen by chance or without His direction. Bare foreknowledge without government robs Him of half His glory.

Reformed belonging: wisdom and might are not idle attributes. Shared: the Name is blessed. v. 21 will say how that might meets kings.

Daniel 2:21

21He changes times and seasons; he removes kings and sets up kings; he gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who have understanding;

Arminian

He changes times and seasons; removes kings and sets up kings; gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who have understanding. Wesley: it is the great part of God’s power and prerogative to change times. Daniel attributes to God what heathens attributed to nature or chance. God only doth rule and over-rule all by His providence.

Arminian rule: the Most High rules—this changing is real. It does not empty kings of agency or the wise of study. Nebuchadnezzar still rages, still decrees, still will fall; Daniel still asked and still received. God governs a world in which times turn and rulers rise without making every royal yes a puppet-string. Match the hub: a King who governs, provides, and judges, leaving creaturely choice as a real second cause. Wisdom given to the wise is gift meeting minds that actually understand.

Reformed

Calvin: God is the true fountain of wisdom and virtue, and diffuses them through heaven and earth. He changes times; we must not ascribe this to fortune or to nature as supreme. He appoints and removes kings. Men fancy they acquire power by talents, birth, or accident, and thought of God is excluded. Neither from the east nor the west, but God alone is the judge. He gives wisdom to the wise: whoever boasts of becoming wise by innate strength has not originated the intellect. All proof of wisdom in the world ought to illustrate His glory, not obscure it.

Reformed rule: effectual providence, not a bare permission after the fact. Times and kings move by His actual hand, including the means. Second causes remain; they are established, not erased. Match the hub: nothing happens but what He has knowingly and willingly decreed, yet He is not the author of a tyrant’s cruelty as cruelty.

Key contrast Both confess the Most High rules: He changes times and seasons; He removes kings and sets them up (2:21). Arminians typically hear real human agency under that rule—kings still command, wise men still study, Daniel still asks—governed without irresistible determination of every choice. Reformed readers typically hear effectual rule, not a bare permission: God appoints and removes as He pleases, and whatever wisdom appears springs from Him (Calvin).

Daniel 2:22

22he reveals deep and hidden things; he knows what is in the darkness, and the light dwells with him.

Arminian

He reveals deep and hidden things; knows what is in darkness; light dwells with Him. Wesley’s prophet will not fall abruptly on the dream but prepare the king for the true God. Hidden things are God’s to open.

Arminian revealing: the forgotten dream sat in darkness until mercy answered prayer. Light with God does not make human knowing unnecessary; it makes it possible. The court still cannot see unless He gives.

Reformed

Calvin: he proceeds further—when understanding is rare, the gift shines more clearly. He speaks of the gift of prophecy. Light dwells with God: men are in clouds; there is no pure and perfect light but in God alone. He dwells in light unapproachable unless He deigns to stretch forth His hand. Seek illumination; by His Spirit He conducts beyond the skies.

Reformed revealing: hidden things open because light is with Him, not because Daniel climbed. Shared: He knows the dark. The interpretation coming is that light, not a guild’s science.

Daniel 2:23

23To you, O God of my fathers, I give thanks and praise, for you have given me wisdom and might, and have now made known to me what we asked of you, for you have made known to us the king's matter.”

Arminian

Thanks and praise to the God of the fathers: You gave wisdom and might, made known what we asked, made known to us the king’s matter. Wesley: by “we,” piety and modesty; he and his companions begged this skill, and he does not arrogate it.

Arminian thanks: “what we asked” keeps the prayers of v. 18 on the table. Gift and asking together. No boast over Babylon’s wise, and no claim that four lads originated the vision.

Reformed

Calvin: he turns to God and distinguishes Israel’s God from all fictions—not by the authority of the fathers as if men added to God, but by the covenant. If I have any strength or understanding, I ascribe it all to Thee. Thou hast revealed what we demanded; he first confesses the gift peculiar to himself, then extends the benefit to companions snatched from death.

Reformed thanks: even the “we asked” is inside grace already given. Shared: God of the fathers, and the matter made known. The court is next.

Daniel 2:24

24Therefore Daniel went in to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon. He went and said thus to him: “Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon; bring me in before the king, and I will show the king the interpretation.”

Arminian

To Arioch: do not destroy the wise men; bring me in; I will show the interpretation. Wesley: Daniel by prudence and piety saved all the magicians’ lives. Mercy toward the guild is not endorsement of their arts.

Arminian spare: a real plea for other men’s lives. The Most High who changes times still meets a captain’s ear. Daniel does not need the Magi dead to be right. Agency under rule: he goes, he speaks, Arioch can still refuse.

Reformed

Calvin: some Magi already slain; he asks the remainder spared. Not because the arts should stand—they are diabolical—but persons were being killed without just process. If not punished for a just reason, spare the persons through equity. He looks to the cause; iniquity not yet ripe for destruction by this rage. God had taken Daniel into His confidence.

Reformed spare: the word to Arioch is gift and means. Shared: do not destroy them. Interpretation, not slaughter, will empty the boast.

Daniel 2:25

25Then Arioch brought in Daniel before the king in haste and said thus to him: “I have found among the exiles from Judah a man who will make known to the king the interpretation.”

Arminian

Arioch brings him in haste: I have found among the exiles of Judah a man. Wesley: the Lord held the governor’s hands (2:13). Arioch’s “I found” is court speech; the finding was a search to kill.

Arminian found: a captain’s haste and a Judean exile. No culture-war on palaces. Human ushering under the God who sets up kings. Daniel is still the man who asked for time.

Reformed

Calvin: how Arioch speaks as if of an obscure unknown, though Daniel had already requested time—courtiers praise their own offices, or access was through this channel. Sons of transmigration: captives.

Reformed found: even the boast “I have found” sits inside providence. Shared: he is brought in. The king’s question follows from despair, not from faith yet.

Daniel 2:26

26The king declared to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, “Are you able to make known to me the dream that I have seen and its interpretation?”

Arminian

To Daniel, called Belteshazzar: are you able to make known dream and interpretation? Wesley: by this name the king took courage as if he might expect some great thing; the word signifies the keeper of secret treasure.

Arminian able: the king still thinks in terms of a man’s ability. Daniel will refuse that frame (v. 30). The question is honest unbelief, not a cue for politics.

Reformed

Calvin: the king uses these words through despair of all interpretation. Error imbued him: Magi alone possessors of wisdom. God drew this interrogation from the proud king to render His grace in Daniel more illustrious. The less hope in the king, the more dignity in the revelation.

Reformed able: even the question is drawn. Shared: are you able? The answer will move the ability to heaven.

Daniel 2:27

27Daniel answered the king and said, “No wise men, enchanters, magicians, or astrologers can show to the king the mystery that the king has asked,

Arminian

No wise men, enchanters, magicians, or astrologers can show this mystery. Wesley: they confessed knowledge and revelation must come from God; what Daniel did was not of human strength. He does not mock a guild for sport.

Arminian cannot: true limit of flesh, already named in 2:10–11. Clearing the floor is pastoral, not a war on learning. Liberal skill remains; this mystery does not.

Reformed

Calvin: names of wise men need not detain us. Comparison: the dream was not natural and had nothing in common with human conjectures. Like Paul on the gospel—heavenly mystery not comprehended by the most learned. True reason: peculiar revelation of the Spirit.

Reformed cannot: God locked what nature and art cannot pick. Shared: the guild cannot show it. v. 28 names who can.

Daniel 2:28

28but there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will be in the latter days. Your dream and the visions of your head as you lay in bed are these:

Arminian

There is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries; He has made known what will be in the latter days. Wesley: the prophet’s wisdom—he does not fall abruptly upon the dream, but first prepares this lofty king and by degrees labours to win him to the knowledge of the true God.

Arminian God in heaven: the Most High who changes times now speaks to a pagan king. Revelation is sincere gift; the king is responsible to hear. “Latter days” will mean, in the next range, the stone and Christ’s kingdom—historic Christian reading, not a modern rapture fight.

Reformed

Calvin: there is a God in heaven who reveals secrets, in contrast to Magi. He shews what will come at the extremity of the days—the advent of Christ as a kind of renewal; the gospel a kind of perfection of all things. He persists so the king may treat the vision with gravity: God the author, or the words are thrown away.

Reformed God in heaven: the revealer is this God, not Apollo, not chance. Shared with Wesley: prepare the king for the true God. The dream itself is next.

Daniel 2:29

29To you, O king, as you lay in bed came thoughts of what would be after this, and he who reveals mysteries made known to you what is to be.

Arminian

As you lay in bed, thoughts of what would be after this; He who reveals mysteries made it known. Wesley: he remembered the fact in general (2:3); now Daniel names the thoughts as God’s sending.

Arminian thoughts: a king’s real musing under the Revealer. Ambition about “after this” is creaturely; God uses it without authoring it as righteousness. Second causes stand: he did think, he did forget, he did rage.

Reformed

Calvin: God the author of the dream, that the king listen with sobriety. Unless seriously affected he would despise the interpretation. The king’s thoughts ascended—revolved in the brain—anxious about futurity; greatest monarchs dream of empire after death. Thoughts could not profit unless God unveiled the future. He who reveals secrets has explained what shall happen.

Reformed thoughts: even the pillow’s ambition is inside the sting God fastened. Shared: made known to you. Ability is still not Daniel’s.

Daniel 2:30

30But as for me, this mystery has been revealed to me, not because of any wisdom that I have more than all the living, but in order that the interpretation may be made known to the king, and that you may know the thoughts of your mind.

Arminian

Revealed to me not because of any wisdom I have more than all the living, but that the king may know. Wesley: that the interpretation may be manifest to the king, and that thou mayest be better instructed and satisfied in thy mind.

Arminian not because: he refuses surplus. Gift is mercy to a king and to four who asked. No Pelagian leftover in the interpreter, and no fatalism that makes the king a set piece. He is to know the thoughts of his mind—responsible hearing under the God who gives wisdom.

Reformed

Calvin: he meets the objection—if God alone reveals, how can a mortal? He transfers all glory to God. Not in the wisdom which belongs to me. Natural talents, even remarkable, do not reach this. This secret was not made known on account of any wisdom in me beyond the rest of mankind—he does not claim to be superior; he leaves that in doubt. God uses him as instrument. Neither the dream nor the vision occurred in vain.

Reformed not because: the interpreter is means, not source. Shared with Wesley: for the king’s knowing. The image of gold, silver, bronze, and iron is the next range.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on mercy sought, a night vision, and the God who changes times and kings—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Daniel 2:17–30.

Arminian Notable voices

“He changeth—God can make the sun go back or stand still, as in Ahaz and Joshua’s time; it is the great part of God’s power and prerogative to change times. Daniel here attributes that to God, which Heathens attributed to nature, or chance. God only, that made all by his power, doth rule, and over-rule all by his providence. Observe the prophet’s wisdom, he does not fall abruptly upon the dream, but first prepares this lofty king for it, and by degrees labours to win him to the knowledge of the true God.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Dan 2:21, 28)

Reformed Notable voices

“It is he who changes times and portions of time. In so many changes we must remember that the Providence of God shines forth. God so changes empires, and times, and seasons, that we should learn to look up to him. The Lord is said to appoint kings, and to raise them from the rest of mankind as he pleases. He gives wisdom to the wise: men have no wisdom but what springs from God. This secret was not made known to me on account of any wisdom in me. There is a God in heaven who reveals secrets.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Dan 2:21–28)
