Passage

Daniel 2

Verses 1–16 · ESV · Voices

In his second year Nebuchadnezzar is troubled by dreams he cannot recover. Magicians, enchanters, sorcerers, and Chaldeans are summoned; they ask to hear the dream first. The king demands both dream and interpretation on pain of death, then a decree to destroy all the wise men. Daniel, sought with his companions, answers Arioch with prudence and asks the king for time to show the interpretation.

Daniel 2:1

1In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuchadnezzar had dreams; his spirit was troubled, and his sleep left him.

Arminian

Second year: dreams, a troubled spirit, sleep gone. Wesley: one dream of many parts; he remembered the fact in general but could not repeat it. The Lord hath ways to affright the greatest men in the midst of their security. A court, not a culture-war on palaces.

Arminian affright: real terror in a real king under the Most High. God can trouble sleep without turning the man into a puppet. The dream is from above; the tossing is still Nebuchadnezzar’s. Match the hub: a King who governs and judges, leaving creaturely fear as a real second cause.

Reformed

Calvin: uncommon, not his first dream. God did not suffer him to rest. The dream sent from heaven; then, astonished, he slept again and forgot the vision. God blotted remembrance to increase the sting, drawing him by secret chains until Daniel should give both dream and meaning.

Reformed affright: effectual rule, not a bare permission after a bad night. The forgotten dream is God’s actual hand. Shared with Wesley: the Lord affrights; the king is troubled. How that rule meets the king’s rage is the fork already named in 1:2.

Daniel 2:2

2Then the king commanded that the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers, and the Chaldeans be summoned to tell the king his dreams. So they came in and stood before the king.

Arminian

Magicians, enchanters, sorcerers, Chaldeans summoned to tell the dreams. Wesley: astrologers who pretended skill in natural and supernatural things; sorcerers using diabolical arts; Chaldeans a national, noble name the magicians assumed.

Arminian court: a king’s ordinary channel. Summoning them is policy, not a sermon against “expertise.” They will fail; the failure is theirs. Daniel is not in the room yet.

Reformed

Calvin: the dream caused the king to feel God to be its author. Terror compelled him to summon all the Magi. The art of conjecturing from dreams is rash; a true interpreter does not follow fixed rules but waits for God’s gift.

Reformed summons: God uses even this call to empty the court of its boast. Shared: they stand before the king. Liberal learning is not the same as this claim to read a forgotten dream.

Daniel 2:3

3And the king said to them, “I had a dream, and my spirit is troubled to know the dream.”

Arminian

“I had a dream; my spirit is troubled to know it.” Wesley: he remembered the fact in general, not perfectly; the impression left great perplexity. The Lord affrights the greatest in their security.

Arminian knowing: he wants to know. Desire and dread are creaturely under God’s troubling. He will ask wrongly; the asking is still asking.

Reformed

Calvin: great confidence of the Chaldeans will meet a riddle divinely set. The king confesses ignorance; the object exceeds him. God speaks to unbelievers in broken accents; seeing they do not see.

Reformed knowing: the trouble is God’s sting still working. Shared: he is troubled to know. Interpretation will not come from the room he has filled.

Daniel 2:4

4Then the Chaldeans said to the king in Aramaic, “O king, live forever! Tell your servants the dream, and we will show the interpretation.”

Arminian

In Aramaic: “O king, live forever! Tell the dream, we will show the interpretation.” Wesley: from hence all is written in the Chaldee language to the eighth chapter. A court greeting, not a culture fight about tongues.

Arminian boast: they promise interpretation if they may hear. Human confidence under a God who has hidden the dream. The yes they offer is real and empty.

Reformed

Calvin: not a simple prayer—they order the king to be cheerful, as if explanation were at hand. Impostors liberal in words. They pretend a science of which they know nothing.

Reformed boast: God will make a laughingstock of this confidence. Shared: they ask to be told. Daniel’s later gift will not be this art sold for pay.

Daniel 2:5

5The king answered and said to the Chaldeans, “The word from me is firm: if you do not make known to me the dream and its interpretation, you shall be torn limb from limb, and your houses shall be laid in ruins.

Arminian

The word is firm: dream and interpretation, or torn limb from limb, houses ruined. Wesley will mark one decree he will not retract (v. 9). Tyranny is named as tyranny, not as a slogan against empire.

Arminian word: a king’s real sentence. The Most High rules; this cruelty is still Nebuchadnezzar’s. Agency is not canceled by the sting of v. 1.

Reformed

Calvin: he requires more than they professed. God wished to make a laughingstock of their boasting. The wrath is furious, beyond wild beasts; the Magi, as far as concerned the king, were innocent of this crime. The Lord wished the miracle observed: if the king had let them depart, the anxiety might have been buried.

Reformed word: God allows the anger to burn without restraint so Daniel will be seen. Shared: the sentence is death. Injustice and providence are not the same thing.

Daniel 2:6

6But if you show the dream and its interpretation, you shall receive from me gifts and rewards and great honor. Therefore show me the dream and its interpretation.”

Arminian

Gifts, rewards, great honor if they show both. Wesley’s later piety in Daniel will refuse to arrogate the skill. Here the king buys what cannot be sold.

Arminian honor: a real bribe. Magicians might have taken it; they cannot earn it. No culture-war on courts and pay. The fork is not money; it is whether revelation is gift.

Reformed

Calvin: he tries flattery where horror failed. Magi made a gain of predictions; when knowledge is saleable it is adulterated. Where avarice reigns, truth is extinguished. The king will not spare expense, and is furious when the reward buys nothing.

Reformed honor: the market cannot purchase what God has locked. Shared: gifts are offered. Daniel will later ask for time, not for pay.

Daniel 2:7

7They answered a second time and said, “Let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will show its interpretation.”

Arminian

A second time: tell us the dream, we will interpret. Wesley’s magicians will soon confess that revelation must come from God. This still pretends otherwise.

Arminian repeat: they double the same ask. Persistence here is not faith; it is the only tool they have. Human limits are being told the truth about themselves.

Reformed

Calvin: a fair reply that does not mitigate wrath. He is hurried by diabolic vehemence, governed by God’s secret counsel, that Daniel’s explanation might be more noticed. They persist in foolish self-conceit, blinded by the devil.

Reformed repeat: even their true limit (we need the dream) serves the larger emptying. Shared: they cannot start without the facts. God has removed the facts.

Daniel 2:8

8The king answered and said, “I know with certainty that you are trying to gain time, because you see that the word from me is firm—

Arminian

“You are trying to gain time; the word from me is firm.” Wesley: one decree, not retracted. A king reads delay as fraud. Delay will later be Daniel’s request for prayer, not stalling.

Arminian time: the charge is half right about the Magi and will be wholly wrong about Daniel. Time can be prudence or evasion. Agency will show which.

Reformed

Calvin: he loads them with a new crime—impostors who delude. If they deceived others it arose from being self-deceived. The king pursues the blindest impulse of his rage. Divinely tormented, he cannot rest until explanation comes.

Reformed time: the rage that will not wait is still inside the sting God fastened. Shared: the word is firm. Daniel’s later day will be gift, not the Magi’s stall.

Daniel 2:9

9if you do not make the dream known to me, there is but one sentence for you. You have agreed to speak lying and corrupt words before me till the times change. Therefore tell me the dream, and I shall know that you can show me its interpretation.”

Arminian

One sentence; lying words till times change; tell the dream so I know you can interpret. Wesley: I will not retract my sentence.

Arminian sentence: a tyrant’s logic—prove the gift by doing what no man can. The demand is unjust; it is still a demand. “Till the times change” will soon be answered by the God who changes times (v. 21), not by court bargains.

Reformed

Calvin: another argument to convict them of cunning. They boast they can interpret a dream still unknown. Rashness and fraud detected. Always the same fury until God brings Daniel forward.

Reformed sentence: God uses even this false test. The true interpreter will tell the dream first because God gives both. Shared: one sentence stands. Times do not change by Magi waiting.

Daniel 2:10

10The Chaldeans answered the king and said, “There is not a man on earth who can meet the king's demand, for no great and powerful king has asked such a thing of any magician or enchanter or Chaldean.

Arminian

No man on earth can meet this; no great king has asked it. Wesley: the magicians confessed that knowledge and revelation must come from God, and therefore what Daniel did was not of any human strength.

Arminian limit: they speak truly about man. The confession is not yet faith in Israel’s God. Human inability is named without a culture fight about “elites.” The next verse will misplace the dwelling of God.

Reformed

Calvin: they excuse themselves; the king will not admit it, impelled by wrath and by divine instinct to show Magi, sorcerers, and astrologers mere impostors. God wished to extol His servant Daniel and separate him from the herd.

Reformed limit: their true sentence (no man) is God’s means of emptying the guild. Shared: no man on earth. The God of heaven will not stay outside the story.

Daniel 2:11

11The thing that the king asks is difficult, and no one can show it to the king except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh.”

Arminian

Difficult; no one can show it except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh. Wesley’s observation: they confessed revelation must come from God. They are right that flesh cannot invent the dream; they are wrong if they mean the Lord will not speak in Babylon.

Arminian dwelling: the Most High may dwell with His people even in exile. Daniel will ask and pray (vv. 16–18). Grace that reveals is gift, not a god locked above flesh. No slogan about incarnation here beyond what the text will show: God does answer from heaven to men on earth.

Reformed

Calvin: slow to confess ignorance; compelled in extremity. The object surpasses human ingenuity. They say gods or angels only can interpret—unbelievers’ persuasion of intermediate deities. Men clothed in flesh cannot so raise themselves as to perceive all secrets. The king’s ears deaf; God spurred him by furies.

Reformed dwelling: they almost tell the truth and then miss the God who draws the king by secret chains. Shared: the thing is too hard for man. Daniel’s God will prove He reveals secrets without becoming a Chaldean idol.

Daniel 2:12

12Because of this the king was angry and very furious, and commanded that all the wise men of Babylon be destroyed.

Arminian

Angry and very furious: destroy all the wise men of Babylon. Wesley will mark that Daniel by prudence and piety saved their lives. The fury is a king’s, not a cue for politics.

Arminian fury: real wrath, real command. The Most High rules; this slaughter order is still Nebuchadnezzar’s sin. Second causes are not theater. God can overrule a decree without having authored the cruelty as cruelty.

Reformed

Calvin: beyond former denunciation to actual command. God wished Daniel brought forward. If superstition fails, madness succeeds. The king a hundredfold more infuriated, left destitute of those from whom he hoped all things. Divinely tormented into this rush.

Reformed fury: God governs even this rage toward the revealing of His servant. He is not the author of the king’s injustice. Shared: the command goes out. Mercy will meet it in a youth not yet summoned.

Daniel 2:13

13So the decree went out, and the wise men were about to be killed; and they sought Daniel and his companions, to kill them.

Arminian

Decree out; wise men about to be killed; they sought Daniel and his companions. Wesley: not called at first because of their youth, which the Chaldeans despised. Three observations: revelation must come from God; the Lord held the governor’s hands so he did not slay Daniel with the first; Daniel by prudence and piety saved the magicians’ lives.

Arminian sought: four youths under a death sentence they did not earn in this scene. Youth despised is a court fact, not a culture-war. The Lord holding hands does not cancel Arioch’s search; it meets it.

Reformed

Calvin: some already slain; Daniel at first not required, then sought when the Magi were dragged out. Tyrants will not undertake inquiry. God justly punishes the Chaldeans; Daniel condemned though not called, the injustice of edicts that do not inquire.

Reformed sought: providence includes the unjust net that catches the innocent, that the miracle may be public. Shared: they look for Daniel to kill him. The next verse is prudence inside that net.

Daniel 2:14

14Then Daniel replied with prudence and discretion to Arioch, the captain of the king's guard, who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.

Arminian

Daniel replied with prudence and discretion to Arioch, captain of the guard. Wesley: the Lord held the governor’s hands; Daniel by prudence and piety saved lives. Asking well is still asking.

Arminian prudence: real counsel under the Most High. Grace assists a composed reply; it does not replace the reply. Match 1:8–9: he purposes, he asks, God gives favor. No Pelagian leftover—he is already the man God has given learning—and no fatalism that treats the decree as unanswerable.

Reformed

Calvin: God wonderfully snatches His own from the jaws of death. Arioch spares him when he slew others: Daniel’s life in the hand and keeping of God, who restrained mind and hand of the prefect. He inquired concerning the counsel and the edict. Presence of mind in a sudden terror is God’s singular gift.

Reformed prudence: the Spirit governs the composure. Shared with Wesley: held hands, and a prudent word. The fork is how that holding meets Daniel’s asking.

Key contrast Both confess the Most High rules this hour: the Lord holds Arioch’s hands (Wesley); God snatches Daniel and restrains the prefect (Calvin). Arminians typically hear real prudence and a real request under that rule—the asking is governed without irresistible determination of every word. Reformed readers typically hear effectual providence, not a bare permission: mind and hand restrained, presence of mind as gift, the king later granting time because God had taken Daniel into His confidence.

Daniel 2:15

15He declared to Arioch, the king's captain, “Why is the decree of the king so urgent?” Then Arioch made the matter known to Daniel.

Arminian

“Why is the decree so urgent?” Arioch makes the matter known. Wesley’s held hands now include a captain who explains instead of striking.

Arminian why: a fair question. Urgency is the king’s; information is Arioch’s. Human speech under God’s holding. Daniel cannot pray about what he has not heard.

Reformed

Calvin: he obliquely blames haste and ingratitude—not called, yet to be slain. Arioch explained; Daniel was ignorant of the whole matter. Terror would have seized him; he was divinely strengthened to seek time composedly.

Reformed why: even the question is gift. Shared: the matter is made known. Time to pray (v. 18) depends on this telling.

Daniel 2:16

16And Daniel went in and requested the king to appoint him a time, that he might show the interpretation to the king.

Arminian

Daniel went in and requested a time, that he might show the interpretation. Wesley: prudence and piety; lives spared. He does not yet have the dream; he asks anyway, which the next verses will fill with prayer.

Arminian request: a real petition to a real king. The Most High who gave favor in 1:9 now meets another ask. Daniel is not bargaining a miracle on the calendar; he seeks room to seek God. Faith that will not stall like the Magi still uses time as means. Second causes stand: the king can refuse.

Reformed

Calvin: Arioch not without fear in giving introduction; God had taken Daniel into His confidence and so bent the prefect. Time granted most probably because Daniel confessed he hoped in God’s kindness and would return with a new revelation; the king would never have permitted doubt. He expected what he obtained.

Reformed request: the audience and the day are God’s hand, including a youth’s words. Shared: he asked, and time is appointed. Thanksgiving and the God who changes times belong to the next range.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a forgotten dream that affrighted a king, a court that could not tell it, and a youth whose prudence met a decree of death—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Daniel 2:1–16.

Arminian Notable voices

“In the second year—this was properly in the fifth year of that king’s reign, but in the second year after Daniel had been brought before the king. Dreams—it was one dream, but of many parts. To know—he remembered the fact in general, but could not repeat it perfectly. The Lord hath ways to affright the greatest men in the world, in the midst of their security. Daniel and his fellows were not called, because of their youth. The magicians confessed that knowledge and revelation must come from God. That the Lord held the governor’s hands, so that he did not slay Daniel presently with the first. That Daniel by his prudence and piety saved all the magicians’ lives.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Dan 2:1–3, 13)

Reformed Notable voices

“God not only wished to hold the king in suspense, but he thus blotted out the remembrance of the dream from his mind, to increase the power of his sting. God inwardly fastened such a sting in the mind of this unbeliever, that he could not rest, but was always wakeful in the midst of his dreaming, because God was drawing him to himself by secret chains. The king’s mind was impelled by diabolic fury. Meanwhile we must remember the origin of this feeling—he was divinely tormented, and could not rest a single moment till he obtained an explanation of his dream. God wished his servant Daniel to be brought forward and to be observed by all men.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Dan 2:1–12)
