Passage

Daniel 7

Verses 15–28 · WEB · Voices

Troubled by his vision, Daniel asks an attendant to explain it. The four beasts are four kingdoms, but the kingdom will finally be given to the saints of the Most High. The fourth beast’s arrogant horn wages war against the saints for a set time until the court sits and his dominion is taken away. Daniel keeps the matter in his heart, shaken by what he has seen.

Daniel 7:15

15“As for me, Daniel, my spirit was grieved within my body, and the visions of my head troubled me.

Arminian

Daniel’s spirit within him is anxious; the visions of his head alarm him. Wesley writes no note here; his next (v. 16): unto one—that is, to an angel, that ministered; the truth—the true meaning of this vision.

In practice: the alarm is no illusion, not a pose. Interpretation is coming; he will ask. Shared: the saints will receive the kingdom; the horn does not last.

Reformed

Calvin: his spirit vanished, as if he suffered some mental deficiency; he was almost lifeless and nearly dead. The visions of his head frightened him—not a panic without cause, but the mystery of the vision God had offered him.

God communicates the magnitude by this fear. Daniel is alarmed, and he will ask. Do not treat the vision coldly.

Daniel 7:16

16I came near to one of those who stood by, and asked him the truth concerning all this. “So he told me, and made me know the interpretation of the things.

Arminian

He approaches one of those who stood by, and asks the truth; so he is told the interpretation. Wesley: unto one—that is, to an angel, that ministered. The truth—the true meaning of this vision.

The angel ministers the meaning. Interpretation is given, not guessed. 13–14 already named the Son of Man; this range interprets the beasts and the horn.

Reformed

Calvin: he came to one of those standing by—without the slightest doubt, one of the angels. Modesty and docility: he flies to an instructor because he is conscious of his own ignorance. The angel spoke and opened the interpretation of the words.

Ask God for the meaning; do not reject what surpasses you. Common to both: an angel tells the truth of the vision.

Daniel 7:17

17‘These great animals, which are four, are four kings, who will arise out of the earth.

Arminian

These great animals, which are four, are four kings, who will arise out of the earth. Wesley writes no note here; his nearest (v. 3): four great beasts—that is, four great monarchies, great, in comparison of particular kingdoms; beasts for their tyrannical oppressions.

For the hearer: four kings, four monarchies, from the earth. Stay with Wesley’s four. They arise; they are creaturely.

Reformed

Calvin: four kingdoms arose; by kingdom he means monarchy. The angel announces the springing up of four kingdoms that the people not be disturbed too much by revolutions. From the earth: the world as a troubled sea.

In practice: four monarchies, not a modern flag. Wesley agrees: four, from the earth. The saints still receive the kingdom in the next verse.

Daniel 7:18

18But the saints of the Most High will receive the kingdom, and possess the kingdom forever, even forever and ever.’

Arminian

The saints of the Most High will receive the kingdom, and possess it forever, forever and ever. Wesley: but the saints—Jesus Christ being their king, they shall reign with him, and possess the kingdom for ever.

Christ is already named in 13–14; here the saints receive what was given to him. Common ground: everlasting kingdom, given, not seized. Not a pre/post/a-mil war. The horn will not last.

Reformed

Calvin: they shall obtain the kingdom for ever, even for ever and ever. The four beasts took the empire that belonged to the sacred lofty ones—God’s elect sons, pilgrims whose citizenship is heaven—yet the saints obtain it at the fullness of times, when Christ, the end of the Law, is revealed.

In practice: delay is not denial. Common ground: the saints receive the kingdom forever. Verses 13–14 already gave dominion to the Son of Man.

Daniel 7:19

19“Then I desired to know the truth concerning the fourth animal, which was different from all of them, exceedingly terrible, whose teeth were of iron, and its nails of bronze; which devoured, broke in pieces, and stamped the residue with its feet;

Arminian

He desires the truth concerning the fourth animal, different, exceedingly terrifying, iron teeth, bronze claws, devouring, breaking, stamping. Wesley writes no note here; his nearest (v. 7): a fourth beast—the Roman empire.

He asks about Rome as Rome, the iron kingdom. The Most High still interprets.

Reformed

Calvin: he wished for the truth concerning the fourth beast, different from the others, very terrible; iron teeth, brazen claws. The subjugation of so many kings by the Romans was a difference worthy of notice; a few robbers growing great until no corner missed their yoke.

In practice: ask about the beast God showed, as Daniel did. The fourth beast, Rome, is different from the rest.

Daniel 7:20

20and concerning the ten horns that were on its head and the other horn which came up, and before which three fell, even that horn that had eyes and a mouth that spoke arrogantly, whose look was more stout than its fellows.

Arminian

Ten horns on its head; the other horn which came up, before which three fell; eyes and a mouth that spoke arrogantly, whose; it seemed greater than its companions. Wesley writes no note here; his nearest (v. 8): another little horn—probably either the Turk or the Romish antichrist. Later (v. 24): and another—this seems to mean the Romish antichrist.

Wesley names a reading, not a license to invent a modern map. Held in common: eyes, mouth, three fell. The court still sits.

Reformed

Calvin: ten horns—provinces; a finite number for an indefinite one. The little horn refers to the Caesars, who attracted the whole government after depriving people and senate of power. Three horns broken: the Caesars craftily diminished people and senate. Eyes: they tyrannized with cunning, not open violence; mouth: they vomited forth reproachful speeches.

A creature with eyes and a mouth. Ten horns, another horn, three fell.

Key contrast Both see the other horn with eyes and a mouth that spoke arrogantly, whose, before which three fell. Arminians hear Wesley: probably the Turk or the Romish antichrist (v. 8), and at v. 24 the Romish antichrist. Reformed readers follow Calvin: Julius Caesar and the Caesars; three horns are people and senate diminished by craft.

Daniel 7:21

21I saw, and the same horn made war with the saints, and prevailed against them,

Arminian

This horn waged war against the saints and prevailed against them. Wesley writes no note here; his nearest (v. 8, 24): the little horn, the Romish antichrist; (v. 25) he will wear out the saints of the Most High.

In practice: the saints are worn, not abandoned. Prevailing is permitted, not final. Both traditions agree: war with the saints until the Ancient of Days comes.

Reformed

Calvin: the little horn made war with the saints so as to prevail against them. Not Antiochus, though that reading is plausible; no war was carried on so continuously against the Church as after the Caesars arose and Christ was made manifest. The Caesars raged until the Church almost disappeared; the little horn prevailed in appearance for a short time.

Prevailing is appearance, timed by God. Common ground: the horn wars and seems to win. The Ancient of Days is about to come.

Key contrast Both confess that this horn warred against the saints and prevailed until the Ancient of Days came. Arminians follow Wesley: the Romish antichrist (vv. 8, 24) whose war wears out the saints until the end of antichrist’s reign. Reformed readers follow Calvin: the Caesars after Christ was made manifest; they raged until the Church almost disappeared, and the horn prevailed only in appearance for a short time.

Daniel 7:22

22until the Ancient of Days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the Most High, and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom.

Arminian

Until the Ancient of Days came, judgment was given to the saints of the Most High, and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom. Wesley writes no note here; his nearest (v. 9): God the king and judge of all, the Ancient of days, because of his eternal deity; and (v. 18) the saints reign with Christ forever.

In practice: the Judge sits; the saints receive. Ancient of Days, judgment for the saints, they possess the kingdom. 13–14 already gave the Son of Man the dominion; do not re-litigate that here. The passage is not a rapture timetable.

Reformed

Calvin: until the Ancient of days came—God erected his tribunal and appeared as judge in the person of Christ. Immediately after the promulgation of the gospel God stretched forth his hand; the saints received the kingdom when the gospel was free and the Church no longer lay prostrate.

God comes when he proves himself the Church’s deliverer. Judgment given; saints possess. This is not a second-advent war between the camps. The horn’s war has an “until”: it lasts only until the Ancient of Days comes and judgment is given to the saints.

Daniel 7:23

23“So he said, ‘The fourth animal will be a fourth kingdom on earth, which will be different from all the kingdoms, and will devour the whole earth, and will tread it down and break it in pieces.

Arminian

The fourth beast: a fourth kingdom on earth, different from all the kingdoms; it shall devour the whole earth, trample it down, break it to pieces. Wesley writes no note here; his nearest (v. 7): a fourth beast—the Roman empire.

For the hearer: different and devouring is still a creature under the Most High. Stay with Rome as Wesley and Calvin both read it. The passage is not a modern map.

Reformed

Calvin: the fourth beast signifies a fourth kingdom, different from all. The Roman Empire was more extensive and powerful than the other monarchies; no single head, a mingled confusion, senate and people—a monster. It will devour, break, tread down the whole earth; such lust for dominion never existed before.

Application: different is still under the court that will sit. Both readings: fourth, Rome, devouring.

Daniel 7:24

24As for the ten horns, ten kings will arise out of this kingdom. Another will arise after them; and he will be different from the former, and he will put down three kings.

Arminian

Ten horns: ten kings shall arise; Another will arise after them, different from the former, and he will put down three kings. Wesley: and another—this seems to mean the Romish antichrist.

Wesley names that figure from history. Common to both: ten kings, then another, three put down.

Reformed

Calvin: ten horns are ten kings under one empire; ten denotes many, not ten literal kings in sequence. Another king, different—the Caesars, to whom the monarchy passed after the triumvirate. He shall afflict three kings: the Caesars diminished people and senate; the beast seemed entire while one little horn held the sway.

Different is still timed. Ten, then another, three down.

Key contrast Both hear ten kings, then another who puts down three. Arminians typically hear Wesley: this other seems to mean the Romish antichrist. Calvinists follow Calvin: the Caesars, different from the republican horns; three kings are the strength of people and senate infringed by craft, not ten literal kings at Rome.

Daniel 7:25

25He will speak words against the Most High, and will wear out the saints of the Most High. He will plan to change the times and the law; and they will be given into his hand until a time and times and half a time.

Arminian

He will speak words against the Most High, wear out the saints, plan to change the times and the law; they will be given into his hand until a time and times and half a time. Wesley: until a time and times—the numbers of Daniel and John seem to agree. Daniel was certainly prophetical in these things, and his prophecy reacheth to the end of times, even of antichrist’s reign.

In practice: the saints are worn, not abandoned. The times are limited. Blasphemy, wearing out, a measured hand.

Reformed

Calvin: this last king a professed enemy of the Church; the Caesars uttered edicts against the Most High and afflicted the saints of the lofty ones. He thought to change times and law—all order, not the Decalogue only. They shall be delivered into his hand: tyrants do nothing without permission. A time, times, and the division of a time: not a year of 365 days; a period whose end is in God’s secret counsel, then shortened for the elect.

Application: given into his hand is still God’s hand. Words against the Most High; the saints worn; the time is bounded. The passage is not a 1,260-year war.

Key contrast Both confess the horn speaks against the Most High, wears out the saints, and holds them for a limited time. Arminians typically side with Wesley: the numbers of Daniel and John seem to agree; the prophecy reaches to the end of times, even of antichrist’s reign. Reformed interpreters usually follow Calvin: the Caesars; time, times, and a division of time are not literal years but a period God has bounded and then shortens.

Daniel 7:26

26“‘But the judgment will be set, and they will take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it to the end.

Arminian

The judgment; his dominion shall be taken away, consumed and to destroy it to the end. Wesley writes no note here; his nearest (v. 11): destroyed—this cannot but be meant of the ruin and judgment of antichrist.

The court sits. The horn’s dominion is taken, consumed, destroyed. The scene is a judgment, not a stalemate. The blaspheming horn does not last.

Reformed

Calvin: the judgment shall sit—God restores order; the world feels his providence. They take away the beast’s power to dissipate and destroy even to the end: final overthrow, an incurable wound, when God executes the penalty of that unbridled ferocity.

In practice: the court that sat in vv. 9–10 sits again here. Dominion taken, destroyed to the end. The saints receive what follows.

Key contrast Both confess the court sits and the horn’s dominion is taken away to the end. Arminians follow Wesley (on v. 11): the ruin and judgment of antichrist. Reformed readers side with Calvin: the fourth beast finally slain, power destroyed even to the end, no hope of a like revival. The passage is not a battleground for rival end-time schemes.

Daniel 7:27

27The kingdom and the dominion, and the greatness of the kingdoms under the whole sky, will be given to the people of the saints of the Most High. His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and all dominions will serve and obey him.’

Arminian

The kingdom and the dominion, and the greatness of the kingdoms under the whole sky given to the people of the saints of the Most High; his kingdom an everlasting kingdom; all dominions will serve and obey him. Wesley writes no note here; his nearest (v. 18): Jesus Christ being their king, they shall reign with him, and possess the kingdom for ever. 13–14 already gave the Son of Man everlasting dominion.

Given to the saints; they reign with their King. Agreed on both sides: everlasting kingdom; all dominions serve him. Do not re-litigate the Son of Man except as this interpretation already given.

Reformed

Calvin: a kingdom, power, and extensive dominion given to the people of the saints. Not the final day of Christ’s advent, but the first preaching of the gospel, when the name of Christ was held in honor. What belongs to the Head is transferred to the body: the Church reigns because Christ reigns. All powers shall serve—some out of all nations, not every person.

Pastorally: given, everlasting, under heaven yet not of the world. The saints receive; his kingdom everlasting; dominions obey him. 13–14 already stated the christology.

Daniel 7:28

28“Here is the end of the matter. As for me, Daniel, my thoughts troubled me greatly, and my face was changed in me; but I kept the matter in my heart.”

Arminian

Here is the end of the matter. Daniel’s thoughts greatly alarm him; his color changes; he keeps the matter in his heart. Wesley: of the matter—of the vision, and the angel’s interpretation.

The vision ends; he keeps it. Alarm does not void trust. Both readings: end of the matter, kept in the heart. The saints still receive the kingdom; the horn still does not last.

Reformed

Calvin: an end was put to the vision, that the faithful rest and look for nothing beyond it. He was disturbed in his thoughts, his countenance changed, lest any think the vision a vanished specter. He laid up the discourse in his heart, a faithful interpreter for the Church.

Pastorally: keep what God showed; do not itch past the end of the matter. Agreed on both sides: alarmed, color changed, kept in the heart. God still gives the kingdom to the saints.

Voices from both camps

Classic Arminian and Reformed comment on Daniel 7:15–28, in the writers’ own words, agreed that the saints receive the everlasting kingdom and the blaspheming horn does not last.

Arminian Notable voices

“Unto one—that is, to an angel, that ministered. The truth—the true meaning of this vision. Four great beasts—that is, four great monarchies. But the saints—Jesus Christ being their king, they shall reign with him, and possess the kingdom for ever. And another—this seems to mean the Romish antichrist. Until a time and times—the numbers of Daniel and John seem to agree. Daniel was certainly prophetical in these things, and his prophecy reacheth to the end of times, even of antichrist’s reign. Of the matter—of the vision, and the angel’s interpretation.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Daniel 7:15–28)

Reformed Notable voices

“His spirit vanished; he was almost lifeless; the visions of his head frightened him. He came to one of those standing by—an angel—and asked the truth. Four kingdoms arose. The saints of the most High shall take the kingdom and possess the kingdom for ever. The little horn relates to Julius Caesar and the other Caesars who succeeded him. The little horn made war with the saints so as to prevail against them: the cruelty of the Caesars after Christ was made manifest. Until the Ancient of days came: God erected his tribunal in the person of Christ after the promulgation of the gospel. The fourth beast signifies a fourth kingdom, the Roman, different from all. Ten horns are ten kings; ten signifies many. A king shall arise different from those—the Caesars. He shall speak words against the most High, wear out the saints, think to change times and law; they shall be given into his hand for a time, times, and the division of a time. The judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion. The kingdom shall be given to the people of the saints of the most High. An end was put to the vision; he laid up the discourse in his heart.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Daniel 7:15–28)
