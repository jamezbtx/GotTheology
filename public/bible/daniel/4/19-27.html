Passage

Daniel 4

Verses 19–27 · ESV · Voices

Daniel is dismayed, then interprets: the tree is the king; a watcher has decreed he will be driven to the beasts until he knows the Most High rules. The stump means the kingdom will be confirmed when he knows Heaven rules. Counsel: break off sins by righteousness and mercy to the oppressed, that there may perhaps be a lengthening of prosperity.

Daniel 4:19

19Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was dismayed for a while, and his thoughts alarmed him. The king answered and said, “Belteshazzar, let not the dream or the interpretation alarm you.” Belteshazzar answered and said, “My lord, may the dream be for those who hate you and its interpretation for your enemies!

Arminian

Daniel dismayed; the king tells him not to fear; Daniel wishes the dream on enemies. Wesley: he foresaw troubles on a king he reverenced; what address and excellent spirit in this short preface. Speak out, let the event be what it will.

Arminian dismay: real pity for a real tyrant. Jeremiah had bid them pray for Babylon’s peace. Loyalty under the Most High is not a culture-war. The interpretation will still be plain.

Reformed

Calvin: sorrow, not ecstasy. He was bound to pray for him; until the seventy years, not lawful to invoke wrath. Faithful to his king though a tyrant. The pious fear God’s judgments even when spared; the impious sleep when threatened. He does not turn aside from unsparing reproof.

Reformed dismay: the Prophet’s middle course—humanity without flattery. Shared: alarmed, then he speaks. The tree is next.

Daniel 4:20

20The tree you saw, which grew and became strong, so that its top reached to heaven, and it was visible to the end of the whole earth,

Arminian

The tree you saw: grown strong, top to heaven, visible to the end of the earth. Recital before the naming. Shared with v. 22: this height is the king’s.

Arminian saw: he walks the king back through his own vision. Means of hearing. The Most High still rules the tree that reached.

Reformed

Calvin: he follows up with perseverance. Calls him lord with cordiality; meanwhile ambassador of the Supreme King, discourse above the king’s command.

Reformed saw: the figure is not in doubt. Shared: the same tree. Beauty and food in v. 21.

Daniel 4:21

21whose leaves were beautiful and its fruit abundant, and in which was food for all, under which beasts of the field found shade, and in whose branches the birds of the heavens lived—

Arminian

Beautiful leaves, abundant fruit, food for all, shade, birds. The gifts of 4:12 now sit on the man who must hear “it is you.”

Arminian food: real usefulness under a flawed crown. Shelter does not cancel the chop. No slogan about empire as such.

Reformed

Calvin: political order as trees on whose fruit all feed. God’s blessing in princes even when they neglect duty; grace not all extinguished.

Reformed food: ordinance, not this man’s equity. Shared: food for all. The naming is v. 22.

Daniel 4:22

22it is you, O king, who have grown and become strong. Your greatness has grown and reaches to heaven, and your dominion to the ends of the earth.

Arminian

It is you, O king. Greatness to heaven, dominion to the ends of the earth. Wesley: thou art high and mighty in the majesty which God hath given thee; to the Caspian, Euxine, Aegean, Mediterranean.

Arminian you: no riddle left. Gifted majesty, real growth. The Most High who gives (4:17) named the giver in the height. Agency of conquest under that gift.

Reformed

Calvin: he does not speak with hesitation or obscurity. Thou art the king. Why? Thou hast become great and strong; magnitude extended to the heavens, power to the ends of the earth.

Reformed you: the interpretation is the decree already. Shared with Wesley: it is you. The watcher-sentence follows.

Daniel 4:23

23And because the king saw a watcher, a holy one, coming down from heaven and saying, ‘Chop down the tree and destroy it, but leave the stump of its roots in the earth, bound with a band of iron and bronze, in the tender grass of the field, and let him be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his portion be with the beasts of the field, till seven periods of time pass over him,’

Arminian

A watcher from heaven: chop down, leave the stump, dew, beasts, seven times. Recital of 4:13–16 as the king’s own sight. Shared: stump is mercy, not annihilation.

Arminian watcher: heaven’s word, already heard. Seven times still to be lived. The king remains a man under the sentence.

Reformed

Calvin: angels called watchers, prompt to fulfill bidding. Seven times—long; he wished to put forth an example for all ages. Cut down, yet root remains.

Reformed watcher: the same host that subscribed in 4:17. Shared: chop, and leave the stump. Interpretation proper is v. 24.

Daniel 4:24

24this is the interpretation, O king: It is a decree of the Most High, which has come upon my lord the king,

Arminian

This is the interpretation: a decree of the Most High upon my lord the king. Wesley’s thundering peal is coming; here the source is named. Not the watchers instead of God—the Most High decrees.

Arminian decree: real sentence on a real lord. Daniel still says “my lord.” Authority of office under a higher decree.

Reformed

Calvin: judgment overhanging. The message sorrowful; kings hardly bear being cited before God’s tribunal. He still elevates the Word above the king.

Reformed decree: Most High, not fortune. Shared: upon my lord the king. The driving out is v. 25.

Daniel 4:25

25that you shall be driven from among men, and your dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field. You shall be made to eat grass like an ox, and you shall be wet with the dew of heaven, and seven periods of time shall pass over you, till you know that the Most High rules the kingdom of men and gives it to whom he will.

Arminian

Driven from men; dwell with beasts; eat grass; wet with dew; seven times; till you know the Most High rules the kingdom of men and gives it to whom He will. Wesley: a thundering peal; wonderful the king could hear it without fury, yet the Lord withheld him. Seven years. Till thou know—how hard for lofty princes to learn this lesson.

Arminian till you know: the goal is knowledge of the hub already named (4:17; 2:21). Real driving, real grass. He remains accountable in the field. Match the hub: a King who governs and judges, leaving this learning as a real second cause—the king must come to know.

Reformed

Calvin: they shall cast thee out from among men; habitation with beasts; grass like an ox. Liberty of the Prophet: he shuts his eyes to worldly grandeur. Until thou acknowledge how great a Lord in the kingdom of men, who delivers it to whomsoever He will. Difficult to attribute supreme power to God; especially when raised, we snatch His honor. Violent medicine to learn humility.

Reformed till you know: effectual teaching by the rod. Match the hub: He actually gives the kingdom. Shared with Wesley: seven times, and the Most High rules. The stump’s mercy is v. 26.

Daniel 4:26

26And as it was commanded to leave the stump of the roots of the tree, your kingdom shall be confirmed for you from the time that you know that Heaven rules.

Arminian

The stump: your kingdom shall be confirmed from the time you know that Heaven rules. Wesley: that God who sits in heaven rules over all. Shared: the stump is mercy. Restoration is tied to knowing, not to a bargain that skips the seven times.

Arminian confirmed: real return of a real kingdom when he knows. Heaven rules—the same as 4:17. Knowing is a human act under that rule, not empty theater.

Reformed

Calvin: God did not treat him so severely as to give no way to clemency. He mitigates extreme rigor to induce him to call upon God and repent, through hope of pardon. Not merely moderate station but former dignity. General doctrine: we are invited to repentance when God puts an end to chastisements. From which thou shalt know there is power in heaven—promise of spiritual grace; he would never have acquired this of his own accord unless touched by the Spirit.

Reformed confirmed: mercy in the root, and the knowing itself is gift. Shared: stump, and Heaven rules. Counsel to break off sins is next—not a fake warning.

Daniel 4:27

27Therefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable to you: break off your sins by practicing righteousness, and your iniquities by showing mercy to the oppressed, that there may perhaps be a lengthening of your prosperity.”

Arminian

Let my counsel be acceptable: break off your sins by practicing righteousness, and your iniquities by showing mercy to the oppressed, that there may perhaps be a lengthening of your prosperity. Wesley: if it may be—Daniel was not certain of pardon for him, nor did he altogether despair of it. Wisdom and tenderness, and yet plainness. The call to repent is real. Alms here are mercy to the oppressed, not a works-gospel invented against a fake warning.

Arminian perhaps: a real counsel a king can take or refuse. Righteousness and mercy are deeds under grace, testimonies of turning, not a purchase of the seven times. Wesley’s “if it may be” keeps the lengthening uncertain: God may pity; Daniel will not promise what he does not know. No Pelagian leftover—the king already lives under the Most High’s decree—and no despair that counsel is pointless.

Reformed

Calvin: may my counsel please thee; break off thy sins by righteousness and pity to the poor. Exhortation to repentance; Prophets touch outward duties of charity as fruits, not as the root. True justice is pity—not papal satisfactions or works of supererogation. We do not redeem sin before God as if beneficence were expiation; we may restore what we took before men. The decree stands, yet the warning treats men kindly; Ahab’s case shows God sometimes tempers the rod when even signs of turning appear. The medicine is to cease from errors and open the eyes.

Reformed perhaps: the call is real, the lengthening is God’s to give, not alms buying a quieter year. Calvin refuses a merit-reading of the poor. Shared with Wesley: repent, show mercy, do not despair. They part on how sure the “perhaps” is, and how far alms reach.

Key contrast Both take the call as real: the tree is the king, the stump is mercy, break off sins. Neither a works-gospel that purchases the kingdom nor a fake warning. Wesley hears “if it may be” as uncertainty of pardon without despair. Calvin hears righteousness and mercy to the poor as the fruit and medicine of repentance, not satisfaction before God; any lengthening is God’s tempering of the rod.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a tree that is the king, a stump left in mercy, and counsel to break off sins—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Daniel 4:19–27.

Arminian Notable voices

“Troubled him—because he fore-saw such troubles coming upon the king for whom he had a high reverence. Let not the dream trouble thee—speak out, let the event be what it will. They shall drive thee—this was such a thundering peal, that it was wonderful the king could endure to hear it without fury boiling in his heart, yet the Lord with-held him. Seven times—seven years. Till thou know—how hard is it for lofty princes to learn this lesson. If it may be—Daniel was not certain of pardon for him, nor did he altogether despair of it. With what wisdom and tenderness does he speak: and yet with what plainness.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Dan 4:19, 25, 27)

Reformed Notable voices

“May my counsel please thee, and break off thy sins by righteousness, and thy iniquities by pity to the poor. Daniel wished to exhort the king to repentance; works are the testimonies to real repentance. True justice consists in pity towards the poor—not works of supererogation, not a satisfaction that redeems fault or penalty before God. The call is real: he might obtain some pardon, even if he had paid the penalty; God always warns by threatening for the purpose of treating men more kindly. We ought always to be prepared to suffer God’s chastisements; yet it is no slight alleviation when we are persuaded of his desire to be propitious.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Dan 4:27)
