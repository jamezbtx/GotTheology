Passage

Daniel 4

Verses 28–37 · ESV · Voices

Twelve months after the warning, Nebuchadnezzar boasts of great Babylon built by his power. A voice from heaven takes the kingdom; he is driven to the beasts until he knows the Most High rules. At the end of the days he lifts his eyes; reason returns; he blesses the Most High, whose will none can stay. Majesty is restored. He praises the King of heaven, who is able to humble the proud.

Daniel 4:28

28All this came upon King Nebuchadnezzar.

Arminian

All this came upon King Nebuchadnezzar. Wesley: with admirable propriety the person changes—these verses in the third person; at v. 34 he speaks again in the first. The dream is no longer a figure.

Arminian came: real history on a named king. The Most High who warned (4:27) now acts. Delay of twelve months (v. 29) was room, not a canceled word.

Reformed

Calvin: the dream executed. God might have struck at once; He waited, and the king grew prouder. The reprobate abuse forbearance and heap a treasure of vengeance.

Reformed came: the word stands. Shared: all this, upon this king. The roof is next.

Daniel 4:29

29At the end of twelve months he was walking on the roof of the royal palace of Babylon,

Arminian

At the end of twelve months, walking on the roof of the royal palace. Wesley’s ease of v. 4 has lasted a year after counsel. Time to break off sins was given; it was not taken.

Arminian twelve months: real days. Creaturely walking under a decree already spoken. No culture-war on palaces. The Most High still rules a man who still strolls.

Reformed

Calvin: some think he was touched and so spared for a year; more likely God withdrew His hand, and the sloth is less excusable. The Prophet’s voice ought to have frightened him as thunder. He does not lay aside pride.

Reformed twelve months: patience, then the blow. Shared: roof of Babylon. The boast is v. 30.

Daniel 4:30

30and the king answered and said, “Is not this great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power as a royal residence and for the glory of my majesty?”

Arminian

Is not this great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power as a royal residence and for the glory of my majesty? The tree names itself. Not a sermon against cities; a man stealing the Giver’s praise.

Arminian built: real bricks, real pride. He did build; the sin is “by my mighty power.” Agency under the Most High who actually gives kingdoms (4:17). The sentence still finds a mouth that can stop.

Reformed

Calvin: which I—using the pronoun with emphasis—have built in the greatness of my valor. He claims all and robs God. Unless the Lord builds the city, the watchman watches in vain. He transfers to himself the glory of Semiramis. Cities are built by industry, and kings may be praised if they leave God’s praise inviolate. When men exalt themselves they bury His blessing.

Reformed built: the boast is the snatching of honor 4:17 forbade. Shared: great Babylon, my power, my majesty. The voice cuts the sentence.

Daniel 4:31

31While the words were still in the king's mouth, there fell a voice from heaven, “O King Nebuchadnezzar, to you it is spoken: The kingdom has departed from you,

Arminian

While the words were still in his mouth, a voice from heaven: the kingdom has departed from you. Immediate, not a culture-war. The Most High answers a still-moving tongue.

Arminian while: real words interrupted. Second causes boast; heaven speaks. The king remains the addressee—“O King”—even as the kingdom leaves.

Reformed

Calvin: God does not now admonish by Prophet or dream; He sends forth His own voice. The word was in the king’s mouth when the voice was heard. Time for repentance had been allowed. They say to thee, O King—not for honor but ridicule. This royal majesty will not hinder God from laying thee prostrate.

Reformed while: the timing is His. Shared: voice, and the kingdom departed. Driving out is v. 32.

Daniel 4:32

32and you shall be driven from among men, and your dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field. And you shall be made to eat grass like an ox, and seven periods of time shall pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules the kingdom of men and gives it to whom he will.”

Arminian

Driven from men; dwelling with beasts; grass like an ox; seven times; until you know the Most High rules the kingdom of men and gives it to whom He will. The hub sentence of 4:17 now becomes the king’s until.

Arminian until you know: the end is knowledge. Real driving, real grass. He must come to know—creaturely under that rule. Match the hub: a King who governs and judges, leaving this until as a real second cause.

Reformed

Calvin: they shall expel thee from among men. Whether madness or conspiracy, the example is memorable. Seven times until thou know there is a lofty ruler in the kingdoms of men. Special clemency: the rod useful, not hardening. Tyrants enclose God in heaven; he must feel God reigns on earth.

Reformed until you know: effectual teaching. Match the hub: He actually gives it. Shared: seven times, Most High rules. Fulfillment is immediate.

Daniel 4:33

33Immediately the word was fulfilled against Nebuchadnezzar. He was driven from among men and ate grass like an ox, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven till his hair grew as long as eagles' feathers, and his nails were like birds' claws.

Arminian

Immediately the word was fulfilled. Driven; ate grass; wet with dew; hair like eagles’ feathers; nails like birds’ claws. Wesley: bereft of understanding, as a man distracted he fled to the woods.

Arminian immediately: no gap after the voice. A real body in the dew. The stump’s mercy (4:26) is still a man among beasts, not annihilation.

Reformed

Calvin: as soon as the voice, he was cast out from mankind. God left him a human form and deprived him of reason. Hair like eagles’ feathers, claws like birds: a horrid spectacle. He differs in nothing from the brutes except the form.

Reformed immediately: the word does what it says. Shared: grass, dew, feathers, claws. The end of the days is v. 34.

Daniel 4:34

34At the end of the days I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted my eyes to heaven, and my reason returned to me, and I blessed the Most High, and praised and honored him who lives forever, for his dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom endures from generation to generation;

Arminian

At the end of the days I lifted my eyes to heaven, and my reason returned, and I blessed the Most High, who lives forever; everlasting dominion. Wesley: God shined upon his soul and gave him understanding to consider his sad state and its causes; he honoured Him by prayer and praise, adoring justice and mercy, giving God the glory of sovereignty and unchangeableness.

Arminian lifted: a real looking up. Reason returns, and he blesses. Grace shines; the eyes still lift. Match the hub: a King who governs and provides, leaving this lifting as a real second cause. The stump’s promise (4:26) meets a man who now knows Heaven rules.

Reformed

Calvin: from which thou shalt know there is power in heaven—he would never have acquired this of his own accord unless touched by the secret movement of the Spirit. God not only punished to humble him but worked in him and changed his mind. Dominion everlasting: he now confesses what 2:44 and 4:3 already said of God.

Reformed lifted: the looking up is inside the return of reason as gift. Shared: eyes to heaven, reason, blessing. v. 35 states the will none can stay.

Daniel 4:35

35all the inhabitants of the earth are accounted as nothing, and he does according to his will among the host of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth; and none can stay his hand or say to him, “What have you done?”

Arminian

Inhabitants of the earth as nothing; He does according to His will among the host of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth; none can stay His hand or say, What have you done? Wesley: a due consideration of God’s infinite greatness makes the creature appear as nothing—nothing to help, nothing to hurt, nothing in duration, nothing without dependence, influence, and support from God. His will—the Lord of hosts, the only absolute and universal monarch of the world.

Arminian will: the Most High rules—this doing is real. It does not empty the lifting of v. 34 or the pride of v. 30 of agency. None can stay His hand; men still boast, still look up, still praise. Match the hub: a King who governs, provides, and judges, leaving creaturely choice as a real second cause. “What have you done?” is silenced as a charge, not as a mouth.

Reformed

Calvin: not only power in heaven but a ruler in the kingdoms of men. He will give it to whom He will. None can stay His hand: government must remain with the one God. Bare foreknowledge without this government robs Him of half His glory. He appoints future events and governs, allowing nothing to happen by chance or without His direction.

Reformed will: effectual rule, not a bare permission after the seven times ran out. The inhabitants are as nothing because He actually does His will among them. Match the hub: nothing happens but what He has knowingly and willingly decreed, yet He is not the author of the king’s pride as pride.

Key contrast Both confess the Most High rules: He does according to His will in heaven and on earth, and none can stay His hand (4:35), as He gives the kingdom to whom He will (4:17) and changes times and kings (2:21). Arminians typically hear real looking up and real praise under that rule. Reformed readers typically hear effectual restoration: reason returned because God worked in him; none stays His hand (Calvin).

Daniel 4:36

36At the same time my reason returned to me, and for the glory of my kingdom, my majesty and splendor returned to me. My counselors and my lords sought me, and I was established in my kingdom, and still more greatness was added to me.

Arminian

Reason returned; majesty and splendor returned; counselors sought him; established; more greatness added. Wesley: the majesty of a king in his countenance again; owned and obeyed; more august than before.

Arminian returned: the stump confirmed (4:26). Real lords seeking a real king. Added greatness is gift, not a prosperity wage for the grass. He is still a man under the King of heaven.

Reformed

Calvin: not a merely moderate station but former dignity, as if never trenched upon. Clemency in the cut. The root remained that the tree might flourish again.

Reformed returned: appointed restoration. Shared: reason, majesty, more added. The last verse is praise, not a building boast.

Daniel 4:37

37Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and extol and honor the King of heaven, for all his works are right and his ways are just; and those who walk in pride he is able to humble.

Arminian

Now I praise and extol and honor the King of heaven; His works are right, His ways just; those who walk in pride He is able to humble. Wesley: thus can the Lord make the stoutest hearts to stoop. This doxology proceeds from his heart. God is truth; He dealt justly for pride, in faithfulness afflicted, in tenderness restored. Able to abase—as declared upon me. What authority had any to say this man was no convert? We can no more doubt of his salvation than of Solomon’s.

Arminian praise: a real mouth, now in the first person. Able to humble is the lesson of the chapter. Walk in pride is still a walk men take; He is able to meet it. Wesley is sure of conversion; Calvin earlier left the edict’s sincerity in doubt. The shared confession is the King of heaven. No culture-war on empire remains in this praise.

Reformed

Calvin: whether the confession is true and genuine repentance is not beyond doubt from the earlier edict; he may still mingle deities. Here the language is higher: King of heaven, works right, ways just, able to humble the proud. God extracted glory among the nations. The proud are His to abase; that ability is the same stretched-out hand as 4:17.

Reformed praise: the ability to humble is effectual, as the seven times proved. Shared with Wesley: works right, ways just, proud humbled. Daniel 4 ends where 2:21 and 4:17 pointed—the Most High rules. How that rule meets a king who looks up remains the fork already named.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a boast on a palace roof, a voice from heaven, and a king who lifts his eyes—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Daniel 4:28–37.

Arminian Notable voices

“Mine understanding returned—God shined upon his soul, and gave him understanding to consider his sad state, and the causes of it. As nothing—a due consideration of God’s infinite greatness makes the creature appear as nothing; nothing without dependence, and influence, and support from God. His will—being the Lord of hosts, and the only absolute and universal monarch of the world. Now I praise—thus can the Lord make the stoutest hearts to stoop, and do him homage. This doxology proceeds from his heart. He is wise, and hath dealt justly with me for my pride, and in very faithfulness hath afflicted me, and in very tenderness hath restored me. Able to abase—he had a just controversy with me, and I have no ground to quarrel with him.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Dan 4:34–37)

Reformed Notable voices

“Which I have built, and that too in the greatness of my valor. By claiming all things as his own, he robs God of all honor. Unless God builds the city, the watchman watches but in vain. While the speech was in the mouth of the king, a voice descended from heaven. Mortals must know God to be a ruler in the kingdoms of men. He will give it to whom he will. He does according to his will among the host of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth. None can stay his hand. Ambition, skill, or wealth do not profit unless God raises them by his stretched out hand.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Dan 4:17, 30–32)
