Passage

Daniel 3

Verses 13–23 · ESV · Voices

In furious rage Nebuchadnezzar brings the three and offers a last chance: worship the image, or the furnace—and who is the god who will deliver? They answer that they need not debate it. Their God is able to deliver; but if not, they will not serve the king’s gods or worship the image. The furnace is heated seven times; mighty men bind them; the flame kills the executioners; the three fall bound into the fire.

Daniel 3:13

13Then Nebuchadnezzar in furious rage commanded that Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego be brought. So they brought these men before the king.

Arminian

Furious rage; they are brought. Wesley’s king who owned their God (2:47) now summons the three he appointed (2:49). Rage is a man’s, not a cue for politics.

Arminian brought: real fury, real bodies before a throne. The Most High still rules. The next words will be theirs; this verse only gets them into the room.

Reformed

Calvin: nothing more troublesome to kings than to see their authority despised. After the king is cool again he will seem to moderate; here he is inflamed. Pride when decrees are disobeyed.

Reformed brought: even the rage sits inside the trial God will use. Shared: they stand before him. The question is whether the charge is true.

Daniel 3:14

14Nebuchadnezzar answered and said to them, “Is it true, O Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, that you do not serve my gods or worship the golden image that I have set up?

Arminian

Is it true that you do not serve my gods or worship the image I set up? The fact is already true (3:12). He names the image as his.

Arminian is it true: a real question, a last opening. They could still bow. Grace that has kept them not-falling does not cancel the hour in which they must speak.

Reformed

Calvin: he addresses them doubtfully and gives a free choice, which implies moderation after the rage. He seems to free them from blame if they will only bow hereafter. The statue which I have created, which I have made: my orders ought to be sufficient.

Reformed is it true: even the pause is in God’s hand. Shared: he asks. v. 15 will add the blasphemy.

Daniel 3:15

15Now if you are ready when you hear the sound of the horn, pipe, lyre, trigon, harp, bagpipe, and every kind of music, to fall down and worship the image that I have made, well and good. But if you do not worship, you shall immediately be cast into a burning fiery furnace. And who is the god who will deliver you out of my hands?”

Arminian

If you are ready when the music sounds, well; if not, the furnace; and who is the god who will deliver you out of my hands? The bargain is on the table: worship and live. Faith that will not take it is vv. 16–18.

Arminian who: a king claiming the last word against every god. The Most High who changes kings (2:21) is being dared. The three are still free to take the music. This is not yet fatalism, and not yet a prosperity deal—only a threat.

Reformed

Calvin: he breaks forth into sacrilegious blasphemy—there is no god who can deliver out of my hand. Kings swell with pride while they pretend piety; they substitute themselves for God. What God? Clearly there is no God. Intolerable arrogance and diabolical fury. Arm yourself with this: nothing pleases God but what He has commanded in His word.

Reformed who: God will answer the taunt in the furnace, not because the king’s mouth forced Him. Shared: deliver out of my hands. Their answer will not accept the frame.

Daniel 3:16

16Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered and said to the king, “O Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter.

Arminian

O Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter. Wesley: we care not; it is vain to debate; the king is resolved to have his will, and we are resolved on the contrary. No long counsel about whether utility might excuse a bow.

Arminian no need: the matter is already settled in a real will under the Most High. They do not bargain. This is not fatalism (whatever will be, will be, so why stand?) and not a prosperity script (answer so that God must pay). It is refusal to treat worship as negotiable. Assisting grace meets three men who actually say no.

Reformed

Calvin: we do not care—the matter is settled. When men entice us to deny the true God we must close our ears and refuse all deliberation. We have already insulted God when we even question the propriety of swerving from His worship. Hypocrites cloak delinquencies with utility; these men would have had the same excuses and did not use them. God’s glory is upheld when worship is without the slightest doubt.

Reformed no need: the Spirit has already fixed the heart. Shared with Wesley: no debate. The able God of v. 17 is next, not as a deal.

Daniel 3:17

17If this be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of your hand, O king.

Arminian

Our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the furnace, and He will deliver us out of your hand. Wesley will complete it: from death or in death. Able is confessed; the timing is God’s. This is hope, not a contract that makes deliverance the price of obedience.

Arminian able: they serve, and He can. Serving is theirs under grace; ability is His. If this verse stood alone it could be twisted into prosperity (He will, therefore we bow not as a trade). v. 18 blocks that. They do not obey in order to be rescued; they obey because He is their God, and they know He is able.

Reformed

Calvin: first reason—God has sufficient power and strength to liberate them. Place our life in God’s hand. Sufficient protection: no method of preserving life better than to throw ourselves on His protection. Be thoroughly sure God will defend a good cause. They bring forward God’s worship: they undertake nothing rashly, but are worshippers of the true God.

Reformed able: confidence in the Guardian, not in a technique. Shared: He is able, and He will deliver—out of the king’s hand, whether by snatching from the fire or through it. v. 18 will name the other side so this hope is not a bargain.

Daniel 3:18

18But if not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden image that you have set up.”

Arminian

But if not, be it known that we will not serve your gods or worship the image you set up. Wesley: all one which way God would honour Himself; resolved to suffer rather than sin, and leave the cause to God. Let him do his worst. God will deliver us either from death, or in death.

Arminian if not: the hinge. Faith that will not bargain. They do not require rescue as the condition of loyalty. They could still fall down; they will not. No Pelagian leftover—they already live by the God they serve—and no fatalism that shrugs as if their yes and no were nothing. Creaturely resolve under the Most High: if He does not snatch them out, they still will not worship. Match the hub: a King who governs and judges, leaving this stand as a real second cause.

Reformed

Calvin: even if we must die, life is not of so much value as to deny God for preserving it. If God be unwilling to deliver us from death, we will not worship thy gods. Two points prepare for martyrdom: persuaded God is protector and will liberate if expedient; and prepared to die, aspiring to a better life. God’s glory more precious than a hundred lives. They accuse the king of arrogating too much—thy statue, thy authority is of no moment, since we know it to be a fictitious deity.

Reformed if not: effectual constancy, not a bare permission after they happened to be brave. The Spirit holds the refusal. Match the hub: nothing happens but what He has knowingly and willingly decreed, yet He is not the author of the king’s fury, and their not-bowing is true obedience, not puppetry.

Key contrast Both camps hear 3:16–18 as faith that will not bargain: God is able, and if He does not deliver from the furnace they still will not worship. This is not a prosperity deal (obedience as a way to purchase rescue) and not fatalism (outcomes fixed, so the stand is empty). Arminians typically stress the real human resolve under assisting grace—they could bow and do not. Reformed readers typically stress Spirit-wrought perseverance: life placed in God’s hand, glory preferred to a hundred lives (Calvin).

Daniel 3:19

19Then Nebuchadnezzar was filled with fury, and the expression of his face was changed against Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. He ordered the furnace heated seven times more than it was usually heated.

Arminian

Filled with fury; his face changed; furnace heated seven times. Wesley: God purposed to shew His power when the king did his worst, and in the thing wherein he dealt proudly, to be above him (v. 20). Rage answers a no that would not deal.

Arminian seven times: real heat, a real face. The Most High still rules. Their “if not” has been heard; the king’s worst is still his.

Reformed

Calvin: God seems at first to desert them; no help appears. This was a living proof of faithfulness. They were prepared to endure everything, treating God’s glory of greater value than their own life. The king’s pride: when they find decrees disobeyed, they swell. Satan occupies the mind; nothing but cruelty. Constancy pleased God though it produced no immediate fruit before the world.

Reformed seven times: God secretly impels the extra heat so the miracle cannot be doubted. Shared: fury, and hotter fire. Binding is next.

Daniel 3:20

20And he ordered some of the mighty men of his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, and to cast them into the burning fiery furnace.

Arminian

Mighty men of the army to bind them and cast them in. Wesley: what did he think—that they would refuse to go, or that their God would defend them from his power, or that if He had, his mighty men could have prevailed? None of this: God purposed to shew His power when the king did his worst.

Arminian bind: real ropes, real soldiers. Agency on the king’s side remains evil and real. The three do not struggle out of a bargain they already refused.

Reformed

Calvin: he chose from his servants the strongest to bind them and cast them in. God removed all doubts by this, so that light would emerge from the darkness when Satan tried to shut it out.

Reformed bind: the strongest men are means to make the deliverance unmistakable. Shared: bound, and cast. Clothes still on in v. 21.

Daniel 3:21

21Then these men were bound in their cloaks, their tunics, their hats, and their other garments, and they were thrown into the burning fiery furnace.

Arminian

Bound in cloaks, tunics, hats, other garments, thrown in. Wesley will mark that all this is expressed with emphasis to make God’s power more glorious in their preservation (v. 23).

Arminian thrown: they go in as they stood—dressed, bound, not stripped for a stunt. Faith that will not bargain has now run out of rooms to talk.

Reformed

Calvin: diligently enumerates whatever tends to prove God’s power. Bound: this might at first suffocate them; they remained untouched and then walked loose. No falsehood of Satan could obscure it.

Reformed thrown: the details are the setup for v. 27’s inspection. Shared: bound in their clothes. The flame will take other men first.

Daniel 3:22

22Because the king's order was urgent and the furnace overheated, the flame of the fire killed those men who took up Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.

Arminian

The order urgent; furnace overheated; the flame killed those who took them up. Wesley: that same heat that slew the executioners might much more easily have killed the three even before they fell down—so preservation will be the more glorious.

Arminian killed: real death at the door. The Most High who is able (v. 17) does not owe the soldiers a bargain either. Urgency is the king’s; the extra heat is his worst.

Reformed

Calvin: since the command was urgent and the furnace heated sevenfold, the extremity of the flame consumed those who executed the orders, while the three walked through the fuel. The king’s slaves consumed by the very smoke; the fire without effect on the servants of God.

Reformed killed: God frustrates the impious by the very heat they chose. Shared: the takers die. The three still have to fall in.

Daniel 3:23

23And these three men, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, fell bound into the burning fiery furnace.

Arminian

These three fell bound into the burning fiery furnace. Wesley: all this expressed with emphasis to make the power of God more glorious in their preservation. The story pauses in the fire. The fourth figure is the next range.

Arminian fell: they really go in. “If not” is now the furnace, not a phrase. They have not worshiped. Deliverance from death or in death is God’s to show; they have already stood.

Reformed

Calvin: they fell down in the furnace of fire. Bound, they could not take care of themselves. The result of the history is their preservation so surprisingly beyond expectation. Nothing safer than to make God the guardian of our life—not that we shall be preserved from every danger, but that we hope for liberation if useful, and do not hesitate to meet death if God so please.

Reformed fell: still in His hand. Shared: three men, bound, in the fire. Who walks there with them belongs to 3:24–30.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a last chance at the furnace door and a faith that will not bargain—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Daniel 3:13–23.

Arminian Notable voices

“We are not careful—there is no need of any answer in this case, for it is in vain for us to debate the matter; the king is resolved to have his will of us, and we are resolved on the contrary. But if not—it was therefore all one to them, which way God would honour himself; they were resolved to suffer rather than sin, and leave the cause to God. Let him do his worst. God will deliver us either from death, or in death.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Dan 3:16, 18)

Reformed Notable voices

“We do not care; the matter is settled. We ought to settle it with ourselves never to be induced by any reason to withdraw from the sincere worship of God. God is sufficiently powerful to preserve us; and then, even if we must die, our life is not of so much value as to deny God for the sake of preserving it. If God be unwilling to deliver us from death, be it known to thee, O king, we will not worship thy gods. When our danger for the truth’s sake is imminent, we should learn to place our life in God’s hand, and then bravely and fearlessly devote ourselves to death.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Dan 3:16–18)
