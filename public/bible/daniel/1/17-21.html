Passage

Daniel 1

Verses 17–21 · ESV · Voices

God gives the four youths learning and skill in all literature and wisdom; Daniel is given understanding in visions and dreams. At the appointed time they stand before Nebuchadnezzar. None is found like them; in every matter of wisdom the king finds them ten times better than the magicians of his realm. Daniel remains until the first year of Cyrus.

Daniel 1:17

17As for these four youths, God gave them learning and skill in all literature and wisdom, and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams.

Arminian

God gave them knowledge and skill; Daniel had understanding in visions and dreams. Wesley will mark the later honor on the low estate of his people. The giving matches 1:2 and 1:9: the Most High rules, including minds.

Arminian “gave”: real learning under that rule. Three years of literature are still study; four youths still labor. Grace does not replace Chaldean primers with magic. Skill is gift meeting diligence, not a slogan, and not a culture fight about “expertise.” Match the hub: a King who governs and provides, leaving creaturely effort as a real second cause. Visions are added to Daniel without emptying the others of what they were given.

Reformed

Calvin: not a reward merely for pulse, as if frugality purchased the Spirit. God had a different intention: to extol Daniel for the prophetic office, and to fit his companions for later government. Literature here is liberal science, not the magical arts God abominates. “Understood dreams and visions” means he was endued with the prophetic spirit. God raised him by degrees to the fitting time.

Reformed “gave”: effectual rule, not a bare permission after they studied well. The learning is God’s actual hand, including the means—the three years, the literature, the singular gift. Match the hub: nothing happens but what He has knowingly and willingly decreed. Shared with Wesley: God gave; the four excel.

Key contrast Both confess the Most High rules: “God gave” learning. Arminians typically hear real study and real skill under that rule—the youths’ labor is governed without irresistible determination of every insight. Reformed readers typically hear effectual providence, not a bare permission: God adorned His servants and endued Daniel with the prophetic spirit (Calvin).

Daniel 1:18

18At the end of the time, when the king had commanded that they should be brought in, the chief of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar.

Arminian

At the end of the days the chief brings them in. Wesley: the king will try their proficiency in presence chamber and council. A set time, a human usher, a real court.

Arminian timing: three years finish because a king commanded and a prefect obeyed. God’s giving in v. 17 does not cancel the calendar. Second causes stand: the chief actually brings them.

Reformed

Calvin: Daniel relates how he and his companions were brought forward at a fixed time. Three years had been appointed for instruction. The prefect produces them. God through a long interval had adorned them with favor, rendering them conspicuous in the palace.

Reformed timing: the appointment is inside providence. Shared: they are brought in when the king said. The comparison of v. 19 is coming as a public prove, not as theater that never happened.

Daniel 1:19

19And the king spoke with them, and among all of them none was found like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. Therefore they stood before the king.

Arminian

The king spoke with them; none like the four; they stood before him. Wesley: presence chamber and council chamber, to try their proficiency; this shews the king’s ability and judgment—how else could he discern their fitness?

Arminian standing: Nebuchadnezzar really inquires and really finds. A pagan king’s discernment is creaturely, under the Most High, not a cue for a culture-war on palaces. Honor on Judah’s youths is gift; the king’s questions are still questions.

Reformed

Calvin: approved by the king and preferred to all the rest. The king and the courtiers ought to entertain such an opinion as should express sincere reverence. God wished to illustrate His own glory, since the king was compelled to wonder how they could surpass all the Chaldeans. What did Nebuchadnezzar propose but to extinguish remembrance of God? God frustrated the tyrant’s plan and made His name more illustrious.

Reformed standing: the preference is God’s hand, including a king’s interview. Shared with Wesley: none like them; they stand. The pride of the Chaldeans is already being answered.

Daniel 1:20

20And in every matter of wisdom and understanding about which the king inquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters that were in all his kingdom.

Arminian

In every matter of wisdom the king inquired, they were ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters of the realm. Wesley: farther confirmation of the king’s care whom he chose for offices of trust. Thus did God pour contempt upon the pride of the Chaldeans, and put honour on the low estate of his people.

Arminian ten times: a king’s measure, not a modern score. Wisdom here is fitness to serve, not a war on “elites.” God honors the low estate; the magicians remain a court fact, not a foil for politics. The youths’ excellence is real under the giving of v. 17.

Reformed

Calvin: foreigners and captives superior, though the monarch had spared no expense on his own people. Whence this superiority? The Chaldeans boasted of wisdom from birth and esteemed other nations barbarians. God whom they worship distributes talent and perception; no one is naturally gifted unless he receives this grace from heaven. God must be glorified because these four very far surpassed the Chaldeans. He usually causes His enemies to gaze with wonder on His power even when they most shun the light.

Reformed ten times: gift distributed from heaven, not luck in the school. Shared: better than the realm’s magicians. Liberal skill is not approval of enchantment.

Daniel 1:21

21And Daniel was there until the first year of King Cyrus.

Arminian

Daniel continued until the first year of Cyrus. Wesley: in the court of Babylon until Cyrus, then the Persian court; honour and high employment all that time; visions still in Cyrus’s third year (ch. 10:1).

Arminian continuance: a life spanning empires. The Most High who gave Jehoiakim into a hand (1:2) and gave favor and learning now keeps a servant through Babylon into Persia. Continuance is gift meeting a man who keeps fearing God—not a prosperity arc, and not fatalism that makes the years a blur.

Reformed

Calvin: he continued to the first year of Cyrus in the discharge of the prophetic office. He flourished among the Chaldeans and Assyrians and was acknowledged as a celebrated Prophet; he interpreted Belshazzar’s vision the night that king was slain. He always obtained the confidence and authority of a Prophet with the kings of Babylon.

Reformed continuance: office kept by the God who gave it. Shared with Wesley: until Cyrus, and not forgotten after. Chapter 1 closes where 1:2 opened—the Most High rules kingdoms. How that rule meets human agency remains the fork already named.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on learning God gave, four youths who stood before a king, and a prophet who continued until Cyrus—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Daniel 1:17–21.

Arminian Notable voices

“Before—both in the presence chamber, and in the council chamber, to try their proficiency; this shews the king’s ability and judgment, how else could he discern their fitness, and their excellency above others. The king enquired—this is a farther confirmation of the king’s noble endowments, and of his great care whom he chose to be in offices of trust. And thus did God pour contempt upon the pride of the Chaldeans, and put honour on the low estate of his people. Continued—in the court of Babylon until Cyrus, and then he was in the Persian court, and he lived in honour and high employment all that time.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Dan 1:19–21)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Prophet here shows how his authority was acquired for exercising the prophetic office with greater advantage. Knowledge and science were given in all literature and wisdom. Daniel was endued with a very singular gift—he was to be an interpreter of dreams. When it is here said Daniel understood dreams and visions, it has the sense of being endued with the prophetic spirit. God wished to raise his servant by degrees, and to bring him forth at the fitting time. God, therefore, must necessarily be glorified, because Daniel and his comrades very far surpassed the Chaldeans.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Dan 1:17–18)
