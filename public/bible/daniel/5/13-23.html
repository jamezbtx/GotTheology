Passage

Daniel 5

Verses 13–23 · ESV · Voices

Daniel is brought in before Belshazzar, offered purple, gold, and third place in the kingdom, and refuses to be paid for the reading. He recites how the Most High gave Nebuchadnezzar kingship and then humbled him, and rebukes Belshazzar for not humbling his heart though he knew all this, for drinking from the temple vessels, and for praising idols while not honoring the God in whose hand is his breath.

Daniel 5:13

13Then Daniel was brought in before the king. The king answered and said to Daniel, “You are that Daniel, one of the exiles of Judah, whom the king my father brought from Judah.

Arminian

Daniel is brought in; the king names him as that exile from Judah whom “my father” brought. Wesley writes no note on this verse. The queen has already named him; the king now faces the man he had passed by.

Arminian pastoral: God can still speak through a neglected servant if the king will hear. Being fetched as a captive does not empty Daniel’s office. The Most High is not a court brand; the exile is the one who can read. Pride still talks down while it asks for help.

Reformed

Calvin: the king does not acknowledge his own folly, but interrogates Daniel as a captive—Art thou that Daniel, of the captives of Judah, whom my father led away? He seems to speak contemptuously, to keep Daniel in servile obedience. He had heard before and said nothing.

Reformed pastoral: forgotten light is still light. God had already signalized Daniel; Belshazzar will use him only when terror has closed every other door. The exile stands because the Most High sent him, not because the throne has grown humble.

Daniel 5:14

14I have heard of you that the spirit of the gods is in you, and that light and understanding and excellent wisdom are found in you.

Arminian

I have heard: the spirit of the gods is in you; light, understanding, and excellent wisdom are found in you. Rumor now does the work the king would not do in peace.

Arminian note: he repeats the queen’s report as if it were news. Light already shown in the grandfather’s day is still available. Wisdom is God’s gift in a servant, not a trick to be hired. The king can still honor what he has heard; hearing is not yet a humbled heart.

Reformed

Calvin: I have heard of thee that the spirit of the gods is in thee. The profane ascribe excellence to heaven, yet mingle God and angels. This sentence shows how Belshazzar depends on reports and displays his slothfulness. He ought to have known the Prophet by personal experience.

Reformed note: rumor is a poor substitute for the light God already placed in the kingdom. Pagan wording cannot hide the gift, and it cannot excuse the years of neglect. God extracts even this praise from a proud mouth.

Daniel 5:15

15Now the wise men, the enchanters, have been brought in before me to read this writing and make known to me its interpretation, but they could not show the interpretation of the matter.

Arminian

The wise men and enchanters have been brought in; they could not show the interpretation. Babylon’s readers stop at plaster again.

Arminian pastoral: failure here is severe kindness. It clears the floor for a servant of the Most High. Hired wisdom cannot buy what God has not given; the king still has a chance to hear the reading without paying for a softer word.

Reformed

Calvin: all the wise men were brought, and they could not do it. God punished him by showing how profitless were all the Chaldeans in whom he trusted at the moment of his extremity. He preferred magi and soothsayers and rejected the holy Prophet.

Reformed pastoral: light refused becomes light withheld. The sealed line is itself judgment. God can address one man and blind a room of magi until the interpreter he ordained is fetched.

Daniel 5:16

16But I have heard that you can give interpretations and solve problems. Now if you can read the writing and make known to me its interpretation, you shall be clothed with purple and have a chain of gold around your neck and shall be the third ruler in the kingdom.”

Arminian

If you can read, purple, gold, and third place. Panic still shops with gifts. Wesley is silent here; on the vessels he had already said the king was triumphing over God.

Arminian pastoral: rewards cannot buy what only the living God gives. The offer is loud; the heart is still proud. Daniel may tell the truth whether the court likes it or not. Grace still calls the proud to humble themselves; this king tries to purchase a reading instead.

Reformed

Calvin: this shows him not sincerely touched by the fear of God. Hidden pride breaks forth in the promise. God had already deprived him of his royal dignity, yet he still wishes to raise others on high in defiance of God. The end of his reign was close, yet in security he offers this dignity.

Reformed pastoral: terror without repentance still hires rank. The wicked dread judgments, yet the pride of their hearts is not corrected. The Most High has already numbered the kingdom; the chain of gold cannot lengthen it.

Daniel 5:17

17Then Daniel answered and said before the king, “Let your gifts be for yourself, and give your rewards to another. Nevertheless, I will read the writing to the king and make known to him the interpretation.

Arminian

Let your gifts be for yourself; give your rewards to another. Nevertheless, I will read and make known the interpretation. The word is not for sale.

Arminian pastoral: Daniel refuses to be paid for the reading and still tells the truth. He is not bargaining with a tyrant; he is serving the Most High. Courage here is a real act of a will kept by God—not a pose, and not a culture-war speech. The interpretation will be true whether the gifts stay or go.

Reformed

Calvin: Daniel rejects the proffered gifts. He treats this impious and desperate king with greater asperity than Nebuchadnezzar, because he had arrived at his last extremity. Let thy gifts remain with thee: he wished to display unconquered greatness of mind and to lay prostrate the king’s foolish arrogance.

Reformed pastoral: the kingdom’s end is at hand; purple is already worthless. God’s prophet will speak, not as a hired decoder, but as a witness. The refusal is itself part of the verdict.

Daniel 5:18

18O king, the Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar your father kingship and greatness and glory and majesty.

Arminian

The Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar kingship, greatness, glory, and majesty. Wesley on that history: the great God will make all men know he rules all in earth too, and sets up at his pleasure whom he will, and plucks them down again.

Arminian note: the gift was real; it was never a license to forget the Giver. Sovereignty here is shared confession, not a party slogan. The Most High gave; the king remained accountable. Belshazzar is about to hear what his house already knew.

Reformed

Calvin: God gave him an empire and magnificence. Empires are bestowed on men by divine power and not by chance; there is no power but of God. His singular providence shines forth in kingdoms. Earthly kings are exalted by the hand of God, not by the chances of fortune.

Reformed note: the throne was a trust. Shared with Wesley: the Most High gave the majesty. Calvin presses the source: no sword remains unless God props it up. This is not a culture fight; it is the doctrine Daniel recites before a proud grandson.

Daniel 5:19

19And because of the greatness that he gave him, all peoples, nations, and languages trembled and feared before him. Whom he would, he killed, and whom he would, he kept alive; whom he would, he raised up, and whom he would, he humbled.

Arminian

Because of the greatness God gave him, peoples trembled. Whom he would, he killed, kept alive, raised, or humbled. The king’s will looked absolute; the sword was still borrowed.

Arminian pastoral: borrowed power can still shed blood. Wesley: how hard it is for lofty princes to learn this lesson. The nations’ fear does not make a man God. The Most High will be known; the grandson who knew this story is without excuse if he will not bow.

Reformed

Calvin: all mortals trembled at the sight of him because God conferred that majesty. Kings are divinely armed with authority. Daniel describes the greatness of royal power, not as lawful cruelty, but as the sword God had entrusted. They must hereafter render an account to the Supreme King.

Reformed pastoral: the tremble of nations is God’s inscription on a throne, not proof that the man is unaccountable. Shared with Wesley: God gave the greatness. The same hand that armed Nebuchadnezzar will judge Belshazzar.

Daniel 5:20

20But when his heart was lifted up and his spirit was hardened so that he dealt proudly, he was brought down from his kingly throne, and his glory was taken from him.

Arminian

Heart lifted up, spirit hardened, he dealt proudly; he was brought down, and his glory taken. Wesley: those that walk in pride he is able to abase; thus can the Lord make the stoutest hearts to stoop.

Arminian pastoral: pride is the king’s; the fall is the Lord’s. Hardening here is a man dealing proudly, not a fate-story that erases guilt. God can still abase the lofty; the lesson was given so this grandson might humble himself. He did not.

Reformed

Calvin: when his heart was exalted and his spirit strengthened in pride, he was cast down from the throne of his kingdom, and they deprived him of his majesty. God never endures to the end the pride of those who forget him when they have obtained the summit of power.

Reformed pastoral: a sudden change proved the point. Shared judgment: pride is judged by the Most High. Calvin names the Giver who also takes glory away. Sovereignty here is pastoral warning, not a weapon against neighbors.

Daniel 5:21

21He was driven from among the children of mankind, and his mind was made like that of a beast, and his dwelling was with the wild donkeys. He was fed grass like an ox, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, until he knew that the Most High God rules the kingdom of mankind and sets over it whom he will.

Arminian

Driven from mankind, mind like a beast, dwelling with wild donkeys, grass like an ox, dew of heaven—until he knew that the Most High rules the kingdom of mankind and sets over it whom he will. Wesley: being bereft of his understanding he fled to the woods; till thou know—how hard for lofty princes to learn this lesson. God shined upon his soul and gave him understanding again.

Arminian note: the end of the stroke is to know, not merely to be crushed. The Most High rules and appoints; the man is meant to confess it. Belshazzar is hearing a family mercy he has treated as a tale.

Reformed

Calvin: God set forth a memorable example in despoiling this king of his reason and intelligence. His body was moistened by the dews of heaven until he acknowledged that the Most High God ruled in the kingdom of men and appoints over it whomsoever he will. In Nebuchadnezzar we see the drunken confidence of kings who think they stand by their own power.

Reformed pastoral: the lesson is the same sentence Daniel now recites. Shared with Wesley: until he knew the Most High rules and sets up whom he will. God humbles so that his reign on earth is confessed. The grandson who forgot this is not uninformed; he is proud.

Daniel 5:22

22And you his son, Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this,

Arminian

You his son have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this. Wesley: from that God whom thou hast despised; thou hast made light of God, and the Lord makes light of thee.

Arminian pastoral: knowledge without a humbled heart is still guilt. He had the story in the house and would not bow. Grace had given light; the refusal is his. This is not a slogan about enemies; it is a call to any heart that knows and will not stoop.

Reformed

Calvin: thou didst know this. Those who sin knowingly and willfully are without the slightest excuse. You have provoked God’s anger on purpose. He ought to have been so affected by that domestic example as to submit himself to God. The example did not profit him.

Reformed pastoral: light in the family became a witness against him. Shared with Wesley: he despised the God he knew. Calvin names the obstinacy: he knew, and he would not humble his heart. Judgment on pride is just.

Key contrast Both condemn Belshazzar for knowing and not humbling himself. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s “thou hast despised” and “made light of God”—the king’s own insult against light he had. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s further word: those who sin knowingly are without excuse, and he provoked God’s anger on purpose after a domestic judgment meant to teach him.

Daniel 5:23

23but you have lifted up yourself against the Lord of heaven. And the vessels of his house have been brought in before you, and you and your lords, your wives, and your concubines have drunk wine from them. And you have praised the gods of silver and gold, of bronze, iron, wood, and stone, which do not see or hear or know, but the God in whose hand is your breath, and whose are all your ways, you have not honored.”

Arminian

Lifted up against the Lord of heaven: vessels brought, wine drunk, idols praised, the God in whose hand is his breath not honored. Wesley: triumphing thereby over God and his people; insulting the great God of heaven and earth.

Arminian pastoral: breath is in God’s hand; the toast was still the king’s choice. Dead things that cannot see were sung; the living God was not glorified. Shared judgment on pride and sacrilege. This is not a culture-war speech; it is a man using holy cups to mock the One who keeps him alive.

Reformed

Calvin: thou hast raised thyself against the Lord of heaven. He used the vessels for luxury and foul debauchery, and praised gods of silver and gold who neither see, nor hear, nor understand; but the living God, in whose hand is thy life and whose are all thy ways, thou hast not honored. This ingratitude was both base and shameful.

Reformed pastoral: the Most High holds his breath while he toasts stone. Shared with Wesley: the vessels were an insult to God. Calvin adds that he defrauded the God on whom he depends. Judgment is not random; pride has come to full term.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Daniel before the king, Nebuchadnezzar’s lesson, and Belshazzar’s unhumbled heart—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Daniel 5:13–23.

Arminian Notable voices

“Interpreted and applied by Daniel. To bring the vessels—Triumphing thereby over God and his people. And praised the gods of gold—At the same time insulting the great God of heaven and earth. The most high ruleth—Nebuchadnezzar and his flatterers conceded he was a god in earth unaccountable to any. But the great God will make all men know he rules all in earth too, and sets up at his pleasure whom he will, and plucks them down again. How hard is it for lofty princes to learn this lesson. From him—From that God whom thou hast despised. Art found wanting—There is no weight nor worth in thee; thou hast made light of God, and the Lord makes light of thee. Thus can the Lord make the stoutest hearts to stoop, and do him homage.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Dan 4:17, 25, 37; 5:2, 4, 24, 27)

Reformed Notable voices

“Here the king does not acknowledge his own folly, but interrogates Daniel as a captive. He had heard of him before and said nothing; now extreme necessity urges him. His confession was not ingenuous, but violently extorted by the secret instinct of God. Daniel rejects the gifts: Let thy gifts remain with thee. He wished to display unconquered greatness of mind and to lay prostrate the king’s foolish arrogance. The Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar a kingdom: empires are bestowed by divine power and not by chance; there is no power but of God. When his heart was lifted up, he was cast down. Thou his son hast not humbled thine heart, though thou knewest all this: you have provoked God’s anger on purpose. Thou hast raised thyself against the Lord of heaven, praised gods who neither see nor hear, and defrauded the living God, in whose hand is thy life.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Daniel (on Daniel 5:13–23)
