Passage

Romans 3

Verses 9–20 · WEB

Paul concludes his case: Jews and Gentiles alike are under sin. He strings together Old Testament texts to show that no one is righteous, no one seeks God, and no one does good. The law silences every mouth and brings knowledge of sin, so no one will be justified by works of the law. Both traditions affirm universal sinfulness. They differ on what “no one seeks God” implies about human ability and about the grace that precedes faith.

Romans 3:9

9What then? Are we better than they? No, in no way. For we previously warned both Jews and Greeks that they are all under sin.

Arminian

Jews are not better off than Gentiles; all are under sin. Arminian readers see the universal need that the gospel meets.

Reformed

Reformed readers see all humanity under the power and guilt of sin.

Romans 3:10

10As it is written, “There is no one righteous; no, not one.

Arminian

“There is no one righteous; no, not one.” Both traditions affirm that no one is righteous before God by nature.

Reformed

Reformed readers see here the doctrine of total depravity.

Romans 3:11

11There is no one who understands. There is no one who seeks after God.

Arminian

“There is no one who understands. There is no one who seeks after God.” Arminian readers agree that fallen people do not seek God on their own. Wesley taught that any seeking after God is the fruit of prevenient grace, which God gives to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the verse as teaching that no one will seek God unless God first regenerates him. Seeking God is evidence of grace already at work.

Key contrast Both agree that no one seeks God apart from grace. Arminian readers see prevenient grace given to all, enabling a response. Reformed readers see regenerating grace given to the elect, producing faith.

Romans 3:12

12They have all turned away. They have together become unprofitable. There is no one who does good, no, not so much as one.”

Arminian

All have turned away and become worthless; no one does good. Both traditions see sin affecting every person.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the corruption of every part of human nature.

Romans 3:13

13“Their throat is an open tomb. With their tongues they have used deceit.” “The poison of vipers is under their lips.”

Arminian

Their throats are open graves and their tongues deceive. Both traditions see sin in human speech.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the corruption of speech as a mark of the heart’s condition.

Romans 3:14

14“Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.”

Arminian

Their mouths are full of cursing and bitterness. Both traditions agree.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the overflow of the heart.

Romans 3:15

15“Their feet are swift to shed blood.

Arminian

Their feet are swift to shed blood. Both traditions see violence as a result of sin.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sin destroying human life.

Romans 3:16

16Destruction and misery are in their ways.

Arminian

Ruin and misery mark their paths. Both traditions agree.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the wages of sin.

Romans 3:17

17The way of peace, they haven’t known.”

Arminian

They have not known the way of peace. Both traditions see the absence of peace with God and others.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that peace comes only through justification.

Romans 3:18

18“There is no fear of God before their eyes.”

Arminian

There is no fear of God before their eyes. Both traditions see the root of sin in the absence of reverence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the loss of the fear of God as the heart of depravity.

Romans 3:19

19Now we know that whatever things the law says, it speaks to those who are under the law, that every mouth may be closed, and all the world may be brought under the judgment of God.

Arminian

The law speaks so that every mouth may be stopped and the whole world held accountable. Arminian readers see universal accountability before God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the law silencing every claim to righteousness.

Romans 3:20

20Because by the works of the law, no flesh will be justified in his sight; for through the law comes the knowledge of sin.

Arminian

No one will be justified by works of the law, because through the law comes knowledge of sin. Both traditions agree that the law cannot save.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the law’s role in revealing sin and driving sinners to Christ.
