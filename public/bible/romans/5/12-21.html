Passage

Romans 5

Verses 12–21 · ESV · Voices

Paul now sets Adam and Christ as two public persons, two federal heads: through one man sin and death entered the world; through the one man Jesus Christ the free gift of righteousness abounds. The trespass is not like the gift. Where sin increased, grace abounded all the more, leading to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

Romans 5:12

12Therefore, just as sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned—

Arminian

Therefore, just as sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned. Wesley will name the figure in v. 14: Adam is a public person, a federal head of mankind, the fountain of sin and death to mankind by his offence. All sinned: not a private copycat, but the race in its head.

Classical Arminians keep federal headship here. Do not invent a Pelagian note: we do not ‘only imitate Adam.’ Death spread to all because all sinned in the one man—Wesley: being then in the loins of their first parent, the common head and representative of them all. Both camps must say this together first. The fork is not whether Adam is head; it is how far the second Adam’s gift runs (vv. 15–19).

Reformed

Sin came through one man, death through sin, death to all because all sinned. Calvin: frivolous was the Pelagian gloss that sin descended by imitation from Adam to the whole human race; for Christ would in this case become only the exemplar and not the cause of righteousness. He speaks not here of actual sin: if everyone for himself contracted guilt, why compare Adam and Christ? Innate and hereditary depravity is what is referred to.

Reformed confession: Adam is federal head, not a bad example we happen to copy. The comparison with Christ requires a real headship on both sides. All sinned in the one man; the whole mass is involved. Do not flatten this into each person’s isolated fall. The second Adam will be cause of righteousness, not a mere pattern.

Romans 5:13

13for sin indeed was in the world before the law was given, but sin is not counted where there is no law.

Arminian

Sin was in the world before the law was given, but sin is not counted where there is no law. The parenthesis explains how death could reign from Adam to Moses (v. 14) even without the Mosaic code. Sin was real; the law of Moses had not yet made it a counted transgression of that statute.

Classical Arminians hear this as support for federal Adam, not for a law-only fall. Death reigned over people who had not sinned in Adam’s own manner of trespass. The ruin is older than Sinai. Grace in the second Adam will have to be older and larger than a Mosaic patch.

Reformed

Sin was in the world before the law; it is not counted where there is no law. Yet death reigned (v. 14). The argument shuts the door on treating Romans 5 as a story of copycat actual sins against a published code.

Reformed accent: hereditary ruin is already in view. If sin were only imitation of a known law, the gap from Adam to Moses would empty Paul’s comparison. Calvin’s question stands: why set Adam over against Christ if each contracted guilt only for himself? The law will later increase the trespass (v. 20); it did not create the death that already reigned.

Romans 5:14

14Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sinning was not like the transgression of Adam, who was a type of the one who was to come.

Arminian

Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sinning was not like Adam’s transgression. Adam was a type of the one who was to come. Wesley: who is the figure of him that was to come—each of them being a public person, and a federal head of mankind. The one, the fountain of sin and death to mankind by his offence; the other, of righteousness and life by his free gift. Thus far the apostle shows the agreement between the first and second Adam.

Classical Arminians take both heads as federal. Adam is not a bad example; Christ is not a good example. Each is a public person. The type is the agreement: one man for the many. The differences begin in v. 15. Keep the federal on this side of the fork, or the second Adam’s gift will shrink into a pattern we copy.

Reformed

Death reigned even over those who had not sinned in the likeness of Adam’s transgression. Adam is a type of the coming one. Calvin’s anti-Pelagian stroke belongs here: if sin were by imitation only, Christ would be only the exemplar and not the cause of righteousness. The type requires two causes, two heads.

Reformed confession: Adam and Christ are both federal. The reign of death from Adam to Moses proves a ruin deeper than copied acts. Christ as antitype is the cause of righteousness for those united to Him. Agreement first: two public persons. Difference next: the gift is not like the trespass.

Romans 5:15

15But the free gift is not like the trespass. For if many died through one man’s trespass, much more have the grace of God and the free gift by the grace of that one man Jesus Christ abounded for many.

Arminian

But the free gift is not like the trespass. If many died through one man’s trespass, much more have the grace of God and the free gift by the grace of that one man Jesus Christ abounded for many. Wesley: the free gift is of many offences unto justification—unto the purchasing it for all men, notwithstanding many offences. The second Adam is federal head as truly as the first; the gift super-abounds.

Classical Arminians hear grace for all in the second Adam, sincerely offered. The many who died in Adam are all; the gift abounds as widely as the ruin, purchasing justification for all men. ‘Abounded for many’ is not a smaller math than the trespass. Those who actually reign in life are those who receive (v. 17), who are in the second Adam by faith. Do not flatten the many of the gift into a hidden list while keeping the many of the death universal. Keep federal on both sides: not imitation of Adam, not mere imitation of Christ.

Reformed

The free gift is not like the trespass: grace abounds much more. Calvin: the other difference is that the benefit of Christ does not come to all men, while Adam has involved his whole race in condemnation. The curse derived from Adam is conveyed by nature and includes the whole mass; to participate in the grace of Christ we must be ingrafted in him by faith. Enough to be man to partake of sin; necessary to be a believer to enjoy the righteousness of Christ.

Reformed reading: the many of the trespass are the race in Adam; the many of the gift are those actually united to the second Adam. Grace abounds to those ingrafted—more than the trespass, not merely as wide as a frustrated offer. Federal on both sides: Adam the cause of death, Christ the cause of righteousness, not exemplars. The gift is not like the trespass because it is received by faith, not conveyed by nature.

Key contrast Both confess that the free gift is not like the trespass, that Adam and Christ are federal heads, and that grace in the one man Jesus Christ super-abounds. Arminians typically hear the gift abounding for many as sincerely for all men, purchased in the second Adam and offered to all. Reformed readers typically hear the many of the gift as those actually united to Christ, ingrafted by faith—the benefit not coming to all men as Adam’s curse came.

Romans 5:16

16And the free gift is not like the result of that one man’s sin. For the judgment following one trespass brought condemnation, but the free gift following many trespasses brought justification.

Arminian

The free gift is not like the result of that one man’s sin: judgment following one trespass brought condemnation; the free gift following many trespasses brought justification. Wesley: the sentence was by one offence to Adam’s condemnation—occasioning the sentence of death to pass upon him, which, by consequence, overwhelmed his posterity. But the free gift is of many offences unto justification—unto the purchasing it for all men, notwithstanding many offences.

Classical Arminians keep both federal. One trespass condemned the race in Adam; the gift meets many offences and purchases justification for all men. Sincerely offered in the second Adam; received by those who believe (v. 19: many = all that believe, constituted righteous). The unlike-ness is mercy’s surplus, not a narrower headship. Do not invent a note in which we only imitate Adam’s fall and Christ’s gift.

Reformed

One trespass, condemnation; many trespasses, a gift that brought justification. The unlike-ness is in the gift’s abundance, not in a weaker headship. Calvin: Adam has involved his whole race; the benefit of Christ does not come to all men. Participation is by being ingrafted in him by faith.

Reformed confession: the gift brought justification—actually, for those in Christ. Federal Adam condemned; federal Christ justifies the many united to Him. The many offences make the gift more wonderful, not more uncertain. Justification here is the verdict God renders, not a purchase left hanging until every hearer takes it.

Key contrast Both keep federal heads and both hear a gift unlike the one-trespass condemnation, meeting many offences with justification. Arminians typically take Wesley’s clause: the gift is unto justification, purchasing it for all men. Reformed readers typically take the gift as actually bringing justification to those ingrafted in Christ, not extended in reality to all.

Romans 5:17

17For if, because of one man’s trespass, death reigned through that one man, much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and the free gift of righteousness reign in life through the one man Jesus Christ.

Arminian

If death reigned through one man because of his trespass, much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and the free gift of righteousness reign in life through the one man Jesus Christ. Wesley: as death passed upon all men, in that all had sinned; so life passed upon all men, (who are in the second Adam by faith,) in that all are justified. Receiving is by faith; the abundance is the second Adam’s federal gift.

Classical Arminians hear a sincere abundance: grace for all in the second Adam, offered to all, received by those who believe. ‘Those who receive’ is the living condition, enabled by grace, not a secret roll. Reigning in life is through the one man Jesus Christ—federal, not imitation. The much-more is the surplus of the gift over the trespass, held out as truly as death was imposed.

Reformed

Death reigned through one man; much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and the free gift of righteousness reign in life through the one man Jesus Christ. Calvin: to partake of the miserable inheritance of sin it is enough to be man; to enjoy the righteousness of Christ it is necessary to be a believer, for a participation of him is attained only by faith. We must be ingrafted in him by faith.

Reformed reading: ‘those who receive’ are those actually united to the second Adam. The abundance is effectual in them; they will reign. Federal Christ, not a pattern. The much-more is certain for the ingrafted, because grace is not conveyed by nature as the curse was. Receiving is the Spirit-given faith of those God unites to Christ, not an independent lever.

Key contrast Both say those who receive the abundance of grace will reign in life through the one man Jesus Christ, and both keep Him a federal head, not a mere example. Arminians typically hear a sincere abundance offered to all and received by faith as the living condition. Reformed readers typically hear those who receive as those ingrafted in Christ by faith, in whom the abundance is actual and the reign certain.

Romans 5:18

18Therefore, as one trespass led to condemnation for all men, so one act of righteousness leads to justification and life for all men.

Arminian

Therefore, as one trespass led to condemnation for all men, so one act of righteousness leads to justification and life for all men. Wesley: as death passed upon all men, in that all had sinned; so life passed upon all men, (who are in the second Adam by faith,) in that all are justified. The first ‘all men’ is the race in Adam; the second is the sincere counterpart in Christ—grace for all in the second Adam, offered to all, life for those in Him by faith.

Classical Arminians refuse to shrink the second ‘all men’ into a hidden list while keeping the first universal. Federal both ways: one trespass condemned all; one act of righteousness is unto justification and life for all men, purchasing it (v. 16) and constituting righteous those who believe (v. 19). Do not invent Pelagian notes on either side. The offer is as wide as the condemnation; actual justification is of those who receive.

Reformed

One trespass, condemnation for all men; one act of righteousness, justification and life for all men. Calvin: he makes this favor common to all, because it is propounded to all, and not because it is in reality extended to all; for though Christ suffered for the sins of the whole world, and is offered through God’s benignity indiscriminately to all, yet all do not receive him. Adam involved his whole race; Christ’s benefit comes to those ingrafted by faith.

Reformed confession: the first ‘all men’ is the mass in Adam by nature. The second ‘all men’ is all who are in Christ—or all kinds to whom the gift is propounded. Federal both ways; not imitation. Justification and life are actual for the many united to the second Adam. The gospel is offered indiscriminately; the verdict is not in reality extended to all.

Key contrast Both keep Adam and Christ as federal heads: one trespass condemned all men; one act of righteousness is unto justification and life. Arminians typically take the second ‘all men’ as sincerely as wide as the first—grace for all in the second Adam, offered to all, life for those in Him by faith. Reformed readers typically take Calvin’s distinction: common because propounded to all, not because in reality extended to all; justification and life actual for those ingrafted in Christ.

Romans 5:19

19For as by the one man’s disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man’s obedience the many will be made righteous.

Arminian

By the one man’s disobedience the many were made sinners; by the one man’s obedience the many will be made righteous. Wesley: as by the disobedience of one man many (that is, all men) were constituted sinners—being then in the loins of their first parent, the common head and representative of them all. So by the obedience of one—by his obedience unto death; by his dying for us. Many—all that believe. Shall be constituted righteous—justified, pardoned.

Classical Arminians keep federal on both sides. The first many are all men in Adam; the second many are all that believe, constituted righteous by Christ’s obedience, not by imitating it. The gift purchased justification for all men (v. 16); those constituted righteous are believers. Do not invent a Pelagian note that we only imitate Adam, or a note that we only imitate Christ. Headship, obedience, constitution: the many are made, not merely inspired.

Reformed

By one man’s disobedience the many were made sinners; by one man’s obedience the many will be made righteous. Calvin: not actual sin each contracted for himself, or Christ would be only exemplar; innate and hereditary depravity, and then participation of Christ’s righteousness only by faith, ingrafted in him. To get Adam’s curse it is enough to be man; to enjoy Christ’s righteousness it is necessary to be a believer.

Reformed confession: the many made sinners are the race in Adam, federally, really. The many made righteous are those in Christ actually justified—united to the second Adam, His obedience theirs, not a pattern they copy. Grace abounds to those so united. Federal both ways; the second many is not coextensive with the first, because the gift is not conveyed by nature.

Key contrast Both keep Adam and Christ as federal heads: the many were constituted sinners in the first by disobedience, and the many are constituted righteous in the second by his obedience, not by imitation. Arminians typically take the first many as all men and the second as all that believe, the obedience purchasing justification for all men sincerely offered. Reformed readers typically take the second many as those actually united to Christ and justified, the benefit not in reality extended to all.

Romans 5:20

20Now the law came in to increase the trespass, but where sin increased, grace abounded all the more,

Arminian

The law came in to increase the trespass, but where sin increased, grace abounded all the more. Sinai did not create the ruin of vv. 12–14; it made the trespass overflow. Grace in the second Adam is not outpaced by that increase.

Classical Arminians hear a sincere overflow: the gift that purchased justification for all men (v. 16) meets the increased trespass and still super-abounds. The law’s coming does not shrink the second Adam’s headship. Abounding grace is offered as truly as the increased trespass is real. Those who receive it (v. 17) find it more than enough.

Reformed

The law came in to increase the trespass; where sin increased, grace abounded all the more. The law is not a third federal head; it aggravates what Adam already brought. Calvin’s much-more of the gift over the curse continues: grace is not a near-match for sin; it overflows.

Reformed accent: the abounding is the abounding named for those who receive (v. 17)—those ingrafted in Christ. The law increases the trespass in the mass; grace abounds all the more in the people united to the second Adam. Super-abundance is effectual mercy, not a frustrated surplus left unapplied.

Romans 5:21

21so that, as sin reigned in death, grace also might reign through righteousness leading to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

Arminian

So that, as sin reigned in death, grace also might reign through righteousness leading to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. The aim of the overflow: a new reign. Sin’s reign was through the first Adam; grace’s reign is through righteousness—the second Adam’s gift—unto life.

Classical Arminians hear a federal close. Grace reigns through righteousness, not through our imitation of Christ. Eternal life is through Jesus Christ our Lord, the public person whose free gift is righteousness and life. The reign is sincerely held out in the gospel; those in the second Adam by faith live under it. Both camps must end where they began: two heads, not two examples.

Reformed

Sin reigned in death; grace reigns through righteousness unto eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. The purpose clause lands the comparison: two reigns, two heads, two outcomes. Calvin: Christ is the cause of righteousness, not the exemplar; participation is by faith, ingrafted in him.

Reformed comfort: grace actually reigns in those united to Christ. Through righteousness—His, received—leading to eternal life. The Lord named at the end is the federal head whose obedience constituted the many righteous. The reign is not a maybe over the whole mass; it is the certain government of grace in the people of the second Adam.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Adam and Christ as federal heads, the free gift unlike the trespass, and the many constituted sinners or righteous—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Romans 5:12–21.

Arminian Notable voices

“Who is the figure of him that was to come — Each of them being a public person, and a federal head of mankind. The one, the fountain of sin and death to mankind by his offence; the other, of righteousness and life by his free gift. Thus far the apostle shows the agreement between the first and second Adam. As death passed upon all men, in that all had sinned; so life passed upon all men, (who are in the second Adam by faith,) in that all are justified. The sentence was by one offence to Adam’s condemnation — Occasioning the sentence of death to pass upon him, which, by consequence, overwhelmed his posterity. But the free gift is of many offences unto justification — Unto the purchasing it for all men, notwithstanding many offences. As by the disobedience of one man many (that is, all men) were constituted sinners — Being then in the loins of their first parent, the common head and representative of them all. So by the obedience of one — By his obedience unto death; by his dying for us. Many — All that believe. Shall be constituted righteous — Justified, pardoned.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Romans 5:14, 16, 19)

Reformed Notable voices

“Frivolous then was the gloss, by which formerly the Pelagians endeavored to elude the words of Paul, and held, that sin descended by imitation from Adam to the whole human race; for Christ would in this case become only the exemplar and not the cause of righteousness. Besides, we may easily conclude, that he speaks not here of actual sin; for if everyone for himself contracted guilt, why did Paul form a comparison between Adam and Christ? It then follows that our innate and hereditary depravity is what is here referred to. The other difference is, that the benefit of Christ does not come to all men, while Adam has involved his whole race in condemnation; for as the curse we derive from Adam is conveyed to us by nature, it includes the whole mass; but that we may come to a participation of the grace of Christ, we must be ingrafted in him by faith. He makes this favor common to all, because it is propounded to all, and not because it is in reality extended to all; for though Christ suffered for the sins of the whole world, and is offered through God’s benignity indiscriminately to all, yet all do not receive him.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Romans (on Romans 5:12, 17–18)
