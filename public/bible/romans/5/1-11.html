Passage

Romans 5

Verses 1–11 · WEB

Justified by faith, believers have peace with God, access to grace, and hope of glory. Even suffering produces endurance, character, and hope, because God’s love has been poured into their hearts by the Spirit. Paul then shows the depth of that love: Christ died for the ungodly while we were still sinners, and if we were reconciled while enemies, much more will we be saved by his life. Both traditions treasure this passage as a basis for assurance; they differ slightly on how certain final salvation is for every justified person.

Romans 5:1

1Being therefore justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ;

Arminian

Justified by faith, we have peace with God through Christ. Arminian readers see peace as the fruit of a justification received by faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see peace as the settled status of those whom God has declared righteous.

Romans 5:2

2through whom we also have our access by faith into this grace in which we stand. We rejoice in hope of the glory of God.

Arminian

Through Christ we have access by faith into the grace in which we stand, and we rejoice in hope of God’s glory. Arminian readers stress “by faith” and the standing believers have as they continue in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a secure standing in grace that leads to glory.

Romans 5:3

3Not only this, but we also rejoice in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces perseverance;

Arminian

We rejoice in suffering, because suffering produces endurance. Both traditions see God using trials for believers’ growth.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s providence ordering suffering for his people’s good.

Romans 5:4

4and perseverance, proven character; and proven character, hope;

Arminian

Endurance produces proven character, and character produces hope. Arminian readers see growth in grace through cooperation with God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sanctification as God’s work in his people through trials.

Romans 5:5

5and hope doesn’t disappoint us, because God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to us.

Arminian

Hope does not disappoint, because God’s love has been poured into our hearts by the Spirit. Wesley saw in this verse the inward experience of God’s love that gives assurance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit’s work as the guarantee that hope will be fulfilled.

Romans 5:6

6For while we were yet weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly.

Arminian

While we were still weak, Christ died for the ungodly. Arminian readers see the ungodly as all sinners, for whom Christ died.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the ungodly as those Christ came to save, and his death as effective for them.

Romans 5:7

7For one will hardly die for a righteous man. Yet perhaps for a good person someone would even dare to die.

Arminian

Rarely would someone die for a righteous person. Paul sets up the contrast with Christ’s love.

Reformed

Reformed readers see human love falling far short of God’s love.

Romans 5:8

8But God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.

Arminian

God shows his love in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Arminian readers see God’s love extended to sinners as sinners, a love that reaches all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the love of God for his people shown in Christ’s death for them before they believed.

Key contrast Both see in verse 8 the clearest proof of God’s love. Arminian readers see it extended to all sinners. Reformed readers see it as God’s particular love for his people, shown before they ever believed.

Romans 5:9

9Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we will be saved from God’s wrath through him.

Arminian

Justified by his blood, much more will we be saved from wrath through him. Arminian readers see strong assurance for those who are justified and remain in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a sure argument from the greater to the lesser: if God justified us, he will certainly save us from wrath.

Romans 5:10

10For if while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we will be saved by his life.

Arminian

If we were reconciled while enemies, much more will we be saved by his life. Arminian readers find comfort in Christ’s ongoing life and intercession for believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the certainty of final salvation for all who have been reconciled.

Key contrast Both find strong assurance in Paul’s “much more.” Reformed readers see the final salvation of all the reconciled as certain. Arminian readers see the same assurance for those who continue in Christ, while reading it alongside the warnings elsewhere in Scripture.

Romans 5:11

11Not only so, but we also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received the reconciliation.

Arminian

We rejoice in God through Christ, through whom we have received reconciliation. Both traditions see joy in God as the goal of salvation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see reconciliation as a gift already received.
