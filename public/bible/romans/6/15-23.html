Passage

Romans 6

Verses 15–23 · WEB

Paul raises the question again in a new form: should believers sin because they are under grace? He answers with the image of slavery. Everyone serves some master, either sin leading to death or obedience leading to righteousness. Believers have been set free from sin and become slaves of God, bearing fruit toward holiness and eternal life. The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ. Both traditions see this as a call to holiness grounded in grace. They differ somewhat on the relation of holiness to final salvation.

Romans 6:15

15What then? Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace? May it never be!

Arminian

Shall we sin because we are under grace? By no means. Both traditions reject antinomianism.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace as producing holiness, not permitting sin.

Romans 6:16

16Don’t you know that when you present yourselves as servants and obey someone, you are the servants of whomever you obey, whether of sin to death, or of obedience to righteousness?

Arminian

You are slaves of the one you obey, either of sin leading to death or of obedience leading to righteousness. Arminian readers see a real choice of masters that believers must continue to make.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the two masters as describing the unregenerate and the regenerate.

Romans 6:17

17But thanks be to God that, whereas you were bondservants of sin, you became obedient from the heart to that form of teaching to which you were delivered.

Arminian

You were slaves of sin but became obedient from the heart to the teaching you received. Arminian readers see a heartfelt response to the gospel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see heart obedience as the work of God, for which Paul thanks God.

Romans 6:18

18Being made free from sin, you became bondservants of righteousness.

Arminian

Set free from sin, you became slaves of righteousness. Both traditions see a change of masters.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the definitive transfer accomplished by grace.

Romans 6:19

19I speak in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh; for as you presented your members as servants to uncleanness and to wickedness upon wickedness, even so now present your members as servants to righteousness for sanctification.

Arminian

Present your members as slaves to righteousness leading to sanctification. Both traditions see sanctification as active and progressive.

Reformed

Reformed readers see believers cooperating with God in sanctification.

Romans 6:20

20For when you were servants of sin, you were free from righteousness.

Arminian

When you were slaves of sin, you were free from righteousness. Both traditions see the bondage of the old life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the inability of the unregenerate to do true good.

Romans 6:21

21What fruit then did you have at that time in the things of which you are now ashamed? For the end of those things is death.

Arminian

Those things brought no fruit and end in death. Both traditions see the futility of sin.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the shame of the former life.

Romans 6:22

22But now, being made free from sin and having become servants of God, you have your fruit of sanctification and the result of eternal life.

Arminian

Now, freed from sin and enslaved to God, you have fruit leading to sanctification, and its end is eternal life. Arminian readers see holiness as the path that leads to final salvation, so that continuing in it matters.

Reformed

Reformed readers see holiness as the certain fruit of salvation and the way God brings his people to eternal life.

Key contrast Both see holiness on the path to eternal life. Arminian readers stress that believers must continue in that path. Reformed readers stress that God ensures his people will walk in it to the end.

Romans 6:23

23For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Arminian

The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord. Both traditions treasure this contrast between wages and gift.

Reformed

Reformed readers see salvation as wholly gift.
