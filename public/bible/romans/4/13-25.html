Passage

Romans 4

Verses 13–25 · WEB

Paul presses the Abraham argument further. The promise that Abraham would inherit the world came through the righteousness of faith, not the law, so that it rests on grace and is guaranteed to all his offspring. Abraham believed God who gives life to the dead, trusting the promise against all natural hope, and his faith was counted as righteousness. Paul says this was written for believers today as well. Both traditions read the passage as a celebration of faith resting on God’s promise. They differ on how faith arises and on what makes the promise “sure.”

Romans 4:13

13For the promise to Abraham and to his offspring that he would be heir of the world wasn’t through the law, but through the righteousness of faith.

Arminian

The promise came through the righteousness of faith, not through the law. Both traditions see the promise resting on faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise grounded in God’s covenant grace.

Romans 4:14

14For if those who are of the law are heirs, faith is made void, and the promise is made of no effect.

Arminian

If heirs come through the law, faith is empty and the promise void. Both traditions see law and promise as different principles.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that any reliance on law would cancel grace.

Romans 4:15

15For the law produces wrath; for where there is no law, neither is there disobedience.

Arminian

The law brings wrath; where there is no law there is no transgression. Both traditions see the law exposing sin.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the law’s condemning function.

Romans 4:16

16For this cause it is of faith, that it may be according to grace, to the end that the promise may be sure to all the offspring, not to that only which is of the law, but to that also which is of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all.

Arminian

It depends on faith so that it may rest on grace and the promise be guaranteed to all the offspring. Arminian readers see faith as the condition that keeps salvation gracious, since faith receives rather than earns. The promise is sure to everyone who believes.

Arminian readers also note that the guarantee belongs to those who continue in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the guarantee resting on God’s grace rather than human performance. Because God gives the faith he requires, the promise cannot fail for any of his people.

Key contrast Both agree that faith keeps the promise a matter of grace. Arminian readers see the promise guaranteed to all who believe and continue in faith. Reformed readers see it guaranteed because God himself secures the faith of the elect.

Romans 4:17

17As it is written, “I have made you a father of many nations.” This is in the presence of him whom he believed: God, who gives life to the dead, and calls the things that are not, as though they were.

Arminian

Abraham believed God, who gives life to the dead and calls into existence what does not exist. Both traditions see faith resting on God’s creative power.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a parallel with regeneration, in which God gives life where there was none.

Romans 4:18

18Against hope, Abraham in hope believed, to the end that he might become a father of many nations, according to that which had been spoken, “So will your offspring be.”

Arminian

In hope against hope Abraham believed. Both traditions see faith trusting the promise despite circumstances.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith as confidence in God’s word.

Romans 4:19

19Without being weakened in faith, he didn’t consider his own body, already having been worn out, (he being about a hundred years old), and the deadness of Sarah’s womb.

Arminian

He did not weaken in faith when he considered his body and Sarah’s barrenness. Both traditions see faith facing reality honestly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith that looks past natural impossibility.

Romans 4:20

20Yet, looking to the promise of God, he didn’t waver through unbelief, but grew strong through faith, giving glory to God,

Arminian

No unbelief made him waver; he grew strong in faith, giving glory to God. Arminian readers see faith as Abraham’s real response, strengthened as he trusted.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God strengthening Abraham’s faith, so that the glory goes to God.

Romans 4:21

21and being fully assured that what he had promised, he was also able to perform.

Arminian

He was fully convinced that God was able to do what he promised. Both traditions agree that faith rests on God’s ability.

Reformed

Reformed readers see assurance grounded in God’s power.

Romans 4:22

22Therefore it also was “credited to him for righteousness.”

Arminian

That is why his faith was counted to him as righteousness. See the note on verse 3. Both traditions agree that faith, not works, is the means.

Reformed

Reformed readers see faith receiving imputed righteousness.

Romans 4:23

23Now it was not written that it was accounted to him for his sake alone,

Arminian

The words were not written for Abraham alone. Both traditions see Scripture applying to all believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see one way of salvation in both testaments.

Romans 4:24

24but for our sake also, to whom it will be accounted, who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,

Arminian

They were written for us also, who believe in him who raised Jesus. Arminian readers see the same offer extended to all who believe.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the promise applied to believers today.

Romans 4:25

25who was delivered up for our trespasses, and was raised for our justification.

Arminian

Jesus was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our justification. Both traditions see the cross and resurrection as the ground of justification.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s resurrection as God’s vindication of his finished work.
