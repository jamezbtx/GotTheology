Passage

Romans 4

Verses 1–12 · WEB

Paul turns to Abraham to show that justification has always been by faith. Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness, before he was circumcised. David also speaks of the blessedness of the one to whom God counts righteousness apart from works. Both traditions treat this as foundational for justification by faith. They differ on the meaning of “counted” and on how faith itself relates to righteousness.

Romans 4:1

1What then will we say that Abraham, our forefather, has found according to the flesh?

Arminian

Paul asks what Abraham found. Both traditions see Abraham as the model of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Abraham as the pattern of justification for all believers.

Romans 4:2

2For if Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about, but not toward God.

Arminian

If Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about, but not before God. Both traditions agree that works give no ground for boasting before God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see boasting excluded by the nature of justification.

Romans 4:3

3For what does the Scripture say? “Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.”

Arminian

“Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.” Wesley and many Arminians read this as God counting faith itself as righteousness, accepting it in place of perfect obedience for Christ’s sake.

Arminian readers stress that faith is not meritorious; God graciously reckons it for righteousness because of Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers usually read the verse as God crediting Christ’s righteousness to the believer through faith. Faith is the instrument that receives righteousness, not the righteousness itself.

The Westminster Confession states that God does not impute faith itself as righteousness but imputes Christ’s obedience and satisfaction, received by faith.

Key contrast Both agree that Abraham was justified by faith, not works. Many Arminians, with Wesley, describe God counting faith as righteousness for Christ’s sake. Reformed readers describe God crediting Christ’s righteousness to the believer, with faith as the instrument that receives it.

Romans 4:4

4Now to him who works, the reward is not counted as grace, but as something owed.

Arminian

To the one who works, wages are counted as owed, not as grace. Both traditions see grace and earning as opposites.

Reformed

Reformed readers see justification as a gift, never a payment.

Romans 4:5

5But to him who doesn’t work, but believes in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is accounted for righteousness.

Arminian

To the one who does not work but believes in him who justifies the ungodly, faith is counted as righteousness. Arminian readers see the gospel’s offer to the ungodly, received by faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God justifying the ungodly, which means the ground of justification lies outside the sinner.

Romans 4:6

6Even as David also pronounces blessing on the man to whom God counts righteousness apart from works:

Arminian

David speaks of the blessedness of the one to whom God counts righteousness apart from works. Both traditions see David as a second witness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see imputed righteousness in David’s words.

Romans 4:7

7“Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, whose sins are covered.

Arminian

Blessed are those whose lawless deeds are forgiven and whose sins are covered. Arminian readers, with Wesley, see justification chiefly as forgiveness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see forgiveness as one part of justification, joined with the imputation of righteousness.

Romans 4:8

8Blessed is the man whom the Lord will by no means charge with sin.”

Arminian

Blessed is the one against whom the Lord will not count sin. Both traditions see the joy of forgiveness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the non-imputation of sin as grounded in Christ bearing it.

Romans 4:9

9Is this blessing then pronounced only on the circumcised, or on the uncircumcised also? For we say that faith was accounted to Abraham for righteousness.

Arminian

Is this blessing for the circumcised only? Paul says faith was counted to Abraham as righteousness. Both traditions see the blessing extended to Gentiles.

Reformed

Reformed readers see one way of justification for all people.

Romans 4:10

10How then was it counted? When he was in circumcision, or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision.

Arminian

Abraham was counted righteous before he was circumcised. Both traditions see that the rite did not produce righteousness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the order of faith before sign.

Romans 4:11

11He received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while he was in uncircumcision, that he might be the father of all those who believe, though they might be in uncircumcision, that righteousness might also be accounted to them.

Arminian

Circumcision was a sign and seal of the righteousness Abraham had by faith. Arminian readers see signs confirming faith already present.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sacraments as signs and seals of God’s covenant, and many connect this with infant baptism as a covenant sign.

Romans 4:12

12He is the father of circumcision to those who not only are of the circumcision, but who also walk in the steps of that faith of our father Abraham, which he had in uncircumcision.

Arminian

Abraham is the father of all who walk in his faith. Both traditions see Abraham’s family defined by faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the one covenant people of God including believing Jews and Gentiles.
