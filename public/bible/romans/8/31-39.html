Passage

Romans 8

Verses 31–39 · WEB

Paul closes Romans 8 with a series of questions and a hymn of confidence. If God is for us, who can be against us? Who can bring a charge against God’s chosen ones, or condemn them, or separate them from Christ’s love? Paul answers that nothing in all creation can separate believers from God’s love in Christ Jesus. Both traditions treasure the passage as a foundation of assurance; they differ on whether a believer can separate himself.

Romans 8:31

31What then shall we say about these things? If God is for us, who can be against us?

Arminian

“If God is for us, who can be against us?” Arminian readers see the confidence of believers who rest in God’s saving purpose.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the conclusion of the golden chain in verses 29–30. Since God has done all that, no opposition can finally prevail.

Romans 8:32

32He who didn’t spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how would he not also with him freely give us all things?

Arminian

God did not spare his own Son but gave him up “for us all.” Arminian readers often note that the same God who gave his Son gives everything else; they also connect the giving of the Son with God’s love for the world (John 3:16).

Reformed

Reformed readers note that “us all” in context refers to the chosen ones of verse 33. If God gave his Son for them, he will certainly give them everything else needed for their salvation. Many Reformed readers cite this verse for definite atonement.

Key contrast Both see the gift of the Son as the guarantee of every other gift. Reformed readers read “us all” as the elect named in verse 33 and see the gift as securing their salvation. Arminian readers read the gift in light of God’s love for the world and apply the promise to all who believe.

Romans 8:33

33Who could bring a charge against God’s chosen ones? It is God who justifies.

Arminian

No one can bring a charge against God’s chosen ones, because God himself justifies them. Arminian readers identify God’s chosen ones as believers, chosen in Christ, and find great comfort in the security of the justified.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the chosen ones as the elect of verses 29–30. Since God, the judge, has justified them, no accusation can stand.

Romans 8:34

34Who is he who condemns? It is Christ who died, yes rather, who was raised from the dead, who is at the right hand of God, who also makes intercession for us.

Arminian

Christ died, was raised, sits at God’s right hand, and intercedes for us. Arminian readers see Christ’s ongoing ministry as the believer’s strength to persevere.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ’s death, resurrection, and intercession as the sure ground of the believer’s security. His intercession is always effective for those for whom he died.

Romans 8:35

35Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Could oppression, or anguish, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword?

Arminian

Paul lists hardships that might seem to separate believers from Christ’s love: tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, danger, and sword. Arminian readers see that no outward circumstance can separate a believer from Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same list and take the implied answer, “nothing,” as a promise that covers every trial.

Romans 8:36

36Even as it is written, “For your sake we are killed all day long. We were accounted as sheep for the slaughter.”

Arminian

Paul quotes Psalm 44:22: God’s people have always suffered. Arminian readers see suffering as normal for believers, not a sign of God’s absence.

Reformed

Reformed readers see suffering as part of God’s plan for his people, which does not threaten their place in his love.

Romans 8:37

37No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us.

Arminian

In all these things believers are more than conquerors through him who loved them. Arminian readers stress that victory comes through Christ as believers continue to trust him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see victory as guaranteed by Christ’s love for his people. They conquer because he holds them.

Romans 8:38

38For I am persuaded that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor things present, nor things to come, nor powers,

Arminian

Paul is persuaded that neither death nor life, angels nor rulers, things present nor things to come can separate believers from God’s love. Arminian readers find deep assurance here. Many add that the list covers external forces and does not address whether a believer can turn away from Christ by his own persistent unbelief.

Reformed

Reformed readers see this as one of the strongest statements of the security of believers in Scripture. The list is meant to be exhaustive, ending with “any other created thing,” which includes the believer himself.

Key contrast Both find in verses 38–39 a powerful assurance that no outside force can separate believers from God’s love. Arminian readers note that the list does not address a believer’s own willful turning from Christ. Reformed readers take “any other created thing” to include the believer and see the promise as unconditional.

Romans 8:39

39nor height, nor depth, nor any other created thing will be able to separate us from God’s love which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Arminian

Nothing in all creation can separate believers from God’s love in Christ Jesus. Arminian readers see the love of God as the believer’s secure home, to be remained in through faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s love in Christ as the unbreakable bond that holds the elect forever.
