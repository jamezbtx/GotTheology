Passage

Romans 8

Verses 1–11 · WEB

Romans 8 opens with no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. What the law could not do because of the flesh, God did by sending his Son, so that the law’s requirement is fulfilled in those who walk by the Spirit. Paul contrasts the mind set on the flesh, which is hostile to God and cannot please him, with the mind set on the Spirit, which is life and peace. Both traditions treasure this passage. They differ on what “cannot” in verses 7–8 says about human ability and on how the Spirit’s indwelling relates to perseverance.

Romans 8:1

1There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who don’t walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.

Arminian

There is no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. Arminian readers see this assurance belonging to all who remain in Christ.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a permanent verdict for all who are united to Christ.

Romans 8:2

2For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus made me free from the law of sin and of death.

Arminian

The law of the Spirit of life has set you free from the law of sin and death. Both traditions see the Spirit’s liberating power.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit applying Christ’s work.

Romans 8:3

3For what the law couldn’t do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God did, sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and for sin, he condemned sin in the flesh,

Arminian

God condemned sin in the flesh by sending his Son. Both traditions see substitution in Christ’s death.

Reformed

Reformed readers see penal substitution.

Romans 8:4

4that the ordinance of the law might be fulfilled in us who don’t walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.

Arminian

The law’s requirement is fulfilled in us who walk by the Spirit. Arminian readers, especially Wesleyans, see real holiness produced by the Spirit.

Reformed

Reformed readers see both imputed righteousness and Spirit-produced obedience.

Romans 8:5

5For those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit, the things of the Spirit.

Arminian

Those who live by the flesh set their minds on the flesh; those who live by the Spirit set their minds on the Spirit. Both traditions see two orientations of life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the difference between unregenerate and regenerate people.

Romans 8:6

6For the mind of the flesh is death, but the mind of the Spirit is life and peace;

Arminian

The mind of the flesh is death, but the mind of the Spirit is life and peace. Both traditions agree.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the outcome of each orientation.

Romans 8:7

7because the mind of the flesh is hostile toward God, for it is not subject to God’s law, neither indeed can it be.

Arminian

The mind of the flesh is hostile to God and cannot submit to God’s law. Arminian readers agree that fallen people cannot submit to God on their own. They teach that prevenient grace is given to all, so that everyone receives the ability to respond.

Reformed

Reformed readers see total inability: the unregenerate cannot and will not submit to God. Only the regenerating work of the Spirit changes this.

Key contrast Both affirm that the fleshly mind cannot submit to God on its own. Arminian readers see prevenient grace overcoming this inability for all. Reformed readers see regeneration overcoming it for the elect.

Romans 8:8

8Those who are in the flesh can’t please God.

Arminian

Those in the flesh cannot please God. See the note on verse 7.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the impossibility of pleasing God apart from regeneration.

Romans 8:9

9But you are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if it is so that the Spirit of God dwells in you. But if any man doesn’t have the Spirit of Christ, he is not his.

Arminian

You are in the Spirit if the Spirit of God dwells in you; anyone without the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him. Both traditions see the Spirit as the mark of a true believer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the indwelling Spirit as evidence of election and the seal of salvation.

Romans 8:10

10If Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin, but the spirit is alive because of righteousness.

Arminian

If Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin, but the spirit is alive because of righteousness. Both traditions see present life amid mortality.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the already and not yet of salvation.

Romans 8:11

11But if the Spirit of him who raised up Jesus from the dead dwells in you, he who raised up Christ Jesus from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit who dwells in you.

Arminian

The one who raised Jesus will give life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit. Both traditions see the resurrection hope.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit as the guarantee of final resurrection.
