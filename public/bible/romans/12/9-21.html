Passage

Romans 12

Verses 9–21 · WEB

Paul describes the shape of love in the Christian community and toward outsiders. Love is sincere, hates evil, and holds to good. It honors others, serves the Lord with zeal, rejoices in hope, endures trouble, prays, shares, and welcomes strangers. Toward enemies it blesses rather than curses, refuses revenge, and leaves judgment to God, overcoming evil with good. Both traditions read this as the fruit of grace. Wesleyan readers often see a picture of the perfect love they hope grace will produce. Reformed readers see the pattern of a life of gratitude.

Romans 12:9

9Let love be without hypocrisy. Abhor that which is evil. Cling to that which is good.

Arminian

Love must be sincere, hating evil and holding to good. Wesleyan readers see “love without hypocrisy” as close to the heart of Christian perfection. Wesley defined that perfection as loving God and neighbor with an undivided heart.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sincere love as the fruit of regeneration. It cannot be produced by effort alone, but grows as the Spirit renews the heart.

Romans 12:10

10In love of the brothers be tenderly affectionate to one another; in honor prefer one another,

Arminian

Be devoted to one another and outdo one another in showing honor. Arminian readers stress the community of believers as the setting where love is practiced and strengthened.

Reformed

Reformed readers see brotherly love as a mark of belonging to God’s family. Honoring others above oneself reflects Christ’s own humility.

Romans 12:11

11not lagging in diligence, fervent in spirit, serving the Lord,

Arminian

Do not lag in zeal; be fervent in spirit, serving the Lord. Arminian readers see a warning against spiritual decline, which they treat as a real danger for believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see zeal as the Spirit’s fire in the believer. Serving the Lord gives meaning to every task.

Romans 12:12

12rejoicing in hope, enduring in troubles, continuing steadfastly in prayer,

Arminian

Rejoice in hope, be patient in trouble, and keep praying. Arminian readers see hope and prayer as the daily disciplines that keep believers in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see hope grounded in God’s sure promises. Patience in affliction rests on confidence that God works all things for good (8:28).

Romans 12:13

13contributing to the needs of the saints, and given to hospitality.

Arminian

Share with saints in need and practice hospitality. Both traditions see love expressed in concrete giving. Arminian readers stress that faith works through love.

Reformed

Reformed readers see generosity as a response to God’s generosity in Christ. The early Reformed churches organized deacons largely around care for the needy.

Romans 12:14

14Bless those who persecute you; bless, and don’t curse.

Arminian

Bless those who persecute you; bless and do not curse. Arminian readers see Jesus’ teaching from the Sermon on the Mount echoed here, as an expression of God’s love for enemies.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace enabling what nature cannot do. Blessing persecutors reflects God’s own patience with sinners.

Romans 12:15

15Rejoice with those who rejoice. Weep with those who weep.

Arminian

Rejoice with those who rejoice; weep with those who weep. Arminian readers stress the empathy that love produces in a community of believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the members of Christ’s body sharing one another’s joys and sorrows, as Paul describes in 1 Corinthians 12:26.

Romans 12:16

16Be of the same mind one toward another. Don’t set your mind on high things, but associate with the humble. Don’t be wise in your own conceits.

Arminian

Live in harmony; do not be proud, but associate with the lowly. Arminian readers see a warning against pride, which can undermine faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see humility as the natural response to grace. Those who know they are saved by grace alone have no grounds to look down on anyone.

Romans 12:17

17Repay no one evil for evil. Respect what is honorable in the sight of all men.

Arminian

Repay no one evil for evil, and do what is honorable in everyone’s sight. Arminian readers see the call to live out the gospel visibly before outsiders.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christian conduct as a witness to the world. Honorable living commends the gospel, even to those who reject it.

Romans 12:18

18If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men.

Arminian

If possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone. Arminian readers note the realism: peace depends partly on others’ choices.

Reformed

Reformed readers see responsibility and limits together. Believers must pursue peace, but they cannot control how others respond.

Romans 12:19

19Don’t seek revenge yourselves, beloved, but give place to God’s wrath. For it is written, “Vengeance belongs to me; I will repay, says the Lord.”

Arminian

Do not take revenge, but leave room for God’s wrath, for vengeance belongs to him. Arminian readers see God’s justice as the reason believers can let go of revenge.

Reformed

Reformed readers see trust in God’s sovereign justice. Because God will set all things right, believers need not do so themselves.

Romans 12:20

20Therefore “If your enemy is hungry, feed him. If he is thirsty, give him a drink; for in doing so, you will heap coals of fire on his head.”

Arminian

If your enemy is hungry, feed him; doing so will heap burning coals on his head. Arminian readers often read the coals as shame that may lead the enemy to repentance.

Reformed

Reformed readers note several readings of the coals, including shame leading to repentance. Either way, kindness to enemies reflects God’s kindness.

Romans 12:21

21Don’t be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.

Arminian

Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good. Arminian readers see a real victory over evil that grace makes possible in daily life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Christian life as warfare in which good is the weapon. Overcoming evil with good imitates Christ, who conquered evil by the cross.
