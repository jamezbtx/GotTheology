Passage

Romans 12

Verses 1–8 · WEB · Voices

Paul turns from doctrine to practice. In view of God’s mercies, believers are to offer their bodies as living sacrifices and be transformed by the renewing of their minds. They should think soberly of themselves and use their varied gifts within the one body of Christ. Both traditions see this as the response of grateful worship to grace, and they largely agree on the passage.

Romans 12:1

1Therefore I urge you, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your spiritual service.

Arminian

By the mercies of God, present your bodies as a living sacrifice. Both traditions see the Christian life as a response to God’s mercy.

Reformed

Reformed readers see gratitude as the motive for obedience. The Heidelberg Catechism organizes the Christian life the same way: guilt, grace, and then gratitude.

Romans 12:2

2Don’t be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what is the good, well-pleasing, and perfect will of God.

Arminian

Do not be conformed to this world but be transformed by renewing your mind. Wesleyan readers see this transformation reaching toward full holiness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see progressive sanctification. Renewal of the mind is gradual and lifelong, worked by the Spirit through the word.

Romans 12:3

3For I say through the grace that was given me, to everyone who is among you, not to think of yourself more highly than you ought to think; but to think reasonably, as God has apportioned to each person a measure of faith.

Arminian

Think of yourself with sober judgment, according to the measure of faith God has assigned. Arminian readers see faith as a gift that people also exercise.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God apportioning faith and gifts according to his will. Since every gift is given, there is no room for pride.

Romans 12:4

4For even as we have many members in one body, and all the members don’t have the same function,

Arminian

Like one body with many members, each with different functions. Arminian readers draw from this that every believer has a real part to play, and that faithful use of gifts matters to the whole body.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the image as teaching that God orders his church, giving different gifts to different members for the common good.

Romans 12:5

5so we, who are many, are one body in Christ, and individually members of one another,

Arminian

We are one body in Christ and members of one another. Arminian readers stress the mutual responsibility this creates. Believers help one another persevere in faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see union with Christ creating fellowship. Being joined to Christ means being joined to everyone else who is in him.

Romans 12:6

6having gifts differing according to the grace that was given to us: if prophecy, let’s prophesy according to the proportion of our faith;

Arminian

Having gifts that differ according to grace, let us use them, including prophecy in proportion to faith. Both traditions see gifts as grace-given.

Reformed

Reformed readers often read “in proportion to faith” as the analogy of faith: prophecy and teaching must agree with the whole body of revealed truth.

Romans 12:7

7or service, let’s give ourselves to service; or he who teaches, to his teaching;

Arminian

Service and teaching are to be exercised faithfully. Arminian readers stress diligence in using what God gives, since gifts can be neglected.

Reformed

Reformed readers see each calling, ordinary or prominent, as a way to serve God. Reformation teaching on vocation drew heavily on texts like this.

Romans 12:8

8or he who exhorts, to his exhorting; he who gives, let him do it with generosity; he who rules, with diligence; he who shows mercy, with cheerfulness.

Arminian

Exhortation, giving, leading, and mercy are to be done with zeal and cheerfulness. Both traditions see gifts exercised in love.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the inward attitude Paul requires: generosity, zeal, and cheerfulness. God looks at the heart behind the gift, not just the act.

What older interpreters in each camp said about the living sacrifice and the one body whose gifts differ by grace.

Arminian Notable voices

“I exhort you—St. Paul uses to suit his exhortations to the doctrines he has been delivering.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Rom 12:1)

Reformed Notable voices

“Paul lays down here the principle from which all the duties of holiness flow, even this—that we are redeemed by the Lord for this end—that we may consecrate to him ourselves and all our members. It is then the beginning of a right course in good works, when we understand that we are consecrated to the Lord; for it hence follows that we must cease to live to ourselves, in order that we may devote all the actions of our life to his service.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Romans (on Rom 12:1)
