Passage

Romans 15

Verses 14–33 · WEB

Paul explains why he has written so boldly and shares his plans. His ministry is a priestly service of bringing the Gentiles as an offering to God, made acceptable by the Holy Spirit. He has preached from Jerusalem to Illyricum, aiming to go where Christ has not been named, and now plans to visit Rome on the way to Spain after delivering an offering to Jerusalem. He asks the Romans to pray for him. These are largely personal verses. Both traditions draw lessons about mission, prayer, and providence, with differing emphases on how God’s will relates to human plans.

Romans 15:14

14I myself am also persuaded about you, my brothers, that you yourselves are full of goodness, filled with all knowledge, able also to admonish others.

Arminian

Paul is confident the Romans are full of goodness and able to instruct one another. Arminian readers see real spiritual maturity in believers who have grown in grace.

Reformed

Reformed readers see goodness and knowledge as fruits of the Spirit in the church. Mutual instruction is a normal part of church life.

Romans 15:15

15But I write the more boldly to you in part as reminding you, because of the grace that was given to me by God,

Arminian

Paul has written boldly to remind them, because of the grace given him. Arminian readers see apostolic ministry as a gift of grace that serves the church’s faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul’s authority as resting on the grace of his calling, not on his own merit.

Romans 15:16

16that I should be a servant of Christ Jesus to the Gentiles, serving as a priest of the Good News of God, that the offering up of the Gentiles might be made acceptable, sanctified by the Holy Spirit.

Arminian

Paul ministers as a priest of the gospel, so that the offering of the Gentiles may be acceptable, sanctified by the Holy Spirit. Arminian readers see the Spirit sanctifying those who respond to the gospel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit’s sanctifying work making the Gentiles acceptable to God. Preaching is the means by which God gathers his people.

Romans 15:17

17I have therefore my boasting in Christ Jesus in things pertaining to God.

Arminian

Paul’s boasting is in Christ Jesus. Arminian readers see boasting rightly directed to Christ, who works through his servants.

Reformed

Reformed readers see all boasting excluded except boasting in Christ (1 Corinthians 1:31).

Romans 15:18

18For I will not dare to speak of any things except those which Christ worked through me for the obedience of the Gentiles, by word and deed,

Arminian

Paul will speak only of what Christ has worked through him to bring about Gentile obedience. Arminian readers see Christ working through human ministry to lead people to the obedience of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as the true agent of mission. Paul is the instrument, and the fruit belongs to Christ.

Romans 15:19

19in the power of signs and wonders, in the power of God’s Spirit; so that from Jerusalem and around as far as to Illyricum, I have fully preached the Good News of Christ;

Arminian

This was done by signs and wonders and the power of the Spirit, from Jerusalem to Illyricum. Arminian readers see the Spirit’s power confirming the gospel to those who hear it.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Spirit’s power accompanying the word. Many Reformed readers see such apostolic signs as tied especially to the founding of the church.

Romans 15:20

20yes, making it my aim to preach the Good News, not where Christ was already named, that I might not build on another’s foundation.

Arminian

Paul aims to preach where Christ has not been named. Arminian readers draw a missionary urgency from this: everyone should have the chance to hear and respond.

Reformed

Reformed readers see pioneer mission as obedience to Christ’s command, confident that God has people in every place (Acts 18:10).

Romans 15:21

21But, as it is written, “They will see, to whom no news of him came. They who haven’t heard will understand.”

Arminian

“Those who were not told about him will see.” Arminian readers see Isaiah’s promise that the gospel will reach the unreached.

Reformed

Reformed readers see prophecy fulfilled in the spread of the gospel, which God ordained.

Romans 15:22

22Therefore also I was hindered these many times from coming to you,

Arminian

This is why Paul has been hindered from coming. Arminian readers see Paul’s real choices about priorities shaping his travel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s providence ordering Paul’s path, including his delays.

Romans 15:23

23but now, no longer having any place in these regions, and having these many years a longing to come to you,

Arminian

Having no more room for work in these regions, Paul longs to come to them. Arminian readers see strategic planning in mission.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul’s plans as wise stewardship under God’s direction.

Romans 15:24

24whenever I travel to Spain, I will come to you. For I hope to see you on my journey, and to be helped on my way there by you, if first I may enjoy your company for a while.

Arminian

Paul hopes to see them on his way to Spain. Arminian readers see Paul’s plans made in hope and open to God’s guidance.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Paul makes plans while submitting them to God’s will (v32).

Romans 15:25

25But now, I say, I am going to Jerusalem, serving the saints.

Arminian

Paul is going to Jerusalem to serve the saints. Arminian readers see practical love as part of Christian faithfulness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the offering as an expression of the unity of Jew and Gentile in one body.

Romans 15:26

26For it has been the good pleasure of Macedonia and Achaia to make a certain contribution for the poor among the saints who are at Jerusalem.

Arminian

Macedonia and Achaia were pleased to contribute for the poor among the saints. Arminian readers see freely chosen generosity as a fruit of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see generosity as a response to grace and a mark of fellowship.

Romans 15:27

27Yes, it has been their good pleasure, and they are their debtors. For if the Gentiles have been made partakers of their spiritual things, they owe it to them also to serve them in material things.

Arminian

The Gentiles owe it to them, having shared in their spiritual blessings. Arminian readers see gratitude producing service.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the Gentiles’ debt to Israel for the blessings of the covenant, a theme of Romans 11.

Romans 15:28

28When therefore I have accomplished this, and have sealed to them this fruit, I will go on by way of you to Spain.

Arminian

When Paul has delivered this fruit, he will go on to Spain. Arminian readers see faithfulness in completing what one has begun.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul’s diligence in his calling, carried out under God’s providence.

Romans 15:29

29I know that when I come to you, I will come in the fullness of the blessing of the Good News of Christ.

Arminian

Paul will come in the fullness of Christ’s blessing. Arminian readers see confidence that God will bless faithful ministry.

Reformed

Reformed readers see confidence resting on God’s promise to bless his word.

Romans 15:30

30Now I beg you, brothers, by our Lord Jesus Christ and by the love of the Spirit, that you strive together with me in your prayers to God for me,

Arminian

Paul urges them to strive with him in prayer. Arminian readers see prayer as real participation in God’s work that changes outcomes.

Reformed

Reformed readers see prayer as an ordained means by which God accomplishes his will.

Key contrast Both see prayer as truly mattering. Arminian readers stress that God responds to prayer in ways that shape what happens. Reformed readers stress that God has ordained prayer as a means by which his sovereign purposes are carried out.

Romans 15:31

31that I may be delivered from those who are disobedient in Judea, and that my service which I have for Jerusalem may be acceptable to the saints,

Arminian

Paul asks to be delivered from unbelievers in Judea and that his service be accepted. Arminian readers see the real uncertainty Paul faced, entrusted to God in prayer.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul seeking God’s protection. The answer came in God’s way, through arrest and the journey to Rome in chains.

Romans 15:32

32that I may come to you in joy through the will of God, and together with you, find rest.

Arminian

That by God’s will Paul may come to them with joy. Arminian readers see Paul’s plans submitted to God’s will.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s will as determining the outcome, which Paul gladly accepts.

Romans 15:33

33Now the God of peace be with you all. Amen.

Arminian

The God of peace be with you all. Arminian readers see peace as God’s gift to all his people.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the God of peace as the one who reconciled sinners through Christ.
