Passage

Romans 11

Verses 11–24 · WEB

Israel’s stumbling is not final. Through their fall salvation has come to the Gentiles, to provoke Israel to jealousy, and their full inclusion will mean life from the dead. Paul then uses the image of an olive tree: natural branches were broken off because of unbelief, wild branches were grafted in by faith, and Gentiles must not boast, because God can cut them off and graft Israel back in. Arminian readers find here a clear warning that believers can be cut off; Reformed readers read the warning in terms of the visible people of God and as a means of perseverance.

Romans 11:11

11I ask then, did they stumble that they might fall? May it never be! But by their fall salvation has come to the Gentiles, to provoke them to jealousy.

Arminian

Israel did not stumble so as to fall beyond recovery. Their trespass brought salvation to the Gentiles, to provoke Israel to jealousy. Arminian readers see that hardening is neither total nor final.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s wise purpose in using Israel’s unbelief to bring salvation to the nations.

Romans 11:12

12Now if their fall is the riches of the world, and their loss the riches of the Gentiles, how much more their fullness!

Arminian

If Israel’s failure means riches for the world, their fullness will mean much more. Both traditions see a hopeful future for Israel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a future blessing tied to Israel’s full inclusion.

Romans 11:13

13For I speak to you who are Gentiles. Since then as I am an apostle to Gentiles, I glorify my ministry,

Arminian

Paul speaks to Gentiles and magnifies his ministry. Arminian readers see the apostle using every means to reach people.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul’s ministry as part of God’s plan for both Gentiles and Jews.

Romans 11:14

14if by any means I may provoke to jealousy those who are my flesh, and may save some of them.

Arminian

Paul hopes to provoke his people to jealousy and save some of them. Arminian readers note that he speaks of saving some through his efforts, which assumes their response matters.

Reformed

Reformed readers see human means used by God to bring his elect to faith.

Romans 11:15

15For if the rejection of them is the reconciling of the world, what would their acceptance be, but life from the dead?

Arminian

If their rejection meant reconciliation for the world, their acceptance will be life from the dead. Interpreters debate whether this refers to resurrection or spiritual revival.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same debate, and many see a future turning of Israel to Christ.

Romans 11:16

16If the first fruit is holy, so is the lump. If the root is holy, so are the branches.

Arminian

If the root is holy, so are the branches. Both traditions see Israel’s patriarchs as the root of God’s people.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the covenant promises to the patriarchs as the foundation of the people of God.

Romans 11:17

17But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, being a wild olive, were grafted in among them and became partaker with them of the root and of the richness of the olive tree,

Arminian

Some branches were broken off and wild branches grafted in. Arminian readers see individuals and peoples being removed or added depending on faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the olive tree as the covenant people, which includes believing Gentiles and from which unbelieving Israel was removed.

Romans 11:18

18don’t boast over the branches. But if you boast, remember that it is not you who support the root, but the root supports you.

Arminian

Gentiles must not boast, because the root supports them. Both traditions see humility as the only fitting response.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace leaving no room for boasting.

Romans 11:19

19You will say then, “Branches were broken off, that I might be grafted in.”

Arminian

The Gentile might say branches were broken off so he could be grafted in. Paul anticipates arrogance.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the danger of pride in God’s gifts.

Romans 11:20

20True; by their unbelief they were broken off, and you stand by your faith. Don’t be conceited, but fear;

Arminian

They were broken off because of unbelief, and you stand by faith; do not be proud, but fear. Arminian readers see faith as the condition of remaining in the tree and unbelief as the reason for removal.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same connection between faith and standing, and take the call to fear as a means of keeping believers humble and dependent.

Romans 11:21

21for if God didn’t spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you.

Arminian

If God did not spare the natural branches, he will not spare you. Arminian readers take this as a real warning to believers.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a warning that keeps the church watchful.

Romans 11:22

22See then the goodness and severity of God. Toward those who fell, severity; but toward you, goodness, if you continue in his goodness; otherwise you also will be cut off.

Arminian

See the kindness and severity of God: kindness to you, “if you continue in his goodness; otherwise you also will be cut off.” Arminian readers see this as one of the clearest conditional statements in Paul. Continuing in God’s kindness is necessary, and believers who do not continue can be cut off.

Wesley and later Arminians cite this verse alongside Hebrews 6 and John 15 as evidence that final apostasy is possible.

Reformed

Reformed readers read the warning as addressed to the Gentile church as a body and to individuals within the visible church. Those who are truly united to Christ will continue, and the warning is one of the means God uses to keep them. Some Reformed interpreters see the warning chiefly as applying to Gentile Christianity as a whole, which could be removed if it turned from faith.

Key contrast Arminian readers see a conditional promise that shows a believer can be cut off. Reformed readers apply the warning to the visible church and see it as a means by which God keeps true believers, who will continue.

Romans 11:23

23They also, if they don’t continue in their unbelief, will be grafted in, for God is able to graft them in again.

Arminian

If they do not continue in unbelief, they will be grafted back in. Arminian readers see that hardening can be reversed by faith, so no one is fixed in unbelief by God’s decree.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s power to restore Israel and his mercy toward those who turn to Christ.

Romans 11:24

24For if you were cut out of that which is by nature a wild olive tree, and were grafted contrary to nature into a good olive tree, how much more will these, which are the natural branches, be grafted into their own olive tree?

Arminian

If wild branches were grafted in against nature, how much more can natural branches be restored. Both traditions see hope for Israel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s power and faithfulness to his covenant people.
