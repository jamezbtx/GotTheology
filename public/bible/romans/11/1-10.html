Passage

Romans 11

Verses 1–10 · WEB

Has God rejected his people? Paul’s answer is no. He himself is an Israelite, and as in Elijah’s day, God has kept a remnant chosen by grace. The rest were hardened, as Scripture foretold. Reformed readers see in the “election of grace” a clear statement of unconditional election. Arminian readers see a remnant that believes, with hardening as God’s judgment on persistent unbelief.

Romans 11:1

1I ask then, did God reject his people? May it never be! For I also am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.

Arminian

God has not rejected his people; Paul himself is an Israelite. Arminian readers see God’s faithfulness to Israel continuing through believing Jews.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s covenant faithfulness preserved in a chosen remnant.

Romans 11:2

2God didn’t reject his people, whom he foreknew. Or don’t you know what the Scripture says about Elijah? How he pleads with God against Israel:

Arminian

God has not rejected his people whom he foreknew. Arminian readers often read “foreknew” here as God’s prior relationship with Israel as a nation.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s foreknowledge as his prior choosing love, which guarantees that his people are not cast off.

Romans 11:3

3“Lord, they have killed your prophets. They have broken down your altars. I am left alone, and they seek my life.”

Arminian

Elijah complained that he alone was left. Both traditions see how faith can feel isolated in times of widespread unbelief.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that God’s people are often more numerous than they appear.

Romans 11:4

4But how does God answer him? “I have reserved for myself seven thousand men who have not bowed the knee to Baal.”

Arminian

God reserved seven thousand who had not bowed to Baal. Arminian readers see a faithful remnant who refused idolatry.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that God says he reserved them for himself, which places the initiative with God.

Romans 11:5

5Even so too at this present time also there is a remnant according to the election of grace.

Arminian

There is a remnant according to the election of grace. Arminian readers see the remnant as those who have believed, chosen in Christ through faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the remnant chosen by God’s grace alone, which explains why some Israelites believe.

Key contrast Both see a remnant chosen by grace. Reformed readers see an election that precedes and produces faith. Arminian readers see the remnant as believers chosen in Christ, with faith as the condition.

Romans 11:6

6And if by grace, then it is no longer of works; otherwise grace is no longer grace. But if it is of works, it is no longer grace; otherwise work is no longer work.

Arminian

If by grace, then not by works. Arminian readers agree that election excludes works and argue that faith is not a work.

Reformed

Reformed readers see grace and works as mutually exclusive, which rules out any human contribution as the ground of election.

Romans 11:7

7What then? That which Israel seeks for, that he didn’t obtain, but the chosen ones obtained it, and the rest were hardened.

Arminian

Israel did not obtain what it sought, but the elect did, and the rest were hardened. Arminian readers see hardening as judgment on those who sought righteousness by works.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the elect obtaining salvation by grace and the rest hardened under God’s just judgment.

Key contrast Arminian readers see the rest hardened as a judgment on their persistent pursuit of righteousness by works. Reformed readers see the distinction between the elect and the hardened as rooted in God’s sovereign will, with the hardening just.

Romans 11:8

8According as it is written, “God gave them a spirit of stupor, eyes that they should not see, and ears that they should not hear, to this very day.”

Arminian

God gave them a spirit of stupor. Arminian readers see God giving people over to the blindness they chose, as in Romans 1.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a judicial act of God in response to sin.

Romans 11:9

9David says, “Let their table be made a snare, a trap, a stumbling block, and a retribution to them.

Arminian

David prays that their table become a snare. Arminian readers see the consequences of rejecting God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s judgment on those who reject his anointed.

Romans 11:10

10Let their eyes be darkened, that they may not see. Always keep their backs bent.”

Arminian

Let their eyes be darkened. Both traditions see the seriousness of judicial hardening, which verse 11 shows is not final for Israel as a whole.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the hardening as purposeful, leading to the salvation of the Gentiles.
