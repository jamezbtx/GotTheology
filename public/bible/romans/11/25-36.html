Passage

Romans 11

Verses 25–36 · ESV · Voices

Paul discloses a mystery: a partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in; in this way all Israel will be saved; the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable; God has consigned all to disobedience that he may have mercy on all; then a doxology of unsearchable judgments, all things from him and through him and to him.

Romans 11:25

25Lest you be wise in your own sight, I do not want you to be unaware of this mystery, brothers: a partial hardening has come upon Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in.

Arminian

Wesley: Hardness in part is happened to Israel, till—Israel therefore is neither totally nor finally rejected. The mystery is this divine conduct: part of Israel is hardened for a time, not the whole people forever. “Until” names an end; “partial” names a limit. Do not be wise in your own sight—Gentile pride that writes Israel off.

Classical Arminians hear both Gentile inclusion and Israel’s future. The fullness of the Gentiles is the appointed “until,” the coming-in that will convince Israel (Wesley on v. 26). Hardening is real and judicial, as in 11:7–10; it is not total reprobation of Jacob’s line. The people remain; the door remains; the mystery is mercy’s timetable, not a closed book.

Reformed

A partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. Partial: not every Israelite is hardened (the remnant of 11:5). Until: the hardening is bounded by Gentile fullness, after which, Calvin will say, the Jews return from their defection.

Reformed reading: “Lest you be wise in your own sight” kills the Gentile boast of vv. 18–20. The mystery is God’s one plan: Gentiles come in, and Israel is not discarded. Fullness of the Gentiles is real inclusion in the olive tree. Hardening of the rest (v. 7) stands; so does the remnant; so does the future return. Do not read “partial” as if no one is truly hardened, and do not read “until” as if God has no purpose for the Jews.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take the partial hardening as hardness in part until—neither total nor final rejection of Israel—so the people may yet believe, convinced by Gentile fullness. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take the same partial hardening as the present defection of many Jews (not the remnant) until the Gentiles come in, after which the Jews return and the Israel of God gathered from both is complete.

Romans 11:26

26And in this way all Israel will be saved, as it is written, “The Deliverer will come from Zion, he will banish ungodliness from Jacob”;

Arminian

Wesley: And so all Israel shall be saved—being convinced by the coming of the Gentiles. “In this way” is the Gentile fullness of v. 25: the people of Israel come to faith when they see the nations in. All Israel is the nation turning, not a secret list that was never in danger.

Classical Arminians keep both: Gentiles really come in, and all Israel will be saved as the people coming to faith. The Deliverer from Zion is Christ; ungodliness banished from Jacob is actual repentance. Do not dissolve “Jacob” into a Gentile-only church, and do not treat “all Israel” as every Israelite already elect irrespective of faith. They will be saved as they believe.

Reformed

Calvin: I extend the word Israel to all the people of God: when the Gentiles shall come in, the Jews also shall return from their defection; and thus shall be completed the salvation of the whole Israel of God, gathered from both.

Reformed hinge: “all Israel” is the one people—Gentiles grafted in and Jews restored—not every ethnic Israelite, and not Gentiles instead of Jews. The Deliverer from Zion banishes ungodliness from Jacob: Christ saves the Israel of God. Keep both inclusions. Do not erase the Jewish return Calvin names, and do not turn “all Israel” into a census of every descendant of Abraham after the flesh.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “all Israel will be saved” as the people of Israel coming to faith, convinced by the coming of the Gentiles—not a hidden roster. Reformed readings, with Calvin, extend Israel to all the people of God: Gentiles come in, Jews return from defection, and thus is completed the whole Israel of God gathered from both.

Romans 11:27

27“and this will be my covenant with them when I take away their sins.”

Arminian

The covenant with them is forgiveness: I take away their sins. Wesley’s “all Israel shall be saved” has this content—not a national label without faith, but sins actually removed. The Deliverer banishes ungodliness from Jacob by taking sins away.

Arminian reading: the “them” is Jacob who believes. Sins taken away is the gospel, received by faith, the same mercy Gentiles have already found (vv. 30–31). Keep Gentile inclusion: this covenant does not evict the grafted. It names how Israel is saved—by the Deliverer, not by descent alone.

Reformed

Calvin’s Israel of God is completed when sins are taken away: the covenant is Christ’s, the forgiveness is real. “With them” is the people gathered from both—Jews returning, Gentiles already in. The citation seals v. 26: salvation is not a change of ethnic branding but the taking away of sins.

Reformed accent: covenant mercy is God’s, not Israel’s leverage. The same Deliverer who saves the remnant now will banish ungodliness from Jacob. Do not treat the covenant as a blank check for unbelief, and do not treat it as Gentiles replacing the fathers’ people. Sins taken away is how the whole Israel of God is saved.

Romans 11:28

28As regards the gospel, they are enemies for your sake. But as regards election, they are beloved for the sake of their forefathers.

Arminian

As regards the gospel they are enemies for your sake: present unbelief that opened the door to Gentiles. As regards election they are beloved for the fathers: the people God chose are not discarded. Wesley’s “neither totally nor finally” still governs. Beloved is national mercy still on the line of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

Classical Arminians hear a double verdict, not a contradiction. Enemies now, because they refuse the gospel; beloved still, because God’s purpose for the people stands. Election here is the choosing of the nation, not an inner list that made their unbelief necessary. The fathers’ sake is covenant love that waits for faith (v. 23), not wages the fathers earned.

Reformed

Enemies as regards the gospel: the present majority that stumbled. Beloved as regards election, for the fathers: Calvin’s return from defection is already pledged. Election is God’s gracious purpose, the root of 11:16, not Israel’s present performance.

Reformed reading: “for your sake” means Gentile inclusion was no accident; “for the sake of their forefathers” means the promise to Abraham is not revoked (v. 29). Beloved names the people God has not cast away (11:2)—the Israel of God that includes the Jewish return. Do not flatten “enemies” into final reprobation of the nation, and do not flatten “beloved” into the salvation of every Israelite without faith.

Romans 11:29

29For the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable.

Arminian

The gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable. Wesley’s Israel is neither totally nor finally rejected: what God gave the fathers—adoption, glory, covenants, promises (9:4)—He has not taken back from the people. National calling stands; therefore all Israel shall be saved as they come to faith.

Arminian reading: irrevocable is God’s fidelity to Israel, not an unconditional guarantee for every individual irrespective of unbelief. The olive tree’s root is still holy (11:16). Gentiles live from that root; they do not replace it. Gifts can be refused (11:20); they are not therefore revoked from the people as such.

Reformed

The gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable because God’s electing purpose does not change. Calvin elsewhere: the calling of God is without repentance. What He promised the fathers He will perform: the remnant now, the Jewish return, the Israel of God gathered from both.

Reformed accent: irrevocable calling is God’s effectual faithfulness, not a national label that saves unbelievers. The same God who hardened the rest (11:7) has not recanted the promise. Gentile inclusion is itself a gift of that calling. Do not reduce this verse to ethnic privilege without faith, and do not make the calling a human offer God might withdraw.

Key contrast Arminian readings typically take the irrevocable gifts and calling as God’s unrevoked national covenant with Israel, so the people are not finally rejected and may yet believe. Reformed readings typically take the same irrevocability as God’s unchanging electing call—the purpose that keeps the remnant and will restore the Jews into the Israel of God—not a covenant that saves apart from faith, and not a calling God might repent of.

Romans 11:30

30For just as you were at one time disobedient to God but now have received mercy because of their disobedience,

Arminian

You Gentiles were once disobedient, but now have received mercy because of Israel’s disobedience. Wesley: suffering each in their turn to revolt from him. The Gentile hour of mercy came through Israel’s slip (11:11), not through Gentile superiority. Mercy is received; it is not a birthright of the graft.

Classical Arminians stress the “received.” Mercy came to those who believed. Disobedience was real; mercy is real; the turn can reverse (v. 31). Gentile inclusion is the first panel of the mystery. Do not boast: you were disobedient too.

Reformed

Gentiles, once shut up in unbelief, have obtained mercy through Israel’s fall. That mercy is God’s, not the Gentiles’ timing. “Because of their disobedience” matches 11:11–15: God used the stumble to reconcile the world.

Reformed reading: you received mercy—God’s sovereign kindness grafted the wild shoot. The disobedience of Israel was not outside His purpose, and the mercy you obtained is not your achievement. The next verse will promise the same mercy to them. Keep both: Gentiles truly received mercy; they did not inherit the tree by nature.

Romans 11:31

31so they too have now been disobedient in order that by the mercy shown to you they also may now receive mercy.

Arminian

They too have now been disobedient so that, by the mercy shown to you, they also may now receive mercy. Wesley: each in their turn. Israel’s present revolt is not the last word; Gentile mercy is the appointed means of Jewish mercy. “May now receive” keeps faith in view: mercy is to be received, as you received it.

Arminian pastoral: the mystery is sequential kindness, not a closed elect list. The same gospel that reached you aims at them. Jealousy (11:11, 14) and Gentile fullness (v. 25) are how they receive mercy. Do not delay that hope, and do not turn “receive” into a mercy that arrives without faith.

Reformed

Calvin: the Jews also shall return from their defection. “In order that” is purpose, not a hope that might fail God’s intent. Mercy shown to Gentiles is the instrument of mercy to Jews. “Now” places both in the one era of the gospel.

Reformed accent: God ordered the turns. Their disobedience sits inside a purpose that ends in mercy for the Israel of God. They receive mercy as the remnant received it and as the returning Jews will: by faith, as God’s gift. Do not treat the purpose as a mere opportunity, and do not treat the mercy as automatic for every Jew.

Romans 11:32

32For God has consigned all to disobedience, that he may have mercy on all.

Arminian

Wesley: God hath shut up all together in disobedience—suffering each in their turn to revolt from him—that he might have mercy upon all. “All” is Jew and Gentile alike, each in their turn, not every person without exception irrespective of faith. Both alike were shut up; both alike are shown mercy to be received by faith.

Classical Arminians refuse two errors: Gentile pride that mercy is theirs alone, and a universalism that empties faith. Mercy on all means the same mercy toward both peoples. Consigned to disobedience is the shared history of revolt, not a decree that none can believe, and not a promise that none will be lost. He may have mercy on all: Jew and Gentile, received by faith, as 11:20–23 already taught.

Reformed

Calvin: God has concluded all under unbelief, that he might have mercy upon all. Paul simply means that both Jews and Gentiles do not otherwise obtain salvation than through the mercy of God. Extremely gross is their folly who hence conclude that all shall be saved.

Reformed hinge: “all” is both peoples, not every individual. Mercy on all is mercy on Jews and Gentiles as such—the only way either obtains salvation. Calvin explicitly rejects reading this as universal salvation. Consigned to disobedience levels the ground: no people boasts; the Israel of God is gathered from both by mercy alone. Do not make “all” a census of the race, and do not make it Jews only.

Key contrast Arminian readings, with Wesley, take “mercy upon all” as Jew and Gentile alike in their turns, mercy received by faith—not every person saved apart from believing. Reformed readings, with Calvin, take “mercy upon all” as both Jews and Gentiles obtaining salvation only through God’s mercy, not every individual: they explicitly reject the folly that all shall be saved.

Romans 11:33

33Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways!

Arminian

Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God. Wesley: this was truly a mystery in the divine conduct. Unsearchable judgments, inscrutable ways: the turns of hardening and mercy, Gentile fullness and Israel’s salvation, shut-up disobedience and mercy on all. The right response is worship, not a chart that pretends to have measured God.

Arminian note: both camps stand here together. The mystery of vv. 25–32 outruns the argument. Riches, wisdom, knowledge—God’s, not ours. Do not use the doxology to cancel the calls to faith (11:20–23), and do not use the argument to silence the doxology.

Reformed

Depth of riches, wisdom, knowledge. The reader has just heard that God concludes all under unbelief in order to have mercy on both peoples, and that the Israel of God is gathered from both. The judgments are unsearchable: remnant, hardening, grafting, return. The ways are inscrutable: not irrational, but not ours to master.

Reformed note: the doxology is shared. Paul does not invite a fight over 11:33. Wisdom that planned mercy on Jews and Gentiles is deeper than either pride. Adore; do not annex the verse to a private system as if the mystery were now small.

Romans 11:34

34“For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor?”

Arminian

Who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor? Isaiah’s question (Isa 40:13) stops the wise-in-their-own-sight (v. 25). Gentile and Jew alike have no seat at the counsel table. The mystery was revealed, not invented.

Both camps can say this without a forced fight. Arminian accent: the God whose ways we do not counsel is the God who has mercy on all in their turns and who still commands faith. Unsearchable does not mean arbitrary; it means we did not advise Him.

Reformed

Who has known? Who has been His counselor? God is not indebted to creaturely advice. The mind of the Lord planned the remnant, the hardening, the fullness of the Gentiles, and the completed Israel of God. No one supplied the plan.

Shared doxology: Reformed accent is the same humility. Election and mercy are God’s; counsel is not ours. The citation levels boasting, including theological boasting. The next verse will add that no one put God in their debt.

Romans 11:35

35“Or who has given a gift to him that he might be repaid?”

Arminian

Who has given a gift to Him that he might be repaid? Job’s question (Job 41:11): God owes no one. Israel did not put Him in debt by the fathers; Gentiles did not put Him in debt by believing. Mercy on all is mercy, not repayment.

Arminian hearing: faith itself is not a gift God must repay. The people who will be saved, and the Gentiles already in, both live from unowed kindness. Shared ground with the other camp: no creditor of God. Keep both inclusions under this verse.

Reformed

Who has given to Him? If by grace, no longer of works (11:6). No father, no Gentile graft, no act of faith as wage, put God under obligation. Mercy on Jews and Gentiles is free or it is not mercy.

Shared doxology: the citation belongs to both. Reformed accent: the unrepayable God is the God of irrevocable calling (v. 29). He gives; He is not repaid. Boasting is closed. Verse 36 will name the direction of all things: from Him, through Him, to Him.

Romans 11:36

36For from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be glory forever. Amen.

Arminian

From Him, through Him, to Him are all things. The mystery, the tree, the turns of disobedience and mercy, Gentile inclusion and all Israel saved—all of it runs from God, through God, and back to God. To Him be glory forever. Amen.

Wesley’s “mystery in the divine conduct” ends in praise, not in a system that explains God away. Arminian close: keep both peoples in the “all things.” Do not let glory to God become an excuse to skip faith, and do not let faith become a gift we gave Him (v. 35). The Amen is worship.

Reformed

From Him, through Him, to Him. The whole argument lands here: the Israel of God gathered from both, mercy on Jews and Gentiles, no creature counselor, no repayment. All things—hardening and grafting, remnant and fullness—are God’s. Glory forever. Amen.

Reformed close: the doxology is not a tie-breaker but the truth both camps must say. The God of 11:33–36 is the God of 11:25–32. Do not shrink “all things” to one nation’s story or to a universalism Paul did not teach. To Him, not to us, be glory.
