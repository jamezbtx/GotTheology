Passage

Romans 2

Verses 12–29 · WEB

Paul continues to level the ground between Jew and Gentile. Those who sin without the law will perish without it, and those under the law will be judged by it, because it is doers of the law, not hearers, who are counted righteous. Gentiles who do what the law requires show that its work is written on their hearts. Paul then confronts the Jewish teacher who relies on the law but breaks it, and concludes that true circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit. The passage raises two questions on which readers in both traditions differ among themselves: whether verse 13 describes a real or hypothetical standard, and who the Gentiles of verses 14–15 are.

Romans 2:12

12For as many as have sinned without the law will also perish without the law. As many as have sinned under the law will be judged by the law.

Arminian

Those who sin without the law perish without it; those under the law are judged by it. Arminian readers draw from this that God judges each person by the light they actually received, which fits their stress on God’s fairness.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that everyone is accountable, whether they have the written law or not. Lack of Scripture does not mean lack of guilt, because all have sinned against the light they had.

Romans 2:13

13For it isn’t the hearers of the law who are righteous before God, but the doers of the law will be justified

Arminian

It is the doers of the law, not the hearers, who will be justified. Arminian readers often see the standard of genuine obedience that faith produces, so that true faith is never merely hearing.

Reformed

Many Reformed readers see a principle of the law that no sinner meets, which prepares for 3:20. Others read it as the obedience of faith that will be vindicated at the judgment.

Key contrast Both affirm that justification is by faith, not works of the law. Interpreters in both traditions differ on whether verse 13 states a standard no one meets or describes the obedience that flows from faith, so the split here is not along party lines.

Romans 2:14

14(for when Gentiles who don’t have the law do by nature the things of the law, these, not having the law, are a law to themselves,

Arminian

Gentiles without the law sometimes do by nature what the law requires. Arminian readers often connect this with prevenient grace, which they hold is at work in every person and enables real moral response.

Reformed

Reformed readers usually see the remnants of God’s image and the work of common grace. Such acts show the law’s demands, but they do not make anyone righteous before God.

Romans 2:15

15in that they show the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience testifying with them, and their thoughts among themselves accusing or else excusing them)

Arminian

They show the work of the law written on their hearts, with their conscience bearing witness. Arminian readers see conscience as a universal gift of grace, light that God gives to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see conscience as part of general revelation, which leaves people without excuse. Some Reformed readers think these Gentiles are Christians, in whom the new covenant promise of the law on the heart is fulfilled.

Romans 2:16

16in the day when God will judge the secrets of men, according to my Good News, by Jesus Christ.

Arminian

God will judge people’s secrets through Christ Jesus. Arminian readers see the judgment reaching the heart, so that God judges sincerely and fairly.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Christ as the appointed judge and the hidden things of the heart exposed. No outward religion can hide inward unbelief.

Romans 2:17

17Indeed you bear the name of a Jew, rest on the law, glory in God,

Arminian

Paul addresses the one who bears the name of Jew and relies on the law. Arminian readers see a warning against trusting covenant privilege rather than living faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the danger of resting on external membership in the covenant. Privilege brings responsibility, not security.

Romans 2:18

18know his will, and approve the things that are excellent, being instructed out of the law,

Arminian

You know God’s will and approve what is excellent. Arminian readers draw from this that knowledge increases accountability; greater light calls for greater obedience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see knowledge without regeneration. Knowing God’s will is not the same as having a heart that loves it.

Romans 2:19

19and are confident that you yourself are a guide of the blind, a light to those who are in darkness,

Arminian

You are sure you are a guide to the blind and a light to those in darkness. Arminian readers see Israel’s calling to bless the nations, which the teacher has turned into pride.

Reformed

Reformed readers see a genuine vocation from God that is undermined by hypocrisy. Israel was called to be a light, but failed to live by that light.

Romans 2:20

20a corrector of the foolish, a teacher of babies, having in the law the form of knowledge and of the truth.

Arminian

A corrector of the foolish, a teacher of children, having in the law the embodiment of knowledge and truth. Both traditions affirm that the law truly reveals God’s truth. Arminian readers stress that this truth must be obeyed, not merely possessed.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the law as a real revelation of God’s will that cannot give life by itself. The teacher has the form of knowledge but lacks inward renewal.

Romans 2:21

21You therefore who teach another, don’t you teach yourself? You who preach that a man shouldn’t steal, do you steal?

Arminian

You who teach others, do you not teach yourself? Do you steal? Arminian readers see the inconsistency between profession and practice that endangers faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the universal sinfulness that Paul is proving. Even the best-instructed person breaks the law.

Romans 2:22

22You who say a man shouldn’t commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who abhor idols, do you rob temples?

Arminian

You who forbid adultery, do you commit adultery? You who abhor idols, do you rob temples? Arminian readers see sins that betray what one claims to believe.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sins of the heart under outward religion. The law reaches deeper than appearances.

Romans 2:23

23You who glory in the law, do you dishonor God by disobeying the law?

Arminian

You who boast in the law dishonor God by breaking it. Arminian readers draw from this the importance of holy living as a witness to God’s character.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that boasting in the law is excluded, since no one keeps it. The only proper boast is in God’s grace.

Romans 2:24

24For “the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you,” just as it is written.

Arminian

God’s name is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you. Arminian readers see the missionary cost of hypocrisy: it turns people away from God.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s glory at stake in the conduct of his people. Hypocrisy obscures the witness God intended.

Romans 2:25

25For circumcision indeed profits, if you are a doer of the law, but if you are a transgressor of the law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.

Arminian

Circumcision has value if you keep the law; if you break it, your circumcision becomes uncircumcision. Arminian readers see covenant signs depending on faithful response.

Reformed

Reformed readers see signs and seals as valuable only when joined with the reality they signify. The sign without faith brings no benefit.

Romans 2:26

26If therefore the uncircumcised keep the ordinances of the law, won’t his uncircumcision be accounted as circumcision?

Arminian

If the uncircumcised keep the law’s requirements, will they not be counted as circumcised? Arminian readers see God accepting the obedient Gentile, which shows that his favor is not limited by ethnicity.

Reformed

Reformed readers often see believing Gentiles in whom the Spirit fulfills the law. Membership in God’s people is defined by inward reality.

Romans 2:27

27Won’t those who are physically uncircumcised, but fulfill the law, judge you, who with the letter and circumcision are a transgressor of the law?

Arminian

The physically uncircumcised who keep the law will judge you who break it. Arminian readers see God judging on the basis of the heart’s response, not outward marks.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the reversal of expectations. Gentiles renewed by grace show the failure of those who rely on outward privilege.

Romans 2:28

28For he is not a Jew who is one outwardly, neither is that circumcision which is outward in the flesh;

Arminian

A Jew is not one outwardly, nor is circumcision outward in the flesh. Arminian readers draw from this that true belonging to God is a matter of living faith, which every person is called to.

Reformed

Reformed readers distinguish the visible covenant community from the true people of God. Outward membership and inward regeneration do not always coincide.

Romans 2:29

29but he is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, not in the letter; whose praise is not from men, but from God.

Arminian

A Jew is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit. Arminian readers see the Spirit’s inward work as the mark of true faith, offered to all.

Reformed

Reformed readers see heart circumcision as regeneration, which only God performs (Deuteronomy 30:6). Praise for it comes from God, not people.

Key contrast Both see true circumcision as the Spirit’s inward work. Arminian readers see it as available to all who receive the gospel. Reformed readers see it as regeneration that God sovereignly gives to his people.
