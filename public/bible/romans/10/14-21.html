Passage

Romans 10

Verses 14–21 · WEB

Paul traces the chain that leads to salvation: sending, preaching, hearing, believing, and calling. Faith comes by hearing the word of Christ. Israel has heard, yet many have not obeyed, while Gentiles who were not seeking God have found him. The section ends with God holding out his hands all day long to a disobedient people. Arminian readers find here a sincere offer that Israel refused; Reformed readers agree the offer was sincere and see God’s purpose behind both the hearing and the believing.

Romans 10:14

14How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed? How will they believe in him whom they have not heard? How will they hear without a preacher?

Arminian

Paul asks how people can call on one they have not believed, believe in one they have not heard, or hear without a preacher. Arminian readers see the necessity of preaching for faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the ordinary means God uses to bring his people to faith.

Romans 10:15

15And how will they preach unless they are sent? As it is written: “How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the Good News of peace, who bring glad tidings of good things!”

Arminian

Preachers must be sent, and their feet are beautiful. Both traditions see mission as essential to the church’s life.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God sending preachers as part of his plan to gather his people.

Romans 10:16

16But they didn’t all listen to the glad news. For Isaiah says, “Lord, who has believed our report?”

Arminian

Not all obeyed the gospel; Isaiah asked who had believed the report. Arminian readers see that the gospel can be heard and refused.

Reformed

Reformed readers see that hearing alone does not produce faith; the Spirit must open the heart.

Romans 10:17

17So faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.

Arminian

Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ. Arminian readers see faith as the response to God’s word, enabled by grace that accompanies the message.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the word as the instrument through which the Spirit creates faith.

Key contrast Both agree that faith comes through hearing God’s word. Arminian readers see grace accompanying the word that enables a response. Reformed readers see the Spirit using the word to create faith in the elect.

Romans 10:18

18But I say, didn’t they hear? Yes, most certainly, “Their sound went out into all the earth, their words to the ends of the world.”

Arminian

Israel has heard: their voice has gone out to all the earth. Arminian readers see that Israel’s failure was not for lack of opportunity.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Israel’s accountability for rejecting a message it truly heard.

Romans 10:19

19But I ask, didn’t Israel know? First Moses says, “I will provoke you to jealousy with that which is no nation. I will make you angry with a nation void of understanding.”

Arminian

Moses foretold that God would provoke Israel to jealousy by a people who were not a nation. Both traditions see the inclusion of Gentiles as part of God’s plan.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s plan to use the Gentiles’ salvation for Israel’s good.

Romans 10:20

20Isaiah is very bold and says, “I was found by those who didn’t seek me. I was revealed to those who didn’t ask for me.”

Arminian

God was found by those who did not seek him. Arminian readers see God taking the initiative to reach the Gentiles.

Reformed

Reformed readers see sovereign grace: God found people who were not seeking him.

Romans 10:21

21But about Israel he says, “All day long I stretched out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people.”

Arminian

All day long God stretched out his hands to a disobedient and contrary people. Arminian readers see a sincere and persistent offer that Israel resisted, showing that God’s grace can be refused.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s sincere appeal and Israel’s culpable refusal. They hold that God’s outstretched hands express his genuine desire, while his electing purpose explains why some respond.

Key contrast Both see God’s sincere appeal to Israel. Arminian readers see grace extended and resisted. Reformed readers affirm the sincerity of the appeal and Israel’s guilt while holding that God’s election explains who responds.
