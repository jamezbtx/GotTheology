Passage

Romans 9

Verses 30–33 · ESV · Voices

Gentiles who did not pursue righteousness attained it by faith; Israel who pursued a law of righteousness did not succeed, because they did not pursue by faith but as if it were based on works; they stumbled over the stumbling stone.

Romans 9:30

30What shall we say, then? That Gentiles who did not pursue righteousness have attained it, that is, a righteousness that is by faith;

Arminian

Wesley: the gentiles, who followed not after righteousness—who a while ago had no knowledge of, no care or thought about it—have attained to righteousness, or justification: even the righteousness which is by faith. This is the first conclusion from all that has been said (vv. 14–29).

Here is the Arminian hinge of the chapter: individuals still believe, and they attain by faith. Gentile righteousness is not a secret list they never heard; it is the righteousness of faith. Potter and mercy (9:14–29) named God’s right to fix terms; they do not cancel this attainment. God accepts those who believe.

Reformed

Calvin: nothing appeared more unreasonable than that Gentiles who had no concern for righteousness should be called to obtain it, while Jews who labored in the law were excluded. Paul adds the reason: the righteousness the Gentiles attained was by faith, and hence depends on the Lord’s mercy, not on man’s own worthiness.

Calvin will not let faith become the first cause. The Gentiles could not have obtained righteousness by faith except God had anticipated their faith by his grace; faith itself is a portion of his favor. Secret predestination remains the first cause; those He deigned to embrace when unworthy obtained. Faith is the instrument; God’s purpose explains who believes.

Key contrast Arminian readings typically take Gentile attainment as the chapter’s hinge after potter and mercy: righteousness is by faith, individuals still believe, and 9:14–29 does not cancel a living response. Reformed readings typically take the same faith as real instrument, yet (Calvin) God anticipated their faith by grace; secret predestination is the first cause of who attains.

Romans 9:31

31but that Israel who pursued a law that would lead to righteousness did not succeed in reaching that law.

Arminian

Wesley: Israel—the Jews—although following after the law of righteousness (that law which, duly used, would have led them to faith, and thereby to righteousness) have not attained to the law of righteousness: that justification which is one great end of the law.

They ran and did not arrive. The miss is not that the law was a trap; duly used it would have led to faith. Corporate Israel pursued and failed. Verse 32 will name the failure as their own: not by faith, but as if by works. The potter’s right did not make that pursuit a charade they could not have turned.

Reformed

Calvin: it was no wonder the Jews gained nothing by sedulously following after righteousness; by running out of the way they wearied themselves in vain. Depending on the righteousness of the law, they did not understand the true method of justification. Legal righteousness was the cause that they fell away from the law of righteousness.

The pursuit was real; the path was wrong. Reformed reading does not mock the run. A remnant obtained; the nation as such did not. The miss unfolds the electing purpose already named (vv. 11, 16, 23–24): those appointed to faith obtain; zeal at the wrong goal does not.

Romans 9:32

32Why? Because they did not pursue it by faith, but as if it were based on works. They have stumbled over the stumbling stone,

Arminian

Wesley’s question is the hinge: And wherefore have they not? Is it because God eternally decreed they should not? There is nothing like this to be met with. The apostle gives the reason: because they sought it not by faith—whereby alone it could be attained—but as it were, in effect if not professedly, by works. They stumbled at that stumblingstone—Christ crucified.

Both camps read the “because.” Classical Arminians take it as the last word on why they missed: responsible unbelief, not a secret decree they could not resist. The stone is Christ; the fault is unbelief; the works-path is theirs. Potter and mercy in 9:14–29 do not cancel this clause. Individuals still resist.

Reformed

Calvin: they are deservedly rejected who attempt to attain salvation by trusting in their own works; they abolish faith, without which no salvation can be expected. Faith and the merits of works are contrary. They stumbled at Christ, through whom alone a way is open to righteousness.

Do not deny the “because they did not pursue it by faith” clause—both camps read it. The fork is whether that unbelief is the last word or sits under election. Calvin: do not set this cause above the secret predestination of God, counted as the first cause; the wickedness of the ungodly affords a reason and occasion for the judgments of God. Unbelief is real; those appointed to faith obtain.

Key contrast Both camps read “because they did not pursue it by faith.” Arminians, with Wesley, take that because as the last word: not an eternal decree they could not resist, but unbelief and works; the stone is Christ, the fault is theirs. Reformed readers, with Calvin, take the same unbelief as real yet second: secret predestination is the first cause; those appointed to faith obtain, and stumbling sits under election.

Romans 9:33

33as it is written, “Behold, I am laying in Zion a stone of stumbling, and a rock of offense; and whoever believes in him will not be put to shame.”

Arminian

Wesley: Foretold by their own prophet. Behold, I lay in Sion—I exhibit in my church what, though it is in truth the only sure foundation of happiness, yet will be in fact a stumblingstone and rock of offence—an occasion of ruin to many, through their obstinate unbelief.

The stone is Christ. “Whoever believes in him will not be put to shame”: the promise is as wide as the whoever. Ruin is through obstinate unbelief, not a fate that made believing impossible. Ties back to 9:14–29: potter and mercy named God’s right; this verse names believer and unbeliever in responsible terms. The fault remains unbelief.

Reformed

Calvin: Christ is appointed for ruin to the unbelieving, but for life and resurrection to the godly. By calling him the stone of stumbling, Paul reminds us it is no wonder they made no progress who through wilful stubbornness stumbled at the rock of offence when God had showed the way so plainly. Yet this stumbling does not properly belong to Christ viewed in himself; it happens through the wickedness of men.

Scripture is fulfilled: Zion’s stone was laid, and Israel stumbled as written. Faith is the instrument (“whoever believes”); God’s purpose explains who believes. The whoever does not flatten election into a mere offer; it names how those appointed obtain, and it stands as written.

Key contrast Arminian readings typically take the stone as Christ and the ruin as obstinate unbelief (Wesley): whoever believes will not be ashamed; the fault is resistible unbelief, not a decree. Reformed readings typically take the same stone as fulfilling Scripture under God’s purpose: appointed for ruin to the unbelieving and for life to the godly (Calvin); faith is the instrument, election explains who believes.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on faith, works, and the stumbling stone—Gentiles attaining righteousness by faith, Israel pursuing as if by works and stumbling at Christ—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Romans 9:30–33.

Arminian Notable voices

“What shall we say then—What is to be concluded from all that has been said but this, That the gentiles, who followed not after righteousness—Who a while ago had no knowledge of, no care or thought about, it. Have attained to righteousness—Or justification. Even the righteousness which is by faith. Israel—The Jews although following after the law of righteousness—That law which, duly used, would have led them to faith, and thereby to righteousness. Have not attained to the law of righteousness. And wherefore have they not? Is it because God eternally decreed they should not? There is nothing like this to be met with: the apostle gives us this good reason, Because they sought it not by faith—Whereby alone it could be attained. But as it were—In effect, if not professedly, by works. For they stumbled at that stumblingstone—Christ crucified. Behold, I lay in Sion—I exhibit in my church, what, though it is in truth the only sure foundation of happiness, yet will be in fact a stumblingstone and rock of offence—An occasion of ruin to many, through their obstinate unbelief.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on Rom 9:30–33)

Reformed Notable voices

“They do what is absurd and invert all order, who strive to assign and set up causes above the secret predestination of God, which he has previously taught us is to be counted as the first cause. But as this is superior to all other causes, so the corruption and wickedness of the ungodly afford a reason and an occasion for the judgments of God. The righteousness which the Gentiles attained was by faith; and it hence depends on the Lord’s mercy, and not on man’s own worthiness. The Gentiles could not have obtained righteousness by faith, except God had anticipated their faith by his grace; and so faith itself is a portion of his favor. They stumbled at Christ, through whom alone a way is open to the attainment of righteousness. He is appointed for ruin to the unbelieving, but for life and resurrection to the godly.”
John Calvin · Commentary on Romans (on Rom 9:30–33)
