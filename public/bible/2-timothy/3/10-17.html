Passage

2 Timothy 3

Verses 10–17 · ESV · Voices

Timothy is set over against the impostors: he has followed Paul’s teaching, conduct, faith, love, and sufferings, and the Lord rescued Paul from them all. All who desire a godly life in Christ will be persecuted, while evil people go from bad to worse. Timothy must continue in what he learned; the sacred writings are able to make him wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus, and all Scripture is God-breathed—profitable so that the man of God may be complete.

2 Timothy 3:10

10You, however, have followed my teaching, my conduct, my aim in life, my faith, my patience, my love, my steadfastness,

Arminian

You, however: Timothy is not classed with the creeping impostors. He has followed Paul’s teaching, conduct, aim, faith, patience, love, and steadfastness—doctrine married to a life.

Arminian pattern: following is a real apprenticeship under grace. The same cluster can be neglected if Timothy turns. What he has already followed is gift and task together: stay in the path you learned, not in the form that denies power.

Reformed

Paul contrasts Timothy with the men of vv. 1–9 by a lived curriculum: teaching, conduct, aim, faith, patience, love, steadfastness. The gospel produces a recognizable shape in the messenger.

Reformed comfort: Timothy’s following is evidence of grace already at work. God uses an apostolic life as a means; the servant’s pattern is not a second savior, but it is how the same faith is handed on in the open.

2 Timothy 3:11

11my persecutions and sufferings that happened to me at Antioch, at Iconium, and at Lystra—which persecutions I endured; yet from them all the Lord rescued me.

Arminian

The résumé includes persecutions at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra—places Timothy knows. Paul endured; yet from them all the Lord rescued him. Endurance and rescue are both named.

Arminian pastoral: the Lord really rescues, and the servant really endures. Grace does not cancel the blows; it keeps the man through them. Remembering past rescues is meant to steady Timothy for the next round, not to promise a painless office.

Reformed

Paul endured, and the Lord rescued him from all those persecutions. The history is concrete: Antioch, Iconium, Lystra. Deliverance is the Lord’s, not Paul’s luck.

Reformed accent: the same God who appointed the apostle’s path also extracted him from the violence. Endurance is not bare grit; it is the Lord keeping His servant so that the mission continues.

2 Timothy 3:12

12Indeed, all who desire to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted,

Arminian

Wesley: all that are resolved to live godly—count the cost; out of Christ there is no godliness; they shall suffer persecution, more or less; there is no exception. Desire here is a holy resolve, not a mood.

Arminian honesty: godliness in Christ draws resistance. Enabling grace makes the godly life possible; it does not buy exemption. If you want a Christianity with no cost, you want a different Christ than Paul’s.

Reformed

All who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will be persecuted: the rule is general, not a rare accident for missionaries only. Union with Christ includes sharing His world’s hatred.

Reformed stamina: God has ordained that the godly path run through opposition. The desire itself is His work in the believer; the persecution does not mean He has left them. The Lord who rescued Paul (v. 11) still shepherds those He has made hungry for godliness.

2 Timothy 3:13

13while evil people and impostors will go on from bad to worse, deceiving and being deceived.

Arminian

Evil people and impostors worsen: deceiving and being deceived. Falsehood is a loop; the captors become captives of their own lie.

Arminian warning: this trajectory is not fate you must join. It is what happens when the truth is refused. Deceit thickens with practice. Continue in what you learned (v. 14); do not apprentice yourself to a downward spiral and call it complexity.

Reformed

Impostors go from bad to worse, deceiving and being deceived: judgment includes being handed over to the lie one tells. The spiral is moral, not merely intellectual.

Reformed reading: God is not the author of their deceit, yet He judicially lets the unrepentant ripen. The church should expect impostors to intensify, not politely plateau. That expectation keeps Timothy from panic and from compromise.

2 Timothy 3:14

14But as for you, continue in what you have learned and have firmly believed, knowing from whom you learned it

Arminian

But as for you: the contrast is personal. Continue in what you learned and firmly believed, knowing the teachers—Lois, Eunice, Paul—whose lives matched the doctrine.

Arminian charge: continuance is commanded because departure is possible. Grace that taught Timothy also enables him to stay; it does not make the command ornamental. Knowing from whom you learned it is a means: trusted witnesses help the soul hold what it received.

Reformed

Continue: remain in the things learned and believed, with the faces of the teachers in view. God often ties the Word to a lineage of faithful mouths.

Reformed instinct: the command to continue is a means by which God keeps His own. Timothy’s history of learning is not a ladder he built; it is the track of grace. Knowing the teachers guards him against novelty that arrives without that holy pedigree.

2 Timothy 3:15

15and how from childhood you have been acquainted with the sacred writings, which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus.

Arminian

From childhood Timothy knew the sacred writings. They are able to make one wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus—Scripture’s aim is saving wisdom, and the appointed way is faith in the Son.

Arminian soteriology: the writings sincerely make wise unto salvation; faith is the living condition by which that wisdom is received. Childhood acquaintance is a gift, not a substitute for believing. The same Scriptures still call; the hearer must continue in the faith they teach, not treat them as a family heirloom only.

Reformed

The sacred writings are able to make wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. Scripture is God’s instrument; Christ is the object of the faith that saves.

Reformed soteriology: ability here is the Word’s sufficiency as means, not a bare possibility left to chance. God uses the Scriptures to give saving wisdom; faith in Christ is His appointed way, and that faith is itself gift. Childhood acquaintance is providence preparing Timothy for the Christ the writings preach.

Key contrast Both camps confess that the sacred writings make wise for salvation through faith in Christ, not apart from Him. Arminian readers stress a sincere, resistible means: Scripture offers saving wisdom, received by those who believe. Reformed readers stress effectual means: God uses Scripture to give the elect that wisdom, and the faith through which they are saved is His gift.

2 Timothy 3:16

16All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness,

Arminian

All Scripture is breathed out by God—profitable for teaching, reproof, correction, and training in righteousness. Wesley: the Spirit not only once inspired the writers, but continually inspires, supernaturally assists, those who read with earnest prayer; hence the profit for doctrine, conviction, amendment, and training the children of God.

Arminian reading: inspiration of the text and assistance of the reader belong together. Sufficiency does not cancel prayer. The Word is God’s breath; the same Spirit helps those who come to it rather than resisting. Profit is real where the Scriptures are used, not where they are admired and left closed.

Reformed

Calvin: this distinguishes our religion—that God has spoken, and the prophets were organs of the Spirit, uttering what they were commissioned to declare. Lay it down as settled: the Law and the Prophets are not a doctrine according to human pleasure, but dictated by the Holy Spirit.

Reformed reading: God-breathed means the origin is divine dictation in the sense Calvin named, not a later religious glow on human insight. Scripture is profitable because God authored it. Those who profit are given eyes; illumination is the Spirit’s work in the elect, not a second inspiration that rewrites the page.

Key contrast The fork is how inspiration and profit meet the reader. Wesley heard a God-breathed text plus the Spirit’s ongoing supernatural assistance to those who read with earnest prayer. Calvin heard a text dictated by the Spirit, sufficient as God’s speech, with profit belonging to those who first settle that origin—and, in Reformed hearing, to those given eyes to see.

2 Timothy 3:17

17that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work.

Arminian

The aim of the God-breathed Word is a complete man of God, equipped for every good work. Scripture is not a hobby text; it outfits a servant for the whole of obedience.

Arminian pastoral: equipped is for works that must actually be done. Grace makes the works possible; the man of God must take up the equipment. Completeness here is fitness for holy labor, not a claim that prayer, church, and the Spirit’s help are now unnecessary.

Reformed

Calvin: Paul asserts absolutely that the Scripture is sufficient for perfection. The man of God is made complete and fitted for every good work by this Word, not by a rival canon of speculation.

Reformed comfort: sufficiency means God has not left His servant under-supplied. Every good work the man of God is called to is within the reach of the Scripture’s teaching, reproof, correction, and training. Completeness is God’s provision in His Word, applied by the Spirit He breathed.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on godly persecution, the sacred writings, and Scripture breathed out by God—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside 2 Timothy 3:10–17.

Arminian Notable voices

“All that are resolved to live godly—Therefore count the cost. Out of Christ there is no godliness. Shall suffer persecution—More or less. There is no exception. All scripture is inspired of God—The Spirit of God not only once inspired those who wrote it, but continually inspires, supernaturally assists, those that read it with earnest prayer. Hence it is so profitable for doctrine, for the reproof or conviction of them that are in error or sin, for the correction or amendment of whatever is amiss, and for instructing or training up the children of God in all righteousness.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on 2 Tim 3:12, 16)

Reformed Notable voices

“This is a principle which distinguishes our religion from all others, that we know that God hath spoken to us, and are fully convinced that the prophets did not speak at their own suggestion, but that, being organs of the Holy Spirit, they only uttered what they had been commissioned from heaven to declare. Whoever then wishes to profit in the Scriptures, let him first of all, lay down this as a settled point, that the Law and the Prophets are not a doctrine delivered according to the will and pleasure of men, but dictated by the Holy Spirit. He asserts absolutely, that the Scripture is sufficient for perfection.”
John Calvin · Commentary on 2 Timothy (on 2 Tim 3:16–17)
