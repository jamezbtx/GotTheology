Passage

2 Timothy 2

Verses 14–19 · WEB · Voices

Timothy must remind the church not to quarrel about words, but to present himself to God as an approved worker, properly handling the Word of Truth. Irreverent babble spreads like gangrene—Hymenaeus and Philetus have already upset some—yet God’s firm foundation stands with its double seal.

2 Timothy 2:14

14Remind them of these things, charging them in the sight of the Lord that they don’t argue about words to no profit, to the subverting of those who hear.

Arminian

Remind them of these things—the gospel just recited—and charge them in the sight of the Lord that they don’t argue about words. Word-fights do no good; they ruin hearers. The trustworthy saying is to be pressed, not replaced by clever disputes.

A ruined hearer is a spiritual casualty, not a stage effect. Charge before God because people can still be overturned. Ministry that loves ingenuity more than edification harms those who might yet believe.

Reformed

Calvin: these things are of such importance that a good minister ought never to be weary of exhibiting them. Solemn charging before the Lord is meant to strike terror. Disputing about words is condemned as useless and as subverting the hearers; Satan uses quarrels to disturb the weak and overthrow their faith.

Edification is the only aim. Speculative theology that tends to ostentation is a danger to the church. God preserves his people, and he uses this severe charge as a means to fence them from ruinous talk.

2 Timothy 2:15

15Give diligence to present yourself approved by God, a workman who doesn’t need to be ashamed, properly handling the Word of Truth.

Arminian

Wesley: Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth—duly explaining and applying the whole scripture, so as to give each hearer his due portion.

Approval is before God, not a crowd. Rightly handling is giving each hearer what the whole Scripture assigns—warning, promise, command. A worker can be ashamed if he twists the word; the charge assumes a stewardship of truth.

Reformed

Calvin: study to shew thyself approved by God, not by a crowded assembly. Be workmen in building the Church, not lazy disputants. Dividing aright is a judicious allotment of the word suited to the profit of the hearers; that is the rule by which all interpretation is tried.

Eyes fixed on God check foolish eagerness for display. The pastor cuts the bread for the family, not mutilating or staying on the surface. God approves the worker; the worker still labors not to blush before him.

2 Timothy 2:16

16But shun empty chatter, for it will go further in ungodliness,

Arminian

Avoid irreverent babble: it does not stay verbal. It will go further in ungodliness. Empty talk is a path, not a pastime.

People can be led—hearers move toward ungodliness by what they drink in. The minister’s avoidance is love for souls who might still turn, not mere taste.

Reformed

Calvin: shun profane and unmeaning noises; they are a labyrinth or whirlpool into which men plunge themselves more and more, growing to greater ungodliness.

Babble is a contagion God tells his servant to refuse. The slide into ungodliness is authentic in the visible church. Avoidance is obedience, a means of guarding the flock.

2 Timothy 2:17

17and those words will consume like gangrene, of whom is Hymenaeus and Philetus:

Arminian

Their talk spreads like gangrene: named men, Hymenaeus and Philetus, are not abstractions. Public error needs public marking when the infection is spreading.

To spare one teacher at the cost of many hearers is false kindness. Gangrene language treats false doctrine as a disease that can still be cut back if met early.

Reformed

Calvin: gangrene, if not speedily met, spreads to adjoining parts and consumes until it kills. False doctrines, once given entrance, spread till they have completed the destruction of the Church. Paul points out the plagues so all may be on guard; where many are in danger, dissimulation is cruel.

God preserves a remnant, and he uses timely exposure. Hymenaeus and Philetus are written down for every age. Early resistance is pastoral mercy, not a lack of patience.

2 Timothy 2:18

18men who have erred concerning the truth, saying that the resurrection is already past, and overthrowing the faith of some.

Arminian

They have erred concerning the truth: the resurrection, they say, has already happened—an allegorical dodge that empties hope. They are upsetting the faith of some. Some means people whose confidence in the gospel can actually be overturned.

Swerving is a departure from truth once held. Upsetting the faith of some is why v. 14 forbade word-fights and why the resurrection of v. 8 must be remembered as future bodily hope, not a spiritualized past.

Reformed

Calvin: they departed from the truth by giving out that the resurrection was already past, an allegorical trick Satan uses to overthrow the article of the resurrection of the flesh. From the beginning the faith of some was subverted; such an example should excite diligence.

Some in the visible church are overturned; that scandal is met in v. 19 by God’s foundation. The error is not a small timing dispute; it guts the gospel Paul just recited. God keeps his elect, and he keeps them by this warning against a plague that already takes some.

2 Timothy 2:19

19However, God’s firm foundation stands, having this seal: “The Lord knows those who are his,” and, “Let every one who names the name of the Lord depart from unrighteousness.”

Arminian

God’s firm foundation stands, sealed on both sides. Wesley: the foundation of God—his truth and faithfulness—standeth fast, never overthrown; on the one side, The Lord knoweth those that are his; on the other, Let every one who nameth the name of the Lord depart from unrighteousness. Indeed, they only are his who depart from iniquity.

Wesley stresses the second inscription as evidence they are his. Naming the Lord without departing from iniquity is empty glory. The Lord knows his own, and his own are known in the departing. Hymenaeus does not overthrow God’s faithfulness; he shows that a name without holiness differs from being his.

Reformed

Calvin: the foundation of God standeth sure. The levity of men cannot hinder God from preserving his Church. He reminds us of election, called a foundation: the elect rest on an immovable foundation because their salvation is in God’s hand; they do not stand by their own strength. The Lord knoweth who are his; they who went out from us were not of us. Let every one that calleth on Christ depart from iniquity.

Calvin stresses the first inscription as election’s comfort—God’s secret knowledge of his own—then the call to holiness on the same seal. Apostasy of the prominent does not unseal the elect. The second sentence still binds: profession without departing from iniquity is not to be sported with.

Key contrast Both read a double seal that cannot be torn apart. Wesley stresses the second—“depart from unrighteousness”—as the evidence they are his: indeed, they only are his who depart. Calvin typically stresses the first—“the Lord knows those who are his”—as the comfort of election, then the matching call to holiness when hypocrites are exposed.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Arminian and Reformed writers of the past on rightly handling the word of truth, gangrene-like error, and the sealed foundation of God, in their own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth—Duly explaining and applying the whole scripture, so as to give each hearer his due portion. The foundation of God—His truth and faithfulness. Standeth fast—Can never be overthrown; being as it were sealed with a seal, which has an inscription on each side: on the one, The Lord knoweth those that are his; on the other, Let every one who nameth the name of the Lord depart from iniquity. Indeed, they only are his who depart from iniquity.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament (on 2 Tim 2:15–19)

Reformed Notable voices

“The foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his. The levity or treachery of men cannot hinder God from preserving his Church to the last. He reminds us of the election of God, which he metaphorically calls a foundation. The elect do not depend on changing events, but rest on a solid and immovable foundation, because their salvation is in the hand of God. Amidst so great weakness of our flesh, the elect are nevertheless beyond the reach of danger, because they do not stand by their own strength, but are founded on God. Let every one that calleth on the name of Christ depart from iniquity.”
John Calvin · Commentary on 2 Timothy (on 2 Tim 2:19)
