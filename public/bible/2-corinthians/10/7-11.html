Passage

2 Corinthians 10

Verses 7–11 · WEB

Paul tells his critics to look at what is in front of them. He belongs to Christ as much as they do, and his authority exists to build the church up. He quotes the charge that his letters are weighty but his presence weak, and insists that his deeds will match his words. Both traditions see here a model of authority used for edification.

2 Corinthians 10:7

7Do you look at things only as they appear in front of your face? If anyone trusts in himself that he is Christ’s, let him consider this again with himself, that even as he is Christ’s, so we also are Christ’s.

Arminian

Look at what is plainly before you. If someone is confident he belongs to Christ, let him remember that Paul does too. Arminian readers note Paul’s refusal to let rivals claim Christ for themselves alone.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same point and note the uncertainty of the Greek: it can be read as a command, a question, or a statement about judging by appearances. Scholars in both traditions differ on this, and it does not split them doctrinally.

2 Corinthians 10:8

8For even if I boast somewhat abundantly concerning our authority, which the Lord gave for building you up and not for casting you down, I will not be ashamed,

Arminian

Paul will not be ashamed if he boasts about the authority the Lord gave him, which is for building them up, not tearing them down. Arminian readers highlight the purpose of authority: to strengthen the church.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise stress that church authority is delegated by Christ and limited by its purpose of edification. Both agree that authority used to crush people misuses what Christ gave.

2 Corinthians 10:9

9that I may not seem as if I desire to terrify you by my letters.

Arminian

Paul does not want to seem to be frightening them with his letters. Arminian readers see his concern that his words be received as care, not intimidation.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same pastoral sensitivity. Paul’s letters were firm but meant to heal. Both read this as continuity with verse 8’s purpose of building up.

2 Corinthians 10:10

10For, “His letters”, they say, “are weighty and strong, but his bodily presence is weak, and his speech is despised.”

Arminian

Critics said his letters were weighty and strong but his presence weak and his speech contemptible. Arminian readers see Paul quoting the complaint directly in order to answer it.

Reformed

Reformed readers note that Paul’s opponents judged by outward impressiveness, a standard Paul rejects throughout this letter, where God’s power is shown in weakness. Both traditions see this as background for Paul’s defense in chapters 10–13.

2 Corinthians 10:11

11Let such a person consider this, that what we are in word by letters when we are absent, such are we also in deed when we are present.

Arminian

Paul says that what he is in letters when absent, he will be in action when present. Arminian readers see a call for his critics to reckon honestly with his consistency.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same warning that his gentleness should not be mistaken for weakness. Integrity between word and deed is the point, and both traditions agree.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
