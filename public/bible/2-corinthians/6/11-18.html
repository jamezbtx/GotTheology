Passage

2 Corinthians 6

Verses 11–18 · WEB

Paul opens his heart to the Corinthians and asks them to open theirs in return. He warns them not to be unequally yoked with unbelievers, for righteousness and iniquity, light and darkness, Christ and Belial have nothing in common. The church is the temple of the living God, called to separate from idolatry and enjoy God as Father. Both traditions share this call to holiness; differences here are mainly about application.

2 Corinthians 6:11

11Our mouth is open to you, Corinthians. Our heart is enlarged.

Arminian

Our mouth is open to you; our heart is enlarged. Arminian readers see Paul’s frank affection for a church that has doubted him.

Reformed

Reformed readers see pastoral warmth: Paul’s openness invites reconciliation between apostle and church.

2 Corinthians 6:12

12You are not restricted by us, but you are restricted by your own affections.

Arminian

You are not restricted by us but by your own affections. Arminian readers see the problem lying in the Corinthians’ hearts, not Paul’s.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. The barrier is their narrow affection.

2 Corinthians 6:13

13Now in return—I speak as to my children—you also open your hearts.

Arminian

Open your hearts in return, as my children. Arminian readers see a fatherly plea for mutual love.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same pastoral appeal.

2 Corinthians 6:14

14Don’t be unequally yoked with unbelievers, for what fellowship do righteousness and iniquity have? Or what fellowship does light have with darkness?

Arminian

Do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers. Arminian readers see a call to avoid binding partnerships that compromise devotion to Christ. Many apply it to marriage and other close alliances.

Reformed

Reformed readers also apply it broadly, while noting that the immediate concern is idolatry (v.16). Righteousness and iniquity, light and darkness, cannot share the same allegiance.

2 Corinthians 6:15

15What agreement does Christ have with Belial? Or what portion does a believer have with an unbeliever?

Arminian

What agreement has Christ with Belial? Arminian readers see stark spiritual opposition between Christ and evil powers.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree; Belial is a name for Satan or worthlessness.

2 Corinthians 6:16

16What agreement does a temple of God have with idols? For you are a temple of the living God. Even as God said, “I will dwell in them and walk in them. I will be their God and they will be my people.”

Arminian

You are a temple of the living God. Arminian readers see God dwelling among His people as the ground of their call to holiness. The WEB reads you are; many critical editions read we are, with the same point.

Reformed

Reformed readers see covenant promises from Leviticus 26:12 and Ezekiel 37:27 fulfilled in the church. The variant between you and we does not change the meaning: God’s people are His dwelling.

2 Corinthians 6:17

17Therefore “ ‘Come out from among them, and be separate,’ says the Lord. ‘Touch no unclean thing. I will receive you.

Arminian

Come out and be separate. Arminian readers see a call to holiness and separation from idolatry.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same call drawn from Isaiah 52:11, applied to the church.

2 Corinthians 6:18

18I will be to you a Father. You will be to me sons and daughters,’ says the Lord Almighty.”

Arminian

I will be a Father to you, and you will be my sons and daughters. Arminian readers rejoice in adoption into God’s family, open to all who come.

Reformed

Reformed readers see adoption as a covenant blessing secured by grace, echoing 2 Samuel 7:14.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
