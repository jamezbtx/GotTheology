Passage

2 Corinthians 5

Verses 11–15 · WEB

Because Paul knows the fear of the Lord, he persuades people, and he is open before God. He is not boasting for himself but giving the Corinthians a reply to those who boast in appearances. The love of Christ compels him, for one died for all, so all died, and those who live should live for him who died and rose. Both traditions share Paul’s motive for ministry; verses 14–15 are a classic point of difference on the scope of Christ’s death.

2 Corinthians 5:11

11Knowing therefore the fear of the Lord, we persuade men, but we are revealed to God, and I hope that we are revealed also in your consciences.

Arminian

Knowing the fear of the Lord, we persuade men. Arminian readers see reverent awe before the coming judgment as fuel for urgent persuasion. People are called to respond to the gospel.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same reverent motive. Persuasion is the God-ordained means by which He calls His people, and Paul’s openness before God guards his sincerity.

2 Corinthians 5:12

12For we are not commending ourselves to you again, but speak as giving you occasion of boasting on our behalf, that you may have something to answer those who boast in appearance and not in heart.

Arminian

We are not commending ourselves again but giving you a reason to boast in us. Arminian readers see Paul defending his ministry against rivals who prize outward show.

Reformed

Reformed readers see Paul contrasting boasting in appearance with what is in the heart. God judges the heart, and true ministry is measured there.

2 Corinthians 5:13

13For if we are beside ourselves, it is for God. Or if we are of sober mind, it is for you.

Arminian

If we are beside ourselves, it is for God; if sober, it is for you. Arminian readers take this as Paul’s answer to charges of madness or ecstatic excess. All he does serves God and the church.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same point. Whether in spiritual intensity or sober reasoning, Paul is not serving himself.

2 Corinthians 5:14

14For the love of Christ compels us; because we judge thus: that one died for all, therefore all died.

Arminian

The love of Christ compels us: one died for all, therefore all died. Arminian readers see Christ’s death as provided for every person, so the gospel can be sincerely offered to all. All died is often read as all being included in Christ’s representative death, with its saving benefit received through faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers usually read all here in light of union with Christ: the all who died in Him are those who now live for Him (v.15). Many Reformed interpreters hold that Christ’s death effectively secures the salvation of His people, while affirming that the gospel is offered freely to all.

Key contrast Both say Christ died for all and that His love drives the mission. Arminian readers typically take all as every person, with benefits received by faith. Reformed readers typically read all as all who are united to Christ, though Reformed views on the atonement’s extent vary.

2 Corinthians 5:15

15He died for all, that those who live should no longer live to themselves, but to him who for their sakes died and rose again.

Arminian

He died for all so that those who live should live for him. Arminian readers see the universal provision of verse 14 joined to a particular response: those who live are those who accept new life through faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the purpose of Christ’s death achieved in those who live: His death and resurrection produce a people who no longer live for themselves.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
