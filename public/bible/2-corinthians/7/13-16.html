Passage

2 Corinthians 7

Verses 13–16 · WEB

Titus’s refreshed spirit confirms the Corinthians’ receptive response to Paul’s correction. Paul celebrates restored trust and affection after a painful episode. This renewed confidence prepares for his appeal concerning the collection in chapters 8–9.

2 Corinthians 7:13

13Therefore we have been comforted. In our comfort we rejoiced the more exceedingly for the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by you all.

Arminian

The Corinthians’ welcome has refreshed Titus and deepened Paul’s comfort. Arminian readers see the shared joy that follows reconciliation. Both traditions recognize that the way a congregation receives a fellow worker can strengthen ministry far beyond its own gathering.

Reformed

Reformed readers see God’s comfort spreading through the restored fellowship of his people. Titus’s refreshed spirit becomes a further reason for Paul to rejoice. The outcome of correction is renewed communion, not continuing suspicion.

2 Corinthians 7:14

14For if in anything I have boasted to him on your behalf, I was not disappointed. But as we spoke all things to you in truth, so our glorying also which I made before Titus was found to be truth.

Arminian

Paul’s favorable words about the Corinthians have been borne out by their response to Titus. Arminian readers see encouragement vindicated through actual obedience. His confidence did not excuse their wrong but left room to expect a faithful response.

Reformed

Reformed readers see consistency between truthful ministry and the congregation’s renewed conduct. Paul is glad his commendation proved reliable rather than embarrassing Titus. Both traditions value truthful encouragement that neither flatters nor treats people as beyond restoration.

2 Corinthians 7:15

15His affection is more abundantly toward you, while he remembers all of your obedience, how with fear and trembling you received him.

Arminian

Titus remembers their obedient and serious welcome, and his affection grows. Arminian readers see a responsive congregation rebuilding trust. Fear and trembling convey the gravity of receiving correction, not a license for leaders to keep people intimidated.

Reformed

Reformed readers see reverent responsiveness to the ministry Titus brought. Obedience deepens affection rather than creating a cold relationship of control. Both traditions regard humble reception of correction as compatible with warm, restored fellowship.

2 Corinthians 7:16

16I rejoice that in everything I am confident concerning you.

Arminian

Paul closes this account with renewed confidence in the Corinthians. Arminian readers see encouragement rooted in their repentant response, inviting continued faithfulness. Both traditions read his joy as relational assurance, not a prediction that no further pastoral difficulties can arise.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the restoration of trust after a painful episode. Paul can now approach the collection with confidence rather than estrangement. His generous words encourage obedience without implying that the congregation has become incapable of sin.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
