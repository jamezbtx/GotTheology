Passage

Psalms 34

Verses 1–22 · ESV · Voices

Of David, when he changed his behavior before Abimelech, so that he drove him out, and he went away. The psalmist vows to bless the LORD at all times with continual praise; boasts in the LORD so the humble hear and are glad; summons others to magnify and exalt the name together; testifies that he sought the LORD, who answered and delivered him from all his fears; describes those who look to him as radiant and unashamed; recalls this poor man crying and the LORD saving him from all troubles; confesses the angel of the LORD encamping around those who fear him; invites all to taste and see that the LORD is good and blesses the refugee in him; calls saints to fear the LORD who lack no good thing while young lions hunger; teaches children the fear of the LORD as the path of life, guarded tongue, turned evil, done good, and pursued peace; sets the LORD's eyes and ears toward the righteous and his face against evildoers; promises hearing and deliverance when the righteous cry; draws near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit; acknowledges many afflictions of the righteous yet total deliverance, kept bones, and the wicked slain by affliction; and closes with the LORD redeeming his servants' life so that none who take refuge in him will be condemned.

Psalms 34:1

1I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall continually be in my mouth.

Arminian

I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall continually be in my mouth. After Abimelech drove him out, David answers deliverance with lifelong blessing—praise not boxed to calm days.

Arminian pastoral: shared—bless the LORD at all times. Let praise stay in the mouth when fear returns; do not save thanksgiving for safer weather.

Reformed

Calvin: David promises to keep in remembrance during his whole life the goodness bestowed upon him. Ordinary daily help calls for continual praise; a more illustrious preservation marks special obligation never to cease setting forth his praises. Distinguish heart-praise from hypocrites' empty tongue.

Reformed pastoral: shared—continual praise from the heart. Heap benefit upon benefit into lifelong blessing; refuse a mouth that thanks God only when danger is already gone.

Psalms 34:2

2My soul makes its boast in the Lord; let the humble hear and be glad.

Arminian

My soul makes its boast in the Lord; let the humble hear and be glad. Wesley: Shall boast—Shall glory in this, that I have so powerful and so gracious a master. The humble—The righteous. Soul-boast in the LORD gladdens the lowly who overhear it.

Arminian pastoral: shared—boast in the LORD so the humble may be glad. Trade self-glory for Master-glory; let your testimony lift the crushed, not advertise you.

Reformed

Calvin: soul means the seat of the affections—whole-heart boasting in God alone lest so great a deliverance be forgotten. The humble (anavim) are not all the afflicted merely, but those humbled and subdued, ready to abase themselves; they share joy because David's case is a general pledge of grace, and hope lifts their hearts.

Reformed pastoral: shared—boast in Jehovah that the humble may hear and recover. Make deliverance public medicine for the lowly; refuse private pride that never gladdens another.

Psalms 34:3

3Oh, magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name together!

Arminian

Oh, magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name together! Wesley: Together—Not in place, for David was now banished from the place of God's publick worship, but in affection: let our souls meet, and let our praises meet in the ears of the all-hearing God. Exile does not cancel united exaltation.

Arminian pastoral: shared—magnify the LORD with others. When place is denied, keep affection's meeting; exalt the name together in the ears of the all-hearing God.

Reformed

Calvin: another fruit of thanksgiving—inducing others by example; he calls all the godly to unite heartily and with one consent. Learn from many helps to abound in hope; when each recites personal benefits, all are animated publicly to praise. We give thanks publicly so men may witness gratitude and follow the example.

Reformed pastoral: shared—exalt his name together. Turn private rescue into congregational magnification; let one deliverance recruit many mouths.

Psalms 34:4

4I sought the Lord, and he answered me and delivered me from all my fears.

Arminian

I sought the Lord, and he answered me and delivered me from all my fears. Seeking is answered; fears—plural—are met by deliverance, not by pretended iron nerves.

Arminian pastoral: shared—seek him when fears multiply. Bring the whole cluster of terrors to prayer; expect answer and deliverance, not a demand that you feel nothing.

Reformed

Calvin: seeking here means recourse for help; God answered. Fears mean dangers that sorely disquieted—cruel death, betrayal to Saul, Achish's hatred. Even David, known for bravery, was terrified and agitated; mark this so that fear need not prevent calling upon God.

Reformed pastoral: shared—sought, answered, delivered from all fears. Do not let effeminacy or heroism-myth silence prayer; frightened saints may still seek and be heard.

Psalms 34:5

5Those who look to him are radiant, and their faces shall never be ashamed.

Arminian

Those who look to him are radiant, and their faces shall never be ashamed. Wesley: Lightened—Comforted and encouraged. Ashamed—They were not disappointed of their hope. Looking to him brightens faces that hope will not put to shame.

Arminian pastoral: shared—look to him and be radiant. Trade shame-anxiety for hope that is not disappointed; faces brighten where the eyes rest on the LORD.

Reformed

Calvin: they look and flow to him; faces not ashamed—fruit of the favor shown to David. A mirror is set forth in which men may behold God's face serene and merciful; the poor and afflicted dare lift eyes and resort freely. Or: the darkened are enlightened and the ashamed reclothe countenances with cheerfulness.

Reformed pastoral: shared—look to him without final shame. Use David's deliverance as the mirror that frees timid approaches; refuse a hope that expects disappointment from God.

Psalms 34:6

6This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him and saved him out of all his troubles.

Arminian

This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him and saved him out of all his troubles. Wesley: This man—David. The poor man's cry is heard; all troubles are the sphere of saving, not some only.

Arminian pastoral: shared—cry as this poor man. Do not wait to stop being poor before you pray; the LORD hears and saves out of all troubles.

Reformed

Calvin: David introduces the godly speaking of himself—This poor man cried—so the weight of example encourages them. God invites all the poor to cry; they contemplate in David what belongs to the common benefit. He is as willing to hear all the afflicted who direct sighs and cries with the same faith as he heard David.

Reformed pastoral: shared—the poor man's cry is a pattern, not a museum piece. Join the invitation; expect hearing for the same faith, not for identical biography.

Psalms 34:7

7The angel of the Lord encamps around those who fear him, and delivers them.

Arminian

The angel of the Lord encamps around those who fear him, and delivers them. Wesley: The angel—The angels, the singular number being put for the plural. Fearers of the LORD live inside an encamped deliverance.

Arminian pastoral: shared—fear him under the angel's camp. Rest when surrounded; deliverance is posted around those who fear, not reserved for the fearless.

Reformed

Calvin: God is able to deliver, and in mercy to our infirmity often defends by angels' ministration. One angel stands for the ministry of many—ministering spirits for the heirs of salvation. However many enemies surround us, angels armed with invincible power watch and array on every side.

Reformed pastoral: shared—confess encamped deliverance. Let angelic guard confirm faith without displacing God as the Church's true wall; fear him and sleep inside his posted help.

Psalms 34:8

8Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good! Blessed is the man who takes refuge in him!

Arminian

Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good! Blessed is the man who takes refuge in him! Wesley: O taste—Make trial, of it by your own experience of it. Good—Merciful and gracious. Goodness is not rumor only—taste, see, take refuge, and be blessed.

Arminian pastoral: shared—taste and see that the LORD is good. Make trial by experience; do not leave mercy as hearsay. Blessed is the refugee in him—come under that shelter.

Reformed

Calvin: taste shows men are without taste who devour gifts without relishing them or ungratefully conceal them. Stir the senses; bring a palate that God's goodness may be known by actual experience. Blessed is the man who trusts in him: God never disappoints those who seek his favor; our unbelief alone impedes his large and bountiful satisfying.

Reformed pastoral: shared—taste, see, trust. Refuse dulled devouring of mercies; let experienced goodness confirm the blessing of refuge, and blame unbelief—not God's scarcity—when the soul stays empty.

Key contrast Both urge tasting and seeing that the LORD is good and bless the one who takes refuge. Arminians typically hear Wesley's plain invitation: make trial by your own experience of merciful goodness. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's edge: God never disappoints those who seek his favor, and our unbelief alone is the impediment to his large bounty.

Psalms 34:9

9Oh, fear the Lord, you his saints, for those who fear him have no lack!

Arminian

Oh, fear the Lord, you his saints, for those who fear him have no lack! Wesley: Fear—Reverence, serve, and trust him: for fear is commonly put for all the parts of God's worship. Saints' fear is whole worship, and lack is answered there.

Arminian pastoral: shared—fear the LORD as saints. Reverence, serve, and trust together; expect no-lack under that fear, not under anxious grasping.

Reformed

Calvin: exhortation to holiness and righteousness that opens a channel for blessings. Men often provide by fraud and plunder; the faithful feel stirrings to imitate. David promises it will be well if they keep themselves in the fear of God opposed to deceitful counsels. God never forsakes those who walk righteously; yield him the honor of expecting more from him than the wicked from unlawful practices.

Reformed pastoral: shared—fear Jehovah and refuse want-driven fraud. Integrity is safer than cunning; stay in the fear that has no lack rather than howl among the wolves.

Psalms 34:10

10The young lions suffer want and hunger; but those who seek the Lord lack no good thing.

Arminian

The young lions suffer want and hunger; but those who seek the Lord lack no good thing. Fierce self-provision can still starve; seekers of the LORD lack no good thing—shared providence against predator-confidence.

Arminian pastoral: shared—seek the LORD rather than envy the lions. When strong hunters go hungry, keep seeking; good things are timed by his hand, not by your ferocity.

Reformed

Calvin: apposite comparison—God provides for his people while lions, fiercest of beasts, prowl famished. Sooner shall lions perish with hunger than God disappoint the righteous and sincere who seek food from his hand. If the good sometimes know penury, his hand succors in due season when they know not where to turn.

Reformed pastoral: shared—seekers lack no good thing though lions hunger. Cast care on paternal bounty; wait the season of succor without baptizing plunder as necessity.

Psalms 34:11

11Come, O children, listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord.

Arminian

Come, O children, listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord. Deliverance turns catechist: the rescued father calls children to learn the fear of the LORD.

Arminian pastoral: shared—come and listen to be taught fear. Treat the fear of the LORD as learnable wisdom after mercy, not as a mood you wait to feel.

Reformed

Calvin: with increased earnestness he exhorts just and harmless conduct. Calling them children allays froward affections; come and hearken imply laying aside wilfulness to become docile. Fear of the Lord stands for the rule of a pious and holy life—virtue is in many mouths, but few know what it is to serve God.

Reformed pastoral: shared—hear the fear of Jehovah taught. Soften stubborn ears; let meekness receive the rule of life that mere talk of virtue never supplies.

Psalms 34:12

12What man is there who desires life and loves many days, that he may see good?

Arminian

What man is there who desires life and loves many days, that he may see good? Wesley: Life—A long and happy life, begun in this world and continued for ever in the next. Good—prosperity or happiness. Desire of life is assumed; the path to seeing good is about to be named.

Arminian pastoral: shared—admit you want life and good days. Let that honest desire drive you into the fear of the LORD, including hope that stretches beyond this age.

Reformed

Calvin: not asking whether any desire happiness—all do—but censuring blindness: scarcely one in a hundred studies to purchase peace by just means. Nearly the whole world is deceived promising happy life from any source other than the divine blessing on the sincere and upright.

Reformed pastoral: shared—desire life, then take the just path. Stop chasing good days by violence and fraud; seek the blessing God gives the upright heart.

Key contrast Both assume the universal desire for life and good days and point to the fear of the LORD as the true path. Arminians typically hear Wesley: life begun in this world and continued for ever in the next. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's censure of the world's folly: scarcely any purchase peace by just means, while most chase happiness apart from the blessing God gives the upright.

Psalms 34:13

13Keep your tongue from evil and your lips from speaking deceit.

Arminian

Keep your tongue from evil and your lips from speaking deceit. Wesley: Evil—From all manner of evil speaking, from all injurious, false and deceitful speeches. The fear of the LORD first bridles the mouth.

Arminian pastoral: shared—keep tongue and lips. Refuse injurious, false, and deceitful speech; life that loves many days starts with a watched mouth.

Reformed

Calvin: a rare virtue—truthful discourse free from deceit. Not first a wide net for every slander theory, but speech that does not ensnare neighbors. Nothing is harder than making speech a true representation of the heart; exercise strict control lest the tongue run riot.

Reformed pastoral: shared—guard tongue from evil and lips from deceit. Make words match the heart before God; do not buy "good days" with ensnaring speech.

Psalms 34:14

14Turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it.

Arminian

Turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it. Wesley: Depart—From all sin. Do good—Be ready to perform all good offices to all men. Seek—Study by all means possible to live peaceable with all men. Pursue it—follow hard after it when it seems to flee.

Arminian pastoral: shared—turn, do good, seek and pursue peace. Do not stop at refusing harm; chase peace when it runs, and ready good offices toward all.

Reformed

Calvin: abstain from evil and devote yourselves to doing good to neighbors. Begin with not wronging, then refuse the excuse that mere non-injury is enough—aid one another. Seek peace and pursue it by gentleness and forbearance even with the fretful; yet with limitation: when the wicked defy God in open war, loyalty must oppose them. In personal affairs be meek and condescending.

Reformed pastoral: shared—turn from evil, do good, pursue peace. Add active kindness to abstinence from harm; chase peace without treason against God's honor.

Psalms 34:15

15The eyes of the Lord are toward the righteous and his ears toward their cry.

Arminian

The eyes of the Lord are toward the righteous and his ears toward their cry. Watched righteousness and heard cries belong together—shared attentive care.

Arminian pastoral: shared—live as seen and heard. Cry without theatrical despair; his eyes and ears are already toward the righteous.

Reformed

Calvin continues the doctrine of deliverance: eyes upon the righteous and ears open to their cry mark fatherly attention, not distant inspection. The godly are not unnoticed petitioners.

Reformed pastoral: shared—trust eyes and ears toward you. Let watched cry replace frantic self-advertisement; providence listens as well as looks.

Psalms 34:16

16The face of the Lord is against those who do evil, to cut off the memory of them from the earth.

Arminian

The face of the Lord is against those who do evil, to cut off the memory of them from the earth. Wesley: The face—His anger, which discovers itself in the face. Favor's opposite is face-against, memory cut off.

Arminian pastoral: shared—do not treat evil as forgotten. The face against evildoers warns; flee that anger rather than bargain with it.

Reformed

Calvin: the face of the LORD against evildoers balances eyes toward the righteous—remembrance cut off from the earth. Malice does not write the last public record; God does.

Reformed pastoral: shared—fear the face against evil. Refuse envy of those whose memory God means to cut off; stay under eyes of favor, not face of anger.

Psalms 34:17

17When the righteous cry for help, the Lord hears and delivers them out of all their troubles.

Arminian

When the righteous cry for help, the Lord hears and delivers them out of all their troubles. Echo of the poor man's story: cry, hear, deliver—out of all troubles again.

Arminian pastoral: shared—cry for help expecting full deliverance. All troubles are within his hearing; keep crying as the righteous, not as those who have already quit.

Reformed

Calvin: the righteous cry and Jehovah hears and delivers from all troubles—doctrine exemplified for those tempted to think providence idle when pain lasts. Hearing is not partial toward a favored few anecdotes only.

Reformed pastoral: shared—cry, be heard, be delivered from all. Hold "all" when troubles stack; the pattern of v. 6 is the church's standing hope.

Psalms 34:18

18The Lord is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit.

Arminian

The Lord is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit. Wesley: Nigh—Ready to hear and succour them. To—Those whose spirits are truly humbled under the hand of God, and the sense of their sins, whose hearts are subdued, and made obedient to God's will and submissive to his providence. Nearness meets brokenness—pastoral center of the psalm.

Arminian pastoral: shared—bring a broken heart and crushed spirit to the near LORD. Humility under his hand and sense of sin is not disqualification; it is the place he succours and saves.

Reformed

Calvin: God is deliverer even when the faithful are brought very low and half-dead. That he is nigh when hearts fail is incredible to human sense—yet his power shines when he raises from the grave. Nothing more opposes true patience than Stoic loftiness; we are not truly humbled until affliction of heart abases us before God so he may raise us. Sweet consolation: God departs not even when we are overwhelmed and almost deprived of life.

Reformed pastoral: shared—the LORD is near the brokenhearted and saves the crushed. Drop Stoic self-command as the mark of faith; receive nearness in faintness, and let him raise you from the dust.

Key contrast Both preach the LORD near to the brokenhearted and saving the crushed in spirit—shared pastoral mercy. Arminians typically hear Wesley: spirits truly humbled under God's hand and the sense of sin, hearts subdued to his will and providence. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: nearness when we are half-dead, against Stoic loftiness, so true affliction of heart abases us that God may raise us.

Psalms 34:19

19Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the Lord delivers him out of them all.

Arminian

Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the Lord delivers him out of them all. Honest tally: many afflictions—then total deliverance. Quantity of pain does not cancel the LORD's out-of-them-all.

Arminian pastoral: shared—count afflictions without counting God out. Many does not mean abandoned; expect deliverance out of them all, not exemption from them all.

Reformed

Calvin: anticipates the doubt—how God cares for the righteous if they are continually harassed. They remain subject to many miseries so that, tested, they show invincible constancy and experience God as deliverer. Without trials faith languishes and piety stays hidden. He can deliver a thousand times as once; never lose courage whatever evils threaten.

Reformed pastoral: shared—many afflictions, deliverance from them all. Read trials as testing under care, not as proof of neglect; hold constancy and cry for the out-of-them-all.

Psalms 34:20

20He keeps all his bones; not one of them is broken.

Arminian

He keeps all his bones; not one of them is broken. Wesley: Bones—All the parts and members of their bodies. Kept bones image thorough preservation under many afflictions.

Arminian pastoral: shared—trust thorough keeping. Affliction may be many without being final ruin; the Keeper numbers what the enemy would shatter.

Reformed

Calvin: illustrates protection—not one bone broken; compare Christ's word that hairs are numbered (Luke 12:7). The clause is applied in John 19:36 to Christ and represented as receiving fulfillment in him—yet the psalm's comfort for the righteous stands in God's careful preservation through danger.

Reformed pastoral: shared—he keeps all his bones. Rest in numbered care through affliction; let preservation language steady you without turning the verse into a promise of painless life.

Key contrast Both confess God's careful preservation—not one bone broken—under many afflictions. Arminians typically stay with Wesley's plain sense: bones as all parts and members of the body kept. Reformed readers typically also note, with Calvin's tradition, the Gospel's application of the line to Christ (John 19:36) while still reading the psalm as comfort for the righteous under God's numbered care.

Psalms 34:21

21Affliction will slay the wicked, and those who hate the righteous will be condemned.

Arminian

Affliction will slay the wicked, and those who hate the righteous will be condemned. Affliction's end differs by camp: the righteous are delivered through it; the wicked are slain by it, and haters stand condemned.

Arminian pastoral: shared—do not envy the wicked's present ease. Affliction and condemnation wait on hatred of the righteous; stay on the delivered side.

Reformed

Calvin: malice (or misery) shall slay the wicked—their wickedness falls on their own heads. Those who hate the righteous shall be destroyed: a wall of brass for the godly among numerous enemies already devoted to destruction.

Reformed pastoral: shared—affliction slays the wicked; haters are condemned. Take courage behind that wall; refuse terror that treats hatred as sovereign.

Psalms 34:22

22The Lord redeems the life of his servants; none of those who take refuge in him will be condemned.

Arminian

The Lord redeems the life of his servants; none of those who take refuge in him will be condemned. Closing pastoral beatitude: redeemed life, refuge, no condemnation—the psalm's last word over the brokenhearted and the hunted.

Arminian pastoral: shared—take refuge and rest un-condemned. Serve under redemption, not under fear of final sentence; none who shelter in him are cast off.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah redeems the soul of his servants—preservation that first adjudges to death before God appears as redeemer; those who demand speedy show of power intercept grace. Principal mark of servants: they trust in God on whom salvation depends. Those who trust shall not perish.

Reformed pastoral: shared—the LORD redeems his servants; refugees are not condemned. Trust through the death-shadow where redemption appears; do not quit refuge for a faster human verdict.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on continual blessing after deliverance, boasting that gladdens the humble, seeking answered and fears scattered, the angel encamped around those who fear, tasting and seeing that the LORD is good, the fear that lacks no good thing, the taught path of tongue and peace, eyes toward the righteous and nearness to the brokenhearted, many afflictions yet full deliverance, and none who take refuge condemned—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 34:1–22.

Arminian Notable voices

“When—A psalm made upon that occasion, though not at that time, when he counterfeited madness. Wherein whether he sinned or not, is matter of dispute; but this is undoubted, that his deliverance deserved this solemn acknowledgment. Abimelech—Called Achish, 1 Sam. xxi, 10. But Abimelech seems to have been the common name of the kings of the Philistines. Shall boast—Shall glory in this, that I have so powerful and so gracious a master. The humble—The righteous. Together—Not in place, for David was now banished from the place of God's publick worship, but in affection: let our souls meet, and let our praises meet in the ears of the all-hearing God. Lightened—Comforted and encouraged. Ashamed—They were not disappointed of their hope. This man—David. The angel—The angels, the singular number being put for the plural. O taste—Make trial, of it by your own experience of it. Good—Merciful and gracious. Fear—Reverence, serve, and trust him: for fear is commonly put for all the parts of God's worship. Life—A long and happy life, begun in this world and continued for ever in the next. Good—In which he may enjoy good, prosperity or happiness. Evil—From all manner of evil speaking, from all injurious, false and deceitful speeches. Depart—From all sin. Do good—Be ready to perform all good offices to all men. Seek—Study by all means possible to live peaceable with all men. Pursue it—Do not only embrace it gladly when it is offered, but follow hard after it, when it seems to flee away from thee. The face—His anger, which discovers itself in the face. Nigh—Ready to hear and succour them. To—Those whose spirits are truly humbled under the hand of God, and the sense of their sins, whose hearts are subdued, and made obedient to God's will and submissive to his providence. Bones—All the parts and members of their bodies.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 34

Reformed Notable voices

“David extols God's greatness, promising lifelong remembrance of a distinguished deliverance. Bless Jehovah at all times; his praise continually in the mouth—yet from the heart, not hypocrites' empty tongue. My soul shall boast in Jehovah; the humble (afflicted and subdued, ready to abase themselves) hear and are glad, recovering hope from David's pledge of grace. Magnify Jehovah with me; public thanks animate others. I sought Jehovah, and he answered, and delivered me from all my fears—terrors of cruel death, betrayal, and Achish's hatred; even David was disquieted, that fear need not silence prayer. They look to him and flow to him; faces not ashamed—a mirror of God's merciful face for the afflicted. This poor man cried, and Jehovah heard: God invites all the poor who cry with the same faith. The angel of Jehovah encamps round about them that fear him and delivers—God often guards by angelic ministry, though his power alone would suffice. Taste and see that Jehovah is good; blessed is the man who trusts in him: unbelief alone impedes his large bounty. Fear Jehovah, ye saints; no want to them that fear him—integrity safer than fraud; sooner shall lions hunger than God fail the sincere. Come, children; I will teach the fear of Jehovah: keep tongue from evil and lips from deceit; turn from evil, do good; seek peace and pursue it even when it flees. Jehovah is nigh to the broken of heart and saves the bruised of spirit—near when we are half-dead; Stoic loftiness is opposed to true patience. Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but Jehovah delivers from them all; he keeps all his bones. Malice slays the wicked; Jehovah redeems the soul of his servants, and those who trust in him shall not perish.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 34
