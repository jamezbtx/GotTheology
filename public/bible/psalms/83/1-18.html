Psalms 83

Verses 1–18 · WEB · Voices

A Song. A Psalm of Asaph. O God, do not keep silence—enemies make an uproar, crafty plans against his people and treasured ones, conspiring to wipe out Israel's name. Edom, Ishmaelites, Moab, Hagrites, Gebal, Ammon, Amalek, Philistia, Tyre, and Asshur join as the strong arm of Lot's children. Do to them as to Midian, Sisera, and Jabin; make nobles like Oreb and Zeeb, princes like Zebah and Zalmunna, who seized God's pastures. Make them whirling dust and chaff; pursue with tempest; fill faces with confusion, that they may seek the LORD's name—yet put to shame forever and perish in disgrace, that men may know the LORD alone is Most High over all the earth.

Psalms 83:1

1God, don’t keep silent. Don’t keep silent, and don’t be still, God.

Arminian

O God, don’t keep silent. Don’t keep silent, and don’t be still, God! Threefold plea against divine silence in the face of assault.

The urgency is plain. Ask God to speak and act—silence here is not indifference forever, but the felt delay the church may name in prayer.

Reformed

Calvin: in great danger the nation and king are in deepest distress; repetition—hold not thy peace, keep not silence, be not still—intimates that if God intends succor he must make haste. Wait patiently when he delays; yet he condescends to infirmity that cries for speed.

The psalmist begs God not to keep silent. Earnest prayer under conspiracy is faith, not unbelief.

Psalms 83:2

2For, behold, your enemies are stirred up. Those who hate you have lifted up their heads.

Arminian

For, behold, your enemies are stirred up. Those who hate you have lifted up their heads. Common confession: the uproar is against God; lifted heads mark insolent hatred of him.

Name the tumult as enmity toward God, not only toward us. Behold is the psalmist pointing heavenward.

Reformed

Calvin: enemies are tumultuous; those who hate thee have lifted up the head. The quarrel is framed as hatred of God himself.

Uproar and raised heads. The Church's foes are counted God's enemies when they aim at his people.

Psalms 83:3

3They conspire with cunning against your people. They plot against your cherished ones.

Arminian

They conspire with cunning against your people. They plot against your cherished ones. Wesley: Hidden ones—Israel, God's secret ones, his peculiar treasure. Craft and consultation target the treasured people.

"Cherished" / hidden ones marks God's valuing care. Crafty counsel is answered by prayer, not matching cunning alone.

Reformed

Calvin: crafty design against thy people and consulted against thy hidden ones—God's flock kept as his treasure.

Craft against treasured ones. The Church's hiddenness is preciousness, not abandonment.

Psalms 83:4

4“Come,” they say, “let’s destroy them as a nation, that the name of Israel may be remembered no more.”

Arminian

Come, they say, let’s destroy them as a nation, that the name of Israel may be remembered no more! The plot is national erasure—name and memory destroyed.

Genocide of the name is blasphemy against the God who named Israel. Cry the intent without caricaturing every neighbor as Midian.

Reformed

Calvin: Come, cut them off from being a nation; let Israel's name be no more remembered—the design is extinction of the people of God.

Wipe out the nation and name. Historic confederacy language; pray protection of the remnant God named.

Psalms 83:5

5For they have conspired together with one mind. They form an alliance against you.

Arminian

For they have conspired together with one mind. They form an alliance against you they make a covenant— Shared: one-accord conspiracy is covenanted enmity against God.

"Against you" locates the league. Human covenants against God do not bind heaven.

Reformed

Calvin: consulted together with one consent; confederate against thee—the war on Israel is war on God.

Conspiracy and covenant against God. Accord among enemies does not outvote the LORD.

Psalms 83:6

6The tents of Edom and the Ishmaelites; Moab, and the Hagrites;

Arminian

“The tents of Edom and the Ishmaelites, Moab and the Hagrites.” Wesley: tabernacles—people dwelling in them; Ishmaelites—posterity of Ishmael; Hagarens from Hagar. Both readings hold: named peoples around Israel form the list.

Historic neighbors named—read the map, not a modern scorecard. The psalm catalogs a coalition.

Reformed

Calvin: the long list of enemies who conspired supports reading the psalm in a setting of multiplied hosts against Judea.

Edom, the Ishmaelites, Moab, and the Hagrites are named. Specific names ground the prayer in history.

Psalms 83:7

7Gebal, Ammon, and Amalek; Philistia with the inhabitants of Tyre;

Arminian

Gebal, Ammon, and Amalek; Philistia with the inhabitants of Tyre; Wesley: Gebal—Arabian people on Canaan's southern border. The ring of foes widens—Gebal, Ammon, Amalek, Philistia, Tyre.

Keep the geography. Many borders, one assault on God's people.

Reformed

Calvin: distant and near peoples mustered until Judea was well-nigh overwhelmed.

The list runs from Gebal to Tyre. Multitude of foes is reason for prayer, not despair.

Psalms 83:8

8Assyria also is joined with them. They have helped the children of Lot. Selah.

Arminian

Assyria also has joined them and lent its strength to the children of Lot; Wesley: Of Lot—Moab and Ammon. Asshur strengthens Lot's children; Selah.

The strong arm is borrowed muscle for Moab and Ammon's cause. Pause at Selah before asking God to answer as of old.

Reformed

Calvin: Asshur joined; they have helped the children of Lot. The coalition's power is confessed before God.

Asshur as strong arm of Lot's children. Name the help enemies receive; still appeal to God.

Psalms 83:9

9Do to them as you did to Midian, as to Sisera, as to Jabin, at the river Kishon;

Arminian

Do to them as you did to Midian, as to Sisera, as to Jabin, at the river Kishon, Shared: imprecation appeals to remembered victories—Midian, Sisera, Jabin—not to private invention of cruelty.

Ask God to do again what he has done in deliverance history. Fair reading: patterned justice, not bloodlust for its own sake.

Reformed

Calvin: Do to them as to Midian, Sisera, Jabin—faithful call to remembrance occasions when God relieved his people at the last extremity; he who put enemies to flight and rescued sheep from wolves can do so again.

Midian and Kishon as precedent. Imprecation here is memory-fueled plea for the Church's deliverance.

Psalms 83:10

10who perished at Endor, who became as dung for the earth.

Arminian

“Who perished at Endor, who became as dung for the earth.” Historic ruin at En-dor—enemies as dung for the ground—is the measure recalled.

Stark image of total overthrow. Remember God's past judgments without gloating in the flesh.

Reformed

Calvin: they perished at Endor; became manure for the earth—miracle of old deliverance rehearsed to strengthen present hope.

Destroyed at En-dor. Past judgment fuels present prayer for succor.

Psalms 83:11

11Make their nobles like Oreb and Zeeb, yes, all their princes like Zebah and Zalmunna,

Arminian

Make their nobles like Oreb and Zeeb, yes, all their princes like Zebah and Zalmunna, Shared: named Midianite leaders become the template for nobles and princes.

Oreb, Zeeb, Zebah, Zalmunna—ask for leadership collapse like Gideon's day. Target is oppressive command, prayed under God.

Reformed

Calvin: Make princes like Oreb and Zeeb, Zebah and Zalmunna—Gideon's victories remembered; God needs no vast army to overthrow proud captains.

Nobles and princes like Midian's chiefs. Pray the LORD, not the sword-arm of revenge, to empty their ranks.

Psalms 83:12

12who said, “Let’s take possession of God’s pasture lands.”

Arminian

They said, “Let’s take possession of God’s pasture lands.” The crime includes seizing God's pastures as private spoil.

Pastures of God—holy inheritance treated as loot. The taunt justifies the plea.

Reformed

Calvin: Who said, Let’s take possession of the habitations of God—sacrilegious claim on what God allotted his people.

The enemies plan to seize God’s pasture lands. Enmity against the Church is robbery of what God named his.

Psalms 83:13

13My God, make them like tumbleweed, like chaff before the wind.

Arminian

Wesley: A wheel—unstable and soon removed, though they promise sure possession. Instability images—wheel, whirling dust, chaff—answer proud permanence.

The words “My God” make the plea personal. My God" personalizes the plea. Ask for their plans to spin away like chaff—not for cartoon cruelty.

Reformed

Calvin: Make them like a whirling ball and stubble before the wind—ungodly inflated with pride must be laid prostrate and put to shame before pride abates.

He asks that they be made like whirling dust and chaff. Pride of destroyers is met by God-made instability.

Psalms 83:14

14As the fire that burns the forest, as the flame that sets the mountains on fire,

Arminian

As the fire that burns the forest, as the flame that sets the mountains ablaze, Wesley: mountains—woods on mountains that burn with irresistible violence in hot countries. Fire and flame figures for overwhelming pursuit.

Natural violence of brushfire images divine chase. Keep the metaphor as prayer, not a manual.

Reformed

Calvin: as fire burns a forest and flame kindles the mountains—images of irresistible judgment pursuing the proud.

Forest fire and blazing mountains. God may pursue as tempest follows flame.

Psalms 83:15

15so pursue them with your tempest, and terrify them with your storm.

Arminian

“So pursue them with your tempest, and terrify them with your storm!” Tempest and hurricane are God's, asked as pursuit and terror.

May you pursue—optative prayer. Terror here aims to stop erasure of Israel, under God's storm.

Reformed

Calvin: pursue with thy tempest; terrify with thy whirlwind—pride of Church-destroyers answered by God's storm.

He asks God to pursue them with tempest and storm. Fair imprecation: both camps hear zeal for God's people, not a caricature of sadism.

Psalms 83:16

16Fill their faces with confusion, that they may seek your name, LORD.

Arminian

Fill their faces with confusion, that they may seek your name, LORD. Wesley: May seek—May own and worship thee as the only true God. Shame is means; seeking the Name is the stated end.

Wesley hears possible owning and worship. Pray shame that turns faces toward the LORD—without pretending every foe will love him.

Reformed

Calvin: Fill faces with confusion, that they may seek thy name—not true repentance or genuine conversion, but forced slavish submission like Pharaoh; the wicked briefly give glory then return to madness. Desire is that stripes compel acknowledgment so fury is restrained.

Shame unto seeking the Name. Calvin hedges: temporary forced confession, not saving change—read with v. 17.

Key contrast Both refuse to read the imprecation as a blank check for private hatred: shame is asked so God's name is honored. Arminians underline Wesley's note—shame that they may own and worship the LORD as only true God—keeping room for acknowledgment that glorifies him. Reformed interpreters usually emphasize Calvin's caution—seeking here may be forced and slavish like Pharaoh, restraining fury rather than proving true repentance—especially beside the forever shame of v. 17. Fair both sides: holy zeal for the Name, no caricature of either camp.

Psalms 83:17

17Let them be disappointed and dismayed forever. Yes, let them be confounded and perish;

Arminian

Let them be disappointed and dismayed forever, confounded and perishing; shared: shame, dismay, and disgrace are prayed against those bent on wiping out Israel.

Forever marks settled judgment on persistent destroyers. Hold this with v. 16's seeking—without softening judicial language into sentiment, and without enjoying ruin.

Reformed

Calvin: ashamed and terrified perpetually; confounded and perish—would not fit if v. 16 were a prayer for their true repentance; reprobate often chastised yet incorrigible.

Forever shame and perish in disgrace. Judicial finality against those who will not cease destroying the Church.

Psalms 83:18

18that they may know that you alone, whose name is the LORD, are the Most High over all the earth.

Arminian

“That they may know that you alone, whose name is the LORD, are the Most High over all the earth.” Shared close: knowledge of Jehovah alone as Most High over all the earth is the goal of the whole plea.

Attributes and covenant Name—LORD / JEHOVAH—stand over every coalition. The end is knowledge of God, not Israel’s bragging rights.

Reformed

Calvin: let them know that thou, thy name Jehovah, thou alone art Most High over all the earth. Judgment serves the confession of God's unique supremacy.

Know the LORD alone, Most High over all the earth. Imprecation ends in theology: God's Name above the nations.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Classic Arminian and Reformed comment on Psalm 83:1–18, in the writers’ own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“Hidden ones—Thy people of Israel, called God's hidden or secret ones, to intimate the respect God has to them as his peculiar treasure. The tabernacles—The people dwelling in them. Ishmaelites—Posterity of Ishmael; others supposed Hagarens from grandmother Hagar. Gebal—An Arabian people on the southern border of Canaan. Of Lot—Moab and Ammon. A wheel—Whereas they promise themselves a sure possession, let them be like a wheel, unstable and soon removed. The mountains—Woods upon the mountains, which in hot countries burn with irresistible violence. May seek—May own and worship thee as the only true God. That men may know that thou, whose name alone is JEHOVAH, art the most high over all the earth.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 83

Reformed Notable voices

“Composed amid dreadful wars—Ammonites and Moabites mustering distant forces against Judea; a prayer full of earnestness: Hold not thy peace; keep not silence; be not still—if God intends succor, haste is needed. Enemies tumultuous; they hate God and lift the head. Crafty design against thy people and hidden ones—God's treasured flock. Come, cut them off from being a nation; erase Israel's name—conspiracy against God himself. Do to them as to Midian, Sisera, Jabin—remember miracles that rescued poor sheep from wolves. Make them like a whirling ball and stubble; pursue with tempest. Fill faces with shame that they may seek thy name—not true repentance, but forced slavish submission like Pharaoh, that fury may be restrained; let them be ashamed and perish, that they may know Jehovah alone is Most High over all the earth.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 83
