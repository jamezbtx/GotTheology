Passage

Psalms 94

Verses 1–23 · ESV · Voices

O God of vengeance, shine forth; rise, Judge of the earth, and repay the proud. How long shall the wicked exult, pour out arrogant words, crush the people, kill widow, sojourner, and fatherless, saying the Lord does not see? Fools are summoned to understand: the Planter of the ear hears; the Former of the eye sees; the Discipliner of nations rebukes; the Lord knows that man's thoughts are breath. Blessed is the one he disciplines and teaches from his law—rest until a pit is dug for the wicked—for he will not forsake his people. Justice will return; the upright follow it. Who rises for me? If the Lord had not helped, the soul would dwell in silence; when the foot slips, steadfast love holds up; when cares multiply, consolations cheer. Wicked rulers framing injustice by statute cannot ally with him. The Lord is stronghold and rock; he will bring back their iniquity and wipe them out.

Psalms 94:1

1O Lord, God of vengeance, O God of vengeance, shine forth!

Arminian

O Lord, God of vengeance, shine forth! Shared: double title; plea that the Avenger appear.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley has no extra lemma on the opening; the plain cry stands—ask the just God to show himself, not private revenge dressed as piety.

Reformed

Calvin: God of vengeances—titles fit the situation; shine forth means some actual manifestation as judge. Not that God needs reminding; the faithful embolden themselves to vehement prayer. When wicked indulge excesses, remember he never ceases to be judge of the earth.

Reformed pastoral: shared—shine forth, God of vengeance. Retributive justice, not a vindictive spirit; put up the Spirit-taught prayer when he seems hidden.

Psalms 94:2

2Rise up, O judge of the earth; repay to the proud what they deserve!

Arminian

Rise up, O judge of the earth; repay the proud what they deserve. Shared: earth-Judge; repayment to pride.

Arminian pastoral: the rise is courtroom, not mob. Deserve names the moral claim without licensing cruelty in the petitioner.

Reformed

Calvin: Lift up thyself, Judge of the earth—render reward to the proud. Ascending the tribunal to punish sin and preserve order. Phraseology is for us who feel overlooked unless he help openly.

Reformed pastoral: shared—rise, judge, repay the proud. Confidence that governance of the world is his office.

Psalms 94:3

3O Lord, how long shall the wicked, how long shall the wicked exult?

Arminian

O Lord, how long shall the wicked exult? Shared: doubled how long; length of triumph.

Arminian pastoral: importunity is justified by duration. The question is prayer, not unbelief's sneer.

Reformed

Calvin: how long twice repeated—impunity granted so long they became shameless. Triumph is boasting exultation of the intoxicated wicked. God may subject the Church to protracted persecution without having forsaken her.

Reformed pastoral: shared—how long the wicked exult. Delay is not desertion; pray through long forbearance.

Psalms 94:4

4They pour out their arrogant words; all the evildoers boast.

Arminian

They pour out arrogant words; evildoers boast. Wesley: Utter—pour forth freely, constantly, abundantly as a fountain; Hard things—insolent, intolerable words against thee and thy people. Shared: fountain of insolence.

Arminian pastoral: name the speech as fountain-abuse. Boast is not strength; it is poured wickedness.

Reformed

Calvin: they pour forth, speak hard things—nabang means rush/boil forth; reckless words under no fear. Workers of iniquity lift themselves; glory in guilt. Pray for help under wild misrule.

Reformed pastoral: shared—arrogant words; boast. Church under misgovernment still cries to God.

Psalms 94:5

5They crush your people, O Lord, and afflict your heritage.

Arminian

They crush your people, O Lord, and afflict your heritage. Shared: people and heritage under the boot.

Arminian pastoral: the injury is against God's own. Heritage language fuels intercession, not despair.

Reformed

Calvin: break in pieces thy people and afflict thy inheritance—intolerable that God's peculiar heritage be trampled. Safety is dear as his workmanship and as his heritage; wronged, betake to God with more confidence.

Reformed pastoral: shared—crush people; afflict heritage. Prefer this prayer when intestine enemies oppress.

Psalms 94:6

6They kill the widow and the sojourner, and murder the fatherless;

Arminian

They kill the widow and the sojourner, and murder the fatherless. Shared: triple assault on the unprotected.

Arminian pastoral: compassion's special objects are named. Cruelty here is contempt of divine authority.

Reformed

Calvin: God commanded equity generally and specially protected orphan, widow, stranger. Cruelty to them is singular impiety; little children even beasts spare—monstrous that men assault them. Alleviate by prayer that God undertake their defense.

Reformed pastoral: shared—widow, sojourner, fatherless murdered. Imitate the Psalmist: pray God to defend them.

Psalms 94:7

7and they say, “The Lord does not see; the God of Jacob does not perceive.”

Arminian

They say the Lord does not see; the God of Jacob does not perceive. Wesley: Of Jacob—he who takes that name but has no regard to his people. Shared: practical atheism of providence.

Arminian pastoral: the taunt empties the covenant name. Answer by faith that Jacob's God does regard his flock.

Reformed

Calvin: not always gross blasphemy in words, but despising judgments as if God took no cognisance. Violence shows brutish security concealing them from the Almighty—virtual denial of providence and of God as judge.

Reformed pastoral: shared—"does not see/perceive." Lowest depravity casts off fear of God.

Psalms 94:8

8Understand, O dullest of the people! Fools, when will you be wise?

Arminian

Understand, O dullest of the people! Fools, when will you be wise? Shared: rebuke of stupid folly among the people.

Arminian pastoral: wisdom's question is urgent. Belonging to Abraham's line makes folly more inexcusable.

Reformed

Calvin: stupid among the people—stronger than mere fools; belonging to Abraham's posterity (Deut 4) aggravates. Or rulers leveled with the herd. When will ye be wise?—obstinate adherence to folly.

Reformed pastoral: shared—understand; fools, when wise? Revelation makes dullness worse, not better.

Psalms 94:9

9He who planted the ear, does he not hear? He who formed the eye, does he not see?

Arminian

He who planted the ear—does he not hear? Who formed the eye—does he not see? Wesley: Planted—excellent structure of the ear; Formed—accurate workmanship of the eye. Shared: Creator argument for cognisance.

Arminian pastoral: design of ear and eye preaches against practical atheism. Do not humanize God with parts; hear and see are his perfect observation.

Reformed

Calvin: if men see and hear by faculties from the Creator, God who formed eye and ear must possess most perfect observation. Argument from order of nature against eluding his view.

Reformed pastoral: shared—Planter hears; Former sees. Creature senses refute the claim that God does not perceive.

Psalms 94:10

10He who disciplines the nations, does he not rebuke? He who teaches man knowledge—

Arminian

He who disciplines the nations—does he not rebuke? Who teaches man knowledge— Wesley: Know—men's thoughts, words, and actions. Shared: from nations' correction to personal rebuke; from teaching to knowing.

Arminian pastoral: the greater-to-less holds. Nations under discipline leave no room for private exemption.

Reformed

Calvin: he that chastises nations, shall he not correct?—from greater to less; or Gentiles punished, then Jews who heard the word all the more. Folly to flatter with impunity when peoples perish. He teaches knowledge—your shrewdness is a drop from his fullness.

Reformed pastoral: shared—disciplines nations; teaches knowledge. Eluding God by craft is madness.

Psalms 94:11

11the Lord—knows the thoughts of man, that they are but a breath.

Arminian

The Lord knows the thoughts of man, that they are but a breath. Wesley: Thoughts—he knoweth secret things; your thoughts, much more practices. Shared: known vanity of human devisings.

Arminian pastoral: breath/vanity undercuts the boast of v. 4. Secret counsel is already weighed.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah knows thoughts of men that they are vain—mists of delusion scatter before the Judge. Wicked cannot deceive God by subtle refuges; they deceive themselves.

Reformed pastoral: shared—knows thoughts; they are breath. Stand before the Judge who stamps vanity on deep counsels.

Psalms 94:12

12Blessed is the man whom you discipline, O Lord, and whom you teach out of your law,

Arminian

Blessed is the man you discipline and teach out of your law. Wesley: Blessed—afflictions accompanied with Divine instructions are great and true blessings. Shared: rod plus law-teaching equals beatitude.

Arminian pastoral: do not call raw pain blessed; call instructed affliction blessed. Receive the law's teaching under the rod.

Reformed

Calvin: blessed whom God instructed and taught out of his law—wisdom for composure under the cross is not natural; it comes from God's school and Scriptures. True patience is cheerful submission based on confidence in grace, not Stoic stubbornness.

Reformed pastoral: shared—discipline; teach from your law. Word and Spirit support under long trouble.

Key contrast Both call the disciplined and law-taught man blessed. Arminians, with Wesley, typically stress that afflictions joined to divine instruction are themselves great blessings to receive. Reformed readers, with Calvin, typically stress the school of the Word: patience under the cross is taught from above so the Church is not swallowed by despair while tyrants misuse law. Shared: rod and teaching together; blessing is not bare pain.

Psalms 94:13

13to give him rest from days of trouble, until a pit is dug for the wicked.

Arminian

To give him rest from days of trouble, until a pit is dug for the wicked. Shared: rest timed with the wicked's pit.

Arminian pastoral: rest is gift in the meantime. The pit's digging is God's work, not the sufferer's project.

Reformed

Calvin: rest from days of evil while the pit is dug—wicked left on earth as a body on a bed till burial. Mount the watchtower; houses of the living are granted to those already devoted to destruction until the grave is ready.

Reformed pastoral: shared—rest; pit for the wicked. Faith waits the issue without envying temporary ease of oppressors.

Psalms 94:14

14For the Lord will not forsake his people; he will not abandon his heritage;

Arminian

For the Lord will not forsake his people; he will not abandon his heritage. Shared: double negation of forsaking.

Arminian pastoral: heritage again (v. 5). Crushing does not equal casting off—cling to the promise.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah will not cast off his people nor forsake his inheritance—gratuitously adopted; he watches the Church as his heritage. Fly to this when affliction suggests desertion.

Reformed pastoral: shared—will not forsake people or heritage. Adoption underwrites patience.

Psalms 94:15

15for justice will return to the righteous, and all the upright in heart will follow it.

Arminian

Justice will return to the righteous; the upright in heart will follow it. Wesley: But—God will declare himself righteous judge and establish justice; Follow—approve and imitate. Shared: justice restored; upright trail it.

Arminian pastoral: return of judgment is public rectitude. Imitate—do not only cheer from the stands.

Reformed

Calvin: judgment will return unto righteousness—government fitted to justice; God brings forth openly the justice that seemed clouded. Upright follow him with greater alacrity when deliverance is seen.

Reformed pastoral: shared—justice returns; upright follow. Confusion ends in ordered righteousness.

Psalms 94:16

16Who rises up for me against the wicked? Who stands up for me against evildoers?

Arminian

Who rises up for me against the wicked? Who stands up against evildoers? Wesley: Rise—defend me; looked hither and thither, none appeared; God alone helped. Shared: human aid absent.

Arminian pastoral: the loneliness is real. Looking around is honest; settling on God alone is faith.

Reformed

Calvin: who will rise/stand for me?—destitute of human aid; cries as if at the moment of danger. Immediately: had not God helped, he must have despaired. Miraculous rescue when deserted magnifies grace.

Reformed pastoral: shared—who rises? who stands? God's single help outranks empty alliances.

Psalms 94:17

17If the Lord had not been my help, my soul would soon have lived in the land of silence.

Arminian

If the Lord had not been my help, my soul would soon have lived in the land of silence. Wesley: Dwelt—place of silence, the grave. Shared: near death-quiet without help.

Arminian pastoral: silence = grave. Help is the difference between living prayer and the pit.

Reformed

Calvin: life dwelling in silence—the dead lie without feeling. No means of preserving life but God's undelayed interposition.

Reformed pastoral: shared—without help, land of silence. Commend kindness that snatches from present death.

Psalms 94:18

18When I thought, “My foot slips,” your steadfast love, O Lord, held me up.

Arminian

When I thought, "My foot slips," your steadfast love held me up. Shared: slipping foot; hesed props.

Arminian pastoral: the inner sentence of falling meets covenant love. Admit the slip-thought; receive the hold.

Reformed

Calvin: if I said, My foot has fallen—thy kindness held me up. Near present death made interposition conspicuous. Sense may say fallen while prayer continues—hope not wholly cut off.

Reformed pastoral: shared—foot slips; steadfast love holds. Mercy props what would otherwise drop.

Psalms 94:19

19When the cares of my heart are many, your consolations cheer my soul.

Arminian

When cares of my heart are many, your consolations cheer my soul. Wesley: Thoughts—various perplexing thoughts; Comforts—promises and remembrance of former care and kindness. Shared: many cares; divine consolations.

Arminian pastoral: pile of cares meets pile of comforts. Remember promises and past kindness under anxiety.

Reformed

Calvin: in multitude of thoughts/anxieties, thy comforts delight—secret joy from above preserves from being swallowed. God enlarges in straits according to magnitude of trial.

Reformed pastoral: shared—many cares; consolations cheer. Two states: fear below, comfort from above.

Psalms 94:20

20Can wicked rulers be allied with you, those who frame injustice by statute?

Arminian

Can wicked rulers be allied with you, who frame injustice by statute? Wesley: Shall—wilt thou take part with unrighteous powers; A law—by unrighteous decrees. Shared: no fellowship with legalized mischief.

Arminian pastoral: statute does not sanctify oppression. Ask the question as confession of God's purity.

Reformed

Calvin: shall throne of iniquities have fellowship with thee, framing molestation for law?—tyrants under color of law, not common robbers. A throne abused by wickedness has nothing in common with God; iniquity never meets his approbation.

Reformed pastoral: shared—no alliance with injustice-by-statute. Legal forms can be tools of oppression; God is not partner.

Psalms 94:21

21They band together against the life of the righteous and condemn the innocent to death.

Arminian

They band together against the life of the righteous and condemn the innocent to death. Shared: conspiracy; innocent blood condemned.

Arminian pastoral: stigma plus sentence. Character assassination rides with legal murder—bring both to God.

Reformed

Calvin: they gather as a troop against the soul of the righteous; condemn innocent blood—leading persons, public convention, college of judges as band of robbers. Double trial: violence and injurious charges. God formerly tried servants thus; learn to bear both.

Reformed pastoral: shared—band against life; condemn innocent. Submissive endurance under unrighteous courts.

Psalms 94:22

22But the Lord has become my stronghold, and my God the rock of my refuge.

Arminian

But the Lord has become my stronghold, and my God the rock of my refuge. Shared: fortress turn; rock of refuge.

Arminian pastoral: but—pivot from statute-thrones to the Rock. Claim the stronghold aloud.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah has been my fortress—sufficient help in God's single protection against force and fury of many. Hide and bid defiance to assault from that height.

Reformed pastoral: shared—stronghold; rock of refuge. One fortress outmatches assembled wickedness.

Psalms 94:23

23He will bring back on them their iniquity and wipe them out for their wickedness; the Lord our God will wipe them out.

Arminian

He will bring back their iniquity and wipe them out; the Lord our God will wipe them out. Shared: recoil of iniquity; doubled wipe-out; our God.

Arminian pastoral: their own wickedness returns. Our God binds the Church to the Judge who finishes the case.

Reformed

Calvin: he shall repay their iniquity and cut them off—evil devised against the people recoils; they fall into their own pit. Said twice: he shall cut them off—the Lord our God shall cut them off. Our God reminds: he will not forget his inheritance.

Reformed pastoral: shared—bring back iniquity; wipe out. Slow hearts need the doubled assurance.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the God of vengeances shining forth, how long the wicked triumph, the ear-Planter who hears, blessed discipline from the law, a people not forsaken, consolations in many cares, and no fellowship with a throne of iniquity—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 94:1–23.

Arminian Notable voices

“Utter—pour forth freely, constantly, abundantly, as a fountain. Hard things—insolent words against thee and thy people. Of Jacob—he who takes that name but has no regard to his people. Planted—excellent structure of the ear; Formed—curious workmanship of the eye. Know—men's thoughts, words, and actions. Thoughts—he knoweth secret things; your thoughts, much more your practices. Blessed—afflictions accompanied with Divine instructions are great blessings. But—God will declare himself a righteous judge and establish justice; Follow—approve and imitate. Rise—defend me; none appeared; God alone helped. Dwelt—place of silence, the grave. Thoughts—perplexing cares; Comforts—promises and remembrance of former kindness. Shall—wilt thou take part with unrighteous powers; A law—by unrighteous decrees.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 94

Reformed Notable voices

“O Jehovah, God of vengeances, shine forth; Judge of the earth, render to the proud. How long shall the wicked triumph—length of impunity hardens audacity. They pour forth hard things; break in pieces thy people; slay widow, stranger, fatherless. They say God shall not see—virtual denial of providence. Understand, ye stupid among the people: he that planted the ear, shall he not hear? He that chastises nations, shall he not correct? Jehovah knows thoughts of men are vanity. Blessed is the man instructed and taught out of thy law—to give rest while the pit is dug for the wicked. Jehovah will not cast off his people. Judgment will return unto righteousness. Who will rise for me? Unless Jehovah had been help, my soul had dwelt in silence. If I said my foot has fallen, thy kindness held me up; in multitude of thoughts thy comforts delight. Shall the throne of iniquities have fellowship with thee, framing mischief by law? Jehovah has been my fortress; he shall repay their iniquity and cut them off.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 94
