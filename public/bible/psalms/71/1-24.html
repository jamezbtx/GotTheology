Passage

Psalms 71

Verses 1–24 · ESV · Voices

An aged believer’s plea: refuge and rock from youth through gray hairs; enemies say God has forsaken him; he asks not to be cast off in old age, vows endless praise of mighty deeds, and looks to be revived to tell the next generation—tongue talking of God’s righteousness all the day.

Psalms 71:1

1In you, O LORD, do I take refuge; let me never be put to shame!

Arminian

Wesley: Confounded—ashamed of vain hopes. Refuge in the LORD; never-shame is the ask.

Arminian pastoral: taking refuge is active trust. Grace invites the flight; the believer must still hide in God rather than in self.

Reformed

Calvin: flees to God as refuge; prays never to be ashamed. Old-age faith begins where all faith begins—in God.

Reformed accent: shared—refuge first. Shame’s threat is answered by where one hides.

Psalms 71:2

2In your righteousness deliver me and rescue me; incline your ear to me, and save me!

Arminian

Wesley: deliverance asked in God’s righteousness; ear inclined; save.

Arminian note: righteousness here is God’s faithful saving character. Shared: rescue is begged, not earned by the aged saint’s résumé.

Reformed

Calvin: deliver in thy righteousness—rescue grounded in God’s right dealing and promise.

Reformed pastoral: shared—ear incline and save are mercy’s verbs.

Psalms 71:3

3Be to me a rock of refuge, to which I may continually come; you have given the command to save me, for you are my rock and my fortress.

Arminian

Wesley: Strong habitation—rock where I may safely continually dwell; Given charge—commanded care to save.

Arminian hope: continually come—access is ongoing. Shared: rock and fortress are God’s; the saint keeps coming.

Reformed

Calvin: rock of habitation to which he may continually resort; God has commanded salvation. Fortress titles pile up.

Reformed note: shared—continual coming rests on commanded care.

Psalms 71:4

4Rescue me, O my God, from the hand of the wicked, from the grasp of the unjust and cruel man.

Arminian

Wesley: rescue from wicked, unjust, cruel grasp. Enemies are named by character.

Arminian pastoral: specific evils drive specific prayer. Shared seriousness about cruel hands.

Reformed

Calvin: deliver from the wicked’s hand, from unjust and cruel. God is my God in the plea.

Reformed accent: shared—cruelty is not imagined; it is prayed against.

Psalms 71:5

5For you, O Lord, are my hope, my trust, O LORD, from my youth.

Arminian

Wesley: From my youth—earliest days. Hope and trust are lifelong habits named.

Arminian note: youth-trust remembered. Shared: early faith is gift to be continued, not a phase to outgrow.

Reformed

Calvin: trust from youth—long memory of grace. Hope is the Lord himself.

Reformed pastoral: shared biography of trust underwriting old-age plea.

Psalms 71:6

6Upon you I have leaned from before my birth; you are he who took me from my mother’s womb. My praise is continually of you.

Arminian

Wesley: prenatal lean; took from womb; praise continual. Dependence pre-dates memory.

Arminian hearing: shared—life from the womb is God’s taking. Praise continual is the fitting answer; it must still be offered.

Reformed

Calvin: leaned from the womb; he that took him from his mother’s bowels. Continual praise.

Reformed accent: shared—existence itself is borrowed from God.

Psalms 71:7

7I have been as a portent to many, but you are my strong refuge.

Arminian

Wesley: Marvelous—wonderful as a sign to many; yet God is strong refuge.

Arminian pastoral: being a ‘portent’ can mean startling suffering or signal grace. Shared: refuge holds when public gaze is heavy.

Reformed

Calvin: as a wonder unto many—his trials made him a spectacle; God remains strong refuge.

Reformed note: shared—spectacle-life still hides in God.

Psalms 71:8

8My mouth is filled with your praise, and with your glory all the day.

Arminian

Wesley: mouth filled with praise and glory all day—speech occupation.

Arminian note: filled mouth is chosen doxology. Grace fills; the mouth still speaks glory.

Reformed

Calvin: mouth filled with praise and honor all the day. Old lips busy with God.

Reformed pastoral: shared—all-day glory speech.

Psalms 71:9

9Do not cast me off in the time of old age; forsake me not when my strength is spent.

Arminian

Wesley: Cast me not off—do not reject; Old age—when most feeble. Strength spent is the crisis.

Arminian pastoral: fear of cast-off in weakness is real prayer. Shared: aging saints may ask this without shame; grace meets spent strength.

Reformed

Calvin: cast me not off in old age; forsake not when strength fails. Enemies will soon say God forsook him—hence the urgency.

Reformed accent: shared—spent strength is argument for nearness, not proof of abandonment.

Key contrast Both plead not to be cast off when strength fails. Arminians typically stress that the aging believer must keep seeking refuge; Reformed readers typically stress God’s lifelong care that does not track bodily vigor.

Psalms 71:10

10For my enemies speak concerning me; those who watch for my life consult together

Arminian

Wesley: enemies talk; life-watchers consult. Conspiracy around the frail.

Arminian hearing: shared—watchers for life are cruel. Prayer names the consult.

Reformed

Calvin: enemies speak against him; they that watch for his soul take counsel together.

Reformed note: shared—consultation of predators around the weak.

Psalms 71:11

11and say, “God has forsaken him; pursue and seize him, for there is none to deliver him.”

Arminian

Wesley: Forsaken—so they persuade themselves; none to deliver—their verdict.

Arminian pastoral: the lie ‘God has forsaken’ must be answered by v. 9’s prayer. Shared: enemies theologize falsely to justify chase.

Reformed

Calvin: they say God has forsaken him—pursue; none to deliver. Blasphemous pastoral diagnosis from foes.

Reformed accent: shared—forsaken-talk is enemy propaganda, not God’s last word.

Psalms 71:12

12O God, be not far from me; O my God, make haste to help me!

Arminian

Wesley: nearness and haste begged—echo of Psalm 70’s urgency.

Arminian note: be-not-far and make-haste are twin asks. Grace invites urgent nearness-seeking.

Reformed

Calvin: be not far; make haste to help. Distance feared; speed desired.

Reformed pastoral: shared—my God personalizes the haste.

Psalms 71:13

13May my accusers be put to shame and consumed; with scorn and disgrace may they be covered who seek my hurt!

Arminian

Wesley: shame, consume, scorn, disgrace on hurt-seekers. Imprecation measured to malice.

Arminian hearing: shared seriousness—accusers covered with scorn. Not a grin; a justice plea.

Reformed

Calvin: ashamed and consumed; covered with reproach and dishonor who seek hurt.

Reformed note: shared—hurt-seekers meet judicial shame.

Psalms 71:14

14But I will hope continually and will praise you yet more and more.

Arminian

Wesley: Will hope—still; praise yet more and more. Hope and escalating praise.

Arminian pastoral: continually hope is resolve under grace. Shared: more-and-more praise is the But against v. 11’s lie.

Reformed

Calvin: but I will hope continually; praise more and more. Faith’s counter-speech.

Reformed accent: shared—hope continual is gift exercised.

Psalms 71:15

15My mouth will tell of your righteous acts, of your deeds of salvation all the day, for their number is past my knowledge.

Arminian

Wesley: tell righteousness and salvation all day; number past knowledge—uncountable deeds.

Arminian note: telling is witness. Shared: past-knowledge abundance silences self-credit.

Reformed

Calvin: mouth tells righteousness and salvation all day; numbers beyond counting.

Reformed pastoral: shared—inexhaustible deeds fuel inexhaustible speech.

Psalms 71:16

16With the mighty deeds of the Lord GOD I will come; I will remind them of your righteousness, yours alone.

Arminian

Wesley: Go—walk on; remind of righteousness, yours alone. Solo credit to God.

Arminian hearing: come with mighty deeds—enter life’s scenes carrying God’s acts. Shared: righteousness yours alone blocks boasting.

Reformed

Calvin: go in the strength of the Lord GOD; make mention of thy righteousness, even of thine only.

Reformed accent: shared monergism of righteousness mentioned—thine only.

Psalms 71:17

17O God, from my youth you have taught me, and I still proclaim your wondrous deeds.

Arminian

Wesley: taught from youth; still proclaim wondrous deeds. Pedagogy and proclamation linked.

Arminian pastoral: taught-yet-proclaim—learning becomes speech. Shared: lifelong curriculum of wonders.

Reformed

Calvin: taught from youth; hitherto declared wondrous works. Continuity of disciple-witness.

Reformed note: shared—God the teacher from youth.

Psalms 71:18

18So even to old age and gray hairs, O God, do not forsake me, until I proclaim your might to another generation, your power to all those to come.

Arminian

Wesley: gray hairs plea; until he proclaims might to another generation. Purpose clause: teaching the next.

Arminian hope: forsake-not until witness reaches the next generation. Shared mission in old age—power told to those to come.

Reformed

Calvin: to old age and gray hairs forsake not—until he declares strength to the next generation.

Reformed pastoral: shared—longevity for proclamation, not for ego.

Key contrast Both tie gray-hair perseverance to proclaiming God’s might to the next generation. Arminians typically stress the aged saint’s ongoing duty to tell; Reformed readers typically stress God’s non-forsaking that enables that witness.

Psalms 71:19

19Your righteousness, O God, reaches the high heavens. You who have done great things, O God, who is like you?

Arminian

Wesley: righteousness to high heavens; great things; who is like you—incomparability.

Arminian note: shared doxology—none like God. Great things done ground the who-is-like.

Reformed

Calvin: righteousness very high; great things done; who is like unto thee?

Reformed accent: shared—incomparability of the God of great things.

Psalms 71:20

20You who have made me see many troubles and calamities will revive me again; from the depths of the earth you will bring me up again.

Arminian

Wesley: many troubles seen; revive again; bring up from depths—restoration hope.

Arminian pastoral: God who showed troubles also revives. Shared: depths are not final; bring-up-again is trusted.

Reformed

Calvin: shown many and sore troubles—shall quicken again; bring up from depths of the earth.

Reformed note: shared—same God of trouble and revival.

Psalms 71:21

21You will increase my greatness and comfort me again.

Arminian

Wesley: increase greatness; comfort again—renewed consolation.

Arminian hearing: comfort again matches revive again. Shared: comfort is God’s to give the gray-haired.

Reformed

Calvin: increase greatness; comfort on every side. Restoration language.

Reformed pastoral: shared—comfort again after calamity.

Psalms 71:22

22I will also praise you with the harp for your faithfulness, O my God; I will sing praises to you with the lyre, O Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

Wesley: With the harp—musical praise for faithfulness; Holy One of Israel named.

Arminian note: instruments join vow. Shared: faithfulness and holiness are the themes sung.

Reformed

Calvin: praise with psaltery for truth; sing with harp, O Holy One of Israel.

Reformed accent: shared—Holy One receives lyre and harp.

Psalms 71:23

23My lips will shout for joy, when I sing praises to you; my soul also, which you have redeemed.

Arminian

Wesley: My soul—which thou hast delivered—lips shout; redeemed soul sings.

Arminian pastoral: redeemed soul’s joy is personal. Shared: redemption fuels shouting lips.

Reformed

Calvin: lips shout; soul which thou hast redeemed. Whole person in praise.

Reformed note: shared—redeemed soul is the singer’s identity.

Psalms 71:24

24And my tongue will talk of your righteous help all the day long, for they have been put to shame and disappointed who sought to do me hurt.

Arminian

Wesley: Tongue continually celebrating righteousness; enemies shamed who sought hurt.

Arminian hope: all-day talk of righteous help. Shared ending—hurt-seekers disappointed; tongue occupied with God.

Reformed

Calvin: tongue talks of righteousness all day; they are ashamed who sought hurt. Close matches opening shame-theme.

Reformed pastoral: shared—shame flipped from the refugee (v. 1) onto the hunters.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on lifelong trust from youth to gray hairs, the fear of being cast off in old age, and praise that teaches the next generation—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 71.

Arminian Notable voices

“Confounded—ashamed of their vain hopes. Strong habitation—a rock of habitation, where I may safely and continually dwell. Given charge—commanded and by effectual care ordered. From my youth—from my earliest days. Marvelous—wonderful both for variety and greatness. Cast me not off—do not reject me. Old age—when I am most feeble. Forsaken—so they persuade themselves. God of my praise—the object and matter of my praise. Will hope—I will still hope. Go—walk on in my course. With the harp—I will praise thee with musical instruments. My soul—which thou hast delivered. Tongue—my tongue shall be continually employed in celebrating thy righteousness.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 71)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Psalmist, now advanced in years, flees to God as his rock and fortress, recalling that God has been his trust from youth and even from the womb… He prays that God would not cast him off in the time of old age, when his strength fails, especially as enemies conclude that God has forsaken him… He vows to proclaim God’s wondrous deeds to the next generation and to talk of his righteousness all the day. Faith in old age rests on a long memory of grace and on God’s righteousness, not on remaining bodily strength.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 71)

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
