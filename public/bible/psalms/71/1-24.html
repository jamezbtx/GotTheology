Passage

Psalms 71

Verses 1–24 · WEB · Voices

An aged believer’s plea: refuge and rock from youth through gray hairs; enemies say God has forsaken him; he asks not to be cast off in old age, vows endless praise of mighty deeds, and looks to be revived to tell the next generation—tongue talking of God’s righteousness all the day.

Psalms 71:1

1In you, LORD, I take refuge. Never let me be disappointed.

Arminian

Wesley: Confounded—ashamed of vain hopes. Refuge in the LORD; never-shame is the ask.

Taking refuge is active trust. Grace invites the flight; the believer must still hide in God rather than in self.

Reformed

Calvin: flees to God as refuge; prays never to be ashamed. Old-age faith begins where all faith begins—in God.

Both readings hold: Refuge first. Shame’s threat is answered by where one hides.

Psalms 71:2

2Deliver me in your righteousness, and rescue me. Turn your ear to me, and save me.

Arminian

Wesley: deliverance asked in God’s righteousness; ear inclined; save.

Righteousness here is God’s faithful saving character. Both agree: rescue is begged, not earned by the aged saint’s résumé.

Reformed

Calvin: deliver in thy righteousness—rescue grounded in God’s right dealing and promise.

Ear incline and save are mercy’s verbs.

Psalms 71:3

3Be to me a rock of refuge to which I may always go. Give the command to save me, for you are my rock and my fortress.

Arminian

Wesley: Strong habitation—rock where I may safely continually dwell; Given charge—commanded care to save.

Continually come—access is ongoing. Agreed: rock and fortress are God’s; the saint keeps coming.

Reformed

Calvin: rock of habitation to which he may continually resort; God has commanded salvation. Fortress titles pile up.

Continual coming rests on commanded care.

Psalms 71:4

4Rescue me, my God, from the hand of the wicked, from the hand of the unrighteous and cruel man.

Arminian

Wesley: rescue from wicked, unjust, cruel grasp. Enemies are named by character.

Specific evils drive specific prayer. Shared seriousness about cruel hands.

Reformed

Calvin: deliver from the wicked’s hand, from unjust and cruel. God is my God in the plea.

Cruelty is not imagined; it is prayed against.

Psalms 71:5

5For you are my hope, Lord GOD, my confidence from my youth.

Arminian

Wesley: From my youth—earliest days. Hope and trust are lifelong habits named.

Youth-trust remembered. Early faith is gift to be continued, not a phase to outgrow.

Reformed

Calvin: trust from youth—long memory of grace. Hope is the Lord himself.

Shared biography of trust underwriting old-age plea.

Psalms 71:6

6I have relied on you from the womb. You are he who took me out of my mother’s womb. I will always praise you.

Arminian

Wesley: prenatal lean; took from womb; praise continual. Dependence pre-dates memory.

Life from the womb is God’s taking. Praise continual is the fitting answer; it must still be offered.

Reformed

Calvin: leaned from the womb; he that took him from his mother’s bowels. Continual praise.

Existence itself is borrowed from God.

Psalms 71:7

7I am a marvel to many, but you are my strong refuge.

Arminian

Wesley: Marvelous—wonderful as a sign to many; yet God is strong refuge.

Being a ‘portent’ can mean startling suffering or signal grace. Refuge holds when public gaze is heavy.

Reformed

Calvin: as a wonder unto many—his trials made him a spectacle; God remains strong refuge.

Spectacle-life still hides in God.

Psalms 71:8

8My mouth shall be filled with your praise, with your honor all day long.

Arminian

Wesley: mouth filled with praise and glory all day—speech occupation.

Filled mouth is chosen doxology. Grace fills; the mouth still speaks glory.

Reformed

Calvin: mouth filled with praise and honor all the day. Old lips busy with God.

He wants to speak of God’s glory all day long.

Psalms 71:9

9Don’t reject me in my old age. Don’t forsake me when my strength fails.

Arminian

Wesley: Cast me not off—do not reject; Old age—when most feeble. Strength spent is the crisis.

Fear of cast-off in weakness is prayer. Aging saints may ask this without shame; grace meets spent strength.

Reformed

Calvin: cast me not off in old age; forsake not when strength fails. Enemies will soon say God forsook him—hence the urgency.

Spent strength is argument for nearness, not proof of abandonment.

Key contrast Both plead not to be cast off when strength fails. Arminians emphasize that the aging believer must keep seeking refuge; Reformed commentators typically stress God’s lifelong care that does not track bodily vigor.

Psalms 71:10

10For my enemies talk about me. Those who watch for my soul conspire together,

Arminian

Wesley: enemies talk; life-watchers consult. Conspiracy around the frail.

Watchers for life are cruel. Prayer names the consult.

Reformed

Calvin: enemies speak against him; they that watch for his soul take counsel together.

Consultation of predators around the weak.

Psalms 71:11

11saying, “God has forsaken him. Pursue and take him, for no one will rescue him.”

Arminian

Wesley: Forsaken—so they persuade themselves; none to deliver—their verdict.

The lie ‘God has forsaken’ must be answered by v. 9’s prayer. Enemies theologize falsely to justify chase.

Reformed

Calvin: they say God has forsaken him—pursue; none to deliver. Blasphemous pastoral diagnosis from foes.

Forsaken-talk is enemy propaganda, not God’s last word.

Psalms 71:12

12God, don’t be far from me. My God, hurry to help me.

Arminian

Wesley: nearness and haste begged—echo of Psalm 70’s urgency.

Be-not-far and make-haste are twin asks. Grace invites urgent nearness-seeking.

Reformed

Calvin: be not far; make haste to help. Distance feared; speed desired.

Both hear: My God personalizes the haste.

Psalms 71:13

13Let my accusers be disappointed and consumed. Let them be covered with disgrace and scorn who want to harm me.

Arminian

Wesley: shame, consume, scorn, disgrace on hurt-seekers. Imprecation measured to malice.

Shared seriousness—accusers covered with scorn. Not a grin; a justice plea.

Reformed

Calvin: ashamed and consumed; covered with reproach and dishonor who seek hurt.

Hurt-seekers meet judicial shame.

Psalms 71:14

14But I will always hope, and will add to all of your praise.

Arminian

Wesley: Will hope—still; praise yet more and more. Hope and escalating praise.

Continually hope is resolve under grace. Both see: more-and-more praise is the But against v. 11’s lie.

Reformed

Calvin: but I will hope continually; praise more and more. Faith’s counter-speech.

Hope continual is gift exercised.

Psalms 71:15

15My mouth will tell about your righteousness, and of your salvation all day, though I don’t know its full measure.

Arminian

Wesley: tell righteousness and salvation all day; number past knowledge—uncountable deeds.

Telling is witness. Past-knowledge abundance silences self-credit.

Reformed

Calvin: mouth tells righteousness and salvation all day; numbers beyond counting.

Inexhaustible deeds fuel inexhaustible speech.

Psalms 71:16

16I will come with the mighty acts of the Lord GOD. I will make mention of your righteousness, even of yours alone.

Arminian

Wesley: Go—walk on; remind of righteousness, yours alone. Solo credit to God.

Come with mighty deeds—enter life’s scenes carrying God’s acts. Righteousness yours alone blocks boasting.

Reformed

Calvin: go in the strength of the Lord GOD; make mention of thy righteousness, even of thine only.

Shared monergism of righteousness mentioned—thine only.

Psalms 71:17

17God, you have taught me from my youth. Until now, I have declared your wondrous works.

Arminian

Wesley: taught from youth; still proclaim wondrous deeds. Pedagogy and proclamation linked.

Practically: taught-yet-proclaim—learning becomes speech. Lifelong curriculum of wonders.

Reformed

Calvin: taught from youth; hitherto declared wondrous works. Continuity of disciple-witness.

God the teacher from youth.

Psalms 71:18

18Yes, even when I am old and gray-haired, God, don’t forsake me, until I have declared your strength to the next generation, your might to everyone who is to come.

Arminian

Wesley: gray hairs plea; until he proclaims might to another generation. Purpose clause: teaching the next.

Forsake-not until witness reaches the next generation. Shared mission in old age—power told to those to come.

Reformed

Calvin: to old age and gray hairs forsake not—until he declares strength to the next generation.

Longevity for proclamation, not for ego.

Key contrast Both tie gray-hair perseverance to proclaiming God’s might to the next generation. Arminians underline the aged saint’s ongoing duty to tell; Reformed interpreters underline God’s non-forsaking that enables that witness.

Psalms 71:19

19God, your righteousness also reaches to the heavens. You have done great things. God, who is like you?

Arminian

Wesley: righteousness to high heavens; great things; who is like you—incomparability.

Shared doxology—none like God. Great things done ground the who-is-like.

Reformed

Calvin: righteousness very high; great things done; who is like unto thee?

Incomparability of the God of great things.

Psalms 71:20

20You, who have shown us many and bitter troubles, you will let me live. You will bring us up again from the depths of the earth.

Arminian

Wesley: many troubles seen; revive again; bring up from depths—restoration hope.

God who showed troubles also revives. Depths are not final; bring-up-again is trusted.

Reformed

Calvin: shown many and sore troubles—shall quicken again; bring up from depths of the earth.

Same God of trouble and revival.

Psalms 71:21

21Increase my honor and comfort me again.

Arminian

Wesley: increase greatness; comfort again—renewed consolation.

Comfort again matches revive again. Undisputed: comfort is God’s to give the gray-haired.

Reformed

Calvin: increase greatness; comfort on every side. Restoration language.

He asks to be comforted again after calamity.

Psalms 71:22

22I will also praise you with the harp for your faithfulness, my God. I sing praises to you with the lyre, Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

Wesley: With the harp—musical praise for faithfulness; Holy One of Israel named.

Instruments join vow. No dispute here: faithfulness and holiness are the themes sung.

Reformed

Calvin: praise with psaltery for truth; sing with harp, O Holy One of Israel.

Holy One receives lyre and harp.

Psalms 71:23

23My lips shall shout for joy! My soul, which you have redeemed, sings praises to you!

Arminian

Wesley: My soul—which thou hast delivered—lips shout; redeemed soul sings.

Redeemed soul’s joy is personal. Both affirm: redemption fuels shouting lips.

Reformed

Calvin: lips shout; soul which thou hast redeemed. Whole person in praise.

Redeemed soul is the singer’s identity.

Psalms 71:24

24My tongue will also talk about your righteousness all day long, for they are disappointed, and they are confounded, who want to harm me.

Arminian

Wesley: Tongue continually celebrating righteousness; enemies shamed who sought hurt.

All-day talk of righteous help. Shared ending—hurt-seekers disappointed; tongue occupied with God.

Reformed

Calvin: tongue talks of righteousness all day; they are ashamed who sought hurt. Close matches opening shame-theme.

Shame flipped from the refugee (v. 1) onto the hunters.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Classic Arminian and Reformed comment on Psalm 71, in the writers’ own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“Confounded—ashamed of their vain hopes. Strong habitation—a rock of habitation, where I may safely and continually dwell. Given charge—commanded and by effectual care ordered. From my youth—from my earliest days. Marvelous—wonderful both for variety and greatness. Cast me not off—do not reject me. Old age—when I am most feeble. Forsaken—so they persuade themselves. God of my praise—the object and matter of my praise. Will hope—I will still hope. Go—walk on in my course. With the harp—I will praise thee with musical instruments. My soul—which thou hast delivered. Tongue—my tongue shall be continually employed in celebrating thy righteousness.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 71)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Psalmist, now advanced in years, flees to God as his rock and fortress, recalling that God has been his trust from youth and even from the womb… He prays that God would not cast him off in the time of old age, when his strength fails, especially as enemies conclude that God has forsaken him… He vows to proclaim God’s wondrous deeds to the next generation and to talk of his righteousness all the day. Faith in old age rests on a long memory of grace and on God’s righteousness, not on remaining bodily strength.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 71)

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
