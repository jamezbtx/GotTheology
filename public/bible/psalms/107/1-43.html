Passage

Psalms 107

Verses 1–43 · ESV · Voices

The redeemed are called to give thanks for the Lord's goodness and everlasting steadfast love: wanderers in desert wastes cry and are led to a city; prisoners in darkness who rebelled are freed as bronze doors shatter; fools sick from sin are healed by His sent word; sailors at their wits' end see the storm stilled and reach their haven—each group urged to thank Him for steadfast love and wondrous works. He turns rivers to desert for the evil of inhabitants and deserts to pools for the hungry; He brings princes low and raises the needy; the upright rejoice, wickedness is silenced, and the wise consider His steadfast love.

Psalms 107:1

1Oh give thanks to the Lord, for he is good, for his steadfast love endures forever!

Arminian

Oh give thanks to the Lord, for He is good; His steadfast love endures forever! Wesley opens the psalm as God's providential care for people in distress who cry to Him and are helped.

Both camps: praise frames every deliverance that follows. Classical Arminians hear a sincere call to thank the God whose love endures—mercy that invites the redeemed to speak.

Reformed

Calvin: this verse, also opening the previous psalm, was often answered in chorus—Praise Jehovah, because He is good. The penman inserts thanksgiving where Israel so often began.

Reformed accent: steadfast love forever grounds gratitude before any story of rescue. Shared: give thanks; He is good; love endures.

Psalms 107:2

2Let the redeemed of the Lord say so, whom he has redeemed from trouble

Arminian

Let the redeemed of the Lord say so, whom He has redeemed from trouble. The rescued are not to stay silent; redemption calls for confession of the Deliverer.

Arminian pastoral: "say so" is real invitation—those God has redeemed from trouble are urged to testify. Classical Arminians hear redeeming love that sincerely summons the freed to speak.

Reformed

Calvin: let the redeemed of Jehovah who have returned from captivity come forth and publish His loving-kindness. They are redeemed because God's outstretched hand was their guard; wanderings were not chance.

Reformed confession: redeemed of the Lord name those God has actually brought out. Shared: redeemed; say so; from trouble.

Key contrast Both confess the redeemed must speak of the Lord's rescue. Arminians typically hear a broad summons to all whom God has delivered from trouble to testify. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's particular redeemed—those God has actually brought home under His providence—commanded to publish His loving-kindness.

Psalms 107:3

3and gathered in from the lands, from the east and from the west, from the north and from the south.

Arminian

And gathered in from the lands, from east and west, north and south. Wesley: Gathered—into their own land.

Shared: gathering is God's work across the compass. Classical Arminians hear a God who sincerely gathers the scattered and calls them home.

Reformed

Calvin: whom He has gathered out of the lands from every quarter. Not casual travel but return under providence from exile and danger.

Reformed comfort: gathering proves the Redeemer's hand. Shared: gathered from the lands; every direction.

Psalms 107:4

4Some wandered in desert wastes, finding no way to a city to dwell in;

Arminian

Some wandered in desert wastes, finding no way to a city to dwell in. Wesley: No city—no town inhabited where they might refresh themselves.

Both camps: homelessness without a path is real misery. Classical Arminians preach a God who sees wanderers and later leads them straight.

Reformed

Calvin: they wandered in the solitary desert and found no city of habitation—lonely places where hunger and thirst threaten death unless the Lord rescue.

Reformed accent: desert as stage for providence, not fortune. Shared: wandered; no city to dwell in.

Psalms 107:5

5hungry and thirsty, their soul fainted within them.

Arminian

Hungry and thirsty, their soul fainted within them.

Shared: bodily need empties the soul. Classical Arminians hear faintness that drives sincere cry—need that grace meets without mocking the hungry.

Reformed

Calvin: hungry and thirsty until the soul faints—hourly hazard of death in the desert unless God interposes.

Reformed pastoral: extremity prepares the cry of v. 6. Shared: hungry; thirsty; soul fainted.

Psalms 107:6

6Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and he delivered them from their distress.

Arminian

Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and He delivered them from their distress. Wesley: The Lord—unto Jehovah, the true God; even heathens had some knowledge of Him.

Arminian hearing: cry and deliverance are linked—prayer answered. Classical Arminians glory that God helps those in distress in answer to their prayers.

Reformed

Calvin: in straits they called upon Jehovah. The verbs show continued action: when hope fails, then they cry. God often delivers even those whose hope does not center on Him, constraining acknowledgment that help is from Him alone.

Reformed accent: cry may be instinct under pressure; deliverance still from God. Shared: cried; delivered from distress.

Key contrast Both confess that people cry in trouble and God delivers. Arminians typically emphasize sincere prayer God answers and invites. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's note: even those without settled piety are constrained to call, yet deliverance still proves God alone saves.

Psalms 107:7

7He led them by a straight way till they reached a city to dwell in.

Arminian

He led them by a straight way till they reached a city to dwell in. Wesley: Forth—out of the wilderness.

Shared: God leads; the city is gift. Classical Arminians preach guidance as real kindness toward the lost who cried.

Reformed

Calvin: by God's hand they were led into the right way to a place of lodging. He connects mercy with wondrous works so silence would be impious.

Reformed confession: straight way is providence, not luck. Shared: led; straight way; city to dwell in.

Psalms 107:8

8Let them thank the Lord for his steadfast love, for his wondrous works to the children of man!

Arminian

Let them thank the Lord for His steadfast love, for His wondrous works to the children of man!

Both camps: thanksgiving is owed. Classical Arminians hear "children of man" as wide horizon—works that invite all who see them to praise.

Reformed

Calvin: let them celebrate His mercy and marvelous works before the sons of men. To remain silent after such deliverance would obscure God's glory.

Reformed pastoral: ingratitude after rescue is wickedness. Shared: thank; steadfast love; wondrous works.

Psalms 107:9

9For he satisfies the longing soul, and the hungry soul he fills with good things.

Arminian

For He satisfies the longing soul, and the hungry soul He fills with good things.

Shared: longing and hunger met with good. Classical Arminians preach a God who fills—not with empty promise but with real good for the soul that cries.

Reformed

Calvin: He has satisfied the longing soul and filled the famished with good. The reflective line seals why thanks is due.

Reformed comfort: satisfaction is God's verb. Shared: satisfies; fills with good things.

Psalms 107:10

10Some sat in darkness and in the shadow of death, prisoners in affliction and in irons,

Arminian

Some sat in darkness and in the shadow of death, prisoners in affliction and in irons. Wesley: Darkness—in dark prisons or dungeons.

Both camps: bondage named without soft edges. Classical Arminians hear prisoners whose later cry God still hears.

Reformed

Calvin: another species of danger—darkness and shadow of death, bound in affliction and iron. Afflictions are God's rod; remedy is in His grace alone, not chance.

Reformed accent: captivity as judgment and stage for deliverance. Shared: darkness; shadow of death; irons.

Psalms 107:11

11for they had rebelled against the words of God, and spurned the counsel of the Most High.

Arminian

For they had rebelled against the words of God, and spurned the counsel of the Most High.

Arminian pastoral: rebellion and contempt of counsel are real sins under light. Classical Arminians preach spurning as willful resistance—counsel rejected, not never offered.

Reformed

Calvin: afflictions do not happen by chance. He charges not mere sin but rebellion against God's word—best regulation of life is prompt obedience. Until people see troubles as God's appointment, they will not ask Him for deliverance.

Reformed confession: rebellion explains the irons. Shared: rebelled; spurned counsel.

Key contrast Both confess rebellion against God's words and counsel as the cause of bondage. Arminians typically stress willful spurning of counsel sincerely given. Reformed readers typically stress Calvin's point: all such trouble is by God's appointment, driving the guilty to cry to the only Deliverer.

Psalms 107:12

12So he bowed their hearts down with hard labor; they fell down, with none to help.

Arminian

So He bowed their hearts down with hard labor; they fell down, with none to help. Wesley: Heart—the pride and obstinacy of their hearts; Fell—into hopeless miseries.

Shared: pride brought low; no human helper. Classical Arminians hear labor that humbles so the cry of v. 13 may be sincere.

Reformed

Calvin: hard labor bows the heart; they fall with none to help—hopeless until God acts. Brazen gates and iron bars (v. 16) will enhance the later benefit.

Reformed pastoral: extremity strips false helpers. Shared: bowed down; fell; none to help.

Psalms 107:13

13Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and he delivered them from their distress.

Arminian

Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and He delivered them from their distress.

Both camps: same refrain as the desert cry. Classical Arminians hear repeated mercy—God still answers prisoners who call.

Reformed

Calvin: again they cry and He delivers. Sighs and cries testify against later ingratitude—when in straits they confess God as deliverer; peace must not erase that confession.

Reformed warning: remember the cry after the rescue. Shared: cried; delivered.

Psalms 107:14

14He brought them out of darkness and the shadow of death, and burst their bonds apart.

Arminian

He brought them out of darkness and the shadow of death, and burst their bonds apart.

Shared: out of darkness; bonds broken. Classical Arminians preach liberation as pattern of gospel freedom—bonds that God alone bursts.

Reformed

Calvin: brought out of darkness and shadow of death; bands broken. Deliverance from perpetual slavery magnifies grace.

Reformed comfort: God breaks what humans cannot. Shared: brought out; burst bonds.

Psalms 107:15

15Let them thank the Lord for his steadfast love, for his wondrous works to the children of man!

Arminian

Let them thank the Lord for His steadfast love, for His wondrous works to the children of man!

Both camps: refrain of thanks after prison-rescue. Classical Arminians urge the freed not to mute the praise due.

Reformed

Calvin: again the chorus—celebrate mercy and marvelous works. Ingratitude after wonder is charged as forgetting the Deliverer.

Reformed accent: thanks is duty of the released. Shared: thank; steadfast love; wondrous works.

Psalms 107:16

16For he shatters the doors of bronze and cuts in two the bars of iron.

Arminian

For He shatters the doors of bronze and cuts in two the bars of iron.

Shared: bronze and iron yield to God. Classical Arminians hear strength that frees—no gate too strong for the Lord who answers cry.

Reformed

Calvin: brazen gates and iron bars named to enhance the benefit—chains of perpetual slavery broken asunder.

Reformed confession: only God shatters such doors. Shared: shatters bronze; cuts iron bars.

Psalms 107:17

17Some were fools through their sinful ways, and because of their iniquities suffered affliction;

Arminian

Some were fools through their sinful ways, and because of their iniquities suffered affliction. Wesley: Afflicted—with sickness.

Arminian pastoral: folly is moral, not merely mental—sinful ways bring affliction. Classical Arminians warn that chosen paths of sin still meet a God who can heal when they cry.

Reformed

Calvin: fools afflicted for the way of their transgression. Fools are those who, giving themselves to sensuality, bring destruction; fear of God is wisdom, so shaking off His yoke is folly and fury.

Reformed accent: fatal diseases make grace conspicuous when deliverance comes. Shared: fools; sinful ways; affliction.

Psalms 107:18

18they loathed any kind of food, and they drew near to the gates of death.

Arminian

They loathed any kind of food, and they drew near to the gates of death.

Shared: appetite gone; death's gates near. Classical Arminians hear extremity that still leaves room for the cry of v. 19.

Reformed

Calvin: they loathe all food and approach the gates of death—not slight illness but cases where hope is abandoned, so grace shines when life returns.

Reformed pastoral: near-death frames the word that heals. Shared: loathed food; gates of death.

Psalms 107:19

19Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and he delivered them from their distress.

Arminian

Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and He delivered them from their distress.

Both camps: third cry-and-deliver refrain. Classical Arminians preach that sickness under sin does not bar sincere calling on the Lord.

Reformed

Calvin: they call when reduced to straits, acknowledging they would be undone without His wonderful help.

Reformed comfort: cry in illness still reaches God. Shared: cried; delivered.

Psalms 107:20

20He sent out his word and healed them, and delivered them from their destruction.

Arminian

He sent out His word and healed them, and delivered them from their destruction. Wesley: Word—His command, or blessing.

Arminian hearing: healing word is living command—God speaks and restores. Classical Arminians glory that the same God who wounds by folly can heal by word when they cry.

Reformed

Calvin: He sends His word—God has but to will or speak, and disease and death are expelled. Bodily recovery by the word images how souls live only as His word is apprehended; yet mercy here extends even toward the evil and unthankful.

Reformed confession: word as effective command of the Healer. Shared: sent His word; healed; delivered from destruction.

Key contrast Both confess healing by God's sent word. Arminians typically hear Wesley's command or blessing that restores those who cry. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's efficacious speech—God wills and disease flees—and the analogy that spiritual life likewise depends on His word received.

Psalms 107:21

21Let them thank the Lord for his steadfast love, for his wondrous works to the children of man!

Arminian

Let them thank the Lord for His steadfast love, for His wondrous works to the children of man!

Shared: thanks after healing. Classical Arminians urge the recovered to praise, not only privately but as duty.

Reformed

Calvin: again celebrate mercy before the sons of men. Recovery that baffled human skill must not be muted.

Reformed accent: wondrous works demand voice. Shared: thank; steadfast love; wondrous works.

Psalms 107:22

22And let them offer sacrifices of thanksgiving, and tell of his deeds in songs of joy!

Arminian

And let them offer sacrifices of thanksgiving, and tell of His deeds in songs of joy!

Both camps: thanks becomes sacrifice and song. Classical Arminians hear public joy—deeds told so others may trust the Healer.

Reformed

Calvin: sacrifice of thanksgiving explains how God is robbed if providence is not recognized. To acknowledge His bounty is most acceptable; even nature teaches homage after escape from peril.

Reformed pastoral: silence after healing is theft of glory. Shared: sacrifices of thanksgiving; songs of joy.

Psalms 107:23

23Some went down to the sea in ships, doing business on the great waters;

Arminian

Some went down to the sea in ships, doing business on the great waters;

Shared: ordinary commerce becomes theater of wonder. Classical Arminians hear sailors whose later cry God will still answer.

Reformed

Calvin: those who go down to the sea see God's wonders; their lives often hang on storms He raises and calms—as if brought from the tomb to light.

Reformed accent: sea business under providence. Shared: ships; great waters.

Psalms 107:24

24they saw the deeds of the Lord, his wondrous works in the deep.

Arminian

They saw the deeds of the Lord, His wondrous works in the deep.

Both camps: the deep displays deeds. Classical Arminians preach works meant to be seen and later praised.

Reformed

Calvin: mariners are fit witnesses of God's works—more vast wonders than on land—especially as the next lines show storm and calm by His word.

Reformed confession: deep as classroom of providence. Shared: deeds; wondrous works in the deep.

Psalms 107:25

25For he commanded and raised the stormy wind, which lifted up the waves of the sea.

Arminian

For He commanded and raised the stormy wind, which lifted up the waves of the sea.

Shared: storm answers His command. Classical Arminians refuse chance—wind rises because God speaks.

Reformed

Calvin: He speaks and the winds blow; sudden changes proceed from God's secret appointment, not from the sea's own will.

Reformed accent: command precedes the swell. Shared: commanded; raised the stormy wind.

Psalms 107:26

26They mounted up to heaven; they went down to the depths; their courage melted away in their evil plight;

Arminian

They mounted up to heaven; they went down to the depths; their courage melted away in their evil plight;

Both camps: terror that melts courage. Classical Arminians hear human end so that divine help may be clear.

Reformed

Calvin: they mount to heaven and descend to the deeps—death stares; the picture of peril makes goodness conspicuous when the storm ceases without loss.

Reformed pastoral: melting courage strips self-trust. Shared: mounted; went down; courage melted.

Psalms 107:27

27they reeled and staggered like drunken men and were at their wits' end.

Arminian

They reeled and staggered like drunken men and were at their wits' end.

Shared: skill fails; wit ends. Classical Arminians preach the end of counsel as the door of cry (v. 28).

Reformed

Calvin: they reel like drunkards; all wisdom swallowed up—skill baffled, sails and sounding useless; they give themselves to wind and wave, then cry.

Reformed accent: wits' end is pedagogical. Shared: reeled; staggered; wits' end.

Psalms 107:28

28Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and he delivered them from their distress.

Arminian

Then they cried to the Lord in their trouble, and He delivered them from their distress.

Both camps: fourth cry-and-deliver refrain at sea. Classical Arminians hear prayer when every art fails—and God still delivers.

Reformed

Calvin: all human aid failing, they cry to God—evidence they had been as it were dead. Deliverance from such peril is convincing proof of His care.

Reformed comfort: cry at wits' end still heard. Shared: cried; delivered.

Psalms 107:29

29He made the storm be still, and the waves of the sea were hushed.

Arminian

He made the storm be still, and the waves of the sea were hushed.

Shared: storm still; waves hushed. Classical Arminians glory that the God who raised the wind also stills it for those who cried.

Reformed

Calvin: He makes the storm a calm—not human agency but providence presiding; God restrains raging billows until the deep is smooth as a mirror.

Reformed confession: calm is as much His act as the storm. Shared: storm still; waves hushed.

Psalms 107:30

30Then they were glad that the waters were quiet, and he brought them to their desired haven.

Arminian

Then they were glad that the waters were quiet, and He brought them to their desired haven.

Both camps: gladness and haven are gifts. Classical Arminians preach arrival as kindness after terror—joy that should become praise.

Reformed

Calvin: after great terror, joy; forgetfulness after remarkable deliverance would be striking ingratitude. They have been instructed by storm and calm that life is in God's hand.

Reformed pastoral: haven seals the lesson. Shared: glad; quiet waters; desired haven.

Psalms 107:31

31Let them thank the Lord for his steadfast love, for his wondrous works to the children of man!

Arminian

Let them thank the Lord for His steadfast love, for His wondrous works to the children of man!

Shared: fourth thanksgiving refrain. Classical Arminians urge sailors and all who hear to thank, not whisper only at home.

Reformed

Calvin: gratitude deserves to be magnified in all places, even great assemblies—not only private mention.

Reformed accent: public thanks after public peril. Shared: thank; steadfast love; wondrous works.

Psalms 107:32

32Let them extol him in the congregation of the people, and praise him in the assembly of the elders.

Arminian

Let them extol Him in the congregation of the people, and praise Him in the assembly of the elders. Wesley: Exalt Him—in public assemblies; Elders—magistrates; let them not be ashamed to speak of God's works before the greatest.

Arminian pastoral: praise before people and elders—no fear of rank. Classical Arminians hear open confession as duty of the delivered.

Reformed

Calvin: specific mention of elders—the more wisdom and experience, the more capable of listening and witnessing these praises.

Reformed confession: congregation and elders as proper theater of praise. Shared: extol; congregation; assembly of elders.

Psalms 107:33

33He turns rivers into a desert, springs of water into thirsty ground,

Arminian

He turns rivers into a desert, springs of water into thirsty ground. Wesley: Rivers—well-watered fruitful grounds; Into—dry ground like a barren wilderness.

Shared: God reverses fertility. Classical Arminians hear judgment that can still warn and call to repentance.

Reformed

Calvin: fruitful lands become unfruitful—folly to attribute to chance. God pours wrath by taking blessing; moisture that fed plants is withdrawn.

Reformed accent: landscape change as providence. Shared: rivers to desert; springs to thirsty ground.

Psalms 107:34

34a fruitful land into a salty waste, because of the evil of its inhabitants.

Arminian

A fruitful land into a salty waste, because of the evil of its inhabitants. Wesley: For—He does not inflict these judgments without cause, but for punishment of sin in some and prevention in others.

Arminian hearing: evil of inhabitants is real cause—judgment moral, not arbitrary. Classical Arminians preach warning that fruitful places can be lost through persistent evil.

Reformed

Calvin: the earth is cursed on account of the iniquity of its inhabitants, who prove undeserving of His bounty. Saltness images extreme sterility; judgments are tokens of displeasure summoning sinners before His throne.

Reformed confession: evil explains the salty waste. Shared: fruitful to salt; because of evil.

Key contrast Both confess fruitful land ruined because of inhabitants' evil. Arminians typically hear Wesley's note: judgments punish sin and also prevent it in others—moral warning with room to turn. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: curse for iniquity under God's judicial providence, not chance.

Psalms 107:35

35He turns a desert into pools of water, a parched land into springs of water.

Arminian

He turns a desert into pools of water, a parched land into springs of water. Wesley: Water—into a well-watered and fruitful land.

Both camps: reverse mercy after reverse judgment. Classical Arminians preach a God who can make waste bloom for the hungry He will settle.

Reformed

Calvin: desert turned into a pool displays miraculous power and goodness—parched ground becomes fruitful contrary to former nature.

Reformed comfort: same hand that dries also waters. Shared: desert to pools; parched to springs.

Psalms 107:36

36And there he lets the hungry dwell, and they establish a city to live in;

Arminian

And there He lets the hungry dwell, and they establish a city to live in. Wesley: Hungry—poor people who could not provide for themselves.

Shared: hungry housed; city founded. Classical Arminians hear kindness to the poor who could not secure a home alone.

Reformed

Calvin: the famishing whom the world refuses, expatriated, are comfortably supplied in desert places God blesses with abundance; cities rise where once was no cottage.

Reformed accent: God settles the hungry. Shared: hungry dwell; establish a city.

Psalms 107:37

37they sow fields and plant vineyards and get a fruitful yield.

Arminian

They sow fields and plant vineyards and get a fruitful yield.

Both camps: sowing and planting succeed under God. Classical Arminians preach fruit as gift that should lead to thanks, not pride.

Reformed

Calvin: they sow and plant where cultivation was unknown—the land assumes a new aspect; fruit of annual increase is perennial by design, not a brief anomaly.

Reformed confession: yield traces to blessing. Shared: sow; plant; fruitful yield.

Psalms 107:38

38By his blessing they multiply greatly, and he does not let their livestock diminish.

Arminian

By His blessing they multiply greatly, and He does not let their livestock diminish.

Shared: blessing multiplies; livestock kept. Classical Arminians hear increase as stewardship trust—gift that can be abused if hearts grow secure (v. 39).

Reformed

Calvin: entirely owing to the Divine blessing that those once in want now increase in good things.

Reformed pastoral: multiply by blessing, not bare effort. Shared: by His blessing; multiply; livestock.

Psalms 107:39

39When they are diminished and brought low through oppression, evil, and sorrow,

Arminian

When they are diminished and brought low through oppression, evil, and sorrow. Wesley: They—men who when exalted grow insolent and secure; Low—by God's just judgment.

Arminian pastoral: exaltation without humility invites being brought low. Classical Arminians warn that security after blessing is itself sin God may judge.

Reformed

Calvin: mankind do not forever continue the same—decreased by war, pestilence, or turmoil when God withdraws the grace that was the hidden spring of prosperity.

Reformed accent: diminishment when blessing is withdrawn. Shared: diminished; brought low; oppression, evil, sorrow.

Psalms 107:40

40he pours contempt on princes and makes them wander in trackless wastes;

Arminian

He pours contempt on princes and makes them wander in trackless wastes. Wesley: Contempt—renders them despicable; Wander—banishes them from courts and kingdoms into desolate wildernesses.

Both camps: princes not exempt. Classical Arminians hear leveling justice—rank cannot shield contempt God pours.

Reformed

Calvin: God pours contempt on princes—their inward honor was His gift; when He hurls them from loftiness, even dependants may sport with them. History corroborates this overruling providence.

Reformed confession: majesty of rulers stands by God's gift and falls by His judgment. Shared: contempt on princes; wander in wastes.

Psalms 107:41

41but he raises up the needy out of affliction and makes their families like flocks.

Arminian

But He raises up the needy out of affliction and makes their families like flocks.

Shared: needy raised; families like flocks. Classical Arminians preach reversal that comforts the low and warns the proud.

Reformed

Calvin: as contrast, the poor and ignoble are exalted and houses increased—those held in no estimation suddenly rise. Men would recognize providence if perversity did not blind them.

Reformed comfort: God lifts the needy. Shared: raises the needy; families like flocks.

Psalms 107:42

42The upright see it and are glad, and all wickedness shuts its mouth.

Arminian

The upright see it and are glad, and all wickedness shuts its mouth.

Both camps: upright rejoice; wickedness silenced. Classical Arminians hear moral clarity—providence that gladdens the just and stops the mouth of evil.

Reformed

Calvin: the righteous see and rejoice—faith illuminates so they delight in God's equity and goodness; the wicked remain perplexed and mute, seeing yet not seeing.

Reformed accent: joy of the upright; mouth of wickedness stopped. Shared: upright glad; wickedness shuts its mouth.

Psalms 107:43

43Whoever is wise, let him attend to these things; let them consider the steadfast love of the Lord.

Arminian

Whoever is wise, let him attend to these things; let them consider the steadfast love of the Lord.

Arminian pastoral: wisdom is attending and considering—steadfast love studied in the stories of cry and rescue. Classical Arminians urge every hearer to observe and trust that love.

Reformed

Calvin: men begin to be wise when they turn attention to God's works; all others are fools however sharp they seem. Observe—not bare glance but careful meditation; then they understand Jehovah's loving-kindness, which shines in all He does.

Reformed confession: wisdom equals observing providence and steadfast love. Shared: wise; attend; consider steadfast love.

Key contrast Both confess that wisdom is attending to these deliverances and considering the Lord's steadfast love. Arminians typically emphasize sincere observation that leads any willing heart to trust that love. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: only those given to observe God's works are wise; neglect of providence is folly however clever.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on providence that answers the cry of wanderers, prisoners, the sick, and sailors; judgments that dry fruitful land and mercies that water the desert; princes brought low and the needy raised—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 107:1–43.

Arminian Notable voices

“The psalmist here observes God's providential care of the children of men in general, and shews how he helps those that are in any distress, in answer to their prayers. He instances in banishment and dispersion; captivity and imprisonment; sickness; distress at sea; and the disposal of families and nations. Word—His command, or blessing. For—He doth not inflict these judgments without cause, but for the punishment of sin in some, and the prevention of it in others. Whoso is wise, and will observe these things, even they shall understand the lovingkindness of the Lord.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Psalm 107)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Psalmist teaches that human affairs are not regulated by the fickle and uncertain wheel of fortune, but that we must observe the judgments of God in the different vicissitudes which occur in the world. Adversity and all the ills which mankind endure are to be regarded as so many tokens of God's displeasure, by which he summons them, on account of their sins, before his judicial throne. But prosperity ought also to be attributed to his grace, that he may always receive the praise of being a merciful Father and an impartial Judge. Whosoever is wise, so as to observe these things, shall understand the loving-kindness of Jehovah.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Psalm 107)
