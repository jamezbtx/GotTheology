Passage

Psalms 92

Verses 1–15 · WEB · Voices

A Psalm. A Song for the Sabbath. It is a good thing to give thanks to the LORD and sing to the Most High; declare loving kindness in the morning, and faithfulness by night, with lute, harp, and lyre. Glad in his work; great works; deep thoughts. The stupid cannot know that though the wicked spring up as the grass they are doomed forever—but the LORD is on high forever. Enemies perish; horn exalted; fresh oil; eyes see downfall. The righteous flourish like palm and cedar, planted in the LORD's house, still fruit in old age, to show that the LORD is upright—my rock, no unrighteousness in him.

Psalms 92:1

1It is a good thing to give thanks to the LORD, to sing praises to your name, Most High,

Arminian

It is a good thing to give thanks to the LORD, to sing praises to your name, Most High; Shared: goodness of thanks and sung praise to the Most High.

Sabbath song starts with "It is a good." Praise is fitting work, not leftover.

Reformed

Calvin: good to give thanks; Sabbath not idle but separated to meditate and celebrate divine works; knowing the service meets divine approbation encourages labor in praise.

Good to give thanks; sing to the Most High. Right Sabbath is praise, not vacancy.

Psalms 92:2

2to proclaim your loving kindness in the morning, and your faithfulness every night,

Arminian

“To proclaim your loving kindness in the morning, and your faithfulness every night.” Common confession: morning hesed; night faithfulness declared.

Bookend the day with attributes. Love at dawn; faithfulness at night.

Reformed

Calvin: announce goodness morning and faithfulness night—not dividing the attributes by hours but continuing praise from dawn to latest night.

Steadfast love morning; faithfulness by night. Ingratitude alone leaves us without matter for praise.

Psalms 92:3

3with the ten-stringed lute, with the harp, and with the melody of the lyre.

Arminian

“With the ten-stringed lute, with the harp, and with the melody of the lyre.” Both readings hold: instrumental praise with the song.

Beauty of ordered worship aids declaration. Instruments serve the Name.

Reformed

Calvin: addresses Levite singers to employ instruments—not as if necessary to God, but as helps elevating devotion under the law's pedagogy.

Means of praise serve hearts that savor God's works.

Psalms 92:4

4For you, LORD, have made me glad through your work. I will triumph in the works of your hands.

Arminian

For you, LORD, have made me glad through your work. I will triumph in the works of your hands. Both agree: gladness caused by God's work; joy at his hands' deeds.

Joy has a cause—his work. Sing because he acted.

Reformed

Calvin: made glad in thy works; triumph in works of thy hands—holy joy answering contemplation.

Glad by your work; sing for joy. Brutish indifference (v. 6) is failure to savor this end.

Psalms 92:5

5How great are your works, LORD! Your thoughts are very deep.

Arminian

How great are your works, LORD! Your thoughts are very deep! Wesley: Thoughts—counsels governing world and church. Great works; deep thoughts/counsels.

Depth invites worship, not complaint. Counsels run deeper than headlines.

Reformed

Calvin: how great thy works; thoughts very deep—universe proclaims faithfulness and goodness to the watchful.

Great works; deep thoughts. Depth is praise material.

Psalms 92:6

6A senseless man doesn’t know, neither does a fool understand this:

Arminian

A senseless man doesn’t know, neither does a fool understand this: Wesley: Brutish—led by sense not faith; This—depth of counsels. Folly misses the deep works.

Sense-only living cannot read providence. Ask for faith's understanding.

Reformed

Calvin: brutish man knows not; fool understands not—inability to savor the end designed in God's works causes indifference.

Stupid cannot know; fool cannot understand. Dullness is spiritual before it is intellectual.

Psalms 92:7

7though the wicked spring up as the grass, and all the evildoers flourish, they will be destroyed forever.

Arminian

“Though the wicked spring up as the grass, and all the evildoers flourish, they will be destroyed forever.” Agreed: brief grass-flourish; everlasting doom.

Do not envy tomorrow's cut grass. Flourish is not finale.

Reformed

Calvin: wicked spring as grass to be destroyed forever—ridiculous to envy those doomed to swift withering.

Sprout like grass; doomed forever. Deep thoughts include the end of evil's spring.

Psalms 92:8

8But you, LORD, are on high forever more.

Arminian

But you, LORD, are on high forever. Both hear: contrast—LORD forever exalted.

"But" relocates hope from grass to the Most High. Forever answers "forever" doom of v. 7.

Reformed

Calvin: but thou, LORD, art most high forevermore—stable height over transient wickedness.

The LORD is on high forever. God's height is the psalm's rest.

Psalms 92:9

9For behold, your enemies, LORD, for behold, your enemies shall perish. All the evildoers will be scattered.

Arminian

For behold, your enemies, LORD, for behold, your enemies shall perish. All the evildoers will be scattered. Both see: double behold; enemies perish; evildoers scattered.

Behold twice—certainty. Scattering ends organized evil.

Reformed

Calvin: lo, thine enemies shall perish; workers of iniquity scattered—vindication tied to his forever height.

God’s enemies perish and evildoers are scattered. Justice is part of Sabbath praise.

Psalms 92:10

10But you have exalted my horn like that of the wild ox. I am anointed with fresh oil.

Arminian

But you have exalted my horn like that of the wild ox. I am anointed with fresh oil. Wesley: Anointed—joy at solemnities. Undisputed: horn raised; fresh oil.

Strength and festal joy after enemies' fate. Oil marks glad consecration.

Reformed

Calvin: horn exalted like unicorn/wild ox; anointed with fresh oil—strength and refreshed gladness for the righteous.

Horn exalted; fresh oil. Personal mercy after public judgment.

Psalms 92:11

11My eye has also seen my enemies. My ears have heard of the wicked enemies who rise up against me.

Arminian

My eye has also seen my enemies. My ears have heard of the doom of my evil assailants. No dispute here: eyes and ears witness foes' end.

Testimony senses confirm God's justice. He witnesses God’s justice without a vengeful appetite.

Reformed

Calvin: eye sees desire on enemies; ears hear of the wicked rising against me—fulfilled watchfulness.

Seen downfall; heard doom. Providence becomes audible and visible.

Psalms 92:12

12The righteous shall flourish like the palm tree. He will grow like a cedar in Lebanon.

Arminian

The righteous shall flourish like the palm tree. He will grow like a cedar in Lebanon. Wesley: Palm—constantly green. Both affirm: palm flourish; cedar growth.

Lasting green against grass of v. 7. Character rooted differently.

Reformed

Calvin: righteous flourish like palm; multiplied as cedar—after trials, God shows he had not forgotten them.

Where they meet: Palm; cedar. The righteous endure, while the wicked spring up like grass and fade.

Psalms 92:13

13They are planted in the LORD’s house. They will flourish in our God’s courts.

Arminian

They are planted in the LORD’s house. They will flourish in our God’s courts of our God. Wesley: Planted—fixed by providence in the church. Planted in the house; flourish in courts.

Location matters—house and courts. Flourish is ecclesial, not merely private.

Reformed

Calvin: planted in Jehovah's house; flourish in courts—nearness to worship is the soil of growth.

Planted in the house; flourish in courts. God's dwelling grows his trees.

Psalms 92:14

14They will still produce fruit in old age. They will be full of sap and green,

Arminian

They will still produce fruit in old age. They will be full of sap and green, Wesley: Old age—last days best; grow in grace. Both traditions hear: aged fruitfulness; sap and green.

Refuse the myth that piety expires with youth. Last days can be best days.

Reformed

Calvin: still bud in old age; fat and green—continued vitality displaying God's care into late years.

Fruit in old age; full of sap and green. Perseverance has botanical imagery.

Psalms 92:15

15to show that the LORD is upright. He is my rock, and there is no unrighteousness in him.

Arminian

“To show that the LORD is upright.” He is my rock, and there is no unrighteousness in him. Shared close: aged fruit declares the upright Rock; no unrighteousness in him.

Lives become arguments for God's character. The LORD is a rock with no injustice in him.

Reformed

Calvin: that they may show Jehovah is upright, my rock, and no iniquity in him—flourishing saints are exhibits of his righteousness.

Declare the LORD upright; my rock; no unrighteousness. The Sabbath song ends in theodicy proved in lives.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What earlier interpreters on each side wrote about Psalm 92:1–15.

Arminian Notable voices

“Thoughts—counsels in government of world and church. A brutish man—led by sense, not reason and faith; This—depth of God's counsels. Anointed—joy testified by anointing at solemnities. Palm-tree—constantly green. Planted—fixed by gracious providence in the church. Old age—last days best; grow in grace and blessedness.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 92

Reformed Notable voices

“Good to give thanks; Sabbath for meditating divine works, not idle holiness. Show loving-kindness morning and faithfulness night—continuous praise. Glad in thy works. How great thy works; thoughts very deep—brutish man knows not. Wicked spring as grass to be destroyed forever; thou, LORD, most high forever. Enemies perish; workers of iniquity scattered. Horn exalted like wild ox; fresh oil. Eyes see desire on enemies. Righteous flourish like palm and cedar; planted in Jehovah's house; still bud in old age; fat and green—to show Jehovah upright, my rock, no iniquity in him. Trials first; eventual flourishing displays his righteousness.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 92
