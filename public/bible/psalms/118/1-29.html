Passage

Psalms 118

Verses 1–29 · WEB · Voices

Egyptian Hallel climax: thanksgiving for the Lord's goodness and forever steadfast love, confessed by Israel, Aaron, and all who fear Him. Distress yields to answered freedom; refuge in the Lord outranks trust in man or princes; nations surround, yet the Name cuts off. The Lord is strength, song, and salvation; severe discipline does not end in death. Gates of righteousness open; the stone the builders rejected becomes the cornerstone—the Lord's marvelous doing—and hosanna welcomes the one who comes in the Lord's name.

Psalms 118:1

1Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever.

Arminian

Oh give thanks to the LORD, for he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever! Wesley opens thanksgiving on goodness and everlasting mercy.

Where they meet: goodness and hesed forever. Shared doxology that frames the whole psalm.

Reformed

Calvin: thanksgiving is due because God is good and His mercy everlasting. The refrain will gather Israel, Aaron, and God-fearers.

Give thanks; He is good; steadfast love forever.

Psalms 118:2

2Let Israel now say that his loving kindness endures forever.

Arminian

Let Israel say, "His loving kindness endures forever." National voice rehearses the refrain.

Israel must speak mercy aloud.

Reformed

Calvin: Israel is to confess everlasting mercy as corporate memory and faith.

Let Israel say; steadfast love forever.

Psalms 118:3

3Let the house of Aaron now say that his loving kindness endures forever.

Arminian

Let the house of Aaron now say the same. Priests lead the confession they need as much as the people.

Aaron's house; steadfast love forever.

Reformed

Calvin: Aaron's house must not trust altar without mercy's refrain.

House of Aaron say; steadfast love forever.

Psalms 118:4

4Now let those who fear the LORD say that his loving kindness endures forever.

Arminian

Let those who fear the Lord say it—widening beyond tribe and priesthood to all God-fearers.

The fearers' circle anticipates Gentile inclusion under the same forever mercy.

Reformed

Calvin: all who fear the Lord join the same confession—true religion's common creed.

Both traditions hear: those who fear the Lord; steadfast love forever.

Psalms 118:5

5Out of my distress, I called on the LORD. The LORD answered me with freedom.

Arminian

Out of my distress I called on the LORD; he answered me and set me in a wide place. Prayer from a tight place; answer as spacious freedom.

Calling and answering are interpersonal mercy—distress sincerely heard.

Reformed

Calvin: from straits the psalmist cried; the Lord answered into a broad place. Deliverance is God's act.

Common reading: called; answered; set me free.

Key contrast Both confess that distress-prayer is answered with freedom. Arminians highlight a sincere divine willingness to answer those who call. Reformed readers highlight that the answering Lord effectually delivers His own according to His purpose, the cry itself granted in grace.

Psalms 118:6

6The LORD is on my side. I will not be afraid. What can man do to me?

Arminian

The LORD is on my side. I will not be afraid. What can man do to me? Courage from alliance with God, not from denial of enemies.

Both confess: holy fearlessness. Either reading: the Lord on my side.

Reformed

Calvin: with God as advocate, human threat is relativized. Fear is expelled by presence.

I will not be afraid. What can man do?

Psalms 118:7

7The LORD is on my side among those who help me. Therefore I will look in triumph at those who hate me.

Arminian

The LORD is on my side among those who help me. Therefore I will look in triumph at those who hate me. Help first; triumph as consequence.

Triumph is God's help seen, not personal vindictiveness baptized.

Reformed

Calvin: God as helper secures the outlook of victory over haters—vindication by the Lord.

With the LORD as helper, the psalmist can face his enemies in triumph.

Psalms 118:8

8It is better to take refuge in the LORD, than to put confidence in man.

Arminian

It is better to take refuge in the LORD, than to put confidence in man. Comparative wisdom—refuge outranks human reliance.

Both sides agree: man-trust fails; Lord-refuge holds.

Reformed

Calvin: refuge in the Lord is incomparably better than confidence in flesh.

Both traditions see: better to take refuge in the LORD, than to put confidence in man.

Psalms 118:9

9It is better to take refuge in the LORD, than to put confidence in princes.

Arminian

Better refuge in the Lord than trust in princes. Rank does not redeem unreliability.

Both note: political saviors are not the Rock.

Reformed

Calvin doubles the warning: princes fail as surely as common men when made ultimate trust.

Common confession: better than to put confidence in princes.

Psalms 118:10

10All the nations surrounded me, but in the LORD’s name I cut them off.

Arminian

All the nations surrounded me, but in the LORD’s name I cut them off! Encirclement met by the Name, not by autonomous strength.

Both readings hold: victory credited to the Lord's name.

Reformed

Calvin: surrounded by nations, the king (and the Church in him) prevails only in Jehovah's name.

In the LORD’s name I cut them off!

Psalms 118:11

11They surrounded me, yes, they surrounded me. In the LORD’s name I indeed cut them off.

Arminian

Surrounded on every side—repetition paints pressure; again the Name cuts off.

Both traditions can share this refrain of dependence.

Reformed

Calvin hears intensified siege language answered by the same confession: only the Name saves.

Both agree: surrounded on every side; name of the Lord.

Psalms 118:12

12They surrounded me like bees. They are quenched like the burning thorns. In the LORD’s name I cut them off.

Arminian

Like bees—swarming fury; like fire among thorns—fierce yet quickly spent. The Name still decides.

Agreed: vivid foes, same refuge.

Reformed

Calvin: bee-swarm and thorn-fire images show rage that cannot overthrow those kept by the Name.

Like bees; name of the Lord.

Psalms 118:13

13You pushed me back hard, to make me fall, but the LORD helped me.

Arminian

He was pushed back hard, to make him fall, but the LORD helped him. He was pushed hard, near to falling, but the LORD helped him.

Help meets the falling; grace is timely.

Reformed

Calvin: pushed toward falling, yet upheld by the Lord's help—preservation in extremity.

Both hear: pushed hard; the Lord helped me.

Psalms 118:14

14The LORD is my strength and song. He has become my salvation.

Arminian

The LORD is my strength and song. He has become my salvation. Exodus echo (Ex 15:2)—strength, song, salvation in one Lord.

Both see: salvation is God Himself become ours in deliverance.

Reformed

Calvin: strength and song meet in the God who has become salvation—personal appropriation of redeeming power.

Strength and song; become my salvation.

Psalms 118:15

15The voice of rejoicing and salvation is in the tents of the righteous. “The right hand of the LORD does valiantly.

Arminian

Glad songs of salvation in the tents of the righteous: the right hand of the Lord does valiantly. Households of the righteous become choir lofts.

Undisputed: salvation produces song; the right hand is God's.

Reformed

Calvin: tents of the righteous ring with salvation's joy; valor is ascribed to Jehovah's right hand.

No dispute here: glad songs; right hand does valiantly.

Psalms 118:16

16The right hand of the LORD is exalted! The right hand of the LORD does valiantly!”

Arminian

The right hand of the Lord exalts, does valiantly! Triple right-hand stress—exaltation and valor from God alone.

The right hand of the LORD is exalted.

Reformed

Calvin repeats the right hand so no secondary cause steals the glory of exaltation.

Right hand of the Lord exalts.

Psalms 118:17

17I will not die, but live, and declare the LORD’s works.

Arminian

I will not die, but live, and declare the deeds of the Lord. His life is spared so that he may testify.

Both affirm: preserved life is for recounting deeds, not private hoarding.

Reformed

Calvin: not death but life—and the purpose is to narrate the Lord's deeds publicly.

I will not die, but live, and recount.

Psalms 118:18

18The LORD has punished me severely, but he has not given me over to death.

Arminian

The LORD has punished me severely, but he has not given me over to death. This is severe mercy: discipline without abandonment.

Fatherly discipline aims at restoration; it is not proof of a will to destroy the believer.

Reformed

Calvin: severe discipline stopped short of death—chastening within covenant care, not final rejection.

Paternal correction of those who are His. Disciplined severely; not given over to death.

Key contrast Both confess severe discipline that stops short of death. Arminians hear fatherly correction consistent with a sincere will to save and restore. Calvin’s heirs generally hear covenant chastening of the elect that never becomes final reprobation, appointed in wise sovereignty.

Psalms 118:19

19Open to me the gates of righteousness. I will enter into them. I will give thanks to the LORD.

Arminian

I will enter and give thanks. Access sought for thanksgiving, not tourism.

Where they meet: gates open toward grateful worship.

Reformed

Calvin: gates of righteousness admit the delivered thanksgiving throng to God's house.

Open the gates; enter; give thanks.

Psalms 118:20

20This is the gate of the LORD; the righteous will enter into it.

Arminian

This is the gate of the LORD; the righteous will enter into it. Entrance defined by the Lord and by righteousness He grants and requires.

Both traditions hear: not every gate; this gate.

Reformed

Calvin: the Lord's own gate—the righteous enter. Holiness of access matches the Holy One.

Common reading: gate of the LORD; the righteous will enter.

Psalms 118:21

21I will give thanks to you, for you have answered me, and have become my salvation.

Arminian

I will give thanks to you, for you have answered me, and have become my salvation. Answer and salvation again fused in thanks.

God answered and became his salvation.

Reformed

Calvin returns to answered prayer and God-as-salvation as the content of thanks at the gate.

Thank You; answered; salvation.

Psalms 118:22

22The stone which the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.

Arminian

The stone which the builders rejected has become the cornerstone. Wesley and the whole Church read Christ here: rejected by the builders, made Head of the corner. New Testament citation (Matt 21:42; Acts 4:11; 1 Pet 2:7) seals the messianic reading.

Both camps together: shared messianic christology—Jesus is the rejected stone become cornerstone. Wesleyan Arminians preach this Christ sincerely held out as the Church's foundation for all who believe.

Reformed

Calvin: the stone rejected by the builders is exalted as cornerstone—fulfilled in Christ, refused by the leaders, made head of the Church's building by God's power.

This is messianic christology held in common. Both confess: rejected stone; cornerstone.

Psalms 118:23

23This is the LORD’s doing. It is marvelous in our eyes.

Arminian

This is the Lord's doing; it is marvelous in our eyes. The reversal—rejection to cornerstone—is God's act, wonder for the Church's eyes.

Either reading: marvel, not human engineering. Shared christological wonder.

Reformed

Calvin: the exaltation of the rejected stone is the Lord's doing—marvelous, not explicable as builders' revision of taste.

Wesley concurs: Lord's doing; marvelous in our eyes.

Psalms 118:24

24This is the day that the LORD has made. We will rejoice and be glad in it!

Arminian

This is the day that the LORD has made. We will rejoice and be glad in it. The day of saving reversal—ultimately the day God exalts His Christ—calls for corporate joy.

Both sides agree: rejoice in the Lord's day of salvation.

Reformed

Calvin: the day God made for this marvel demands gladness from the people who see it.

Both traditions see: the day the Lord has made; rejoice and be glad.

Psalms 118:25

25Save us now, we beg you, LORD! LORD, we beg you, send prosperity now.

Arminian

Save us now, we beg you, LORD! LORD, we pray, give us success! Hosanna-cry—salvation and success asked together under God.

Petition is sincere; success is sought as God's gift for His people's good, not autonomous triumph.

Reformed

Calvin: save and prosper—prayers that confess inability and ask ongoing help from the Lord who already delivered.

Save us; give us success.

Key contrast Both pray for salvation and success from the Lord. Arminians hear petitions God sincerely invites and may answer as His people ask in faith. Calvinists hear dependent cries God ordains and answers according to His wise will for the Church's true good.

Psalms 118:26

26Blessed is he who comes in the LORD’s name! We have blessed you out of the LORD’s house.

Arminian

Blessed is he who comes in the LORD’s name; from the LORD’s house we bless you; the Gospels set this on the lips of the crowds for Jesus, and the Church still blesses the Coming One.

Both note: christological welcome. Blessed is He who comes.

Reformed

Calvin: blessing on the one who comes in the Lord's name—applied preeminently to Christ entering His kingdom and house.

Shared messianic greeting: Blessed is he who comes in the LORD’s name! We have blessed you out of the Lord.

Psalms 118:27

27The LORD is God, and he has given us light. Bind the sacrifice with cords, even to the horns of the altar.

Arminian

The Lord is God; He has given us light shine upon us. Bind the sacrifice with cords, even to the altar's horns. Light and sacrifice—revelation and offering.

Common confession: festal consecration under shining light. In Christian reading, the true Sacrifice is Christ; the psalm's joy remains.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah is God; light upon the people; the festal victim bound for the altar—worship that answers illumination.

Light shine upon us; festal sacrifice to the altar.

Psalms 118:28

28You are my God, and I will give thanks to you. You are my God, I will exalt you.

Arminian

You are my God, and I will give thanks to you. You are my God, I will exalt you. Personal appropriation doubled—thanks and extolling.

"my God" is covenant claim answered by willing praise.

Reformed

Calvin: repeated "my God" seals belonging; thanks and extolling are the fit return.

You are my God; thanks; extol You.

Psalms 118:29

29Oh give thanks to the LORD, for he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever.

Arminian

Oh give thanks to the LORD, for he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever! The psalm ends as it began—goodness and everlasting hesed.

Both readings hold: forever mercy bookends distress, victory, and the cornerstone. Arminians and Reformed together close the Hallel stretch in shared thanksgiving.

Reformed

Calvin returns to the opening refrain: goodness and everlasting mercy. All that intervened—distress, Name, stone, hosanna—sits inside this forever love.

Common ground with Wesley: give thanks; He is good; steadfast love endures forever.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

On forever steadfast love, refuge better than princes, and the rejected stone made cornerstone, a few classic voices from each tradition.

Arminian Notable voices

“O give thanks unto the Lord, for he is good; because his mercy endureth for ever. Let Israel now say—and the house of Aaron—and they that fear the Lord. I called upon the Lord in distress; the Lord answered me. It is better to trust in the Lord than to put confidence in man or princes. The Lord is my strength and song, and is become my salvation. I shall not die, but live. The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner—applied in the New Testament to Christ. This is the Lord's doing; it is marvellous in our eyes. Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the Lord.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Psalm 118)

Reformed Notable voices

“The people are taught to celebrate God's goodness and never-failing mercy. From deep distress the faithful cried and were heard. Confidence in men and princes is declared inferior to refuge in God. Surrounded by nations, they prevail only in His name. God becomes their salvation; chastening is severe yet not unto death. The stone rejected by the builders—Christ refused by the rulers of His people—God has made the cornerstone; this is His doing and marvelous in our eyes. Hosanna and blessing meet the One who comes in the Lord's name.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Psalm 118)

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
