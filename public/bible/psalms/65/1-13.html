Passage

Psalms 65

Verses 1–13 · WEB · Voices

To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. A Song. Praise is due in Zion and vows are paid; the Hearer of prayer draws all flesh; God atones when iniquities prevail; the chosen are brought near to be satisfied in His house; awesome deeds answer as hope for the ends of the earth; Creator might stills seas and peoples; and His visiting waters crown the year until wilderness, hills, meadows, and valleys shout for joy.

Psalms 65:1

1Praise waits for you, God, in Zion. Vows shall be performed to you.

Arminian

Praise waits for you, God, in Zion. Vows shall be performed to you shall vows be performed. Wesley: Waiteth—is silent, or is prepared, as a gift ready to be offered. Zion is the address of owed praise and kept vows.

Bring due praise and perform vows. Silence of waiting can be readiness to offer; do not delay what you promised God.

Reformed

Calvin: praise waits for God in Zion—the Church is the place where vows are paid. God is owed silent readiness and spoken gratitude.

Zion's worship is not optional ornament. Perform vows where God has set His name.

Psalms 65:2

2You who hear prayer, all men will come to you.

Arminian

You who hear prayer, all men will come to you. Wesley: All flesh—all men. The Hearer of prayer draws the world.

Shared missionary horizon—all flesh invited to come. Grace that hears prayer is offered widely; come, and keep coming.

Reformed

Calvin: God as hearer of prayer is the magnet of all flesh—Gentiles included in the hope. Access is by His character as the One who hears.

Universal invitation rests on God's hearing. All flesh may come; none come except He draws by the means He appoints.

Psalms 65:3

3Sins overwhelmed me, but you atoned for our transgressions.

Arminian

Sins overwhelmed me, but you atoned for our transgressions. Wesley: Prevail—are too strong for me. Purge—expiate or take away. Overwhelming guilt meets atoning God.

Shared gospel in the psalm—atonement for prevailing iniquities. Confess overwhelm; trust the God who purges. Do not pretend strength against your own sins.

Reformed

Calvin: when iniquities prevail, God purges transgressions—forgiveness is His work, not our self-cleansing. The plural "our" joins the people in need.

Atonement is God's provision. Prevailing sin drives to mercy, not to despair or to works-atonement.

Psalms 65:4

4Blessed is the one whom you choose and cause to come near, that he may live in your courts. We will be filled with the goodness of your house, your holy temple.

Arminian

Blessed is the one whom you choose and cause to come near, that he may live in your courts. We will be filled with the goodness of your house... Wesley: Choosest—to approach unto thee by prayer and sacrifices. Nearness is gift and blessedness.

Election here is unto nearness in worship—blessed are those drawn to God's courts. Common confession: satisfaction is in His house's goodness, not in self.

Reformed

Calvin: Blessed is he whom thou choosest and causest to approach—approach is God's choosing and bringing. Satisfaction with the temple's goodness is the fruit of that choice.

Both traditions share the blessing of being brought near. The courts welcome those God brings; goodness of His house fills what the world cannot.

Key contrast Both bless those God brings near to dwell in His courts and find satisfaction in His house. Arminians hear choosing-and-bringing as gracious drawing unto worship that still calls all flesh (v. 2) to come; Calvinists hear effectual choice that causes approach, so nearness is God's sovereign bringing before it is our arriving.

Psalms 65:5

5By awesome deeds of righteousness, you answer us, God of our salvation. You who are the hope of all the ends of the earth, of those who are far away on the sea.

Arminian

By awesome deeds of righteousness, you answer us, God of our salvation. You who are the hope of all the ends of the earth... Wesley: Terrible—with dreadful judgments against thine and our enemies. Hope reaches farthest seas.

Salvation's God is hope for the ends of the earth. Awesome answers in righteousness encourage global trust, not ethnic monopoly of hope.

Reformed

Calvin: God answers by terrible things in righteousness—deliverances that display justice. He is the confidence of all the ends of the earth.

Both traditions share this hope for the whole world. The God of Israel's salvation is already named as hope of the farthest seas.

Psalms 65:6

6By your power, you form the mountains, having armed yourself with strength.

Arminian

The one who By your power, you form the mountains, having armed yourself with strength. Wesley: Setteth—established them. Creator strength undergirds Redeemer hope.

Mountain-maker is prayer-hearer. Trust the girded might that set the hills when waves of trouble rise.

Reformed

Calvin: by strength He sets fast the mountains—creation power assures the Church that the same God can still tumults.

Cosmic might for covenant care. Girded with power, He is not too weak for your seas.

Psalms 65:7

7You still the roaring of the seas, the roaring of their waves, and the turmoil of the nations.

Arminian

You still the roaring of the seas, the tumult of the peoples. Wesley: Noises—tumults and wars of the nations. Nature and nations hush under Him.

Shared comfort—seas and peoples both roar; both can be stilled. Pray to the Still-er rather than echo the tumult.

Reformed

Calvin: He stilleth the noise of the seas and the tumult of the people—one sovereignty over creation and history.

Political storms are not outside God's rule. The stiller of waves stills peoples.

Psalms 65:8

8They also who dwell in faraway places are afraid at your wonders. You call the morning’s dawn and the evening with songs of joy.

Arminian

Those who live far away stand in awe of your wonders; you make morning and evening shout for joy. Wesley: Outgoings—the successive courses. Dawn and dusk are summoned to joy.

Both traditions hear the daily rhythms of morning and evening join in praise. Live awake to signs that awe the ends of the earth; let morning and evening shout through you.

Reformed

Calvin: dwellers at the ends fear His signs; He makes morning and evening rejoice—ordinary times become liturgy under providence.

Creation's clock is catechesis. Awe at the edges; joy at sunrise and sunset.

Psalms 65:9

9You visit the earth, and water it. You greatly enrich it. The river of God is full of water. You provide them grain, for so you have ordained it.

Arminian

You visit the earth, and water it. You greatly enrich it. The river of God is full of water. You provide them grain, for so you have ordained it. Wesley: Visitest—with thy gracious presence. River—the clouds which drop fatness. Grain is prepared gift.

For the reader: shared gratitude—harvest theology. God visits, waters, enriches, provides; receive bread as prepared kindness, not bare chance.

Reformed

Calvin: God visits the earth and waters it; the river of God is full—His channels never fail. Grain is prepared by His counsel.

Providence in agriculture. The prepared grain silences proud self-sufficiency.

Psalms 65:10

10You drench its furrows. You level its ridges. You soften it with showers. You bless it with a crop.

Arminian

You soften it with showers, and blessing its growth. Wesley: Settlest—makest soft and fit for fruitfulness. Softening showers are blessing.

Ask for softening as for rain. Hard ground in fields and hearts needs shower-blessing; growth is God-touched.

Reformed

Calvin: He watereth the furrows and blesseth the springing—detailed care of the soil images fatherly providence.

Both readings hold: Minute mercies. Softening showers preach a God who tends what He planted.

Psalms 65:11

11You crown the year with your bounty. Your carts overflow with abundance.

Arminian

You crown the year with your bounty. Your carts overflow with abundance. Wesley: Crownest—adornest with plenty. Paths—clouds drop fatness wherever they go.

Shared year-end praise—crown the year by naming His bounty. Overflowing tracks invite overflowing thanks.

Reformed

Calvin: He crowneth the year with goodness; His paths drop fatness—the whole circuit of the year under blessing.

Time itself is crowned by God. Abundance is traced to His wagon tracks, not to luck.

Psalms 65:12

12The wilderness grasslands overflow. The hills are clothed with gladness.

Arminian

The wilderness grasslands overflow. The hills are clothed with gladness. Wesley: Wilderness—even the most barren places. Joy clothes the hills.

Barren places can overflow under God. Hope for wilderness pastures; let joy gird what looked dead.

Reformed

Calvin: the wilderness pastures overflow; hills rejoice—creation shares the feast of providence.

No pocket of earth outside His watering. Joy on the hills answers His visit.

Psalms 65:13

13The pastures are covered with flocks. The valleys also are clothed with grain. They shout for joy! They also sing.

Arminian

The pastures are covered with flocks. The valleys also are clothed with grain. Wesley: Sing—the valleys covered with corn appear to shout and sing. Creation's choir closes the psalm.

Both traditions hear the psalm’s finale as a call to join the shout. Flocks and grain are vestments of praise; add your voice to the valleys' song.

Reformed

Calvin: pastures clothed with flocks, valleys covered with corn, shout for joy—nature's hymn to the Provider.

Let abundance become doxology. The song of fields trains the Church's song.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Voices from both camps

On praise in Zion, atonement for prevailing iniquity, being chosen and brought near, and the God who waters the earth, a few classic voices from each tradition.

Arminian Notable voices

“Waiteth—is silent, or is prepared, as a gift ready to be offered. All flesh—all men. Prevail—are too strong for me. Purge—expiate or take away. Choosest—to approach unto thee by prayer and sacrifices. Terrible—with dreadful judgments against thine and our enemies. Setteth—established them. Noises—tumults and wars of the nations. Outgoings—the successive courses. Visitest—with thy gracious presence. River—the clouds which drop fatness. Settlest—makest soft and fit for fruitfulness. Crownest—adornest with plenty. Wilderness—even the most barren places. Sing—the valleys covered with corn appear to shout and sing.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 65

Reformed Notable voices

“Praise waits for God in Zion; vows are paid there. He who hears prayer is He to whom all flesh shall come. When iniquities prevail, He purges transgressions. Blessed is he whom God chooses and causes to approach His courts—satisfied with the goodness of His house. He answers by terrible things in righteousness and is the confidence of all the ends of the earth. By strength He sets the mountains; He stills seas and the tumult of peoples. He visits and waters the earth; the river of God is full; He prepares grain, softens with showers, crowns the year with goodness until wilderness and valleys shout for joy.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 65
