Passage

Psalms 86

Verses 1–17 · ESV · Voices

A Prayer of David. Incline your ear, O Lord, for I am poor and needy; preserve the godly servant who trusts in you. Be gracious; gladden the soul lifted up to you—for you are good and forgiving, abounding in steadfast love to all who call. There is none like you among the gods; all nations shall worship and glorify your name. Teach me your way; unite my heart to fear your name. Great is your steadfast love; you delivered my soul from Sheol. Insolent men rise, but you are merciful and gracious; turn, strengthen, save the son of your maidservant; show a sign of favor that haters may be ashamed.

Psalms 86:1

1Incline your ear, O Lord, and answer me, for I am poor and needy.

Arminian

Incline your ear, O Lord, and answer me, for I am poor and needy. Shared: poverty and need are a plea before the listening Lord.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley has no extra lemma here; the plain cry stands. Bring need without polishing merit first.

Reformed

Calvin: nothing more suitable to God's nature than to succor the afflicted; the more destitute of human aid, the more inclined is God to help. The Spirit dictates this prayer for the poor.

Reformed pastoral: shared—poor and needy incline the divine ear. Despair need not win; affliction is itself argument for mercy.

Psalms 86:2

2Preserve my life, for I am godly; save your servant, who trusts in you—you are my God.

Arminian

Preserve my life, for I am godly; save your servant, who trusts in you—you are my God. Wesley: Holy—sincerely devoted to thy service. Shared: devotion and trust mark the servant God saves.

Arminian pastoral: godly here is sincere devotion, not self-salvation. Trust names the God who is "my God."

Reformed

Calvin: meek/clemency and trust—not meriting obligation, but showing enemies' cruelty against a merciful man; trust is the mother of true religion. Integrity without lifting eyes to God is vanity.

Reformed pastoral: shared—preserve the devoted servant who trusts. Conscience may approve; still commit hope to God.

Psalms 86:3

3Be gracious to me, O Lord, for to you do I cry all the day.

Arminian

Be gracious to me, O Lord, for to you do I cry all the day. Shared: all-day cry asks grace, not wage.

Arminian pastoral: perseverance in crying is itself piety. Keep the day-long petition alive.

Reformed

Calvin: mercy is to gratify freely—I bring no merit of my own. Daily crying proves hope; cry denotes earnestness of soul.

Reformed pastoral: shared—grace sought all day. Untiring prayer is not presumption; it is faith's labor.

Psalms 86:4

4Gladden the soul of your servant, for to you, O Lord, do I lift up my soul.

Arminian

Gladden the soul of your servant, for to you, O Lord, do I lift up my soul. Shared: joy asked for the servant whose soul is lifted to the Lord.

Arminian pastoral: gladness is gift answering a lifted soul. Lift first; then ask to rejoice.

Reformed

Calvin: the end of mercy sought is removal of sorrow; lifting the soul is the mark of right prayer—no hypocrisy in the cry.

Reformed pastoral: shared—gladden the lifted soul. True prayer rises Godward before it asks for cheer.

Psalms 86:5

5For you, O Lord, are good and forgiving, abounding in steadfast love to all who call upon you.

Arminian

For you, O Lord, are good and forgiving, abounding in steadfast love to all who call upon you. Shared: goodness, pardon, and hesed toward callers.

Arminian pastoral: "all who call" is the open door of the text. Goodness and forgiveness invite every sincere cry.

Reformed

Calvin: God is bountiful and inclined to compassion; pardoning mercy modifies his goodness. Plenteous mercy is yet restricted to the faithful who call; those who obstinately chafe perish. "All" encourages every rank to betake themselves to his mercy.

Reformed pastoral: shared—good, forgiving, abounding in love to callers. The invitation is real; the restriction is against proud refusal, not against humble need.

Key contrast Both confess the LORD is good, forgiving, and abounding in steadfast love toward those who call. Arminians typically stress the breadth of "all who call" as a sincere universal invitation to mercy. Reformed readers typically follow Calvin: plenteous mercy is celebrated, yet joined to faithful calling—not to those who chafe against God—while "all" still beckons every rank to the throne of grace. Shared: attributes of goodness and pardon underwrite the prayer.

Psalms 86:6

6Give ear, O Lord, to my prayer; listen to my plea for grace.

Arminian

Give ear, O Lord, to my prayer; listen to my plea for grace. Shared: prayer and plea for grace seek a listening ear.

Arminian pastoral: ask again after grounding in God's character (v. 5). Grace is the plea's content.

Reformed

Calvin: earnest repetition shows ordinary grief and extreme anxiety; those who pray once and quit when not instantly answered betray cold hearts. Importunity is a sweet savor.

Reformed pastoral: shared—give ear; listen to the plea. Repeat the request into God's bosom without shame.

Psalms 86:7

7In the day of my trouble I call upon you, for you answer me.

Arminian

In the day of my trouble I call upon you, for you answer me. Shared: trouble-day calling rests on God's answering.

Arminian pastoral: call because he answers—confidence, not experiment. Trouble is the day of prayer.

Reformed

Calvin: particular application of the truth that God is merciful to all who call—he will hear when I cry in trouble.

Reformed pastoral: shared—trouble; call; answer. Apply general mercy to this day's need.

Psalms 86:8

8There is none like you among the gods, O Lord, nor are there any works like yours.

Arminian

There is none like you among the gods, O Lord, nor are there any works like yours. Shared: incomparability of the LORD and his works.

Arminian pastoral: false gods fail the works-test. Worship the One whose deeds prove deity.

Reformed

Calvin: heathen fabrications derided—no characteristic of deity in their works. Speculations that skip God's works trifle; works bear witness of him.

Reformed pastoral: shared—none like you; no works like yours. Glory exclusive to the true God.

Psalms 86:9

9All the nations you have made shall come and worship before you, O Lord, and shall glorify your name.

Arminian

All the nations you have made shall come and worship before you, O Lord, and shall glorify your name. Shared: maker of nations; nations shall worship and glorify.

Arminian pastoral: creation grounds the summons to worship. Expect Gentile glory to his name.

Reformed

Calvin: celebrating godliness among the heathen has an eye to Christ's kingdom; Gentiles created by God, so their enlightenment need not seem strange.

Reformed pastoral: shared—nations made shall worship. Gospel horizon softens the announcement of Gentile inclusion.

Psalms 86:10

10For you are great and do wondrous things; you alone are God.

Arminian

For you are great and do wondrous things; you alone are God. Shared: greatness, wonders, exclusive deity.

Arminian pastoral: wonders teach monotheism. Leave rival "gods" without share in his title.

Reformed

Calvin: greatness of works brings nations to worship; Godhead's glory belongs exclusively to the one true God.

Reformed pastoral: shared—great; wondrous; God alone. Contemplation of works births genuine piety.

Psalms 86:11

11Teach me your way, O Lord, that I may walk in your truth; unite my heart to fear your name.

Arminian

Teach me your way, O Lord, that I may walk in your truth; unite my heart to fear your name. Wesley: Truth—way of thy precepts; My heart—knit my whole heart to thyself. Shared: taught way, walk in truth, united heart fearing the Name.

Arminian pastoral: ask to be taught and knit. Whole-heart fear answers divided loyalties.

Reformed

Calvin: unite my heart: the heart is scattered until God gathers it; free will is destitute of both light and uprightness; Spirit's light and gift of uprightness oppose our blindness and fluctuating affections.

Reformed pastoral: shared—teach; walk; unite the heart. Progress in religion deepens sense of need for inward light.

Key contrast Both ask to be taught the LORD's way, to walk in truth, and to have the heart united to fear his name. Arminians typically hear Wesley's "knit my whole heart" as devoted cooperation with grace—sincere concentration of love and fear. Reformed readers typically follow Calvin's stronger anthropology: both powers (understanding and will) are wanting until the Spirit unites a tumultuous heart; free will of itself cannot supply what the prayer seeks. Shared: fear of the Name is the end; divided hearts need God's knitting.

Psalms 86:12

12I give thanks to you, O Lord my God, with my whole heart, and I will glorify your name forever.

Arminian

I give thanks to you, O Lord my God, with my whole heart, and I will glorify your name forever. Shared: whole-heart thanks; forever glory to the Name.

Arminian pastoral: thanksgiving matches the united heart of v. 11. Forever is the horizon of praise.

Reformed

Calvin: engages to celebrate praises not only with mouth but sincere affection, with steadfast perseverance.

Reformed pastoral: shared—whole heart; forever. Gratitude is vowed, not merely felt once.

Psalms 86:13

13For great is your steadfast love toward me; you have delivered my soul from the depths of Sheol.

Arminian

For great is your steadfast love toward me; you have delivered my soul from the depths of Sheol. Wesley: Hell—extreme dangers and miseries. Shared: great hesed; deliverance from Sheol's depths.

Arminian pastoral: lowest dangers magnify mercy. Read Sheol as extremity of misery, not a speculative map.

Reformed

Calvin: singular proof of mercy—thrust down into lowest depths, requiring God's hand stretched out wonderfully. In Christ a still deeper abyss is escaped.

Reformed pastoral: shared—great love; delivered from Sheol's depths. Prolonged life was David's praise; gospel deliverance demands more.

Psalms 86:14

14O God, insolent men have risen up against me; a band of ruthless men seeks my life, and they do not set you before them.

Arminian

O God, insolent men have risen up against me; a band of ruthless men seeks my life, and they do not set you before them. Shared: proud/ruthless assault; no fear of God before them.

Arminian pastoral: name the insolence honestly. Godlessness of foes explains their cruelty.

Reformed

Calvin: proud and strong rush to destroy; no regard to God means extreme excess of cruelty—lusts dare anything without fear of judgment.

Reformed pastoral: shared—insolent band; life sought; God not set before them. Seek remedy in divine mercy next.

Psalms 86:15

15But you, O Lord, are a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness.

Arminian

But you, O Lord, are a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness. Shared: Exodus 34:6 attributes answer the proud.

Arminian pastoral: turn from foes to the Name. Mercy, grace, slowness to anger, hesed and truth are the shield.

Reformed

Calvin: from Exod 34:6—merciful, ready to forgive, long-suffering, abundant in mercy and truth. God by nature gracious; severity compelled by obstinate wickedness. Truth joined to mercy because we distrust promises.

Reformed pastoral: shared—merciful, gracious, slow to anger, abounding in love and faithfulness. Nature of God allays terror under scourges.

Psalms 86:16

16Turn to me and be gracious to me; give your strength to your servant, and save the son of your maidservant.

Arminian

Turn to me and be gracious to me; give your strength to your servant, and save the son of your maidservant. Shared: turn, grace, strength, save the household servant.

Arminian pastoral: apply the attributes of v. 15 to this servant. Strength given is grace's muscle.

Reformed

Calvin: apply mercy to himself; proceeds from free goodness. Servant and son of handmaid urge long ancestral grace—born a household servant—not boasting of services.

Reformed pastoral: shared—turn; be gracious; give strength; save. Destitution of own strength confesses need.

Psalms 86:17

17Show me a sign of your favor, that those who hate me may see and be put to shame because you, Lord, have helped me and comforted me.

Arminian

Show me a sign of your favor, that those who hate me may see and be put to shame because you, Lord, have helped me and comforted me. Shared close: token of favor; haters shamed by help and comfort.

Arminian pastoral: ask a visible good for God's glory before scoffers. Help and comfort are the LORD's signature.

Reformed

Calvin: desire for a token implies feeling forsaken; steadfastness descries light in darkness. Enemies mocked trust; shame them by God's succor.

Reformed pastoral: shared—sign of favor; haters ashamed; helped and comforted. Faith asks a token without despising the hidden God.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the poor and needy heard, the godly servant preserved, daily cry for mercy, goodness and forgiving love to all who call, none like the LORD among the gods, nations worshiping, a united heart to fear his name, deliverance from Sheol's depths, and a token of favor before insolent foes—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 86:1–17.

Arminian Notable voices

“Holy—Sincerely devoted to thy service. Truth—In the way of thy precepts, which are true and right in all things. My heart—Knit my whole heart to thyself. Hell—From extreme dangers and miseries.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 86

Reformed Notable voices

“Incline thy ear; I am poor and needy—nothing more suitable to God's nature than to succor the afflicted. Preserve my soul, for I am meek; save thy servant who trusteth in thee—clemency and trust, not meriting obligation. Have mercy; I cry daily. Thou art good and propitious, of great mercy to all who call—yet restricted to the faithful who call; those who chafe perish. Among the gods none like thee; all nations shall come and worship—eye to Christ's kingdom. Show me thy ways; unite my heart—both light and uprightness are Spirit's gifts; free will destitute of both. Great mercy delivered from the lower grave. The proud rise; but thou art merciful and gracious. Look to me; save the son of thy handmaid; make a sign for good.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 86
