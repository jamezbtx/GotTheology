Passage

Psalms 105

Verses 1–45 · ESV · Voices

Israel is called to thank the Lord and remember His wondrous works: the everlasting covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; protection of the patriarchs; Joseph sent ahead through famine and fetters; fruitfulness in Egypt; Moses and Aaron; the plagues; the exodus with silver and gold; cloud, quail, manna, and water from the rock—so the people might keep His statutes. Praise the Lord.

Psalms 105:1

1Oh give thanks to the Lord; call upon his name; make known his deeds among the peoples!

Arminian

Oh give thanks; call upon His name; make known His deeds among the peoples. The psalm opens as mission-shaped memory—thanks that goes public among the nations.

Both camps: thanksgiving and proclamation belong together. Classical Arminians hear a sincere call to make deeds known widely—the peoples are addressed as hearers of what God has done for Abraham's line.

Reformed

Calvin's psalm magnifies singular grace in selecting and freely adopting one people from all nations. Opening thanks and calling on the name serve that story: deeds made known so the covenant God is confessed.

Reformed accent: praise that publishes particular mercy. Shared: give thanks; call; make known among the peoples.

Psalms 105:2

2Sing to him, sing praises to him; tell of all his wondrous works!

Arminian

Sing to Him; tell of all His wondrous works. Song and storytelling are twin duties—works not left as private family lore.

Shared: wondrous works deserve voice. Classical Arminians stress telling as willing witness under grace.

Reformed

Calvin reads the history that follows as proof that covenant was not word only. Singing and telling prepare the congregation to walk Abraham to Canaan under God's hand.

Reformed pastoral: narrative praise. Shared: sing and tell all His works.

Psalms 105:3

3Glory in his holy name; let the hearts of those who seek the Lord rejoice!

Arminian

Glory in His holy name; let hearts of those who seek the Lord rejoice. Wesley: glory in the God whom you serve as the only true God. Boasting relocated to the Name.

Arminian seek: those who seek rejoice—seeking is real motion of the heart enabled by grace, not a meritorious climb.

Reformed

Calvin's frame: glory in the holy name of the God who chose a people. Seeking hearts rejoice because the Name is trustworthy in the story about to be told.

Reformed accent: glory in the Name that marked Jacob. Shared: seek and rejoice.

Psalms 105:4

4Seek the Lord and his strength; seek his presence continually!

Arminian

Seek the Lord and His strength; seek His presence continually. Wesley: seek the Lord in His strength—in His sanctuary, before the ark called God's strength; face—His gracious presence. Continual seeking, not a single visit.

Both camps: presence sought always. Classical Arminians hear ongoing pursuit—resistible still if hearts cool—yet commanded as sincere duty.

Reformed

Calvin: strength and presence language binds worship to the God of the ark and the God of the exodus about to be rehearsed. Continual seeking matches everlasting covenant.

Reformed pastoral: seek where He has pledged to meet His people. Shared: strength and presence, continually.

Psalms 105:5

5Remember the wondrous works that he has done, his miracles, and the judgments he uttered,

Arminian

Remember wondrous works, miracles, and judgments He uttered. Wesley: judgments—punishments on Egypt by mere word. Memory is obedience; forgetting is the first unfaithfulness.

Shared: remember. Classical Arminians preach memory that can be neglected—hence the command—without denying that God keeps His own remembrance perfectly (v. 8).

Reformed

Calvin will soon say God "remembers" covenant; here the people must remember works and uttered judgments. Miracles are not entertainment; they are covenant enforcement in history.

Reformed accent: remember what God did and said. Shared: works, miracles, judgments.

Psalms 105:6

6O offspring of Abraham, his servant, children of Jacob, his chosen ones!

Arminian

O offspring of Abraham His servant, children of Jacob, His chosen ones! Wesley: of Jacob—the only branch of Abraham's seed to whom the following blessings belong. Address is particular.

Arminian hearing: "chosen ones" names the covenant people called into privilege and responsibility. Classical Arminians confess real election to service and blessing in Israel without reading every "chosen" as unconditional individual reprobation of the nations as such—and still refuse a God who fails His promise.

Reformed

Calvin: seed of Abraham His servant, sons of Jacob His chosen—peculiar favor to Abraham's offspring while God reigns over all nations. The address fixes who is summoned to remember.

Reformed confession: chosen ones as the people God freely adopted from the nations. Shared: Abraham's offspring, Jacob's children, addressed as chosen.

Key contrast Both confess Israel addressed as Abraham's offspring and Jacob's chosen ones. Arminians typically hear covenant election of this people to blessing and vocation, with mercy sincerely published among the peoples (v. 1). Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's peculiar favor: God freely selected and adopted this people from all nations, the following deliverance flowing from that choice.

Psalms 105:7

7He is the Lord our God; his judgments are in all the earth.

Arminian

He is the Lord our God; His judgments are in all the earth. Wesley: God executes judgments upon all nations and people. "Our God" does not shrink His jurisdiction.

Both camps: particular covenant, universal judgment. Classical Arminians refuse a tribal deity; Reformed refuse a covenant without the Judge of all the earth.

Reformed

Calvin links "our God" to judgments through all the earth—reign alike over nations, peculiar favor to Abraham's line. The next verses prove the covenant by history.

Reformed accent: Lord of all, God of ours. Shared: judgments in all the earth.

Psalms 105:8

8He remembers his covenant forever, the word that he commanded, for a thousand generations,

Arminian

He remembers His covenant forever, the word He commanded for a thousand generations. Wesley: remembered—so as to perform it; word—the promise; commanded—established; thousand generations—to all generations. Memory that performs.

Arminian comfort: forever means God does not drop the promise when centuries yawn. Classical Arminians stress faithful performance toward the covenant people and, in gospel fullness, toward all who are in Abraham's seed by faith.

Reformed

Calvin: remembers His covenant forever—celebrates effect and fulfillment. Ancient covenant caused deliverance for the chosen tribes. "Remember" intimates promises do not become obsolete by time; when the world imagines them forgotten, God retains distinct remembrance to accomplish them in due season. Not for a day—God of the seed to a thousand generations.

Reformed comfort: everlasting remembrance effectual for the promised seed. Shared: covenant forever; thousand generations.

Key contrast Both confess God remembers His covenant forever and keeps the word across generations. Arminians typically emphasize faithful performance of the promise and its gospel widening to believers. Reformed readers typically emphasize Calvin's point: the ancient covenant caused deliverance for the chosen tribes; delay never erased God's determinate remembrance.

Psalms 105:9

9the covenant that he made with Abraham, his sworn promise to Isaac,

Arminian

The covenant made with Abraham, His sworn promise to Isaac. Wesley: oath—wherewith He ratified the covenant with Isaac (Gen 26:3). Promise climbs the patriarchs by oath.

Shared: Abraham and Isaac under sworn mercy. Classical Arminians preach oath as assurance to faith, not a puzzle about divine changeableness.

Reformed

Calvin traces the covenant made with Abraham and the oath to Isaac—form and tenor matching accomplishment. Patriarchal oath grounds national rescue.

Reformed accent: sworn promise, not bare wish. Shared: Abraham's covenant; Isaac's oath.

Psalms 105:10

10which he confirmed to Jacob as a statute, to Israel as an everlasting covenant,

Arminian

Confirmed to Jacob as a statute, to Israel as an everlasting covenant. Wesley: a law—that it might be as firm and irrevocable as a law. Statute-strength permanence.

Both camps: everlasting. Classical Arminians hear irrevocable kindness that still calls for keeping statutes (v. 45)—grace that binds without becoming license.

Reformed

Calvin: established to Jacob for a law, to Israel for an everlasting covenant. Fulfillment long suspended still showed the promise did not fail by length of time.

Reformed confession: statute-firm, everlasting. Shared: confirmed to Jacob; everlasting to Israel.

Psalms 105:11

11saying, "To you I will give the land of Canaan as your portion for an inheritance."

Arminian

Saying, "To you I will give the land of Canaan as your portion for an inheritance." Wesley: lot—portion assigned by lot. Land is gift-speech before conquest.

Shared: Canaan promised. Classical Arminians hear a concrete gift God freely binds himself to give; gospel readers may also confess Christ as the heir in whom the promise is yes and amen (2 Cor 1:20), without thinning the land word itself.

Reformed

Calvin: I will give thee the land of Canaan, the cord of your inheritance. The content of the remembered covenant is concrete: land as portion.

Reformed accent: gift of land by word. Shared: Canaan as inheritance promised.

Psalms 105:12

12When they were few in number, of little account, and sojourners in it,

Arminian

When they were few, of little account, and sojourners in it. Weakness is the stage for protection—few, not formidable.

Both camps: sojourner mercy. Classical Arminians hear care for the vulnerable without needing numerical strength to obligate God.

Reformed

Calvin's history begins with patriarchal fragility—few and sojourning—so later fruitfulness and exodus shine as grace, not demographic destiny.

Reformed pastoral: little account, great Keeper. Shared: few sojourners under promise.

Psalms 105:13

13wandering from nation to nation, from one kingdom to another people,

Arminian

Wandering from nation to nation, from one kingdom to another people. Wesley: they went—both in Canaan among seven nations, and in Egypt. Pilgrim geography.

Shared: wandering under watch along the story's road.

Reformed

Calvin: nation to nation under the same covenant God who will rebuke kings. Mobility does not equal abandonment.

Reformed accent: wandering inside remembrance. Shared: from kingdom to kingdom.

Psalms 105:14

14he allowed no one to oppress them; he rebuked kings on their account,

Arminian

He allowed no one to oppress them; He rebuked kings on their account. Patriarchs live under a shield—kings warned off.

Arminian reading: "allowed no one" is real restraint of oppressors. Classical Arminians confess providence that can rebuke kings without denying the kings' agency in the scenes Genesis records.

Reformed

Calvin: protection of the anointed wanderers—God's hand on kings for the patriarchs' sake. Rebuke is effectual enough that oppression is held back.

Reformed accent: kings rebuked on their account. Shared: no oppressor allowed; kings warned.

Psalms 105:15

15saying, "Touch not my anointed ones, do my prophets no harm!"

Arminian

Saying, "Touch not my anointed ones, do my prophets no harm!" Wesley: anointed—Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, consecrated as peculiar people and princes in their families; prophets—because God conversed with them and revealed His will. Anointed and prophets are the patriarchs here.

Both camps: touch-not is God's speech. Shared christology where agreed may later hear royal-prophetic lines without forcing a camp war on typology in this verse.

Reformed

Calvin treats the line as God's charge protecting the patriarchal prophets. The quotation seals why kings were rebuked.

Reformed pastoral: anointed sojourners under ban of heaven. Shared: touch not; do no harm.

Psalms 105:16

16When he summoned a famine on the land and broke all supply of bread,

Arminian

When He summoned a famine on the land and broke all supply of bread. Wesley: staff of bread—bread the support of life. Famine is summoned—called for.

Arminian sovereignty: God can summon famine as judgment and means without being author of Egyptian cruelty later. Classical Arminians affirm real secondary causes of drought and market under that summons.

Reformed

Calvin's narrative logic: famine summoned so Joseph's sending ahead (v. 17) is providence, not coincidence. Breaking bread's staff is the Lord's act in history.

Reformed sovereignty: summoned famine as deliberate means. Shared: God called for famine; bread's supply broke.

Key contrast Both confess that God summoned the famine and broke the supply of bread. Arminians typically hear effectual governance through real natural and social second causes. Reformed readers typically hear the famine as God's intentional means preparing Joseph's mission—not blind ecological luck.

Psalms 105:17

17he had sent a man ahead of them, Joseph, who was sold as a slave.

Arminian

He had sent a man ahead of them, Joseph, who was sold as a slave. Sale and sending in one verse—human crime inside divine mission (cf. Gen 45:5; 50:20).

Arminian reading: brothers sell; God sends. Classical Arminians hold both without making the brothers innocent or God the author of their envy—governance of evil acts without erase of blame.

Reformed

Calvin: Joseph sent ahead—sold as slave—yet the sending is God's. The chain from pit to palace is covenant logistics.

Reformed confession: sent by God, sold by men. Shared: Joseph ahead of them as providence.

Key contrast Both confess Joseph sold as a slave and sent ahead by God. Arminians typically stress dual agency: sinful selling remains culpable under providence that overrules for life. Reformed readers typically stress God's determinate sending through the sale so the covenant family is preserved.

Psalms 105:18

18His feet were hurt with fetters; his neck was put in a collar of iron;

Arminian

His feet hurt with fetters; his neck in a collar of iron. Suffering is not edited out of the success story—iron before lordship.

Shared: real fetters. Classical Arminians refuse a providence that skips the collar; Reformed refuse a collar outside God's watch.

Reformed

Calvin's path: humiliation precedes elevation. Iron collar is the road tested by the word (v. 19).

Reformed pastoral: fetters under the Sender. Shared: hurt feet; iron neck.

Psalms 105:19

19until what he had said came to pass, the word of the Lord tested him.

Arminian

Until what he had said came to pass, the word of the Lord tested him. Wesley: his word—revelation first to Pharaoh in a dream, then to Joseph in interpretation; purged from calamities, preparing release. Testing by the word.

Both camps: word tests. Classical Arminians hear a trial that proves and refines without teaching that God tempts to evil.

Reformed

Calvin: until the word came to pass—the Lord's word tested him. Promise and prison run together until the time.

Reformed accent: tested by the word until fulfillment. Shared: saying comes to pass; word tests Joseph.

Psalms 105:20

20The king sent and released him; the ruler of the peoples set him free;

Arminian

The king sent and released him; the ruler of the peoples set him free. Royal initiative on the surface; covenant timing underneath.

Shared: release by the king. Classical Arminians note Pharaoh's act as real; Reformed note it as instrument.

Reformed

Calvin: king and ruler free him—means of the elevation God intended when He sent Joseph ahead.

Reformed pastoral: release on time. Shared: sent and released; set free.

Psalms 105:21

21he made him lord of his house and ruler of all his possessions,

Arminian

He made him lord of his house and ruler of all his possessions. From collar to keys—house lordship.

Both camps: "He made" points past Pharaoh to the Lord of the psalm. Promotion is gift.

Reformed

Calvin: lord of the house, ruler of possessions—Joseph's authority as providence for the family's coming down.

Reformed accent: made lord by God's story. Shared: house and possessions under Joseph.

Psalms 105:22

22to bind his princes at his pleasure and to teach his elders wisdom.

Arminian

To bind his princes at his pleasure and to teach his elders wisdom. Authority to restrain and to instruct—power with pedagogy.

Shared: princes and elders under Joseph's hand. Wisdom taught to Egypt for Israel's sake.

Reformed

Calvin: binding princes and teaching elders—completeness of rule granted for the famine years.

Reformed pastoral: wisdom in the foreign court. Shared: bind and teach at pleasure.

Psalms 105:23

23Then Israel came to Egypt; Jacob sojourned in the land of Ham.

Arminian

Then Israel came to Egypt; Jacob sojourned in the land of Ham. Wesley: Ham—father of Mizraim/Egyptians (Gen 10:6). Descent into Ham's land is covenant travel.

Both camps: sojourn, not abandonment. Classical Arminians hear a temporary stay under promise of land still unpaid in full.

Reformed

Calvin: Israel in Egypt, Jacob in Ham—stage set for fruitfulness and oppression. Sojourning precedes the exodus joy.

Reformed accent: Ham's land under the Lord's timeline. Shared: Israel came; Jacob sojourned.

Psalms 105:24

24And the Lord made his people very fruitful and made them stronger than their foes.

Arminian

The Lord made His people very fruitful and made them stronger than their foes. Fruitfulness is the Lord's making—Exodus 1 echoed.

Arminian reading: God makes fruitful; growth is real population under blessing. Strength relative to foes prepares the envy of v. 25 without excusing it.

Reformed

Calvin: increased and strengthened by the Lord—grace in demographics before the turning of Egyptian hearts.

Reformed confession: made fruitful, made stronger. Shared: the Lord's making, not Egypt's gift.

Psalms 105:25

25He turned their hearts to hate his people, to deal craftily with his servants.

Arminian

He turned their hearts to hate His people, to deal craftily with His servants. Wesley: turned—that is, suffered them to be turned. The hardest line in the psalm for dual agency.

Arminian sovereignty: hatred is Egypt's sin; "turned" is permission-governance language in Wesley's note—God judges and orders history without authoring the hate as holy. Crafty dealing remains culpable.

Reformed

Calvin reads the turning inside the same providence that sent Joseph and summoned famine: God hardens/turns as Judge while Egypt remains wicked. Hearts turned to hate is not a surprise detour from the covenant plan but the dark road to the exodus.

Reformed sovereignty: effectual turning of hearts in judgment, not bare foresight. Shared: hate and craft against His servants under the Lord's rule of the story.

Key contrast Both confess that Egyptian hatred and craft sit under God's rule of history. Arminians typically follow Wesley's "suffered them to be turned": God governs and judges without being the author of their hate; Egypt's craft remains their sin. Reformed readers typically hear an effectual judicial turning of hearts that advances the covenant purpose, while still charging Egypt with wicked oppression.

Psalms 105:26

26He sent Moses, his servant, and Aaron, whom he had chosen.

Arminian

He sent Moses His servant, and Aaron, whom He had chosen. Sending and choosing again—instruments named.

Arminian hearing: chosen for the mission of deliverance. Classical Arminians confess God's selection of Moses and Aaron for office without requiring that "chosen" here settle the whole ordo of individual salvation.

Reformed

Calvin: Moses sent, Aaron chosen—deliverers appointed by the same God who turned Egypt and remembered Abraham.

Reformed accent: chosen servants for the exodus. Shared: Moses sent; Aaron chosen.

Key contrast Both confess Moses sent and Aaron chosen by God. Arminians typically emphasize vocational election to the deliverance office. Reformed readers typically place this choosing inside the same free adoption of the people that structures the whole psalm.

Psalms 105:27

27They performed his signs among them and miracles in the land of Ham.

Arminian

They performed His signs among them and miracles in the land of Ham. Signs are His; performance is through servants.

Shared: miracles in Ham. Classical Arminians and Reformed alike refuse naturalistic erasure of the plagues in this telling.

Reformed

Calvin: signs and wonders in Ham—public proof of the arm that keeps covenant. Performance answers the "uttered judgments" of v. 5.

Reformed pastoral: signs among them. Shared: His signs; their performance.

Psalms 105:28

28He sent darkness, and made the land dark; they did not rebel against his words.

Arminian

He sent darkness, and made the land dark; they did not rebel against His words. Wesley: they—Moses and Aaron, who inflicted the plague after Pharaoh threatened them. Agents obedient in the dark plague.

Both camps: darkness sent. The "did not rebel" comforts regarding the messengers' fidelity in Wesley's reading.

Reformed

Calvin: darkness sent—first of the rehearsed plagues in this telling. Land made dark by His sending.

Reformed accent: He sent; land dark. Shared: darkness as judgment sign.

Psalms 105:29

29He turned their waters into blood and caused their fish to die.

Arminian

He turned their waters into blood and caused their fish to die. Nile judgment—life-source made death.

Shared: turned waters; fish died. Both camps preach real plague, not mere symbol emptied of force.

Reformed

Calvin's plague chain: waters to blood—undoing Egypt's boast in the river.

Reformed pastoral: turned and caused. Shared: blood and dead fish.

Psalms 105:30

30Their land swarmed with frogs, even in the chambers of their kings.

Arminian

Their land swarmed with frogs, even in the chambers of their kings. Wesley: land—their country; chambers—entered in; kings—Pharaoh, sons, chief nobles. No palace privacy against frogs.

Shared: swarm to the throne room. Judgment ignores velvet curtains.

Reformed

Calvin: frogs even in royal chambers—humiliation of kings who oppressed the servants.

Reformed accent: chambers of kings invaded. Shared: land swarmed.

Psalms 105:31

31He spoke, and there came swarms of flies, and gnats throughout their country.

Arminian

He spoke, and there came swarms of flies, and gnats throughout their country. Wesley: coasts—all their land to the utmost borders. Speech summons pests.

Both camps: He spoke—and they came. Word-agency in judgment.

Reformed

Calvin: He spoke—flies and gnats nationwide. The utterance of v. 5 made visible.

Reformed confession: speech effects the swarm. Shared: flies and gnats at His word.

Psalms 105:32

32He gave them hail for rain, and fiery lightning bolts through their land.

Arminian

He gave them hail for rain, and fiery lightning bolts through their land. Rain replaced by hail and fire—sky turned against the fields.

Shared: gave hail; lightning through the land. Gift-language for judgment.

Reformed

Calvin: hail and fire—agricultural ruin as covenant vengeance on oppressors.

Reformed accent: He gave hail for rain. Shared: fiery bolts through the land.

Psalms 105:33

33He struck down their vines and fig trees, and shattered the trees of their country.

Arminian

He struck down their vines and fig trees, and shattered the trees of their country. Cash crops and shade alike shattered.

Both camps: struck and shattered—verbs of the Lord's war on Egypt's pleasant plantings.

Reformed

Calvin: vines, figs, trees—economic and ecological undoing.

Reformed pastoral: struck down; shattered. Shared: trees of the country broken.

Psalms 105:34

34He spoke, and the locusts came, young locusts without number,

Arminian

He spoke, and the locusts came, young locusts without number. Again speech; again innumerable devourers.

Shared: He spoke; locusts came. Numberless under command.

Reformed

Calvin: locusts at His speech—without number, as the sea's creatures were innumerable under kindness in Psalm 104; here innumerable under judgment.

Reformed accent: speech and locusts. Shared: young locusts without number.

Psalms 105:35

35which devoured all the vegetation in their land and ate up the fruit of their ground.

Arminian

Which devoured all vegetation and ate up the fruit of their ground. Total crop loss—ground stripped.

Both camps: all vegetation. The land that starved Israel by famine (v. 16) now starves Egypt by locust.

Reformed

Calvin: devouring completion before the firstborn blow. Fruit of the ground gone.

Reformed pastoral: ate up the fruit. Shared: vegetation and fruit consumed.

Psalms 105:36

36He struck down all the firstborn in their land, the firstfruits of all their strength.

Arminian

He struck down all the firstborn in their land, the firstfruits of all their strength. Climactic plague—firstborn as firstfruits of strength.

Shared: all the firstborn struck. Both camps take the death of firstborn as historical judgment, not a metaphor to soften. Fairness: grief is real; God's justice in the story is real.

Reformed

Calvin: firstborn struck—firstfruits of strength removed. The last Egyptian word before silver and gold exit.

Reformed confession: He struck down all the firstborn. Shared: firstfruits of strength cut off.

Psalms 105:37

37Then he brought out Israel with silver and gold, and there was none among his tribes who stumbled.

Arminian

Then He brought out Israel with silver and gold, and none among His tribes stumbled. Wesley: feeble—diseased or unable for journey; in so vast a body so oppressed, wonderful. Strength for the march is miracle.

Arminian wonder: brought out with wealth; none stumbled—providence in legs as in plagues. Classical Arminians praise enabling mercy for the journey.

Reformed

Calvin: brought out with silver and gold—plundering as promised; no stumbler in the tribes. Exit under power.

Reformed accent: He brought out; none stumbled. Shared: silver, gold, steady tribes.

Psalms 105:38

38Egypt was glad when they departed, for dread of them had fallen upon it.

Arminian

Egypt was glad when they departed, for dread of them had fallen upon it. Gladness of relief—oppressors relieved to be rid of the people they hated.

Shared: dread fallen; Egypt glad at departure. Irony without cartooning.

Reformed

Calvin: Egypt glad—dread had fallen. The exit is mercy to Israel and judgment-fear to Egypt.

Reformed pastoral: dread and glad departure. Shared: Egypt glad when they left.

Psalms 105:39

39He spread a cloud for a covering, and fire to give light by night.

Arminian

He spread a cloud for a covering, and fire to give light by night. Wesley: covering—to protect from the sun's heat. Cloud and fire as traveling sanctuary.

Both camps: spread cloud; fire by night. Guidance and shade are gifts of the same God who sent darkness on Egypt.

Reformed

Calvin: cloud covering, fire lighting—presence on the road matching presence in the plagues.

Reformed accent: He spread; fire gave light. Shared: cloud by day logic; fire by night.

Psalms 105:40

40They asked, and he brought quail, and gave them bread from heaven in abundance.

Arminian

They asked, and He brought quail, and gave them bread from heaven in abundance. Wesley: first giving of quails (Ex 16)—refreshment notwithstanding their sin in desiring, graciously pardoned; bread—manna from the air called heaven. Asking meets abundance; sin meets pardon.

Arminian mercy: asked and received; pardon alongside provision. Classical Arminians hear generous answer without denying Israel's fault in the fuller Exodus account.

Reformed

Calvin: quail and bread from heaven—abundance in the wilderness from the remembered promise. Asking answered by gift.

Reformed pastoral: brought quail; gave bread in abundance. Shared: heaven's bread for askers.

Psalms 105:41

41He opened the rock, and water gushed out; it flowed through the desert like a river.

Arminian

He opened the rock, and water gushed out; it flowed through the desert like a river. Wesley: river—channels God provided; waters followed Israel in the march. Rock becomes riverbed.

Shared: opened rock; gushed water. Both camps may hear 1 Cor 10's spiritual Rock where they agree christologically, without a camp war on typology method here.

Reformed

Calvin: rock opened—water in desert like a river. Same providence that sent springs in Psalm 104 now splits stone for the covenant people.

Reformed accent: He opened; water gushed. Shared: desert flowing like a river.

Psalms 105:42

42For he remembered his holy promise, and Abraham, his servant.

Arminian

For He remembered His holy promise, and Abraham, His servant. The "for" grounds the whole rescue—memory of holy promise and of Abraham.

Both camps: remembrance as performance (Wesley on v. 8). Classical Arminians stress holy promise kept; Reformed stress the same as Calvin's non-obsolete covenant.

Reformed

Calvin: remembered holy promise and Abraham—returns to v. 8–9. Exodus is covenant fidelity in event form.

Reformed confession: remembered promise and Abraham. Shared: holy promise as the reason.

Psalms 105:43

43So he brought his people out with joy, his chosen ones with singing.

Arminian

So He brought His people out with joy, His chosen ones with singing. Wesley's line of chosen ones again—joy and singing as exit music.

Arminian hearing: chosen ones singing—the people brought out rejoicing. Election to deliverance celebrated in song; the nations still hear deeds (v. 1).

Reformed

Calvin: brought out with joy, chosen ones with singing—peculiar people exiting under grace. Joy is the mark of effectual rescue.

Reformed accent: chosen ones with singing. Shared: people out with joy.

Key contrast Both confess God brought His people out with joy and His chosen ones with singing. Arminians typically hear the covenant people gladly delivered, chosen for this redemption history. Reformed readers typically hear the freely adopted people effectually brought out—joy as the sound of God's keeping His oath.

Psalms 105:44

44And he gave them the lands of the nations, and they took possession of the fruit of the peoples' toil,

Arminian

He gave them the lands of the nations, and they took possession of the fruit of the peoples' toil. Wesley: labour—fruits of labour; cities, vineyards, olive-yards. Gift of others' toil as inheritance.

Both camps: gave and took possession. Hard providence toward the nations driven out is stated, not softened into modern slogans; both camps still confess the Judge of all the earth does right.

Reformed

Calvin: lands of the nations given—possession of peoples' fruit. Promise of Canaan (v. 11) paid in history.

Reformed confession: He gave; they possessed. Shared: lands and fruit as gift.

Psalms 105:45

45that they might keep his statutes and observe his laws. Praise the Lord!

Arminian

That they might keep His statutes and observe His laws. Praise the Lord! Purpose clause: inheritance unto obedience; then Hallelujah. Wesley ends the notes on observing statutes. Grace aims at glad law-keeping.

Arminian pastoral: brought out in order to keep—obedience as living response, enabled, not earning the exodus after the fact. Praise the Lord closes history with doxology. Both camps: statutes and laws matter after silver and gold; Reformed and Arminian together refuse antinomian exodus.

Reformed

Calvin's design statement for the psalm: God conferred innumerable benefits after Egypt that those delivered might faithfully keep His covenant and devote themselves unfeignedly to His service. Inheritance is unto observance; Praise the Lord seals it.

Reformed pastoral: kept by grace for keeping statutes. Shared: that they might keep and observe; Hallelujah.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the everlasting covenant remembered, Abraham's offspring as chosen ones, Joseph sent ahead, hearts turned in Egypt, and a people brought out with joy to keep God's statutes—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 105:1–45.

Arminian Notable voices

“Remembered—so as to perform it. The word—the promise. Commanded—established. Thousand generations—to all generations. Of Jacob—the only branch of Abraham's seed to whom the following blessings belong. Anointed—my prophets, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, consecrated to be his peculiar people. Turned—that is, suffered them to be turned. Feeble—diseased or unable for his journey; which in so vast a body, and in a people so dreadfully oppressed, was wonderful. Labour—the fruits of their labour; their cities, vineyards, olive-yards. That they might observe his statutes, and keep his laws.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Psalm 105)

Reformed Notable voices

“The Psalmist magnifies the singular grace of God displayed in selecting and freely adopting one people from amongst all nations. He hath remembered his covenant for ever: the ancient covenant was the cause of the deliverance granted to the chosen tribes. The Divine promises do not become obsolete by length of time; when the world imagines they are extinguished, God retains as distinct a remembrance of them as ever, that he may accomplish them in due season. He has promised to be the God of Abraham's seed even to a thousand generations. His design in innumerable benefits after Egypt was that those delivered might faithfully keep his covenant and devote themselves unfeignedly to his service.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Psalm 105)
