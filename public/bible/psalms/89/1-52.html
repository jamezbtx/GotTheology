Passage

Psalms 89

Verses 1–52 · WEB · Voices

A Maskil of Ethan the Ezrahite. I will sing of the loving kindness of the LORD forever and make known his faithfulness to all generations. Steadfast love built up forever; faithfulness established in the heavens. Covenant with the chosen; sworn to David—offspring and throne for all generations. Heavens praise wonders; none like the Lord among the holy ones; God of hosts mighty, faithfulness all around; sea ruled; Rahab crushed; heavens and earth his; Tabor and Hermon praise; righteousness and justice the foundation of the throne; hesed and faithfulness go before him. Blessed who learn to acclaim you. Of old in vision: David found, anointed, hand established; foes crushed; firstborn highest of kings; covenant firm; offspring forever as days of heaven; if children forsake—rod and stripes, yet steadfast love not removed, faithfulness not false, oath by holiness. But now cast off and rejected; covenant renounced in appearance; crown defiled in dust; walls breached; foes exalted; throne cast down; days of youth cut short. How long will you hide? Remember short time; where is steadfast love of old sworn to David? Servants mocked; footsteps of the anointed scorned. Blessed be the LORD forever more. Amen, and Amen.

Psalms 89:1

1I will sing of the loving kindness of the LORD forever. With my mouth, I will make known your faithfulness to all generations.

Arminian

I will sing of the loving kindness of the LORD forever. With my mouth, I will make known your faithfulness to all generations. Wesley: Sing—prefaces lest complainers of present miseries argue ingratitude for former mercies. Faithfulness—whatsoever befell us proceeded not from thy unfaithfulness.

Ethan starts with mercy-song before the crown falls (vv. 38–45). Praise first is not denial of pain; it frames the lament.

Reformed

Calvin: opens with mercies and covenant to fortify faith against temptation from begun decay; mercies plural—abundance against felt severity. Sing when tokens hide.

Break through despairing thoughts at prayer's outset by singing what God has sworn.

Psalms 89:2

2I indeed declare, “Love stands firm forever. You established the heavens. Your faithfulness is in them.”

Arminian

For I said, "Declare, Love stands firm forever. You established the heavens. Your faithfulness." Wesley: Establish—as firmly and durably as the heavens. Hesed built; faithfulness fixed like the sky.

"I indeed" is heart-fixed hope under chastisement. Build mercy as an everlasting structure when sight fails.

Reformed

Calvin: "I have said"—hope of loving-kindness fixed despite severe chastisement; erect in the heart that everlasting building of mercy; truth as stable as the heavens.

Built forever; established in the heavens. Fatherly affection continues even in anger.

Psalms 89:3

3“I have made a covenant with my chosen one, I have sworn to David, my servant,

Arminian

I have made a covenant with my chosen one; I have sworn to David my servant: Wesley: Chosen—with David, chosen to the kingdom. God's own speech; covenant and oath to David.

The "You have said" is stronger than our guess. Cling to the sworn word, not to mood.

Reformed

Calvin: God himself speaks—more forcible than the prophet alone; covenant with chosen David by free election; servant by royal call, not self-thrust.

Covenant with the chosen; sworn to David. Election and call ground the throne-promise.

Psalms 89:4

4‘I will establish your offspring forever, and build up your throne to all generations.’” Selah.

Arminian

I will establish your offspring forever, and build up your throne to all generations. Selah. Wesley: Build up—perpetuate the kingdom to thy posterity; promised upon condition, and literally accomplished in Christ. Offspring and throne forever; Selah.

The Selah invites a pause on the word “forever.” Historic line and Christ fulfillment belong in one hearing—hold both without splitting the psalm.

Reformed

Calvin: seed and throne forever—partly successors, strictly verified only in Christ for the Church's common good.

Perpetuity is Christ's before it is any earthly court.

Key contrast Both hear God's oath establishing David's offspring and throne forever, and both locate the fullest permanence in Christ. Arminians typically note Wesley's note that the kingdom was promised upon condition and literally accomplished in Christ—historical Davidic line under terms, Christ the literal fulfillment. Reformed interpreters stress Calvin's free election of David and the irrevocable divine voice: perpetuity belongs to the Mediator for the Church's good, not to human desert. Either reading: hesed and faithfulness undergird the throne-word; holy fear before the God who swears.

Psalms 89:5

5The heavens will praise your wonders, LORD, your faithfulness also in the assembly of the holy ones.

Arminian

Let the heavens will praise your wonders, LORD, your faithfulness also in the assembly of the holy ones! Wesley: Heavens—inhabitants of heaven; Faithfulness—shall be praised. Both sides agree: cosmic praise of wonders and faithfulness among the holy.

Covenant praise widens to heaven's choir. Wonders and faithfulness are not private only.

Reformed

Calvin: from word to works—wonders especially in preserving the Church; truth celebrated in the congregation of the saints.

Magnify the covenant by magnifying the God who keeps it.

Psalms 89:6

6For who in the skies can be compared to the LORD? Who among the sons of the heavenly beings is like the LORD,

Arminian

For who in the skies can be compared to the LORD? Who among the sons of the heavenly beings is like the LORD, Wesley: Among—the highest angels. No peer in the skies or among heavenly beings.

Incomparability is holy fear's first lesson. Do not forge companion gods from bright spirits.

Reformed

Calvin: who in the clouds compared to Jehovah? Among sons of the gods (angels) none like him—admiration must not multiply deities.

None comparable; none like the Lord. Angels remain servants; God alone is God.

Psalms 89:7

7a very awesome God in the council of the holy ones, to be feared above all those who are around him?

Arminian

“A very awesome God in the council of the holy ones, to be feared above all those who are around him?” Wesley: Feared—reverence; Saints—society of angels; All that—encompass his throne. Holy fear in the council; awesome above those around him.

Fear of reverence, not panic superstition. The council itself fears—so should we.

Reformed

Calvin: very terrible in the assembly of the saints; angels as around him like body-guards—keep them in their rank.

Greatly to be feared; awesome above all around him. Holy fear is shared piety.

Psalms 89:8

8LORD, God of Armies, who is a mighty one, like you? LORD, your faithfulness is around you.

Arminian

O LORD, God of Armies, who is a mighty one, like you? LORD, your faithfulness is around you? Wesley: Round thee—like a girdle; appears in all words and works. Hosts' God; unmatched might; faithfulness encircling.

Faithfulness as girdle—steady on every side. Approach with fear and with trust.

Reformed

Calvin: who is a strong God as thou? Truth round about—steadfast in promises before and behind, right and left.

Mighty as you are; faithfulness all around. Fear without being barred from approach.

Psalms 89:9

9You rule the pride of the sea. When its waves rise up, you calm them.

Arminian

You rule the pride of the sea. When its waves rise up, you calm them. Both traditions see: rule over raging sea; rising waves stilled.

Stilling is the Lord's act. Chaos waters answer his word.

Reformed

Calvin: read with v. 10—often of the Red Sea divided for the chosen tribes; past help grounds present confidence.

Rule the sea; still the waves. Exodus-shaped power serves the Church's hope.

Psalms 89:10

10You have broken Rahab in pieces, like one of the slain. You have scattered your enemies with your mighty arm.

Arminian

You have broken Rahab in pieces, like one of the slain. You have scattered your enemies with your mighty arm. Wesley: Rahab—Egypt. Rahab/Egypt crushed; enemies scattered by the mighty arm.

Egypt as carcass is history of grace, not myth theater. Remember scattering when foes rise.

Reformed

Calvin: Egypt overthrown as a wounded man; arm of strength—mode of acting always similar when wisdom requires.

Rahab crushed; enemies scattered. Past deliverance trains trust for present assault.

Psalms 89:11

11The heavens are yours. The earth also is yours, the world and its fullness. You have founded them.

Arminian

The heavens are yours, and the earth is yours as well; you founded the world and its fullness. Both note: heavens, earth, world and fullness—his by founding.

Ownership follows creation. Nothing in the theater is ownerless chance.

Reformed

Calvin: creator who also presides—absurd to think heavens revolve by chance or earth by random will.

Heavens yours; earth yours; you founded them. Providence continues creation's claim.

Psalms 89:12

12You have created the north and the south. Tabor and Hermon rejoice in your name.

Arminian

You have created the north and the south. Tabor and Hermon rejoice in your name. Wesley: Tabor/Hermon—parts of Canaan within and beyond Jordan; Rejoice—fruitful by thy favour. Compass points and mountains praise.

Local mountains joy by favour. Land prosperity is gift-speech, not nature's boast.

Reformed

Calvin: north, south, Tabor, Hermon—common speech: no part of the fabric fails to honor the Creator.

Created them; joyously praise your name. Geography itself is summoned to doxology.

Psalms 89:13

13You have a mighty arm. Your hand is strong, and your right hand is exalted.

Arminian

You have a mighty arm. Your hand is strong, and your right hand.

Power language fuels prayer later when the sword's edge is turned (v. 43).

Reformed

Calvin: arm furnished with power; hand with strength; right hand exalted—to encourage the godly, not empty spectacle.

Mighty arm; strong hand; high right hand. Strength celebrated for faith's sake.

Psalms 89:14

14Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne. Loving kindness and truth go before your face.

Arminian

Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne. Loving kindness and truth go before you. Wesley: Justice and judgment—justice in judging. Common confession: righteousness/justice under the throne; hesed and faithfulness as forerunners.

Throne ornaments are moral, not purple. Hesed and faithfulness process before his face.

Reformed

Calvin: righteousness and judgment pillars of the throne; mercy and truth as pursuivants—God's own sufficient splendor.

Foundation of the throne; hesed and faithfulness go before. Fix faith on these attributes.

Psalms 89:15

15Blessed are the people who learn to acclaim you. They walk in the light of your presence, LORD.

Arminian

Blessed are the people who learn to acclaim you; they walk in the light of your face, LORD; Wesley: Know—enjoy presence and ordinances called by trumpets; Walk—under comfortable favour. Blessed knowers of the shout; walk in face-light.

Festal shout is public worship joy. Walk in light—favour felt, not merely claimed.

Reformed

Calvin: blessed who know the joyful sound—special privilege to taste paternal goodness and walk in light of countenance.

Know the festal shout; walk in the light of your face. Happiness is knowing God as Father.

Psalms 89:16

16In your name they rejoice all day. In your righteousness, they are exalted.

Arminian

“In your name they rejoice all day. In your righteousness, they are exalted.” Wesley: Name—knowledge and remembrance of infinite power and goodness. Both readings hold: day-long exultation in the Name; exaltation in his righteousness.

All-day joy is Name-centered. Righteousness lifts; self-horn does not.

Reformed

Calvin: rejoice in his name; glory in his righteousness—abundant ground; daily marks steadfast perseverance.

Exult in your name; exalted in your righteousness. True magnanimity leans on grace alone.

Psalms 89:17

17For you are the glory of their strength. In your favor, our horn will be exalted.

Arminian

For you are the glory of their strength. In your favor, our horn will be exalted. Wesley: The glory—to thee alone belongs glory of valiant achievements. God the glory of strength; favour exalts the horn.

Give God the glory of every win. Favour, not muscle, lifts the horn.

Reformed

Calvin: glory of their strength—always sustained by present aid; exaltation depends on good pleasure alone.

Glory of their strength. In your favor, our horn. Yield him the praise of preservation.

Psalms 89:18

18For our shield belongs to the LORD, our king to the Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

For our shield belongs to the Lord, our king to the Holy One of Israel. Both agree: shield of the Lord; king belonging to the Holy One.

Defense and kingship are his gifts. Holy One of Israel owns the throne-story.

Reformed

Calvin: king metaphorically a buckler—defense from God; applies properly to Christ the guardian of our welfare.

Shield belongs to the Lord; our king to the Holy One. Temporal shadow points to Christ.

Psalms 89:19

19Then you spoke in vision to your saints, and said, “I have given strength to the warrior. I have exalted a young man from the people.

Arminian

Then you spoke in vision to your saints and said, I have given strength to the warrior. I have exalted a young man from the people. Wesley: Vision—usual prophetic mode; Holy one—prophets, especially Samuel and Nathan; Laid help—provided for my people upon a person of courage and wisdom. Agreed: old vision; help on the mighty; chosen from the people.

Help is granted, not seized. Chosen from the people—low origin, high call.

Reformed

Calvin: then/long since God spoke; help on the mighty whom he endued; exalted one chosen—free will of God, low estate before the hand stretched out.

Spoke in vision; help to one mighty; exalted one chosen. Divine appointment distinguishes this throne.

Psalms 89:20

20I have found David, my servant. I have anointed him with my holy oil,

Arminian

I have found David, my servant, and anointed him with my holy oil; shared: found David; holy oil anointing.

"found" is grace language. Anointing ratifies the servant-king.

Reformed

Calvin: found—free goodness preventing; servant by call; holy oil the cause of elevation, not David's policy.

Found David; anointed with holy oil. God found him; he did not climb alone.

Psalms 89:21

21with whom my hand shall be established. My arm will also strengthen him.

Arminian

“My hand shall be established” with him; “my arm will also strengthen him.” Both hear: hand established with him; arm strengthens.

Ongoing strength, not one-day crowning. The arm stays with the anointed.

Reformed

Calvin: guardian of the kingdom he founded—does not abandon works but carries them to completion.

Hand established; arm shall strengthen. Founder remains protector.

Psalms 89:22

22No enemy will tax him. No wicked man will oppress him.

Arminian

No enemy will tax him. No wicked man will oppress him. Wesley: Exact—not conquer or make tributary. Both see: enemy not outwitting; wicked not humbling.

Promise of protection amid foes. Outwitting fails against the Lord's hand.

Reformed

Calvin: enemy shall not exact; son of iniquity not afflict—throne secured though not without enemies.

Enemy shall not outwit; wicked shall not humble. Root remains until Christ establishes fully.

Psalms 89:23

23I will beat down his adversaries before him, and strike those who hate him.

Arminian

I will beat down his adversaries before him, and strike those who hate him. Undisputed: foes crushed before him; haters struck down.

God fights in front of the king. Hatred of the anointed meets divine stroke.

Reformed

Calvin: break oppressors before his face; strike haters—divine opposition to those who rise against the throne.

Crush foes; strike down haters. The Lord's "I will" is the battle line.

Psalms 89:24

24But my faithfulness and my loving kindness will be with him. In my name, his horn will be exalted.

Arminian

My faithfulness and my loving kindness will be with him. In my name, his horn will be exalted. Faithfulness and hesed with him; horn exalted in the Name.

Presence of hesed and faithfulness is the king's true wealth. The king’s horn is exalted in God’s name, not by self-promotion.

Reformed

Calvin: truth and mercy continue without intermission; horn—glory and power—flourishes by grace for David and Christ's Church.

Faithfulness and steadfast love with him; horn exalted in my name. Grace from first to last.

Psalms 89:25

25I will set his hand also on the sea, and his right hand on the rivers.

Arminian

I will set his hand also on the sea, and his right hand on the rivers. Wesley: Set—establish dominion; sea—Mediterranean; rivers—Euphrates branches; uttermost bounds of the land. Hand on sea; right hand on rivers.

Wide dominion is gift-map. Bounds of promise, not endless appetite.

Reformed

Calvin: extent of kingdom from sea to Euphrates—promise through Moses fulfilled in David's time as type.

Hand on the sea; right hand on the rivers. Scope of rule under God's "I will."

Psalms 89:26

26He will call to me, ‘You are my Father, my God, and the rock of my salvation!’

Arminian

He will call to me, You are my Father, my God, and the rock of my salvation.' Shared: cry—Father, my God, Rock of salvation.

Sonship speech is prayer. Rock of salvation is the cry's ground.

Reformed

Calvin: chief excellence—accounted Son of God in a special sense; ground of Hebrews 1:5 superiority.

You are my Father; my God; Rock of my salvation. Filial cry marks the true king.

Psalms 89:27

27I will also appoint him my firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth.

Arminian

And I will also appoint him my firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth. Wesley: First-born—son with privileges; in some sort David, properly Christ; Higher—more fully in the Messiah. No dispute here: firstborn; highest of earth-kings.

Firstborn privilege peaks in Christ. David sketches; Messiah fills.

Reformed

Calvin: first-born higher than kings—honor transcending men and angels in the person Christ represents.

Firstborn; highest of the kings of the earth. Rank is God's making.

Psalms 89:28

28I will keep my loving kindness for him forever more. My covenant will stand firm with him.

Arminian

My steadfast love I will keep my loving kindness for him forever more. My covenant will stand firm with him. Both affirm: hesed kept forever; covenant firm for him.

Keep and stand firm—God's verbs. Firmness is promised, not wished.

Reformed

Calvin: mercy first, then covenant—gratuitous cause; always merciful that the covenant never fail.

Steadfast love forever; covenant stand firm. Inviolability rests on God's good pleasure.

Psalms 89:29

29I will also make his offspring endure forever, and his throne as the days of heaven.

Arminian

I will also make his offspring endure forever, and his throne as the days of heaven. Wesley: Forever—sit on the throne forever; accomplished only in Christ. Offspring forever; throne as heaven's days.

Heaven-days duration outruns every court. Only Christ bears forever strictly.

Reformed

Calvin: seed forever; throne as days of heaven—everlasting duration found in Christ alone.

Offspring forever; throne as the days of the heavens. Perpetuity names the Mediator.

Psalms 89:30

30If his children forsake my law, and don’t walk in my ordinances;

Arminian

If his children forsake my law, and don’t walk in my ordinances, Shared: true if—children may forsake law and miss his rules.

The covenant honestly names the possibility of forsaking. No soft fiction that sons never stray.

Reformed

Calvin: posterity will fall—unless infinite mercy pardons, no article of covenant would stand; not total extinction of godliness always, but revolt needing pardon.

If children forsake. Adoption does not erase the flesh's falls.

Psalms 89:31

31if they break my statutes, and don’t keep my commandments;

Arminian

“If they break my statutes, and don’t keep my commandments.” Where they meet: violate statutes; not keep commandments—heinous terms on purpose.

Stacked "ifs" cover aggravated breach. Severity of words teaches hate of sin.

Reformed

Calvin: odious terms—forsake, violate, not walk, not keep—even heinous sins in view; patience extended far.

Violate statutes; not keep commandments. Lenity named without baiting liberty.

Psalms 89:32

32then I will punish their sin with the rod, and their iniquity with stripes.

Arminian

“Then I will punish their sin with the rod, and their iniquity with stripes.” Both traditions hear: then—rod for transgression; stripes for iniquity.

"then" is moral sequence. Rod is true; cheap grace is not the text.

Reformed

Calvin: does not promise escape unpunished—that would encourage sin; chastises with fatherly moderation (2 Sam 7:14).

Punish with the rod; iniquity with stripes. The discipline is medicine, not bare vengeance.

Psalms 89:33

33But I will not completely take my loving kindness from him, nor allow my faithfulness to fail.

Arminian

“But I will not completely take my loving kindness from him, nor allow my faithfulness to fail.” But—hesed not removed from him; faithfulness not falsified.

The "but" saves hope under the rod. Love stays with the line even when stripes fall.

Reformed

Calvin: loving-kindness not withdrawn from him—shift of person points to Christ the Head; faithfulness shall not fail; covenant effectual despite human inconstancy.

Will not remove steadfast love; not be false to faithfulness. The rod and the sure promise stand together.

Key contrast Both refuse to cancel the rod or the hesed: forsaking children are visited with stripes, yet steadfast love is not removed from the anointed line. Arminians typically pastor the discipline as moral consequence within a covenant that still holds by God's faithfulness—rod without final divorce. Reformed interpreters typically emphasize Calvin's fatherly moderation and the shift to "from him": pardon and perseverance flow through the sure Head so the Church is not consumed. Discipline and surety belong together; no open denial of wrath, no erasure of the oath.

Psalms 89:34

34I will not break my covenant, nor alter what my lips have uttered.

Arminian

I will not break my covenant, nor alter what my lips have uttered. God will not violate or alter his outgoing word.

He will not requite breach with breach. His lips' word outlasts ours.

Reformed

Calvin: will not break though they break—blots out sins; founded on immutability of his nature.

Will not break my covenant; not alter the word. Fix eyes on the covenant when moods quarrel.

Psalms 89:35

35Once I have sworn by my holiness, I will not lie to David.

Arminian

Once I have sworn by my holiness, I will not lie to David. Once-for-all oath by holiness; no lie to David.

For the church: "once" seals irrevocability. Holiness is the oath's weight.

Reformed

Calvin: once—irrevocable; sworn by holiness (sanctuary-accommodated); compassion for our distrust.

Sworn by my holiness; will not lie to David. Oath added so faith may rest.

Psalms 89:36

36His offspring will endure forever, his throne like the sun before me.

Arminian

His offspring will endure forever, his throne like the sun before me.

The throne’s endurance is compared to the sun. When earthly thrones shake, the simile still points past them.

Reformed

Calvin: sun as witness—more stable than lower elements; cannot be limited to David when throne was shaken; fulfilled in Christ.

Endure forever; throne as long as the sun. Promise outlives Rehoboam-scale losses.

Psalms 89:37

37It will be established forever like the moon, the faithful witness in the sky.” Selah.

Arminian

It will be established forever like the moon, the faithful witness in the sky; Wesley: Witness—the rainbow, Gen 9:12,16.

Wesley hears rainbow-covenant echo. Sky-witness means God stakes permanence publicly.

Reformed

Calvin: established as the moon; faithful witness in heaven—steadfast token; full sense in the Branch from Jesse.

Like the moon forever; faithful witness in the sky. Selah before the "But."

Psalms 89:38

38But you have rejected and spurned. You have been angry with your anointed.

Arminian

But you have rejected and spurned. You have been angry with your anointed. Wesley: But—having declared certainty, he now humbly expostulates. Both confess: but now—cast off, rejected; wrath against the anointed.

The turn is honest prayer, not atheism. Certainty of promise funds the protest.

Reformed

Calvin: complains that prophecy seemed to fail—not charging falsehood; unburdens to resist distrust; anger against the anointed as reason of change in appearance.

Cast off and rejected; wrath against your anointed. Lament inside faith, not against the oath.

Psalms 89:39

39You have renounced the covenant of your servant. You have defiled his crown in the dust.

Arminian

You have renounced the covenant of your servant. You have defiled his crown in the dust. Covenant seeming renounced; crown defiled in dust.

Dust-crown is the scandal. Plead it without rewriting vv. 1–37 as fiction.

Reformed

Calvin: made covenant to cease—to appearance; does not attribute forgetfulness to God; presents temptation so God may succor; crown cast to earth.

Renounced the covenant (in appearance); crown in the dust. Infirmity speaks; faith still holds the chosen king.

Key contrast Both keep the whole arc: verses 1–37's sure covenant must interpret verses 38–45's ruin, not the reverse. Arminians typically follow Wesley's "But": after declaring certainty, Ethan humbly expostulates—present voiding of the crown is lived contradiction pleaded before God, not a theory that God lied. Reformed readers typically follow Calvin: the complaint speaks according to appearance and infirmity while faith remains convinced the chosen king cannot finally be rejected; unburdening before God resists despair. Either reading: crown in the dust does not erase sworn hesed; holy fear and lament pray the promise back into view.

Psalms 89:40

40You have broken down all his hedges. You have brought his strongholds to ruin.

Arminian

You have broken down all his hedges. You have brought his strongholds to ruin. Walls breached; strongholds ruined—God acknowledged as actor.

For today: hedge language—kingdom exposed. Name God as the one who breached, then ask him to rebuild.

Reformed

Calvin: prophet ascribes razing to God—men could not destroy what God set up had anger not kindled.

Breached walls; strongholds in ruins. Sanctified feeling traces calamity to divine anger.

Psalms 89:41

41All who pass by the way rob him. He has become a reproach to his neighbors.

Arminian

All who pass by the way rob him. He has become a reproach to his neighbors. Both sides agree: passers-by plunder; scorn of neighbors.

Open field after broken hedges. Neighbor-scorn wounds the anointed's honor.

Reformed

Calvin: exposed as prey; reproach to neighbors—railings that "Is this God's excellent king?" recoil on God.

Plunder him; scorn of his neighbors. Mockery of the anointed is God's concern.

Psalms 89:42

42You have exalted the right hand of his adversaries. You have made all of his enemies rejoice.

Arminian

You have exalted the right hand of his adversaries. You have made all of his enemies rejoice. Both traditions see: foes' right hand exalted; enemies made to rejoice.

God took the enemy's side in the drama of judgment. Their joy is part of the plea.

Reformed

Calvin: God took part with oppressors—courage and faintness are his; kingdom reduced only when anger incensed.

Exalted foes' right hand; made enemies rejoice. Machinations succeed only under forsaking.

Psalms 89:43

43Yes, you turn back the edge of his sword, and haven’t supported him in battle.

Arminian

Yes, you turn back the edge of his sword, and haven’t supported him in battle. Both note: sword-edge turned; not made to stand in battle.

Blunt blade and failed stand—military shame under wrath. Ask for edge restored.

Reformed

Calvin: blunted edge; not made to stand—further evidences of being forsaken in war.

Turned back the edge; not made him stand. Battle failure is read theologically here.

Psalms 89:44

44You have ended his splendor, and thrown his throne down to the ground.

Arminian

You have ended his splendor, and thrown his throne down to the ground. Splendor ceased; throne cast to the ground.

Glory extinguished; seat overturned. Hold this with sun-and-moon oaths above.

Reformed

Calvin: effaced splendor; throne to the ground—confused spectacle that still drives the saint to the promise.

Splendor to cease; throne to the ground. Apparent discrepancy fuels bold appeal, not atheism.

Psalms 89:45

45You have shortened the days of his youth. You have covered him with shame. Selah.

Arminian

You have shortened the days of his youth. You have covered him with shame. Selah Wesley: Youth—flourishing estate of David's kingdom short, not beyond next successor. Common confession: youth cut short; covered with shame; Selah.

The kingdom has aged before reaching maturity. Shame-covering is public; Selah before "How long."

Reformed

Calvin: kingdom compared to a life—waxed old before complete blossom; shortened youth; shame covered.

Cut short the days of his youth; covered with shame. The dynasty’s brief bloom withers under judgment.

Psalms 89:46

46How long, LORD? Will you hide yourself forever? Will your wrath burn like fire?

Arminian

Will you hide yourself forever? Will your wrath burn like fire? Both readings hold: how long—hide forever?

Prayer replaces mere complaint. Hiding and fire-wrath are pleaded for an end.

Reformed

Calvin: turns to prayer—proof complaint was joined to faith; how long forever denotes continued calamity; afflictions from sins as scourges.

Ask the face; confess wrath's link to sin.

Psalms 89:47

47Remember how short my time is, for what vanity you have created all the children of men!

Arminian

Remember how short my time is, for what vanity you have created all the children of men! Wesley: Short—time of king and kingdom; Wherefore—made us and him for so little purpose after solemn covenant? Short time remembered; vanity of humankind.

Brief reign under oath feels like vanity. Plead the disproportion without charging God as cruel designer.

Reformed

Calvin: brevity of life—if no taste of goodness, creation seems in vain; yet complaint has excess needing pardon; life not profitless if Father-love is tasted.

Remember short time; vanity of the children of man. Mortality sharpens the ask for mercy now.

Psalms 89:48

48What man is he who shall live and not see death, who shall deliver his soul from the power of Sheol? Selah.

Arminian

What man is he who shall live and not see death, who shall deliver his soul from the power of Sheol? Selah Wesley: What man—all mortal and miserable; kings and people die; therefore do not increase affliction. Both agree: none escapes death; none self-delivers from Sheol; Selah.

Death levels kings with everyone else. Ask God not to pile affliction on the already dying.

Reformed

Calvin: what man (geber/strong) shall not see death?—no exemption; deliverance from the grave's hand is not human.

Live and not see death? deliver from Sheol? Death's universality humbles the plea.

Psalms 89:49

49Lord, where are your former loving kindnesses, which you swore to David in your faithfulness?

Arminian

Lord, where are your former loving kindnesses, which you swore to David in your faithfulness?

"where" is covenant memory, not amnesia of God. Old mercies argue present hope.

Reformed

Calvin: former mercies—God cannot be unlike himself; sworn in truth; same ground of liberality for posterity.

Steadfast love of old; swore to David by faithfulness. Unchangeable God is the argument.

Psalms 89:50

50Remember, Lord, the reproach of your servants, how I bear in my heart the taunts of all the mighty peoples,

Arminian

Remember, Lord, the reproach of your servants, how I bear in my heart the taunts of all the mighty peoples; Wesley: How I—we thy servants; king and people as one. Remember servants' mockery; insults borne in the bosom.

The “I” here speaks for both the king and the people. Bosom-borne scorn is intimate pain.

Reformed

Calvin: reproach of servants moves God; insults in the bosom—vomited on the children of God; plural to singular stirs each to pray.

Servants mocked; insults in my heart. Derision of faith is God's business to repress.

Psalms 89:51

51With which your enemies have mocked, LORD, with which they have mocked the footsteps of your anointed one.

Arminian

“With which your enemies have mocked, LORD, with which they have mocked the footsteps of your anointed.” Wesley: Anointed—kings of Judah, or Messiah whose expected coming heathens mocked; footsteps—ways or his coming. Enemies mock the Lord; mock the anointed's footsteps.

Mockery of footsteps hits God's Messiah-hope. Plead the insult as against him.

Reformed

Calvin: enemies of the people are enemies of God; footsteps of Messiah—coming derided as fable; railings against simplicity of faith.

Enemies mock; mock the footsteps of your anointed. Maintain your own cause, O Lord.

Psalms 89:52

52Blessed be the LORD forever more. Amen, and Amen.

Arminian

Blessed be the LORD forever more. Wesley: Blessed—let enemies reproach Messiah-promises; I will bless and encourage myself with them.

The book closes with a doxology after the crown has been thrown in the dust. Blessing is resistance to scorn and self-encouragement in the promises.

Reformed

Calvin: not a scribe's tack-on—praise after free complaint assuages grief; Amen and Amen; hope of deliverance kept alive.

Blessed be the LORD forever more. Whole arc ends in blessing, not in canceled hesed.

Voices from both camps

Classic Arminian and Reformed comment on Psalm 89:1–52, in the writers’ own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“Sing—prefaces lest complainers of present miseries argue ingratitude for former mercies. Faithfulness—whatsoever hath befallen us proceeded not from thy unfaithfulness. Establish—as firmly and durably as the heavens. Chosen—David, chosen to the kingdom. Build up—perpetuate the kingdom to thy posterity; promised upon condition, and literally accomplished in Christ. Heavens—inhabitants of heaven. Feared—reverence; saints—the society of angels. Rahab—Egypt. Tabor and Hermon—parts of Canaan; rejoice by thy favour. Joyful sound—presence and ordinances; walk under influences of favour. First-born—in some sort David, properly Christ. Forever—throne forever; only in Christ. Witness—the rainbow, Gen 9:12,16. But—having declared certainty, he now humbly expostulates. Youth—flourishing estate of David's kingdom short beyond his next successor. Short—time of our king and kingdom; wherefore made us and him for so little purpose? Anointed—kings of Judah, or the Messiah whose coming the heathen mocked. Blessed—let enemies reproach Messiah-promises; I will bless and encourage myself with them.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 89

Reformed Notable voices

“Opens with mercies and covenant to fortify faith against temptation from begun decay: sing mercies even when severity is felt; mercy built forever, truth as stable as the heavens. God himself speaks—covenant with David his chosen by free election, his servant by lawful call; throne forever verified in Christ for the Church's common good. Heavens praise wonders to magnify the covenant's holiness; none among sons of the gods like Jehovah; terrible in the assembly; faithfulness round about him. Sea ruled; Rahab/Egypt crushed; heavens and earth his; Tabor and Hermon rejoice; righteousness and judgment the place of his throne; mercy and truth go before his face. Blessed who know the joyful sound and walk in the light of his countenance; he is the glory of their strength; shield and king from the Holy One. Vision to the godly: help laid on the mighty, David found and anointed; hand established; foes broken; firstborn higher than kings; mercy kept forever; seed as days of heaven. If children forsake—rod and stripes in fatherly moderation, yet loving-kindness not withdrawn nor faithfulness failed; covenant not broken though they break; sworn once by holiness; seed and throne as sun and moon—fulfilled in Christ. But cast off—complaint of appearance, not a charge of falsehood; unburdening before God that faith may hold the promise; walls breached, crown defiled, how long wilt thou hide? Remember short time; where are former mercies sworn to David? Reproach of servants; footsteps of the Anointed mocked. Blessed be Jehovah forever—praise closing the grief.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 89
