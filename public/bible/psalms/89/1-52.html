Psalms 89

Verses 1–52 · ESV · Voices

A Maskil of Ethan the Ezrahite. I will sing of the steadfast love of the Lord forever and make known his faithfulness to all generations. Steadfast love built up forever; faithfulness established in the heavens. Covenant with the chosen; sworn to David—offspring and throne for all generations. Heavens praise wonders; none like the Lord among the holy ones; God of hosts mighty, faithfulness all around; sea ruled; Rahab crushed; heavens and earth his; Tabor and Hermon praise; righteousness and justice the foundation of the throne; hesed and faithfulness go before him. Blessed who know the festal shout. Of old in vision: David found, anointed, hand established; foes crushed; firstborn highest of kings; covenant firm; offspring forever as days of heaven; if children forsake—rod and stripes, yet steadfast love not removed, faithfulness not false, oath by holiness. But now cast off and rejected; covenant renounced in appearance; crown defiled in dust; walls breached; foes exalted; throne cast down; days of youth cut short. How long will you hide? Remember short time; where is steadfast love of old sworn to David? Servants mocked; footsteps of the anointed scorned. Blessed be the Lord forever! Amen and Amen.

Psalms 89:1

1I will sing of the steadfast love of the Lord, forever; with my mouth I will make known your faithfulness to all generations.

Arminian

I will sing of the steadfast love of the Lord, forever; with my mouth I will make known your faithfulness to all generations. Wesley: Sing—prefaces lest complainers of present miseries argue ingratitude for former mercies. Faithfulness—whatsoever befell us proceeded not from thy unfaithfulness. Shared: hesed sung forever; faithfulness made known across generations.

Arminian pastoral: Ethan starts with mercy-song before the crown falls (vv. 38–45). Praise first is not denial of pain; it frames the lament.

Reformed

Calvin: opens with mercies and covenant to fortify faith against temptation from begun decay; mercies plural—abundance against felt severity. Shared: sing when tokens hide.

Reformed pastoral: shared—hesed forever; faithfulness to all generations. Break through despairing thoughts at prayer's outset by singing what God has sworn.

Psalms 89:2

2For I said, "Steadfast love will be built up forever; in the heavens you will establish your faithfulness."

Arminian

For I said, "Steadfast love will be built up forever; in the heavens you will establish your faithfulness." Wesley: Establish—as firmly and durably as the heavens. Shared: hesed built; faithfulness fixed like the sky.

Arminian pastoral: "I said" is heart-fixed hope under chastisement. Build mercy as an everlasting structure when sight fails.

Reformed

Calvin: "I have said"—hope of loving-kindness fixed despite severe chastisement; erect in the heart that everlasting building of mercy; truth as stable as the heavens.

Reformed pastoral: shared—built forever; established in the heavens. Fatherly affection continues even in anger.

Psalms 89:3

3You have said, "I have made a covenant with my chosen one; I have sworn to David my servant:

Arminian

You have said, "I have made a covenant with my chosen one; I have sworn to David my servant: Wesley: Chosen—with David, chosen to the kingdom. Shared: God's own speech; covenant and oath to David.

Arminian pastoral: the "You have said" is stronger than our guess. Cling to the sworn word, not to mood.

Reformed

Calvin: God himself speaks—more forcible than the prophet alone; covenant with chosen David by free election; servant by royal call, not self-thrust.

Reformed pastoral: shared—covenant with the chosen; sworn to David. Election and call ground the throne-promise.

Psalms 89:4

4'I will establish your offspring forever, and build your throne for all generations.'" Selah

Arminian

I will establish your offspring forever, and build your throne for all generations. Selah. Wesley: Build up—perpetuate the kingdom to thy posterity; promised upon condition, and literally accomplished in Christ. Shared: offspring and throne forever; Selah.

Arminian pastoral: pause on forever. Historic line and Christ fulfillment belong in one hearing—hold both without splitting the psalm.

Reformed

Calvin: seed and throne forever—partly successors, strictly verified only in Christ for the Church's common good.

Reformed pastoral: shared—offspring forever; throne for all generations. Perpetuity is Christ's before it is any earthly court.

Key contrast Both hear God's oath establishing David's offspring and throne forever, and both locate the fullest permanence in Christ. Arminians typically note Wesley's lemma that the kingdom was promised upon condition and literally accomplished in Christ—historical Davidic line under real terms, Christ the literal fulfillment. Reformed readers typically stress Calvin's free election of David and the irrevocable divine voice: perpetuity belongs to the Mediator for the Church's good, not to human desert. Shared: hesed and faithfulness undergird the throne-word; holy fear before the God who swears.

Psalms 89:5

5Let the heavens praise your wonders, O Lord, your faithfulness in the assembly of the holy ones!

Arminian

Let the heavens praise your wonders, O Lord, your faithfulness in the assembly of the holy ones! Wesley: Heavens—inhabitants of heaven; Faithfulness—shall be praised. Shared: cosmic praise of wonders and faithfulness among the holy.

Arminian pastoral: covenant praise widens to heaven's choir. Wonders and faithfulness are not private only.

Reformed

Calvin: from word to works—wonders especially in preserving the Church; truth celebrated in the congregation of the saints.

Reformed pastoral: shared—heavens praise; faithfulness in the holy assembly. Magnify the covenant by magnifying the God who keeps it.

Psalms 89:6

6For who in the skies can be compared to the Lord? Who among the heavenly beings is like the Lord,

Arminian

For who in the skies can be compared to the Lord? Who among the heavenly beings is like the Lord, Wesley: Among—the highest angels. Shared: no peer in the skies or among heavenly beings.

Arminian pastoral: incomparability is holy fear's first lesson. Do not forge companion gods from bright spirits.

Reformed

Calvin: who in the clouds compared to Jehovah? Among sons of the gods (angels) none like him—admiration must not multiply deities.

Reformed pastoral: shared—none comparable; none like the Lord. Angels remain servants; God alone is God.

Psalms 89:7

7a God greatly to be feared in the council of the holy ones, and awesome above all who are around him?

Arminian

a God greatly to be feared in the council of the holy ones, and awesome above all who are around him? Wesley: Feared—reverence; Saints—society of angels; All that—encompass his throne. Shared: holy fear in the council; awesome above those around him.

Arminian pastoral: fear of reverence, not panic superstition. The council itself fears—so should we.

Reformed

Calvin: very terrible in the assembly of the saints; angels as around him like body-guards—keep them in their rank.

Reformed pastoral: shared—greatly to be feared; awesome above all around him. Holy fear is shared piety.

Psalms 89:8

8O Lord God of hosts, who is mighty as you are, O Lord, with your faithfulness all around you?

Arminian

O Lord God of hosts, who is mighty as you are, O Lord, with your faithfulness all around you? Wesley: Round thee—like a girdle; appears in all words and works. Shared: Hosts' God; unmatched might; faithfulness encircling.

Arminian pastoral: faithfulness as girdle—steady on every side. Approach with fear and with trust.

Reformed

Calvin: who is a strong God as thou? Truth round about—steadfast in promises before and behind, right and left.

Reformed pastoral: shared—mighty as you are; faithfulness all around. Fear without being barred from approach.

Psalms 89:9

Arminian

You rule the raging of the sea; when its waves rise, you still them. Shared: rule over raging sea; rising waves stilled.

Arminian pastoral: stilling is the Lord's act. Chaos waters answer his word.

Reformed

Calvin: read with v. 10—often of the Red Sea divided for the chosen tribes; past help grounds present confidence.

Reformed pastoral: shared—rule the sea; still the waves. Exodus-shaped power serves the Church's hope.

Psalms 89:10

10You crushed Rahab like a carcass; you scattered your enemies with your mighty arm.

Arminian

You crushed Rahab like a carcass; you scattered your enemies with your mighty arm. Wesley: Rahab—Egypt. Shared: Rahab/Egypt crushed; enemies scattered by the mighty arm.

Arminian pastoral: Egypt as carcass is history of grace, not myth theater. Remember scattering when foes rise.

Reformed

Calvin: Egypt overthrown as a wounded man; arm of strength—mode of acting always similar when wisdom requires.

Reformed pastoral: shared—Rahab crushed; enemies scattered. Past deliverance trains trust for present assault.

Psalms 89:11

11The heavens are yours; the earth also is yours; the world and all that is in it, you have founded them.

Arminian

The heavens are yours; the earth also is yours; the world and all that is in it, you have founded them. Shared: heavens, earth, world and fullness—his by founding.

Arminian pastoral: ownership follows creation. Nothing in the theater is ownerless chance.

Reformed

Calvin: creator who also presides—absurd to think heavens revolve by chance or earth by random will.

Reformed pastoral: shared—heavens yours; earth yours; you founded them. Providence continues creation's claim.

Psalms 89:12

12The north and the south, you have created them; Tabor and Hermon joyously praise your name.

Arminian

The north and the south, you have created them; Tabor and Hermon joyously praise your name. Wesley: Tabor/Hermon—parts of Canaan within and beyond Jordan; Rejoice—fruitful by thy favour. Shared: compass points and mountains praise.

Arminian pastoral: local mountains joy by favour. Land prosperity is gift-speech, not nature's boast.

Reformed

Calvin: north, south, Tabor, Hermon—common speech: no part of the fabric fails to honor the Creator.

Reformed pastoral: shared—created them; joyously praise your name. Geography itself is summoned to doxology.

Psalms 89:13

13You have a mighty arm; strong is your hand, high your right hand.

Arminian

You have a mighty arm; strong is your hand, high your right hand. Shared: mighty arm; strong hand; exalted right hand.

Arminian pastoral: power language fuels prayer later when the sword's edge is turned (v. 43).

Reformed

Calvin: arm furnished with power; hand with strength; right hand exalted—to encourage the godly, not empty spectacle.

Reformed pastoral: shared—mighty arm; strong hand; high right hand. Strength celebrated for faith's sake.

Psalms 89:14

14Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne; steadfast love and faithfulness go before you.

Arminian

Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne; steadfast love and faithfulness go before you. Wesley: Justice and judgment—justice in judging. Shared: righteousness/justice under the throne; hesed and faithfulness as forerunners.

Arminian pastoral: throne ornaments are moral, not purple. Hesed and faithfulness process before his face.

Reformed

Calvin: righteousness and judgment pillars of the throne; mercy and truth as pursuivants—God's own sufficient splendor.

Reformed pastoral: shared—foundation of the throne; hesed and faithfulness go before. Fix faith on these attributes.

Psalms 89:15

15Blessed are the people who know the festal shout, who walk, O Lord, in the light of your face,

Arminian

Blessed are the people who know the festal shout, who walk, O Lord, in the light of your face, Wesley: Know—enjoy presence and ordinances called by trumpets; Walk—under comfortable favour. Shared: blessed knowers of the shout; walk in face-light.

Arminian pastoral: festal shout is public worship joy. Walk in light—favour felt, not merely claimed.

Reformed

Calvin: blessed who know the joyful sound—special privilege to taste paternal goodness and walk in light of countenance.

Reformed pastoral: shared—know the festal shout; walk in the light of your face. Happiness is knowing God as Father.

Psalms 89:16

16who exult in your name all the day and in your righteousness are exalted.

Arminian

who exult in your name all the day and in your righteousness are exalted. Wesley: Name—knowledge and remembrance of infinite power and goodness. Shared: day-long exultation in the Name; exaltation in his righteousness.

Arminian pastoral: all-day joy is Name-centered. Righteousness lifts; self-horn does not.

Reformed

Calvin: rejoice in his name; glory in his righteousness—abundant ground; daily marks steadfast perseverance.

Reformed pastoral: shared—exult in your name; exalted in your righteousness. True magnanimity leans on grace alone.

Psalms 89:17

17For you are the glory of their strength; by your favor our horn is exalted.

Arminian

For you are the glory of their strength; by your favor our horn is exalted. Wesley: The glory—to thee alone belongs glory of valiant achievements. Shared: God the glory of strength; favour exalts the horn.

Arminian pastoral: give God the glory of every win. Favour, not muscle, lifts the horn.

Reformed

Calvin: glory of their strength—always sustained by present aid; exaltation depends on good pleasure alone.

Reformed pastoral: shared—glory of their strength; by your favor our horn. Yield him the praise of preservation.

Psalms 89:18

18For our shield belongs to the Lord, our king to the Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

For our shield belongs to the Lord, our king to the Holy One of Israel. Shared: shield of the Lord; king belonging to the Holy One.

Arminian pastoral: defense and kingship are his gifts. Holy One of Israel owns the throne-story.

Reformed

Calvin: king metaphorically a buckler—defense from God; applies properly to Christ the guardian of our welfare.

Reformed pastoral: shared—shield belongs to the Lord; our king to the Holy One. Temporal shadow points to Christ.

Psalms 89:19

19Of old you spoke in a vision to your godly one, and said: "I have granted help to one who is mighty; I have exalted one chosen from the people.

Arminian

Of old you spoke in a vision to your godly one, and said: "I have granted help to one who is mighty; I have exalted one chosen from the people. Wesley: Vision—usual prophetic mode; Holy one—prophets, especially Samuel and Nathan; Laid help—provided for my people upon a person of courage and wisdom. Shared: old vision; help on the mighty; chosen from the people.

Arminian pastoral: help is granted, not seized. Chosen from the people—low origin, high call.

Reformed

Calvin: then/long since God spoke; help on the mighty whom he endued; exalted one chosen—free will of God, low estate before the hand stretched out.

Reformed pastoral: shared—spoke in vision; help to one mighty; exalted one chosen. Divine appointment distinguishes this throne.

Psalms 89:20

20I have found David, my servant; with my holy oil I have anointed him,

Arminian

I have found David, my servant; with my holy oil I have anointed him, Shared: found David; holy oil anointing.

Arminian pastoral: "found" is grace language. Anointing ratifies the servant-king.

Reformed

Calvin: found—free goodness preventing; servant by call; holy oil the cause of elevation, not David's policy.

Reformed pastoral: shared—found David; anointed with holy oil. God found him; he did not climb alone.

Psalms 89:21

21so that my hand shall be established with him; my arm also shall strengthen him.

Arminian

so that my hand shall be established with him; my arm also shall strengthen him. Shared: hand established with him; arm strengthens.

Arminian pastoral: ongoing strength, not one-day crowning. The arm stays with the anointed.

Reformed

Calvin: guardian of the kingdom he founded—does not abandon works but carries them to completion.

Reformed pastoral: shared—hand established; arm shall strengthen. Founder remains protector.

Psalms 89:22

22The enemy shall not outwit him; the wicked shall not humble him.

Arminian

The enemy shall not outwit him; the wicked shall not humble him. Wesley: Exact—not conquer or make tributary. Shared: enemy not outwitting; wicked not humbling.

Arminian pastoral: promise of protection amid real foes. Outwitting fails against the Lord's hand.

Reformed

Calvin: enemy shall not exact; son of iniquity not afflict—throne secured though not without enemies.

Reformed pastoral: shared—enemy shall not outwit; wicked shall not humble. Root remains until Christ establishes fully.

Psalms 89:23

23I will crush his foes before him and strike down those who hate him.

Arminian

I will crush his foes before him and strike down those who hate him. Shared: foes crushed before him; haters struck down.

Arminian pastoral: God fights in front of the king. Hatred of the anointed meets divine stroke.

Reformed

Calvin: break oppressors before his face; strike haters—divine opposition to those who rise against the throne.

Reformed pastoral: shared—crush foes; strike down haters. The Lord's "I will" is the battle line.

Psalms 89:24

24My faithfulness and my steadfast love shall be with him, and in my name shall his horn be exalted.

Arminian

My faithfulness and my steadfast love shall be with him, and in my name shall his horn be exalted. Shared: faithfulness and hesed with him; horn exalted in the Name.

Arminian pastoral: presence of hesed and faithfulness is the king's true wealth. Name exaltation, not self-brand.

Reformed

Calvin: truth and mercy continue without intermission; horn—glory and power—flourishes by grace for David and Christ's Church.

Reformed pastoral: shared—faithfulness and steadfast love with him; horn exalted in my name. Grace from first to last.

Psalms 89:25

25I will set his hand on the sea and his right hand on the rivers.

Arminian

I will set his hand on the sea and his right hand on the rivers. Wesley: Set—establish dominion; sea—Mediterranean; rivers—Euphrates branches; uttermost bounds of the land. Shared: hand on sea; right hand on rivers.

Arminian pastoral: wide dominion is gift-map. Bounds of promise, not endless appetite.

Reformed

Calvin: extent of kingdom from sea to Euphrates—promise through Moses fulfilled in David's time as type.

Reformed pastoral: shared—hand on the sea; right hand on the rivers. Scope of rule under God's "I will."

Psalms 89:26

26He shall cry to me, 'You are my Father, my God, and the Rock of my salvation.'

He shall cry to me, 'You are my Father, my God, and the Rock of my salvation.' Shared: cry—Father, my God, Rock of salvation.

Reformed

Calvin: chief excellence—accounted Son of God in a special sense; ground of Hebrews 1:5 superiority.

Reformed pastoral: shared—You are my Father; my God; Rock of my salvation. Filial cry marks the true king.

Psalms 89:27

And I will make him the firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth. Wesley: First-born—son with privileges; in some sort David, properly Christ; Higher—more fully in the Messiah. Shared: firstborn; highest of earth-kings.

Reformed

Calvin: first-born higher than kings—honor transcending men and angels in the person Christ represents.

Reformed pastoral: shared—firstborn; highest of the kings of the earth. Rank is God's making.

Psalms 89:28

28My steadfast love I will keep for him forever, and my covenant will stand firm for him.

Arminian

My steadfast love I will keep for him forever, and my covenant will stand firm for him. Shared: hesed kept forever; covenant firm for him.

Arminian pastoral: keep and stand firm—God's verbs. Firmness is promised, not wished.

Reformed

Calvin: mercy first, then covenant—gratuitous cause; always merciful that the covenant never fail.

Reformed pastoral: shared—steadfast love forever; covenant stand firm. Inviolability rests on God's good pleasure.

Psalms 89:29

29I will establish his offspring forever and his throne as the days of the heavens.

Arminian

I will establish his offspring forever and his throne as the days of the heavens. Wesley: Forever—sit on the throne forever; accomplished only in Christ. Shared: offspring forever; throne as heaven's days.

Arminian pastoral: heaven-days duration outruns every court. Only Christ bears forever strictly.

Reformed

Calvin: seed forever; throne as days of heaven—everlasting duration found in Christ alone.

Reformed pastoral: shared—offspring forever; throne as the days of the heavens. Perpetuity names the Mediator.

Psalms 89:30

30If his children forsake my law and do not walk according to my rules,

Arminian

If his children forsake my law and do not walk according to my rules, Shared: real if—children may forsake law and miss his rules.

Arminian pastoral: covenant honesty names forsaking. No soft fiction that sons never stray.

Reformed

Calvin: posterity will fall—unless infinite mercy pardons, no article of covenant would stand; not total extinction of godliness always, but real revolt needing pardon.

Reformed pastoral: shared—if children forsake. Adoption does not erase the flesh's falls.

Psalms 89:31

31if they violate my statutes and do not keep my commandments,

Arminian

if they violate my statutes and do not keep my commandments, Shared: violate statutes; not keep commandments—heinous terms on purpose.

Arminian pastoral: stacked "ifs" cover aggravated breach. Severity of words teaches hate of sin.

Reformed

Calvin: odious terms—forsake, violate, not walk, not keep—even heinous sins in view; patience extended far.

Reformed pastoral: shared—violate statutes; not keep commandments. Lenity named without baiting liberty.

Psalms 89:32

32then I will punish their transgression with the rod and their iniquity with stripes,

Arminian

then I will punish their transgression with the rod and their iniquity with stripes, Shared: then—rod for transgression; stripes for iniquity.

Arminian pastoral: "then" is moral sequence. Rod is real; cheap grace is not the text.

Reformed

Calvin: does not promise escape unpunished—that would encourage sin; chastises with fatherly moderation (2 Sam 7:14).

Reformed pastoral: shared—punish with the rod; iniquity with stripes. Medicine, not bare vengeance.

Psalms 89:33

33but I will not remove from him my steadfast love or be false to my faithfulness.

Arminian

but I will not remove from him my steadfast love or be false to my faithfulness. Shared: but—hesed not removed from him; faithfulness not falsified.

Arminian pastoral: the "but" saves hope under the rod. Love stays with the line even when stripes fall.

Reformed

Calvin: loving-kindness not withdrawn from him—shift of person points to Christ the Head; faithfulness shall not fail; covenant effectual despite human inconstancy.

Reformed pastoral: shared—will not remove steadfast love; not be false to faithfulness. Rod and surety together.

Key contrast Both refuse to cancel the rod or the hesed: forsaking children are visited with stripes, yet steadfast love is not removed from the anointed line. Arminians typically pastor the discipline as real moral consequence within a covenant that still holds by God's faithfulness—rod without final divorce. Reformed readers typically stress Calvin's fatherly moderation and the shift to "from him": pardon and perseverance flow through the sure Head so the Church is not consumed. Shared: discipline and surety belong together; no open denial of wrath, no erasure of the oath.

Psalms 89:34

34I will not violate my covenant or alter the word that went forth from my lips.

Arminian

I will not violate my covenant or alter the word that went forth from my lips. Shared: God will not violate or alter his outgoing word.

Arminian pastoral: he will not requite breach with breach. His lips' word outlasts ours.

Reformed

Calvin: will not break though they break—blots out sins; founded on immutability of his nature.

Reformed pastoral: shared—will not violate my covenant; not alter the word. Fix eyes on the covenant when moods quarrel.

Psalms 89:35

Arminian

Once for all I have sworn by my holiness; I will not lie to David. Shared: once-for-all oath by holiness; no lie to David.

Arminian pastoral: "once" seals irrevocability. Holiness is the oath's weight.

Reformed

Calvin: once—irrevocable; sworn by holiness (sanctuary-accommodated); compassion for our distrust.

Reformed pastoral: shared—sworn by my holiness; will not lie to David. Oath added so faith may rest.

Psalms 89:36

Arminian

His offspring shall endure forever, his throne as long as the sun before me. Shared: offspring forever; throne as the sun before him.

Arminian pastoral: sun-duration language. When earthly thrones shake, the simile still points past them.

Reformed

Calvin: sun as witness—more stable than lower elements; cannot be limited to David when throne was shaken; fulfilled in Christ.

Reformed pastoral: shared—endure forever; throne as long as the sun. Promise outlives Rehoboam-scale losses.

Psalms 89:37

37Like the moon it shall be established forever, a faithful witness in the skies." Selah

Arminian

Like the moon it shall be established forever, a faithful witness in the skies." Selah Wesley: Witness—the rainbow, Gen 9:12,16. Shared: moon-forever; faithful witness in the skies; Selah.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley hears rainbow-covenant echo. Sky-witness means God stakes permanence publicly.

Reformed

Calvin: established as the moon; faithful witness in heaven—steadfast token; full sense in the Branch from Jesse.

Reformed pastoral: shared—like the moon forever; faithful witness in the skies. Selah before the "But now."

Psalms 89:38

38But now you have cast off and rejected; you are full of wrath against your anointed.

Arminian

But now you have cast off and rejected; you are full of wrath against your anointed. Wesley: But—having declared certainty, he now humbly expostulates. Shared: but now—cast off, rejected; wrath against the anointed.

Arminian pastoral: the turn is honest prayer, not atheism. Certainty of promise funds the protest.

Reformed

Calvin: complains that prophecy seemed to fail—not charging falsehood; unburdens to resist distrust; anger against the anointed as reason of change in appearance.

Reformed pastoral: shared—cast off and rejected; wrath against your anointed. Lament inside faith, not against the oath.

Psalms 89:39

39You have renounced the covenant with your servant; you have defiled his crown in the dust.

Arminian

You have renounced the covenant with your servant; you have defiled his crown in the dust. Shared: covenant seeming renounced; crown defiled in dust.

Arminian pastoral: dust-crown is the scandal. Plead it without rewriting vv. 1–37 as fiction.

Reformed

Calvin: made covenant to cease—to appearance; does not attribute forgetfulness to God; presents temptation so God may succor; crown cast to earth.

Reformed pastoral: shared—renounced the covenant (in appearance); crown in the dust. Infirmity speaks; faith still holds the chosen king.

Key contrast Both keep the whole arc: verses 1–37's sure covenant must interpret verses 38–45's ruin, not the reverse. Arminians typically follow Wesley's "But": after declaring certainty, Ethan humbly expostulates—present voiding of the crown is lived contradiction pleaded before God, not a theory that God lied. Reformed readers typically follow Calvin: the complaint speaks according to appearance and infirmity while faith remains convinced the chosen king cannot finally be rejected; unburdening before God resists despair. Shared: crown in the dust does not erase sworn hesed; holy fear and lament pray the promise back into view.

Psalms 89:40

40You have breached all his walls; you have laid his strongholds in ruins.

Arminian

You have breached all his walls; you have laid his strongholds in ruins. Shared: walls breached; strongholds ruined—God acknowledged as actor.

Arminian pastoral: hedge language—kingdom exposed. Name God as the one who breached, then ask him to rebuild.

Reformed

Calvin: prophet ascribes razing to God—men could not destroy what God set up had anger not kindled.

Reformed pastoral: shared—breached walls; strongholds in ruins. Sanctified feeling traces calamity to divine anger.

Psalms 89:41

All who pass by plunder him; he has become the scorn of his neighbors. Shared: passers-by plunder; scorn of neighbors.

Reformed

Calvin: exposed as prey; reproach to neighbors—railings that "Is this God's excellent king?" recoil on God.

Reformed pastoral: shared—plunder him; scorn of his neighbors. Mockery of the anointed is God's concern.

Psalms 89:45

45You have cut short the days of his youth; you have covered him with shame. Selah

Arminian

You have cut short the days of his youth; you have covered him with shame. Selah Wesley: Youth—flourishing estate of David's kingdom short, not beyond next successor. Shared: youth cut short; covered with shame; Selah.

Arminian pastoral: kingdom aged before maturity. Shame-covering is public; Selah before "How long."

Reformed

Calvin: kingdom compared to a life—waxed old before complete blossom; shortened youth; shame covered.

Reformed pastoral: shared—cut short the days of his youth; covered with shame. Brief bloom under judgment.

Psalms 89:46

46How long, O Lord? Will you hide yourself forever? How long will your wrath burn like fire?

Arminian

How long, O Lord? Will you hide yourself forever? How long will your wrath burn like fire? Shared: how long—hide forever? wrath burn like fire?

Arminian pastoral: prayer replaces mere complaint. Hiding and fire-wrath are pleaded for an end.

Reformed

Calvin: turns to prayer—proof complaint was joined to faith; how long forever denotes continued calamity; afflictions from sins as scourges.

Reformed pastoral: shared—how long; hide forever; wrath like fire. Ask the face; confess wrath's link to sin.

Psalms 89:47

47Remember how short my time is! For what vanity you have created all the children of man!

Arminian

Remember how short my time is! For what vanity you have created all the children of man! Wesley: Short—time of king and kingdom; Wherefore—made us and him for so little purpose after solemn covenant? Shared: short time remembered; vanity of humankind.

Arminian pastoral: brief reign under oath feels like vanity. Plead the disproportion without charging God as cruel designer.

Reformed

Calvin: brevity of life—if no taste of goodness, creation seems in vain; yet complaint has excess needing pardon; life not profitless if Father-love is tasted.

Reformed pastoral: shared—remember short time; vanity of the children of man. Mortality sharpens the ask for mercy now.

Psalms 89:48

48What man can live and never see death? Who can deliver his soul from the power of Sheol? Selah

Arminian

What man can live and never see death? Who can deliver his soul from the power of Sheol? Selah Wesley: What man—all mortal and miserable; kings and people die; therefore do not increase affliction. Shared: none escapes death; none self-delivers from Sheol; Selah.

Arminian pastoral: mortality levels kings. Ask God not to pile affliction on the already dying.

Reformed

Calvin: what man (geber/strong) shall not see death?—no exemption; deliverance from the grave's hand is not human.

Reformed pastoral: shared—live and never see death? deliver from Sheol? Death's universality humbles the plea.

Psalms 89:49

49Lord, where is your steadfast love of old, which by your faithfulness you swore to David?

Arminian

Lord, where is your steadfast love of old, which by your faithfulness you swore to David? Shared: where—hesed of old; sworn to David in faithfulness.

Arminian pastoral: "where" is covenant memory, not amnesia of God. Old mercies argue present hope.

Reformed

Calvin: former mercies—God cannot be unlike himself; sworn in truth; same ground of liberality for posterity.

Reformed pastoral: shared—steadfast love of old; swore to David by faithfulness. Unchangeable God is the argument.

Psalms 89:50

50Remember, O Lord, how your servants are mocked, and how I bear in my heart the insults of all the many nations,

Arminian

Remember, O Lord, how your servants are mocked, and how I bear in my heart the insults of all the many nations, Wesley: How I—we thy servants; king and people as one. Shared: remember servants' mockery; insults borne in the bosom.

Arminian pastoral: collective "I"—king and people. Bosom-borne scorn is intimate pain.

Reformed

Calvin: reproach of servants moves God; insults in the bosom—vomited on the children of God; plural to singular stirs each to pray.

Reformed pastoral: shared—servants mocked; insults in my heart. Derision of faith is God's business to repress.

Psalms 89:51

51with which your enemies mock, O Lord, with which they mock the footsteps of your anointed.

Arminian

with which your enemies mock, O Lord, with which they mock the footsteps of your anointed. Wesley: Anointed—kings of Judah, or Messiah whose expected coming heathens mocked; footsteps—ways or his coming. Shared: enemies mock the Lord; mock the anointed's footsteps.

Arminian pastoral: mockery of footsteps hits God's Messiah-hope. Plead the insult as against him.

Reformed

Calvin: enemies of the people are enemies of God; footsteps of Messiah—coming derided as fable; railings against simplicity of faith.

Reformed pastoral: shared—enemies mock; mock the footsteps of your anointed. Maintain your own cause, O Lord.

Psalms 89:52

52Blessed be the Lord forever! Amen and Amen.

Arminian

Blessed be the Lord forever! Amen and Amen. Wesley: Blessed—let enemies reproach Messiah-promises; I will bless and encourage myself with them. Shared close: bless the Lord forever; double Amen.

Arminian pastoral: doxology after dust-crown. Blessing is resistance to scorn and self-encouragement in the promises.

Reformed

Calvin: not a scribe's tack-on—praise after free complaint assuages grief; Amen and Amen; hope of deliverance kept alive.

Reformed pastoral: shared—Blessed be the Lord forever! Amen and Amen. Whole arc ends in blessing, not in canceled hesed.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Ethan the Ezrahite singing steadfast love and faithfulness, the Davidic covenant sworn and built forever, cosmic praise of wonders and holy fear, the anointed found and firstborn of kings, rod for forsaking children yet hesed not removed, then the lament that the crown is cast down and walls breached, how-long prayer, and Amen blessing—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 89:1–52.

Arminian Notable voices

“Sing—prefaces lest complainers of present miseries argue ingratitude for former mercies. Faithfulness—whatsoever hath befallen us proceeded not from thy unfaithfulness. Establish—as firmly and durably as the heavens. Chosen—David, chosen to the kingdom. Build up—perpetuate the kingdom to thy posterity; promised upon condition, and literally accomplished in Christ. Heavens—inhabitants of heaven. Feared—reverence; saints—the society of angels. Rahab—Egypt. Tabor and Hermon—parts of Canaan; rejoice by thy favour. Joyful sound—presence and ordinances; walk under influences of favour. First-born—in some sort David, properly Christ. Forever—throne forever; only in Christ. Witness—the rainbow, Gen 9:12,16. But—having declared certainty, he now humbly expostulates. Youth—flourishing estate of David's kingdom short beyond his next successor. Short—time of our king and kingdom; wherefore made us and him for so little purpose? Anointed—kings of Judah, or the Messiah whose coming the heathen mocked. Blessed—let enemies reproach Messiah-promises; I will bless and encourage myself with them.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 89

Reformed Notable voices

“Opens with mercies and covenant to fortify faith against temptation from begun decay: sing mercies even when severity is felt; mercy built forever, truth as stable as the heavens. God himself speaks—covenant with David his chosen by free election, his servant by lawful call; throne forever verified in Christ for the Church's common good. Heavens praise wonders to magnify the covenant's holiness; none among sons of the gods like Jehovah; terrible in the assembly; faithfulness round about him. Sea ruled; Rahab/Egypt crushed; heavens and earth his; Tabor and Hermon rejoice; righteousness and judgment the place of his throne; mercy and truth go before his face. Blessed who know the joyful sound and walk in the light of his countenance; he is the glory of their strength; shield and king from the Holy One. Vision to the godly: help laid on the mighty, David found and anointed; hand established; foes broken; firstborn higher than kings; mercy kept forever; seed as days of heaven. If children forsake—rod and stripes in fatherly moderation, yet loving-kindness not withdrawn nor faithfulness failed; covenant not broken though they break; sworn once by holiness; seed and throne as sun and moon—fulfilled in Christ. But cast off—complaint of appearance, not a charge of falsehood; unburdening before God that faith may hold the promise; walls breached, crown defiled, how long wilt thou hide? Remember short time; where are former mercies sworn to David? Reproach of servants; footsteps of the Anointed mocked. Blessed be Jehovah forever—praise closing the grief.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 89
