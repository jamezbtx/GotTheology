Passage

Psalms 36

Verses 1–12 · ESV · Voices

To the choirmaster. Of David, the servant of the Lord. The psalmist hears transgression speaking to the wicked deep in the heart—no fear of God before his eyes; the wicked flatters himself so that iniquity cannot be found out and hated, speaks trouble and deceit, has ceased to act wisely and do good, plots trouble on his bed, sets himself in a way that is not good, and does not reject evil; then the song turns to the LORD whose steadfast love extends to the heavens and faithfulness to the clouds, whose righteousness is like the mountains of God and judgments like the great deep, who saves man and beast; precious steadfast love gathers the children of mankind to take refuge in the shadow of his wings, to feast on the abundance of his house and drink from the river of his delights—for with him is the fountain of life, and in his light do we see light; the servant then prays that steadfast love continue to those who know him and righteousness to the upright of heart, that the foot of arrogance not come upon him nor the hand of the wicked drive him away, and points to where the evildoers lie fallen, thrust down, unable to rise.

Psalms 36:1

1Transgression speaks to the wicked deep in his heart; there is no fear of God before his eyes.

Arminian

Transgression speaks to the wicked deep in his heart; there is no fear of God before his eyes. Wesley: No fear—When I consider the manifold transgressions of ungodly men, I conclude within myself, that they have cast off all fear of the Divine majesty.

Arminian pastoral: shared—read the wicked's career as evidence that fear of God has been cast off. Let the diagnosis sober you; do not flatter yourself that you can keep wisdom while despising his majesty.

Reformed

Calvin: Transgression saith to the wicked—the malice of the wicked, though seemingly hidden, speaks aloud in my heart; I am a sure witness of what it says. He penetrates the source: though they cloak malice with dissimulation, the servants of God perceive depravity hidden within. No fear of God before his eyes—sense of good and evil destroyed, they shrink from nothing as if there were no Judge in heaven.

Reformed pastoral: shared—name fearless wickedness without catching its contagion. Hear what ungodliness whispers; keep your own heart under the fear that bridles appetite.

Key contrast Both treat missing fear of God as the root that opens every excess. Arminians typically hear Wesley: considering manifold transgressions, I conclude they have cast off all fear of the Divine majesty. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: hidden malice speaks aloud in the prophet's heart as sure witness—he unveils hypocrisy rather than merely listing outward faults.

Psalms 36:2

2For he flatters himself in his own eyes that his iniquity cannot be found out and hated.

Arminian

For he flatters himself in his own eyes that his iniquity cannot be found out and hated. Wesley: Flattereth—He deceiveth himself with vain persuasions, that God does not mind his sins, or will not punish them. Found—Punish, as the same phrase is used, Numb 32:23.

Arminian pastoral: shared—refuse self-flattery that God will not mind or punish. Iniquity that cannot be found out is a lie you tell your eyes; ask for honesty before discovery becomes public shame.

Reformed

Calvin: he flatters himself in his own eyes until his iniquity be found hateful—nourishing vices by flatteries; while falsely flattering themselves they proceed until iniquity becomes hateful to all men, yet they are not affected with displeasure against themselves. They abolish distinction between good and evil and lull conscience into insensibility.

Reformed pastoral: shared—break the polish you put on your worst acts. When others already hate what you excuse, stop applauding yourself; let conscience wake before reprobate dulness sets in.

Key contrast Both expose self-flattery that hides iniquity from the sinner's own eyes. Arminians typically hear Wesley: vain persuasion that God does not mind sins or will not punish—Found as punish. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: the wicked smooth over sin until it is hateful to all, yet remain unmoved in themselves, abolishing good and evil.

Psalms 36:3

3The words of his mouth are trouble and deceit; he has ceased to act wisely and do good.

Arminian

The words of his mouth are trouble and deceit; he has ceased to act wisely and do good. Wesley: Left off—Once he had some degrees of wisdom, but now he is become an open apostate. Mouth and life agree in ruin.

Arminian pastoral: shared—trace open apostasy back to ceased wisdom and refused good. Trouble and deceit in the mouth are not cleverness; they mark the exit from acting wisely.

Reformed

Calvin: not only circumventing others by wiles, but destitute of uprightness; frivolous excuses and vain pretexts reject sound doctrine. They purposely suppress understanding of good and evil because they have no desire to become better. If we turn aside, the remedy is to open understanding; refusing instruction indicates depravity altogether desperate.

Reformed pastoral: shared—do not starve the light that would lead you back. When the mouth runs trouble and deceit, seek understanding again; refusal to be taught is itself the danger signal.

Key contrast Both see ceased wisdom and refused good as a settled turn, not a slip. Arminians typically hear Wesley: once he had some degrees of wisdom, now an open apostate. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: purposeful suppression of understanding and rejection of instruction—a mark of desperate depravity.

Psalms 36:4

4He plots trouble while on his bed; he sets himself in a way that is not good; he does not reject evil.

Arminian

He plots trouble while on his bed; he sets himself in a way that is not good; he does not reject evil. Wesley: Deviseth—Freely, from his own inclination, when none are present to provoke him to it. Night invents what day will enact.

Arminian pastoral: shared—watch the bed where no tempter stands beside you. Plotting alone from your own inclination is freer guilt; train the night toward examination, not toward trouble.

Reformed

Calvin: wickedness of a secret and determined character—even withdrawn from sight they form schemes of mischief; without temptation or evil example they devise of their own accord. Stubbornness: they set themselves in a crooked way and abhor not evil—wilfully shutting eyes they rush until they yield as slaves of wickedness. Contrast: the godly on their beds exclude evil thoughts and examine their lives.

Reformed pastoral: shared—fill solitude with a different meditation. When alone, refuse to invent mischief; let abhorrence of evil stay awake on the bed.

Psalms 36:5

5Your steadfast love, O Lord, extends to the heavens, your faithfulness to the clouds.

Arminian

Your steadfast love, O Lord, extends to the heavens, your faithfulness to the clouds. Wesley: Thy mercy—Mine enemies are cruel and perfidious, but thou art infinite in mercy, and faithfulness. Heavens—Is infinite and incomprehensible. Faithfulness—The truth both of thy threatenings against thine enemies, and of thy promises made to good men. Clouds—Far above our reach.

Arminian pastoral: shared—lift from cruel enemies to infinite steadfast love and faithfulness. Heaven-high mercy and cloud-high truth are not abstractions; they steady the heart when men prove perfidious.

Reformed

Calvin: after speaking of great depravity, afraid of contagion, David recovers by a different theme—the world is full of God's goodness and righteousness. Mercy unto the heavens; truth unto the clouds—faithfulness in accomplishing promises. Do not limit views to wickedness that confounds all things; elevate thoughts to secret providence associated with perfect righteousness.

Reformed pastoral: shared—when malice floods the mind, quit the subject for God's mercy and truth. Measure the heavens of steadfast love; refuse to catch the wicked's infection while you condemn them.

Psalms 36:6

6Your righteousness is like the mountains of God; your judgments are like the great deep; man and beast you save, O Lord.

Arminian

Your righteousness is like the mountains of God; your judgments are like the great deep; man and beast you save, O Lord. Wesley: Mountains—Stedfast and unmoveable: eminent and conspicuous to all men. Judgments—The executions of thy counsels. Deep—Unsearchable, as the ocean. Man—The worst of men; yea, the brute-beasts have experience of thy care and kindness.

Arminian pastoral: shared—confess mountain-steadfast righteousness and ocean-deep judgments. Even man and beast are under his saving care; let that widen trust when human counsel fails.

Reformed

Calvin: righteousness as mountains of God—excellent, glory shining; judgments a great deep—to whatever side we turn, all disposed by just judgment. Greater is the depth of providence than the flood of wickedness. Thou preservest man and beast—since care extends to the irrational creation, much more does he provide for men; fortify faith by this when doubt arises.

Reformed pastoral: shared—ravish the mind with the depth of his judgments. When human affairs confuse, remember providence deeper than the abyss of evil; care for beasts pledges care for you.

Psalms 36:7

7How precious is your steadfast love, O God! The children of mankind take refuge in the shadow of your wings.

Arminian

How precious is your steadfast love, O God! The children of mankind take refuge in the shadow of your wings. Wesley: Loving-kindness—Though all thine attributes be excellent, yet, above all, thy mercy is most excellent, or precious and amiable. Refuge under wings answers precious love.

Arminian pastoral: shared—prize steadfast love above every other excellence you name. Take refuge under his wings; precious mercy is for trusting children of mankind, not for spectators only.

Reformed

Calvin: because mercy is great and clearly manifested, the children of men put trust under its shadow. Though language refers generally to sons of men, what follows applies to the faithful alone—God gathers mortal offspring under his wings as the hen cherishes chickens. The ungodly never taste the sweetness so as to enjoy repose under his wings.

Reformed pastoral: shared—trust under the shadow because love is precious. Taste fatherly care; do not run to excess while refusing the wings that would shelter you.

Psalms 36:8

8They feast on the abundance of your house, and you give them drink from the river of your delights.

Arminian

They feast on the abundance of your house, and you give them drink from the river of your delights. Wesley: Satisfied—Who trust in thee. Fatness—With those delightful provisions, which thou hast prepared for them in heaven. The river—Which denotes both their plenty, and their perpetuity.

Arminian pastoral: shared—expect abundance and river-delights for those who trust. Feast language points beyond thin rations; let hope taste plenty and perpetuity prepared in his house.

Reformed

Calvin: fatness of God's house—abundance of good things laid up for children who commit themselves to his protection. Under it are comprehended blessings necessary to present happiness and comfort as well as eternal blessedness; earthly use connected with faith. The ungodly glut bodies while souls stay empty; the faithful alone are satisfied because gifts are a pledge of fatherly love. Meat, drink, and river denote complete fullness and overflowing abundance.

Reformed pastoral: shared—receive house-abundance as pledge, not as license to glut without gratitude. Drink the river of delights with faith that names the Giver.

Key contrast Both promise rich satisfaction in God's house for those who trust under his wings. Arminians typically hear Wesley: delightful provisions prepared in heaven; the river marking plenty and perpetuity. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: abundance covering present-life needs and eternal blessedness, lawfully enjoyed only as a pledge of fatherly love—while the ungodly glut and stay famished in soul.

Psalms 36:9

9For with you is the fountain of life; in your light do we see light.

Arminian

For with you is the fountain of life; in your light do we see light. Wesley: Life—It is in God as in a fountain, and from him is derived to us—that glorious and blessed and endless life worthy of the name. Light—In the light of thy glorious presence, fully manifested when we see thee face to face; joy, comfort, and happiness—light discovering, then light discovered or enjoyed.

Arminian pastoral: shared—confess the fountain of life and the light by which we see light. Do not hunt life or light apart from him; derive both from the fountain and the presence.

Reformed

Calvin: confirms the doctrine—there is not a drop of life without him or which flows not from his grace. Light: men are destitute of light except as the Lord shines; if true of this life, how behold heavenly light unless the Spirit enlighten? The godly alone perceive they derive light from God and without it would continue buried in darkness.

Reformed pastoral: shared—own every drop of life and every beam of sight as from him. Refuse the fantasy of self-lit life; ask the shining that alone lets us see.

Psalms 36:10

10Oh, continue your steadfast love to those who know you, and your righteousness to the upright of heart!

Arminian

Oh, continue your steadfast love to those who know you, and your righteousness to the upright of heart! Prayer turns from praise to plea: prolong steadfast love and righteousness toward knowers and the upright-hearted.

Arminian pastoral: shared—ask continued steadfast love for those who know him. Knowledge and uprightness of heart mark the people who plead for prolonging mercy; keep asking, not presuming yesterday's favor needs no tomorrow.

Reformed

Calvin: Prolong thy mercy to them that know thee, and thy righteousness to the upright in heart—continue to manifest mercy to the end. Righteousness connected as effect: defending his people, vindicating innocence, avenging wrongs, proving faithful. True believers know God and are upright in heart—godliness springs from knowledge of God; faith disposes to uprightness; we know him aright when we place entire confidence in him.

Reformed pastoral: shared—pray prolonged mercy for the knowing and upright. Tie righteousness to his defense of his own; depend on favor first, then rest in his faithful vindication.

Psalms 36:11

11Let not the foot of arrogance come upon me, nor the hand of the wicked drive me away.

Arminian

Let not the foot of arrogance come upon me, nor the hand of the wicked drive me away. Wesley: The foot—Of my proud and insolent enemies. Come—So as to overthrow me. Personal plea under the general prayer for the godly.

Arminian pastoral: shared—ask that arrogant feet and wicked hands be restrained. Name the threat of overthrow without matching arrogance; seek his block on the trampling foot.

Reformed

Calvin: applies the general prayer to himself—asks nothing apart from other godly, yet as one of the upright desires favor. Foot of pride and hand of the wicked: they rush boldly, lifting feet to tread and hands ready to wrong; he entreats restraint and confesses danger unless God comes speedily.

Reformed pastoral: shared—pray protection among the upright, not as a private exception. Ask God to halt the proud foot and removing hand before they drive you from his care.

Psalms 36:12

12There the evildoers lie fallen; they are thrust down, unable to rise.

Arminian

There the evildoers lie fallen; they are thrust down, unable to rise. Wesley: There—He seems as it were to point at the place, as if it were already done. Faith points at a fall not yet visible to flesh.

Arminian pastoral: shared—speak of the evildoers' fall as sure. Pointing "there" trains hope; do not wait to praise only after your eyes already see them down.

Reformed

Calvin: derives confidence from prayer, not doubting he has obtained. By the eye of faith, as from a watchtower, he beholds their destruction and speaks with confidence as if realized. Leave to providence the period when God rises to judgment; they totter in a slippery place even in prosperity; finally utter destruction with no expected rising.

Reformed pastoral: shared—view the fall of workers of iniquity by faith before it arrives. Refuse to hasten God's timing out of impatient desire; hold "there" as watchtower certainty, not as your private schedule for revenge.

Key contrast Both treat the fallen evildoers as already located by faith—"there"—before sight catches up. Arminians typically hear Wesley: he points at the place as if it were already done. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: from a watchtower faith sees destruction as certain, yet leaves the timing of judgment to providence and will not hasten vengeance by impatient desire.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on transgression that speaks in the wicked's heart, self-flattery that hides iniquity, ceased wisdom and bed-plotted trouble, steadfast love high as the heavens and faithfulness to the clouds, mountain righteousness and deep judgments that save man and beast, precious love and refuge under wings, house-abundance and the river of delights, the fountain of life and light by which we see light, prolonged mercy for those who know God, and the faith that already points to fallen evildoers—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 36:1–12.

Arminian Notable voices

“No fear—When I consider the manifold transgressions of ungodly men, I conclude within myself, that they have cast off all fear of the Divine majesty. Flattereth—He deceiveth himself with vain persuasions, that God does not mind his sins, or will not punish them. Found—Punish, as the same phrase is used, Numb 32:23. Faithfulness—The truth both of thy threatenings against thine enemies, and of thy promises made to good men. The clouds—Is far above our reach, greater and higher than we can apprehend. Mountains—Stedfast and unmoveable: eminent and conspicuous to all men. Judgments—The executions of thy counsels. Deep—Unsearchable, as the ocean. Man—The worst of men; yea, the brute-beasts have experience of thy care and kindness. Loving-kindness—Though all thine attributes be excellent, yet, above all, thy mercy is most excellent, or precious and amiable. Satisfied—Who trust in thee. Fatness—With those delightful provisions, which thou hast prepared for them in heaven. The river—Which denotes both their plenty, and their perpetuity. Life—It is in God as in a fountain, and from him is derived to us—that glorious and blessed and endless life which alone is worthy of the name. Light—In the light of thy glorious presence, fully manifested when we see thee face to face; joy and comfort and happiness—light discovering, then light discovered or enjoyed. The foot—Of my proud and insolent enemies. Come—So as to overthrow me. There—He seems as it were to point at the place, as if it were already done.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 36

Reformed Notable voices

“Transgression saith to the wicked in the midst of my heart: the malice of the wicked, though seemingly hidden, speaks aloud in my heart, and I am a sure witness of what it says. He speaks not of outward faults only but penetrates the source—though they cloak malice with dissimulation, servants of God perceive depravity hidden within; abandoned despisers, hardened, indulge without fear. No fear of God before his eyes: sense of good and evil destroyed, they shrink from nothing as if no Judge sat in heaven. He flatters himself in his own eyes until iniquity is found hateful—nourishing vices by flatteries; while all others are offended, they applaud themselves and lull conscience. Words of iniquity and deceit; they purposely suppress understanding of good and evil, refusing instruction—a mark of desperate depravity. He meditates iniquity upon his bed; without temptation they devise mischief of their own accord, set themselves in a way not good, and abhor not evil. Afraid of contagion from condemning the wicked, David recovers by contemplating God's mercy unto the heavens and truth unto the clouds—faithfulness in promises; righteousness as mountains of God; judgments a great deep filling heaven and earth; he preserves man and beast. How precious is loving-kindness: children of men trust under the shadow of his wings—God gathers mortal offspring as a hen her chickens; the ungodly never taste so as to repose there. Abundantly satisfied with the fatness of his house—abundance for those who commit themselves to him, present blessings and eternal, lawfully enjoyed as pledge of fatherly love; river of pleasures. With thee is the fountain of life; in thy light we see light—not a drop of life without him; men destitute of light except as the Lord shines, and the Spirit alone enlightens for heavenly sight. Prolong mercy to them that know thee, and righteousness to the upright in heart; let not the foot of pride come upon me nor the hand of the wicked remove me. There the workers of iniquity are fallen: by the eye of faith, as from a watchtower, he beholds their destruction as if realized; leave to providence the time of judgment; they are thrust down and shall not rise.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 36
