Passage

Psalms 106

Verses 1–48 · ESV · Voices

Israel is called to praise the Lord for His goodness and everlasting steadfast love, then confesses: we and our fathers have sinned—forgetting wonders at the Red Sea, craving in the wilderness, envying Moses and Aaron, the calf at Horeb, despising the pleasant land, Baal of Peor, Meribah, and mixing with the nations—yet God saved for His name's sake, heard their cry, remembered His covenant, and is asked to gather them from the nations for thanks and praise. Amen; Praise the Lord.

Psalms 106:1

1Praise the Lord! Oh give thanks to the Lord, for he is good, for his steadfast love endures forever!

Arminian

Praise the Lord! Give thanks for He is good; His steadfast love endures forever. Wesley frames this psalm as the history of Israel's rebellion against the goodness celebrated in Psalm 105—yet it begins and ends with Hallelujah.

Both camps: praise is the right frame even when confession follows. Classical Arminians hear a sincere call to thank the God whose love endures—mercy that invites repentance rather than shutting the door.

Reformed

Calvin: the prophet begins with praises so the people may cherish good hope; past favors encourage reconciliation and keep despair at bay. Praise is not denial of sin but the soil of hope.

Reformed accent: mercy that endures forever grounds the confession that follows. Shared: give thanks; He is good; steadfast love forever.

Psalms 106:2

2Who can utter the mighty deeds of the Lord, or declare all his praise?

Arminian

Who can utter the mighty deeds of the Lord, or declare all His praise? The deeds outstrip the tongue; praise is attempted, never exhausted.

Shared: God's works surpass speech. Classical Arminians hear this as invitation to attempt praise anyway—not as a barrier that silences the willing heart.

Reformed

Calvin: no one who concentrates every energy on praising God finds himself adequate—the grandeur overpowers the senses. Yet this stirs us up to praise to the utmost; heart-praise is pleasing though perfection fails.

Reformed pastoral: inadequacy is not an excuse for silence. Shared: mighty deeds; all His praise beyond full utterance.

Psalms 106:3

3Blessed are they who observe justice, who do righteousness at all times!

Arminian

Blessed are they who observe justice, who do righteousness at all times. Wesley and Calvin alike refuse lip-praise detached from life. Blessing rests on those who keep judgment and work righteousness continually.

Arminian hearing: "at all times" is sincere command under enabling grace—perseverance in righteousness as duty, not a claim that any finish sinless.

Reformed

Calvin: praises of the lip alone are not acceptable; heart and deportment must concur. Keep judgment (sincere affection) and work righteousness (external works); perseverance is required—empty profession and inconsistent virtue fail.

Reformed accent: genuine godliness is briefly described. Shared: observe justice; do righteousness always.

Psalms 106:4

4Remember me, O Lord, when you show favor to your people; help me when you save them,

Arminian

Remember me, O Lord, when You show favor to Your people; help me when You save them. Wesley: Me—he speaks in the name and on behalf of the whole nation; with those favors God usually and peculiarly gives His people.

Arminian pastoral: the singer asks to share the people's salvation—grace sought as real favor, not pretended interest.

Reformed

Calvin: remember me with the goodwill You bear toward Your people—grant the same unmerited kindness conferred on Your people, that I may never be cut off from Your Church. Visit me with Your salvation; "remember" suits a time when the faithful fear God has forgotten them.

Reformed comfort: desire to share elect favor as summit of happiness. Shared: remember; favor; salvation help.

Psalms 106:5

5that I may look upon the prosperity of your chosen ones, that I may rejoice in the gladness of your nation, that I may glory with your inheritance.

Arminian

That I may look upon the prosperity of Your chosen ones, rejoice in the gladness of Your nation, glory with Your inheritance. Wesley: See—enjoy; Chosen—Your chosen people, Israelites indeed; Gladness—such joy as formerly afforded; Glory—occasion to glory in God's goodness; Inheritance—congregation of Your people.

Both camps: joy is corporate. Classical Arminians hear chosen ones as the covenant people whose good is sincerely desired for all who belong.

Reformed

Calvin: that I may see the good of Your chosen—enjoy the blessings constantly realized by the elect; rejoice in the joy of Your nation and glory with Your heritage. The prophet clings to belonging to God's flock even in a mournful church state.

Reformed confession: share the good of the chosen. Shared: prosperity, gladness, glory with the inheritance.

Key contrast Both confess desire to share the good of God's chosen people and nation. Arminians typically hear covenant people addressed with sincere favor offered in the gospel. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's elect goodwill: unmerited kindness toward those God has chosen, sought as the summit of happiness.

Psalms 106:6

6Both we and our fathers have sinned; we have committed iniquity; we have done wickedness.

Arminian

Both we and our fathers have sinned; we have committed iniquity; we have done wickedness. Wesley: Glory—as our fathers did (in shared guilt). Triple confession presses enormity home.

Arminian pastoral: no feigned humility—we and they stand guilty. Classical Arminians preach confession that opens the way to pardon sincerely held out.

Reformed

Calvin: the prophet, though speaking as one man, dictates prayer for the whole Church. From first to last the fathers were malign and perverse; descendants are not better; having confessed, they ask remission. Pardon comes after we confess ourselves guilty; hardness shuts out grace.

Reformed accent: rigid self-examination before God's tribunal. Shared: we and our fathers; iniquity; wickedness.

Psalms 106:7

7Our fathers, when they were in Egypt, did not consider your wondrous works; they did not remember the abundance of your steadfast love, but rebelled by the sea, at the Red Sea.

Arminian

Our fathers in Egypt did not consider wondrous works; they did not remember the abundance of steadfast love, but rebelled by the sea, at the Red Sea. Wesley: At the sea—when those wonders were newly done and fresh in memory.

Both camps: forgetfulness at the shoreline of miracle is inexcusable. Classical Arminians hear rebellion as real resistance of light already given.

Reformed

Calvin: from the commencement of emancipation they were ungrateful and rebellious. Folly here is vile stupidity—blind in matters so plain that even the blind might behold them. They remembered not: forgetfulness from stupid indifference, and that quickly.

Reformed accent: invincible perversity met by inconceivable kindness. Shared: did not consider; did not remember; rebelled at the sea.

Psalms 106:8

8Yet he saved them for his name's sake, that he might make known his mighty power.

Arminian

Yet He saved them for His name's sake, that He might make known His mighty power. Wesley: Saved them—that He may vindicate His name from blasphemous reproaches if they had been destroyed.

Arminian hearing: name's sake is God's reputation among the nations—mercy that still saves a rebellious people without making sin cheap. Classical Arminians glory that God acts for His name while sincerely calling the saved to trust.

Reformed

Calvin: Israelites were saved not on account of deserving, but because God had regard to His own glory—lest His holy name become a reproach among the heathen. Antithesis between God's name and men's merits: out of regard to His glory He finds in us no cause to save us.

Reformed confession: salvation for the Name, not for desert. Shared: for His name's sake; make known mighty power.

Key contrast Both confess Israel saved for God's name's sake, not for desert. Arminians typically emphasize vindication of the Name among the nations and mercy that still calls the rebellious. Reformed readers typically emphasize Calvin's antithesis: God finds in us no meritorious cause; glory alone moves the rescue.

Psalms 106:9

9He rebuked the Red Sea, and it became dry, and he led them through the deep as through a desert.

Arminian

He rebuked the Red Sea, and it became dry; He led them through the deep as through a desert. Wesley: Led them—as securely as if they had walked upon dry land.

Shared: creation rebuked for the people's path. Classical Arminians preach a God who answers rebellion with miracle—grace preceding the praise of v. 12.

Reformed

Calvin: sea rebuked—power at whose command waters receded and a free passage opened. They walked through depths as through a desert; the path was smooth by astonishing power alone.

Reformed accent: nature altered for a perverse people. Shared: rebuked; dry; led through the deep.

Psalms 106:10

10So he saved them from the hand of the foe and redeemed them from the power of the enemy.

Arminian

So He saved them from the hand of the foe and redeemed them from the power of the enemy.

Both camps: save and redeem are God's verbs. Classical Arminians hear real deliverance offered as pattern of gospel rescue—foe and enemy named so thanks may be specific.

Reformed

Calvin: saved from the hand of the enemy; delivered from the hand of the wicked. Distinction between Israel and Egypt in the same sea magnifies the miracle.

Reformed comfort: redemption is particular in that hour. Shared: saved; redeemed from the enemy's power.

Psalms 106:11

11And the waters covered their adversaries; not one of them was left.

Arminian

And the waters covered their adversaries; not one of them was left.

Shared: total overthrow of the foe. Classical Arminians refuse to mute judgment when celebrating mercy—the same sea that opened for Israel closed on Egypt.

Reformed

Calvin: waters covered their oppressors; not one left. Instantaneous difference between peoples shows God made the distinction.

Reformed accent: judgment as clear as deliverance. Shared: covered; not one left.

Psalms 106:12

12Then they believed his words; they sang his praise.

Arminian

Then they believed His words; they sang His praise.

Arminian pastoral: belief and song after the miracle—real response under grace. Classical Arminians also warn that such faith can prove temporary if the heart does not continue (v. 13).

Reformed

Calvin: they believed and sang—not to their commendation, but to increase guilt twofold. Overpowered by grandeur, they were compelled to believe and give glory, then immediately relapsed. Temporary faith, not voluntary regenerating faith—compulsion of sensed power, soon gone.

Reformed warning: forced reverence is not persevering trust. Shared: believed His words; sang His praise.

Key contrast Both confess they believed and sang after the sea. Arminians typically hear a real response that may yet be forsaken if they do not wait on God. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's temporary, compelled faith that quickly forgot—proof of deeper unbelief.

Psalms 106:13

13But they soon forgot his works; they did not wait for his counsel.

Arminian

But they soon forgot His works; they did not wait for His counsel. Wesley: Soon—even within three days (Ex 15:22–23); Waited not—they did not wait patiently for supplies in the manner and time God thought fit.

Arminian hearing: forgetting and not waiting are sins of a people who could have remembered—resistible grace despised. Shared: soon forgot; did not wait.

Reformed

Calvin: they made haste and forgot; did not attend to His counsel. Inconstancy: believed only a very short time. They did not suspend desires till fitting opportunity; carnal desire goes before God's counsel.

Reformed accent: despising benefits is the root of ingratitude. Shared: soon forgot; did not wait for counsel.

Psalms 106:14

14But they had a wanton craving in the wilderness, and put God to the test in the desert;

Arminian

But they had a wanton craving in the wilderness, and put God to the test in the desert. Wesley: Lusted—for flesh.

Both camps: craving that tests God. Classical Arminians preach testing God as limiting Him to our means—sin of impatient desire under providence.

Reformed

Calvin: lusted greatly and tempted God. To tempt God is not to acquiesce in His will but to desire more than He is willing to grant—limiting God to means of their devising; rejecting the way they ought to follow.

Reformed pastoral: fleshly lusts oppose God's rule of the affections. Shared: wanton craving; put God to the test.

Psalms 106:15

15he gave them what they asked, but sent a wasting disease among them.

Arminian

He gave them what they asked, but sent a wasting disease among them. Wesley: Souls—into their bodies; inordinate desire of pampering bodies occasioned destroying them.

Arminian pastoral: answered prayer that wounds—gift without blessing. Classical Arminians warn that God may grant the request and withhold the soul's good.

Reformed

Calvin: gave them their desire but sent leanness into their soul—gratified inordinate desires so that those who loathed manna received leanness. Blessing withheld from the food they immoderately longed for.

Reformed accent: judgment inside the gift. Shared: gave what they asked; wasting / leanness.

Psalms 106:16

16When men in the camp were jealous of Moses and Aaron, the holy one of the Lord,

Arminian

When men in the camp were jealous of Moses and Aaron, the holy one of the Lord. Wesley: The saint—consecrated for the priesthood; envy and rebellion not only against Aaron but against God Himself.

Shared: jealousy against God's appointed servants. Classical Arminians hear rebellion against office as rebellion against the God who set it.

Reformed

Calvin: they envied Moses and Aaron—diabolic pride rising against God; to refuse their rule was to resist God's authority. Aaron the saint of Jehovah: Divinely invested authority; Dathan and Abiram rebelled against God.

Reformed accent: envy of the holy office is war on God. Shared: jealous of Moses and Aaron, the holy one.

Psalms 106:17

17the earth opened and swallowed up Dathan, and covered the company of Abiram.

Arminian

The earth opened and swallowed up Dathan, and covered the company of Abiram.

Both camps: earth as judgment. Classical Arminians preach this as warning that God answers conspiracy against His servants—history that should humble, not harden.

Reformed

Calvin: heinousness seen in magnitude of punishment. Earth swallowed alive Dathan and Abiram; yet the people, so far from being bettered, conducted themselves the more perversely.

Reformed warning: terrible vengeance that still left many unhumbled. Shared: earth opened; Dathan; Abiram covered.

Psalms 106:18

18Fire also broke out in their company; the flame burned up the wicked.

Arminian

Fire also broke out in their company; the flame burned up the wicked.

Shared: fire from God on the wicked company. Classical Arminians refuse to soften the flame—justice is part of the Name praised in v. 1.

Reformed

Calvin: fire coming down consumed them. God charged faults of a few upon the body because murmurings showed the same distemper; punishment fell on captains, design to spare the people at large.

Reformed accent: selective severity, corporate guilt. Shared: fire; flame burned the wicked.

Psalms 106:19

19They made a calf in Horeb and worshiped a metal image.

Arminian

They made a calf in Horeb and worshiped a metal image. Wesley: A calf—when just brought out by wonders, having seen plagues on Egyptian idolaters, law newly delivered in tremendous manner.

Arminian pastoral: idolatry at the foot of revelation—willful exchange under light. Shared: calf; metal image.

Reformed

Calvin: abandoned true worship and made a calf; brutal stupidity worshipping before the molten image. Intention to worship God this way still robs Him of honor.

Reformed confession: image-worship is departure from the true God. Shared: made a calf; worshiped a metal image.

Psalms 106:20

20They exchanged the glory of God for the image of an ox that eats grass.

Arminian

They exchanged the glory of God for the image of an ox that eats grass. Wesley: Their glory—God, who was indeed their glory; into the golden image of an ox which must be fed by them, unlike the true God who fed Israel.

Both camps: glory swapped for grass-eater. Classical Arminians hear Rom 1:23 echoed—glory exchanged is real apostasy.

Reformed

Calvin: changed their glory into the likeness of an ox that eats grass. God robbed of honor; glory tarnished. God had clothed them with His glory—madness to substitute the inanimate form of an ox.

Reformed accent: exchange of glory is base unbelief. Shared: exchanged glory for an ox that eats grass.

Psalms 106:21

21They forgot God, their Savior, who had done great things in Egypt,

Arminian

They forgot God, their Savior, who had done great things in Egypt.

Arminian hearing: forgot their Savior—memory refused after rescue. Classical Arminians stress that forgetting is culpable when great things have been done before their eyes.

Reformed

Calvin: forgot God their Preserver who had done great things in Egypt. Sin not through ignorance only but wilfully—God made known, then cast off for brutish idolatry.

Reformed accent: inexcusable blindness after condescending revelation. Shared: forgot God their Savior; great things in Egypt.

Psalms 106:22

22wondrous works in the land of Ham, and awesome deeds by the Red Sea.

Arminian

Wondrous works in the land of Ham, and awesome deeds by the Red Sea.

Shared: Egypt and Red Sea piled as evidence. Classical Arminians preach memory of Ham and the sea as fuel for present faith.

Reformed

Calvin: wondrous works in the land of Ham, terrible things at the Red Sea. Miracles notable and terrible—base to shut eyes and give themselves to idolatry.

Reformed pastoral: darkness should vanish before such knowledge. Shared: wondrous works; awesome deeds by the sea.

Psalms 106:23

23Therefore he said he would destroy them—had not Moses, his chosen one, stood in the breach before him, to turn away his wrath from destroying them.

Arminian

Therefore He said He would destroy them—had not Moses, His chosen one, stood in the breach before Him, to turn away His wrath from destroying them. Wesley: Breach—God made a wall about them; they made a breach by sins; Moses by prevailing intercession hindered destruction.

Arminian pastoral: intercession turns wrath—Moses as type of the one Mediator. Classical Arminians glory that God hears a chosen intercessor for a guilty people.

Reformed

Calvin: He said He would destroy them unless Moses His chosen stood in the breach to turn away wrath. God purposed to strike with terror; in secret counsel He had resolved upon forgiveness. Moses interposed as intercessor like a soldier in the breach.

Reformed comfort: chosen intercessor; wrath turned. Shared: destroy; Moses stood in the breach; wrath turned.

Key contrast Both confess Moses stood in the breach and turned wrath. Arminians typically emphasize prevailing intercession as pattern of mediation sincerely applied. Reformed readers typically emphasize Calvin's frame: secret purpose to pardon, public threat to humble; Moses as God's chosen intercessor for the people.

Psalms 106:24

24Then they despised the pleasant land, having no faith in his promise.

Arminian

Then they despised the pleasant land, having no faith in His promise. Wesley: Despised—preferring Egypt and former bondage (Num 14:3–4).

Arminian hearing: no faith in the promise—unbelief as refusal to trust a word God meant. Classical Arminians preach the pleasant land rejected by unbelieving hearts.

Reformed

Calvin: despised the pleasant land; did not believe His word. Canaan was holy habitation and pledge of heavenly inheritance—basest ingratitude. Not believing, they refused the heritage and rebelled.

Reformed accent: unbelief despises the sworn gift. Shared: despised the pleasant land; no faith in His promise.

Psalms 106:25

25They murmured in their tents, and did not obey the voice of the Lord.

Arminian

They murmured in their tents, and did not obey the voice of the Lord. Wesley: The voice—God's command that they should boldly enter.

Both camps: tent-murmur and refused obedience. Classical Arminians hear the voice as sincere command they would not keep.

Reformed

Calvin: murmured in their tents; did not listen to the voice of Jehovah. Rebellion in the camp as if rising against God.

Reformed pastoral: murmur is disobedience voiced. Shared: murmured; did not obey the Lord's voice.

Psalms 106:26

26Therefore he raised his hand and swore to them that he would make them fall in the wilderness,

Arminian

Therefore He raised His hand and swore to them that He would make them fall in the wilderness. Wesley: Lifted up—He sware; dreadful irrevocable oath (Num 14).

Shared: oath of judgment. Classical Arminians refuse to soften the raised hand—promise of fall is as sure as promise of land was.

Reformed

Calvin: lifted up His hand against them to destroy them in the desert—swearing. God restrained full anger yet; the threatening itself should have subdued pride.

Reformed accent: sworn judgment in the wilderness. Shared: raised hand; swore; fall in the wilderness.

Psalms 106:27

27and would make their offspring fall among the nations, scattering them among the lands.

Arminian

And would make their offspring fall among the nations, scattering them among the lands. Wesley: Overthrow—He sware also that He would punish sins in posterity.

Arminian pastoral: scattering threatened—national sin with generational cost. Classical Arminians still hold each generation accountable under grace offered anew.

Reformed

Calvin: destroy their seed among the heathen; scatter them throughout the lands. Holy Land preserved by Moses' prayers; dispersion would have been awful.

Reformed warning: offspring under the oath of scatter. Shared: offspring fall; scatter among the lands.

Psalms 106:28

28Then they yoked themselves to the Baal of Peor, and ate sacrifices offered to the dead;

Arminian

Then they yoked themselves to the Baal of Peor, and ate sacrifices offered to the dead. Wesley: Joined—communion with him as God's people have with God in worship.

Both camps: yoke to Baal; meals with the dead. Classical Arminians hear spiritual adultery—covenant broken by willing communion with idols.

Reformed

Calvin: joined themselves to Baal-peor; ate sacrifices of the dead. Perfidy: forsaking true worship, broken holy union as spouse. Ate what bound them to death instead of the living God.

Reformed accent: betrothal violated. Shared: Baal of Peor; sacrifices to the dead.

Psalms 106:29

29they provoked the Lord to anger with their deeds, and a plague broke out among them.

Arminian

They provoked the Lord to anger with their deeds, and a plague broke out among them.

Shared: provocation and plague. Classical Arminians preach deeds that kindle anger—sin that invites temporal judgment while mercy still waits (vv. 30–31).

Reformed

Calvin: provoked God by their works; plague broke out. Another plague as guard—chastisements fruitless when hearts are not bettered.

Reformed accent: wrath answered by plague. Shared: provoked; plague broke out.

Psalms 106:30

30Then Phinehas stood up and intervened, and the plague was stayed.

Arminian

Then Phinehas stood up and intervened, and the plague was stayed.

Arminian pastoral: one man's zeal stops plague—intervention as righteous act God accepts. Classical Arminians hear courage under grace that turns judgment.

Reformed

Calvin: Phinehas stood up and executed justice; plague stopped. Kind interposition; most effectual way to quench anger is when the sinner judges sin. Plague ceased at the punishment of a single person as people shrunk from the wickedness.

Reformed comfort: justice that stays plague. Shared: Phinehas stood; plague stayed.

Psalms 106:31

31And that was counted to him as righteousness from generation to generation forever.

Arminian

And that was counted to him as righteousness from generation to generation forever. Wesley: And—it was accepted and rewarded of God as an act of justice and piety.

Arminian hearing: counted for righteousness—reward of a faithful act, not a second gospel. Classical Arminians distinguish this reckoning from justification by faith alone in Christ.

Reformed

Calvin: deed imputed for righteousness from generation to generation. Honor on one casts reproach on the people. Work imputed as God imputes the faithful's works—by free grace, not intrinsic merit; faith alone procures righteousness for persons and works.

Reformed confession: gracious reckoning, not merit of congruity. Shared: counted as righteousness forever.

Psalms 106:32

32They angered him at the waters of Meribah, and it went ill with Moses on their account,

Arminian

They angered Him at the waters of Meribah, and it went ill with Moses on their account.

Both camps: Meribah wounds Moses. Classical Arminians preach the people's sin bringing grief on the servant—corporate guilt that does not erase Moses' own rashness (v. 33).

Reformed

Calvin: provoked Him at the waters of strife; it turned out ill to Moses on their account. Clamor against Moses was virtually against God; heinous that God did not spare His chosen servant.

Reformed accent: people hard-dealt Moses into judgment. Shared: Meribah; ill with Moses on their account.

Psalms 106:33

33for they made his spirit bitter, and he spoke rashly with his lips.

Arminian

For they made his spirit bitter, and he spoke rashly with his lips. Wesley notes the people's role in pushing Moses' spirit.

Arminian pastoral: bitter spirit, rash lips—even the meek can stumble when provoked. Classical Arminians hold both the people's guilt and Moses' accountability.

Reformed

Calvin: they grieved his spirit so that he spoke with his lips. Moses' gentle spirit fanned by perversity; he spoke unadvisedly, "Can God give water out of the rock?" Indignation burned; he did not calmly wait God's commandment.

Reformed warning: provocation does not excuse rash speech. Shared: spirit bitter; spoke rashly.

Psalms 106:34

34They did not destroy the peoples, as the Lord commanded them,

Arminian

They did not destroy the peoples, as the Lord commanded them.

Shared: incomplete obedience. Classical Arminians hear command refused—lenity that disobeys is not mercy when God has spoken judgment.

Reformed

Calvin: did not destroy the nations Jehovah commanded. Remiss in driving out; refused to execute righteous vengeance; land left liable to pollution. Two extremes: over-rigor and defeating justice by lenity—adhere to God's command.

Reformed accent: sparing when commanded to destroy is sin. Shared: did not destroy; as the Lord commanded.

Psalms 106:35

35but they mixed with the nations and learned to do as they did.

Arminian

But they mixed with the nations and learned to do as they did.

Arminian pastoral: mix and learn—imitation of the nations. Classical Arminians warn that association without holiness spreads corruption.

Reformed

Calvin: mingled with the heathen and learned their works. Foolish humanity issued in defilement. Nothing more dangerous than associating with the ungodly—prone to follow vice.

Reformed warning: mixture teaches their works. Shared: mixed; learned to do as they did.

Psalms 106:36

36They served their idols, which became a snare to them.

Arminian

They served their idols, which became a snare to them.

Both camps: idols as snare. Classical Arminians hear service that traps—worship that binds the worshiper.

Reformed

Calvin: served their idols; occasion of their overthrow. Confutes empty distinctions that claim honor without divine homage—image-worship condemned.

Reformed accent: service of idols snares and overthrows. Shared: served idols; snare.

Psalms 106:37

37They sacrificed their sons and their daughters to the demons;

Arminian

They sacrificed their sons and their daughters to the demons. Wesley: Devils—they did not worship God as pretended, but devils in their idols; spirits in images were evil spirits.

Arminian pastoral: child-sacrifice to demons—zeal without truth. Classical Arminians refuse "good intentions" as cover for horror.

Reformed

Calvin: sacrificed sons and daughters unto devils. Inconsiderate zeal is flimsy pretext; burning zeal increased guilt. Unlike Abraham's obedience, this was intemperate zeal casting off natural affection.

Reformed confession: demon-worship, not God. Shared: sacrificed children to the demons.

Psalms 106:38

38they poured out innocent blood, the blood of their sons and daughters, whom they sacrificed to the idols of Canaan, and the land was polluted with blood.

Arminian

They poured out innocent blood… sacrificed to the idols of Canaan, and the land was polluted with blood.

Both camps: innocent blood pollutes the land. Classical Arminians preach land-guilt that cries for judgment—holiness of the inheritance violated.

Reformed

Calvin: shed innocent blood of sons and daughters; land defiled with blood. Religious frenzy enhanced by cruelty; doubly wicked—defiling the land meant for God's worship and butchering children.

Reformed accent: pollution of the holy land. Shared: innocent blood; land polluted.

Psalms 106:39

39Thus they became unclean by their acts, and played the whore in their deeds.

Arminian

Thus they became unclean by their acts, and played the whore in their deeds.

Arminian hearing: unclean; played the whore—covenant adultery in deeds. Classical Arminians hear spiritual unfaithfulness as real breach God still may heal (vv. 44–45).

Reformed

Calvin: polluted with their own works; went a whoring after their own inventions. False worship devised without divine sanction is impurity; holiness in worship comes from the word.

Reformed accent: human inventions corrupt service. Shared: unclean by acts; played the whore in deeds.

Psalms 106:40

40Then the anger of the Lord was kindled against his people, and he abhorred his heritage;

Arminian

Then the anger of the Lord was kindled against His people, and He abhorred His heritage.

Shared: anger against His own people. Classical Arminians hear heritage abhorred yet not finally cast off—discipline inside covenant love.

Reformed

Calvin: wrath waxed hot against His people; He abhorred His inheritance. Severe punishment confirms no trivial offense. Sacred heritage delivered to abominations of the heathen—awful vindictive wrath.

Reformed confession: anger and abhorrence as judgment. Shared: anger kindled; abhorred His heritage.

Psalms 106:41

41he gave them into the hand of the nations, so that those who hated them ruled over them.

Arminian

He gave them into the hand of the nations, so that those who hated them ruled over them.

Both camps: given into enemies' hand. Classical Arminians preach rule of haters as just consequence—yet cry still heard (v. 44).

Reformed

Calvin: delivered into hands of the heathen; enemies ruled. Those who refused His yoke delivered to tyrants; those who would not endure paternal sway subdued under enemies' feet—retributive justice.

Reformed accent: given over as judgment. Shared: hand of the nations; haters ruled.

Psalms 106:42

42Their enemies oppressed them, and they were brought into subjection under their power.

Arminian

Their enemies oppressed them, and they were brought into subjection under their power.

Arminian pastoral: oppression and subjection—low estate that should humble. Classical Arminians call for repentance under the rod.

Reformed

Calvin: adversaries subdued them; afflicted under their hand. Bondage and oppression; folly that they were not truly humbled under God's almighty hand.

Reformed warning: subjection without repentance. Shared: oppressed; brought into subjection.

Psalms 106:43

43Many times he delivered them, but they were rebellious in their purposes and were brought low through their iniquity.

Arminian

Many times He delivered them, but they were rebellious in their purposes and were brought low through their iniquity. Wesley: Counsel—by forsaking God's way and following their own inventions.

Arminian hearing: many deliverances, repeated rebellion—grace resisted again and again. Classical Arminians glory that God kept delivering while purposes stayed rebellious.

Reformed

Calvin: many times He delivered them; they provoked Him with their counsel and were oppressed by iniquity. Benefits could not bend them; after wonderful deliverances they backslid. Nevertheless groanings were heard.

Reformed accent: repeated mercy, repeated revolt, just lowering. Shared: many times delivered; rebellious; brought low.

Key contrast Both confess many deliverances met by continued rebellion and being brought low. Arminians typically emphasize resistible mercy repeatedly offered and refused. Reformed readers typically emphasize Calvin's unwearied kindness striving with perversity while iniquity justly oppresses.

Psalms 106:44

44Nevertheless, he looked upon their distress, when he heard their cry.

Arminian

Nevertheless, He looked upon their distress, when He heard their cry.

Both camps: nevertheless—look and hearing. Classical Arminians hear cry met by attention—God not deaf to distressed rebels who call.

Reformed

Calvin: He saw when they were in straits; heard their cry. Though most deserving of afflictions, groanings heard—unwearied kindness.

Reformed comfort: look and hearing after rebellion. Shared: looked on distress; heard their cry.

Psalms 106:45

45For their sake he remembered his covenant, and relented according to the abundance of his steadfast love.

Arminian

For their sake He remembered His covenant, and relented according to the abundance of His steadfast love. Wesley: Repented—changed His course and dealing with them.

Arminian pastoral: covenant remembered; relenting according to abundant love—mercy that changes dealings without denying holiness. Classical Arminians preach abundance of steadfast love as sincere hope for the gathered (v. 47).

Reformed

Calvin: remembered His covenant toward them; it repented Him according to the greatness of His mercies. Covenant declares gracious pardon; "repent" expresses no change in God but in the mode of administering corrections—Scripture accommodates to our capacity.

Reformed confession: covenant memory and great mercies. Shared: remembered covenant; relented; abundance of steadfast love.

Key contrast Both confess God remembered His covenant and relented according to abundant steadfast love. Arminians typically hear changed dealings in mercy toward those who cry. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: no change in God Himself; covenant grace and great mercies alter the administration of judgment.

Psalms 106:46

46He caused them to be pitied by all those who held them captive.

Arminian

He caused them to be pitied by all those who held them captive.

Arminian hearing: pity from captors—God turns hearts. Classical Arminians preach providence over even enemy compassion.

Reformed

Calvin: made them find pity from those who carried them captive. Hearts of all men under God's control—to harden or soften. When anger appeased, cruelty changed into mercy; wolves transformed into lambs.

Reformed accent: God softens captors. Shared: caused to be pitied by captors.

Psalms 106:47

47Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the nations, that we may give thanks to your holy name and glory in your praise.

Arminian

Save us, O Lord our God, and gather us from among the nations, that we may give thanks to Your holy name and glory in Your praise.

Both camps: save and gather for thanks and glory. Classical Arminians hear prayer for gathering sincerely offered—end is praise, not mere safety.

Reformed

Calvin: Save us… gather us from among the heathen, to praise Your holy name and glory in Your praise. Penned in sad dispersion; covenant remembrance fuels hope of forgiveness. End of redemption: celebrate the Name.

Reformed pastoral: gather for praise. Shared: save; gather; thanks; glory in praise.

Psalms 106:48

48Blessed be the Lord, the God of Israel, from everlasting to everlasting! And let all the people say, "Amen!" Praise the Lord!

Arminian

Blessed be the Lord, the God of Israel, from everlasting to everlasting! And let all the people say, "Amen!" Praise the Lord!

Arminian pastoral: doxology and Amen—people consent to bless God even under chastening. Classical Arminians end where they began: Hallelujah.

Both camps: everlasting blessing; corporate Amen; Praise the Lord.

Reformed

Calvin: Blessed be Jehovah… let all the people say Amen. Praise ye Jehovah. Regulates prayers so dejected captives may still render thanks; meek submission under correction. Consent to praises though privately and publicly overwhelmed.

Reformed comfort: bless God in affliction; Amen of the people. Shared: everlasting blessing; Amen; Praise the Lord.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on Israel's rebellion remembered, salvation for the Lord's name's sake, Moses standing in the breach, covenant mercy after many deliverances, and a people gathered to give thanks—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 106:1–48.

Arminian Notable voices

“The foregoing psalm was an history of God's goodness to Israel; this is an history of their rebellion against him. Saved them—that he may vindicate his name from the blasphemous reproaches which would have been cast upon it if they had been destroyed. Breach—God had made a wall about them; but they had made a breach in it by their sins, at which the Lord might enter to destroy them; which he had certainly done, if Moses by his prevailing intercession had not hindered him. Repented—changed his course and dealing with them. It begins and ends with Hallelujah.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Psalm 106)

Reformed Notable voices

“This psalm acknowledges that remarkable benefits had been turned to a bad account; yet it is not so much reproof as confession of sins in order to obtaining pardon. The Israelites were saved, not on account of their deserving to be so, but because God had a regard to his own glory—that his holy name might not become a reproach among the heathen. He remembered his covenant towards them, and it repented him according to the greatness of his mercies. The word repent expresses no change in God, but only in the mode of administering his corrections.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Psalm 106)
