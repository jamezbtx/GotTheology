Passage

Psalms 69

Verses 1–36 · ESV · Voices

To the choirmaster: according to Lilies. Of David. Waters to the neck, zeal for the house, gall and sour wine, imprecations on persecutors, then praise that pleases more than sacrifice and a promise that God will save Zion.

Psalms 69:1

1Save me, O God! For the waters have come up to my neck.

Arminian

Waters to the neck—save me. Wesley: waters mean tribulations. The cry is desperate and real.

Arminian pastoral: drowning distress does not cancel prayer; grace enables the call when the soul is nearly under.

Reformed

Calvin: waters enter unto the heart—suffocation forced them in. Even a courageous man reaches the brink of despair. Shared: save me is the first word.

Psalms 69:2

2I sink in deep mire, where there is no foothold; I have come into deep waters, and the flood sweeps over me.

Arminian

Deep mire, no foothold; flood sweeps. Standing is gone.

Both camps hear utter helplessness—deliverance must come from outside the slough.

Reformed

Calvin: in mire there is no springing up; flood brings disorder. Shared: no foothold left.

Psalms 69:3

3I am weary with my crying out; my throat is parched. My eyes grow dim with waiting for my God.

Arminian

Weary crying; throat parched; eyes dim waiting.

Arminian note: waiting is persevering faith that can still grow faint—keep crying.

Reformed

Calvin: weary does not mean he quit; hoarse throat and failing eyes mark untiring perseverance. Shared: waiting for God.

Psalms 69:4

4More in number than the hairs of my head are those who hate me without cause; mighty are those who would destroy me, those who attack me with lies. What I did not steal must I now restore?

Arminian

Hate without cause; restore what I did not steal. Wesley: restored for peace’s sake. John 15:25 cites without-cause hatred of Christ.

Arminian pastoral: innocent suffering under lies is real.

Reformed

Calvin: mortal hatred kingdom-wide; without cause; liars. Good conscience under false accusation. Shared christology: hated without cause.

Psalms 69:5

5O God, you know my folly; the wrongs I have done are not hidden from you.

Arminian

You know my folly; wrongs not hidden. Wesley: innocent toward enemies, yet guilty of many sins against God.

Arminian weight: confession before God remains necessary even when enemies lie.

Reformed

Calvin: irony—he flees to God as defender of his cause; integrity known to God. Shared: nothing hidden.

Key contrast Both place folly before God while enemies lie. Arminians typically stress sincere confession of real faults toward God; Reformed readers typically hear ironic appeal from calumny to the God who knows.

Psalms 69:6

6Let not those who hope in you be put to shame through me, O Lord God of hosts; let not those who seek you be brought to dishonor through me, O God of Israel.

Arminian

Let not hopers be shamed through me. Wesley: if they see me forsaken they will be discouraged.

Arminian pastoral: one believer’s fall can stumble seekers.

Reformed

Calvin: set forth as example of hope or despair; prayer that seekers not be ashamed in him. Shared: corporate stake.

Psalms 69:7

7For it is for your sake that I have borne reproach, that dishonor has covered my face.

Arminian

For your sake—reproach and dishonor. Wesley: for obedience and zeal for glory.

Arminian note: reproach for God’s sake is costly discipleship.

Reformed

Calvin: cause is God’s; hatred for the anointing. Shared: for-your-sake suffering.

Psalms 69:8

8I have become a stranger to my brothers, an alien to my mother's sons.

Arminian

Stranger to brothers; alien to mother’s sons.

Both camps take the alienation seriously—loyalty to God can estrange kin.

Reformed

Calvin: when devotion displeases brethren, follow God, not flesh and blood. Shared: alien at home.

Psalms 69:9

9For zeal for your house has consumed me, and the reproaches of those who reproach you have fallen on me.

Arminian

Zeal for your house consumed me; reproaches of you fell on me. Wesley: applied more fully to Christ—John 2:17 and Romans 15:3.

Arminian christology: David’s zeal prefigures Christ’s.

Reformed

Calvin: fighting God’s quarrel; Evangelists apply to Christ; Paul applies the second member to the faithful. Shared messianic reading.

Key contrast Both hear David and, more fully, Christ. Shared christology on zeal and transferred reproach.

Psalms 69:10

10When I wept and humbled my soul with fasting, it became my reproach.

Arminian

Weeping and fasting became reproach. Wesley: wept for their impiety; they derided him.

Arminian note: pious sorrow can be mocked—still do it.

Reformed

Calvin: signs of pure zeal turned into mockery. Shared: fasting mocked.

Psalms 69:11

11When I made sackcloth my clothing, I became a byword to them.

Arminian

Sackcloth; byword. Wesley: proverb of reproach.

Both: humiliation garb becomes joke.

Reformed

Calvin: mourning tokens turned to jest. Shared: byword.

Psalms 69:12

12I am the talk of those who sit in the gate, and the drunkards make songs about me.

Arminian

Talk of the gate; drunkards’ songs. Wesley: vain idle persons in gates and markets.

Arminian pastoral: public scorn is real pressure; prayer remains the answer.

Reformed

Calvin: even judges join the mockery. Shared: song of drunkards.

Psalms 69:13

13But as for me, my prayer is to you, O Lord. At an acceptable time, O God, in the abundance of your steadfast love answer me in your saving faithfulness.

Arminian

Prayer at an acceptable time; steadfast love; saving faithfulness. Wesley: according to saving faithfulness.

Arminian hope: acceptable time is sought and waited for—pray on.

Reformed

Calvin: driven from the world to God; hope shining in darkness. Shared: abundance of mercy.

Psalms 69:14

14Deliver me from sinking in the mire; let me be delivered from my enemies and from the deep waters.

Arminian

Deliver from mire, enemies, deep waters.

Both: salvation must lift out of what already swallowed.

Reformed

Calvin: previously sunk; now prays not to sink—hope looking to the issue. Shared: deliver me.

Psalms 69:15

15Let not the flood sweep over me, or the deep swallow me up, or the pit close its mouth over me.

Arminian

Flood, deep, pit’s mouth. Total engulfment feared.

Arminian plea: ask that the pit not close—grace hears distress.

Reformed

Calvin: let not weight of affliction swallow. Shared: pit language.

Psalms 69:16

16Answer me, O Lord, for your steadfast love is good; according to your abundant mercy, turn to me.

Arminian

Steadfast love good; abundant mercy—turn to me.

Arminian pastoral: plead mercy that can be sought.

Reformed

Calvin: hard to believe God merciful when He seems angry; only His character sustains hope. Shared: love and mercy.

Psalms 69:17

17Hide not your face from your servant, for I am in distress; make haste to answer me.

Arminian

Hide not your face; distress; make haste.

Arminian note: haste prayed for is sincere dependence.

Reformed

Calvin: hide-face fear from delayed help; still God’s servant. Shared: make haste.

Psalms 69:18

18Draw near to my soul, redeem me; ransom me because of my enemies!

Arminian

Draw near; redeem; ransom because of enemies.

Both: God must come close; enemies press the need.

Reformed

Calvin: Draw near implies felt distance; faith still asks nearness. Shared: redeem me.

Psalms 69:19

19You know my reproach, and my shame and my dishonor; my foes are all known to you.

Arminian

You know reproach, shame, dishonor; foes known.

Arminian comfort: God knows—pray from that knowledge.

Reformed

Calvin: Providence not overcast; God witness of grief. Shared: all before thee.

Psalms 69:20

20Reproaches have broken my heart, so that I am in despair. I looked for pity, but there was none, and for comforters, but I found none.

Arminian

Reproach broke the heart; no pity, no comforters.

Arminian pastoral: broken heart still looks—and may find none but God.

Reformed

Calvin: not without struggle; no human relief. Shared: broken heart.

Psalms 69:21

21They gave me poison for food, and for my thirst they gave me sour wine to drink.

Arminian

Poison for food; sour wine for thirst. Wesley: metaphorically David, properly Christ.

Arminian christology: Spirit intended Christ’s sufferings principally here.

Reformed

Calvin: John rightly sees fulfillment when soldiers give vinegar. Shared messianic reading.

Key contrast Both land messianic fulfillment at the cross without inventing an Arminian/Reformed split on the vinegar.

Psalms 69:22

22Let their own table before them become a snare; and when they are at peace, let it become a trap.

Arminian

Table become snare and trap. Wesley: punishment answers the sin of gall.

Arminian note: imprecation is serious judicial plea, not private spite.

Reformed

Calvin: Spirit-guided imprecation within duty; not reckless revenge. Shared: table/snare seriousness.

Key contrast Both take imprecation seriously. Arminians typically hear a real judicial cry under oppression; Reformed readers typically stress Spirit-kept bounds and judgment on the reprobate.

Psalms 69:23

23Let their eyes be darkened, so that they cannot see, and make their loins tremble continually.

Arminian

Eyes darkened; loins tremble. Wesley: minds judicially blinded; strength taken.

Arminian pastoral: judicial hardening warns against shutting eyes.

Reformed

Calvin: deprive of counsel and strength. Shared: darkening.

Psalms 69:24

24Pour out your indignation upon them, and let your burning anger overtake them.

Arminian

Pour indignation; burning anger.

Both refuse to soft-pedal divine anger against persecutors of the godly.

Reformed

Calvin: imprecations to terrify frantic enemies and comfort believers. Shared: pour out.

Psalms 69:25

25May their camp be a desolation; let no one dwell in their tents.

Arminian

Camp desolate; empty tents. Related judgment language appears in Acts 1:20.

Arminian note: desolation of the hostile camp is prayed as public justice.

Reformed

Calvin: wrath may reach posterity per the law’s threat. Shared: desolate habitation.

Psalms 69:26

26For they persecute him whom you have struck down, and they recount the pain of those you have wounded.

Arminian

Persecute whom you struck; recount the wounded’s pain. Wesley: barbarous cruelty; triumph in calamities.

Arminian weight: piling on the God-struck is extra guilt.

Reformed

Calvin: smitten as God’s child under discipline; persecuting the wounded adds damnation. Shared: cruelty aggravates.

Psalms 69:27

27Add to them punishment upon punishment; may they have no acquittal from you.

Arminian

Punishment upon punishment; no acquittal. Wesley: give up to lusts; not partake mercy.

Arminian reading: exclusion from mercy is a real sentence prayed against hardened foes.

Reformed

Calvin: give over to reprobate mind; not enter righteousness. Shared: no acquittal.

Psalms 69:28

28Let them be blotted out of the book of the living; let them not be enrolled among the righteous.

Arminian

Blotted from book of living; not enrolled with righteous. Wesley: eternal life.

Arminian pastoral: enrollment language warns—apostasy is not a game.

Reformed

Calvin: book of life as election purpose; hypocrites blotted as openly rejected; elect finally not cut off. Shared seriousness; classic fork on perseverance.

Key contrast Both treat blotting as ultimate. Arminians typically hear real exclusion from life; Reformed readers typically hear judgment revealing who were never written among the righteous.

Psalms 69:29

29But I am afflicted and in pain; let your salvation, O God, set me on high!

Arminian

Afflicted and in pain; salvation set me on high. Wesley: out of enemies’ reach.

Arminian hope: salvation lifts the poor sufferer who still asks.

Reformed

Calvin: broken humility vs raging revenge; salvation shall exalt. Shared: set on high.

Psalms 69:30

30I will praise the name of God with a song; I will magnify him with thanksgiving.

Arminian

Praise with song; magnify with thanksgiving.

Both: praise is the fitting answer when God saves.

Reformed

Calvin: elevated with joy; God magnified among men by praise. Shared: song.

Psalms 69:31

31This will please the Lord more than an ox or a bull with horns and hoofs.

Arminian

Pleases more than ox or bull. Wesley: hearty praise over glorious legal sacrifices.

Arminian note: thanksgiving is the sacrifice God prefers—offer it.

Reformed

Calvin: spiritual service preferred to Levitical pomp. Shared: better than bull.

Psalms 69:32

32When the humble see it they will be glad; you who seek God, let your hearts revive.

Arminian

Humble glad; seekers’ hearts revive. Wesley: dejected revived.

Arminian pastoral: one deliverance revives many seekers.

Reformed

Calvin: effects extend to others; pledge of grace. Shared: hearts revive.

Psalms 69:33

33For the Lord hears the needy and does not despise his own people who are prisoners.

Arminian

Lord hears needy; does not despise prisoner-people. Wesley: prison or affliction for his sake.

Arminian comfort: needy prayer is heard.

Reformed

Calvin: deliverance of one signals care for all oppressed. Shared: hears the needy.

Psalms 69:34

34Let heaven and earth praise him, the seas and everything that moves in them.

Arminian

Heaven, earth, seas praise.

Both widen praise beyond the individual.

Reformed

Calvin: hyperbole teaching ardor; concern for Church preservation. Shared: all creation praise.

Psalms 69:35

35For God will save Zion and build up the cities of Judah, and people shall dwell there and possess it;

Arminian

God will save Zion; build Judah; dwell and possess. Wesley: church and people; literal then heavenly Canaan.

Arminian hope: Zion’s future is promised and believed.

Reformed

Calvin: speaks for the Church; blessing from the sanctuary. Shared: save Zion.

Psalms 69:36

36the offspring of his servants shall inherit it, and those who love his name shall dwell in it.

Arminian

Offspring of servants inherit; those who love his name dwell.

Arminian note: loving the name is the mark of dwellers—love grace enables and requires.

Reformed

Calvin: true seed who love the name, not hypocrite boasters. Shared: love his name.

Key contrast Both end on lovers of the Name inheriting. Arminians typically stress love as living response; Reformed readers typically stress the true seed vs hypocrites.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on drowning distress, zeal for God’s house, vinegar at the cross, and serious imprecation—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 69.

Arminian Notable voices

“Zeal—That fervent love which I have for thy house and service, and glory, and people. Eaten—Exhausted my spirits. Upon me—I have been as deeply affected with thy reproaches, as with mine own. This tho' truly belonging to David, yet was also directed by the spirit of God in him, to represent the disposition and condition of Christ, in whom it was more fully accomplished, to whom therefore it is applied in the New Testament, the first part of it, John 2:17, and the latter, Rom 15:3. Vinegar—These things were metaphorically fulfilled in David, but properly in Christ.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 69:9, 21)

Reformed Notable voices

“For the zeal of thy house hath eaten me up… David… affirms that he is fighting in God’s quarrel… by the zeal for the Church of God with which his soul was inflamed… It is, therefore, not surprising to find the Evangelists applying this passage to Christ (John 2:17). In like manner, Paul, in Romans 15:3… They put gall into my meat… the Apostle John justly declares that this Scripture was fulfilled when the soldiers gave Christ vinegar to drink upon the cross (John 19:28–30).”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 69:9, 21)
