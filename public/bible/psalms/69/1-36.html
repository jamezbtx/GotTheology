Passage

Psalms 69

Verses 1–36 · WEB · Voices

To the choirmaster: according to Lilies. Of David. Waters to the neck, zeal for the house, gall and sour wine, imprecations on persecutors, then praise that pleases more than sacrifice and a promise that God will save Zion.

Psalms 69:1

1Save me, God, for the waters have come up to my neck!

Arminian

Waters to the neck—save me. Wesley: waters mean tribulations. The cry is desperate and authentic.

Drowning distress does not nullify prayer; grace enables the call when the soul is nearly under.

Reformed

Calvin: waters enter unto the heart—suffocation forced them in. Even a courageous man reaches the brink of despair. Both hear: save me is the first word.

Psalms 69:2

2I sink in deep mire, where there is no foothold. I have come into deep waters, where the floods overflow me.

Arminian

Deep mire, no foothold; flood sweeps. Standing is gone.

Both camps hear utter helplessness—deliverance must come from outside the slough.

Reformed

Calvin: in mire there is no springing up; flood brings disorder. No foothold left.

Psalms 69:3

3I am weary with my crying. My throat is dry. My eyes fail looking for my God.

Arminian

Weary crying; throat parched; eyes dim waiting.

Waiting is persevering faith that can still grow faint—keep crying.

Reformed

Calvin: weary does not mean he quit; hoarse throat and failing eyes mark untiring perseverance. Both see: waiting for God.

Psalms 69:4

4Those who hate me without a cause are more than the hairs of my head. Those who want to cut me off, being my enemies wrongfully, are mighty. I have to restore what I didn’t take away.

Arminian

Hate without cause; restore what I did not steal. Wesley: restored for peace’s sake. John 15:25 cites without-cause hatred of Christ.

Innocent suffering under lies is deeply felt.

Reformed

Calvin: mortal hatred kingdom-wide; without cause; liars. Good conscience under false accusation. Shared christology: hated without cause.

Psalms 69:5

5God, you know my foolishness. My sins aren’t hidden from you.

Arminian

You know my folly; wrongs not hidden. Wesley: innocent toward enemies, yet guilty of many sins against God.

Confession before God remains necessary even when enemies lie.

Reformed

Calvin: irony—he flees to God as defender of his cause; integrity known to God. Nothing hidden.

Key contrast Both place folly before God while enemies lie. Arminians typically underline sincere confession of faults toward God; Reformed readers hear ironic appeal from calumny to the God who knows.

Psalms 69:6

6Don’t let those who wait for you be shamed through me, Lord GOD of Armies. Don’t let those who seek you be brought to dishonor through me, God of Israel.

Arminian

Let not hopers be shamed through me. Wesley: if they see me forsaken they will be discouraged.

One believer’s fall can stumble seekers.

Reformed

Calvin: set forth as example of hope or despair; prayer that seekers not be ashamed in him. Undisputed: corporate stake.

Psalms 69:7

7Because for your sake, I have borne reproach. Shame has covered my face.

Arminian

For your sake—reproach and dishonor. Wesley: for obedience and zeal for glory.

Reproach for God’s sake is costly discipleship.

Reformed

Calvin: cause is God’s; hatred for the anointing. For-your-sake suffering.

Psalms 69:8

8I have become a stranger to my brothers, an alien to my mother’s children.

Arminian

Stranger to brothers; alien to mother’s sons.

Both camps take the alienation seriously—loyalty to God can estrange kin.

Reformed

Calvin: when devotion displeases brethren, follow God, not flesh and blood. Alien at home.

Psalms 69:9

9For the zeal of your house consumes me. The reproaches of those who reproach you have fallen on me.

Arminian

Zeal for your house consumed me; reproaches of you fell on me. Wesley: applied more fully to Christ—John 2:17 and Romans 15:3.

David’s zeal prefigures Christ’s.

Reformed

Calvin: fighting God’s quarrel; Evangelists apply to Christ; Paul applies the second member to the faithful. Shared messianic reading.

Key contrast Both hear David and, more fully, Christ. Shared christology on zeal and transferred reproach.

Psalms 69:10

10When I wept and I fasted, that was to my reproach.

Arminian

Weeping and fasting became reproach. Wesley: wept for their impiety; they derided him.

Pious sorrow can be mocked—still do it.

Reformed

Calvin: signs of pure zeal turned into mockery. No dispute here: fasting mocked.

Psalms 69:11

11When I made sackcloth my clothing, I became a byword to them.

Arminian

Sackcloth; byword. Wesley: proverb of reproach.

Both: humiliation garb becomes joke.

Reformed

Calvin: mourning tokens turned to jest. Byword.

Psalms 69:12

12Those who sit in the gate talk about me. I am the song of the drunkards.

Arminian

Talk of the gate; drunkards’ songs. Wesley: vain idle persons in gates and markets.

Public scorn is pressure; prayer remains the answer.

Reformed

Calvin: even judges join the mockery. Both affirm: song of drunkards.

Psalms 69:13

13But as for me, my prayer is to you, LORD, in an acceptable time. God, in the abundance of your loving kindness, answer me in the truth of your salvation.

Arminian

Prayer at an acceptable time; steadfast love; saving faithfulness. Wesley: according to saving faithfulness.

Acceptable time is sought and waited for—pray on.

Reformed

Calvin: driven from the world to God; hope shining in darkness. Where they meet: abundance of mercy.

Psalms 69:14

14Deliver me out of the mire, and don’t let me sink. Let me be delivered from those who hate me, and out of the deep waters.

Arminian

Deliver from mire, enemies, deep waters.

Both: salvation must lift out of what already swallowed.

Reformed

Calvin: previously sunk; now prays not to sink—hope looking to the issue. Both traditions hear: deliver me.

Psalms 69:15

15Don’t let the flood waters overwhelm me, neither let the deep swallow me up. Don’t let the pit shut its mouth on me.

Arminian

Flood, deep, pit’s mouth. Total engulfment feared.

Ask that the pit not close—grace hears distress.

Reformed

Calvin: let not weight of affliction swallow. Common reading: pit language.

Psalms 69:16

16Answer me, LORD, for your loving kindness is good. According to the multitude of your tender mercies, turn to me.

Arminian

Steadfast love good; tender mercies, turn to me.

Plead mercy that can be sought.

Reformed

Calvin: hard to believe God merciful when He seems angry; only His character sustains hope. Love and mercy.

Psalms 69:17

17Don’t hide your face from your servant, for I am in distress. Answer me speedily!

Arminian

Hide not your face; distress; make haste.

Haste prayed for is sincere dependence.

Reformed

Calvin: hide-face fear from delayed help; still God’s servant. Make haste.

Psalms 69:18

18Draw near to my soul and redeem it. Ransom me because of my enemies.

Arminian

Draw near; redeem; ransom because of enemies.

Both: God must come close; enemies press the need.

Reformed

Calvin: Draw near implies felt distance; faith still asks nearness. Both confess: redeem me.

Psalms 69:19

19You know my reproach, my shame, and my dishonor. My adversaries are all before you.

Arminian

You know reproach, shame, dishonor; foes known.

God knows—pray from that knowledge.

Reformed

Calvin: Providence not overcast; God witness of grief. All before thee.

Psalms 69:20

20Reproach has broken my heart, and I am full of heaviness. I looked for some to take pity, but there was none; for comforters, but I found none.

Arminian

Reproach broke the heart; no pity, no comforters.

Broken heart still looks—and may find none but God.

Reformed

Calvin: not without struggle; no human relief. Broken heart.

Psalms 69:21

21They also gave me poison for my food. In my thirst, they gave me vinegar to drink.

Arminian

Poison for food; sour wine for thirst. Wesley: metaphorically David, properly Christ.

Spirit intended Christ’s sufferings principally here.

Reformed

Calvin: John rightly sees fulfillment when soldiers give vinegar. Shared messianic reading.

Key contrast Both land messianic fulfillment at the cross without inventing an Arminian/Reformed split on the vinegar.

Psalms 69:22

22Let their table before them become a snare. May it become a retribution and a trap.

Arminian

Table become snare and trap. Wesley: punishment answers the sin of gall.

Imprecation is serious judicial plea, not private spite.

Reformed

Calvin: Spirit-guided imprecation within duty; not reckless revenge. Table/snare seriousness.

Key contrast Both take imprecation seriously. Arminian readers find a judicial cry under oppression; Reformed interpreters emphasize Spirit-kept bounds and judgment on the reprobate.

Psalms 69:23

23Let their eyes be darkened, so that they can’t see. Let their backs be continually bent.

Arminian

Eyes darkened; loins tremble. Wesley: minds judicially blinded; strength taken.

Judicial hardening warns against shutting eyes.

Reformed

Calvin: deprive of counsel and strength. Darkening.

Psalms 69:24

24Pour out your indignation on them. Let the fierceness of your anger overtake them.

Arminian

He asks God to pour out his indignation and burning anger.

Both refuse to soft-pedal divine anger against persecutors of the godly.

Reformed

Calvin: imprecations to terrify frantic enemies and comfort believers. Pour out.

Psalms 69:25

25Let their habitation be desolate. Let no one dwell in their tents.

Arminian

He prays that their camp would be left desolate and their tents empty. Related judgment language appears in Acts 1:20.

Desolation of the hostile camp is prayed as public justice.

Reformed

Calvin: wrath may reach posterity per the law’s threat. Either reading: desolate habitation.

Psalms 69:26

26For they persecute him whom you have wounded. They tell of the sorrow of those whom you have hurt.

Arminian

Persecute whom you struck; recount the wounded’s pain. Wesley: barbarous cruelty; triumph in calamities.

Piling on the God-struck is extra guilt.

Reformed

Calvin: smitten as God’s child under discipline; persecuting the wounded adds damnation. Both sides agree: cruelty aggravates.

Psalms 69:27

27Charge them with crime upon crime. Don’t let them come into your righteousness.

Arminian

Punishment upon punishment; no acquittal. Wesley: give up to lusts; not partake mercy.

Exclusion from mercy is a sentence prayed against hardened foes.

Reformed

Calvin: give over to reprobate mind; not enter righteousness. Both traditions see: no acquittal.

Psalms 69:28

28Let them be blotted out of the book of life, and not be written with the righteous.

Arminian

Blotted from book of living; not enrolled with righteous. Wesley: eternal life.

Enrollment language warns—apostasy is not a game.

Reformed

Calvin: book of life as election purpose; hypocrites blotted as openly rejected; elect finally not cut off. Shared seriousness; classic fork on perseverance.

Key contrast Both treat blotting as ultimate. Arminians typically hear exclusion from life; Reformed commentators often hear judgment revealing who were never written among the righteous.

Psalms 69:29

29But I am in pain and distress. Let your salvation, God, protect me.

Arminian

Afflicted and in pain; salvation set me on high. Wesley: out of enemies’ reach.

Salvation lifts the poor sufferer who still asks.

Reformed

Calvin: broken humility vs raging revenge; salvation shall exalt. Set on high.

Psalms 69:30

30I will praise the name of God with a song, and will magnify him with thanksgiving.

Arminian

Praise with song; magnify with thanksgiving.

Both: praise is the fitting answer when God saves.

Reformed

Calvin: elevated with joy; God magnified among men by praise. Song.

Psalms 69:31

31It will please the LORD better than an ox, or a bull that has horns and hoofs.

Arminian

Pleases more than ox or bull. Wesley: hearty praise over glorious legal sacrifices.

Thanksgiving is the sacrifice God prefers—offer it.

Reformed

Calvin: spiritual service preferred to Levitical pomp. Better than bull.

Psalms 69:32

32The humble have seen it, and are glad. You who seek after God, let your heart live.

Arminian

Humble glad; seekers’ hearts revive. Wesley: dejected revived.

One deliverance revives many seekers.

Reformed

Calvin: effects extend to others; pledge of grace. Both note: hearts revive.

Psalms 69:33

33For the LORD hears the needy, and doesn’t despise his captive people.

Arminian

Lord hears needy; does not despise prisoner-people. Wesley: prison or affliction for his sake.

Needy prayer is heard.

Reformed

Calvin: deliverance of one signals care for all oppressed. Hears the needy.

Psalms 69:34

34Let heaven and earth praise him; the seas, and everything that moves therein!

Arminian

Heaven, earth, seas praise.

Both widen praise beyond the individual.

Reformed

Calvin: hyperbole teaching ardor; concern for Church preservation. All creation praise.

Psalms 69:35

35For God will save Zion, and build the cities of Judah. They shall settle there, and own it.

Arminian

God will save Zion; build Judah; dwell and possess. Wesley: church and people; literal then heavenly Canaan.

Zion’s future is promised and believed.

Reformed

Calvin: speaks for the Church; blessing from the sanctuary. Save Zion.

Psalms 69:36

36The children also of his servants shall inherit it. Those who love his name shall dwell therein.

Arminian

Offspring of servants inherit; those who love his name dwell.

Loving the name is the mark of dwellers—love grace enables and requires.

Reformed

Calvin: true seed who love the name, not hypocrite boasters. Love his name.

Key contrast Both end on lovers of the Name inheriting. Arminians stress love as living response; Reformed readers underline the true seed vs hypocrites.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Classic comment on drowning distress, zeal for God’s house, vinegar at the cross, and serious imprecation, from both sides of the debate.

Arminian Notable voices

“Zeal—That fervent love which I have for thy house and service, and glory, and people. Eaten—Exhausted my spirits. Upon me—I have been as deeply affected with thy reproaches, as with mine own. This tho' truly belonging to David, yet was also directed by the spirit of God in him, to represent the disposition and condition of Christ, in whom it was more fully accomplished, to whom therefore it is applied in the New Testament, the first part of it, John 2:17, and the latter, Rom 15:3. Vinegar—These things were metaphorically fulfilled in David, but properly in Christ.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 69:9, 21)

Reformed Notable voices

“For the zeal of thy house hath eaten me up… David… affirms that he is fighting in God’s quarrel… by the zeal for the Church of God with which his soul was inflamed… It is, therefore, not surprising to find the Evangelists applying this passage to Christ (John 2:17). In like manner, Paul, in Romans 15:3… They put gall into my meat… the Apostle John justly declares that this Scripture was fulfilled when the soldiers gave Christ vinegar to drink upon the cross (John 19:28–30).”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 69:9, 21)
