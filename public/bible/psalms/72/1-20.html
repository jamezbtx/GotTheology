Passage

Psalms 72

Verses 1–20 · WEB · Voices

Of Solomon. A royal prayer that God’s justice and righteousness clothe the king’s son: he judges the poor, crushes the oppressor, endures with sun and moon, rules from sea to sea, receives tribute from kings, delivers the needy, and is blessed forever—so the earth fills with his fame. The prayers by David, the son of Jesse are ended.

Psalms 72:1

1God, give the king your justice; your righteousness to the royal son.

Arminian

Wesley: Judgments—wherewith thou governest; Righteousness—justice and Spirit-fruits for the king’s son. The prayer asks God-given equity for the throne.

Shared royal/messianic frame—justice is gift before it is policy. The king (and greater Son) must receive what only God gives; hearers still pray for righteous rule.

Reformed

Calvin: Solomon asks the spirit of judgment and righteousness for the king; principally Christ’s kingdom is intended. Justice and righteousness are God’s to bestow on the royal son.

Throne ethics begin as divine gift. The Anointed rules by what God gives, not by self-made equity.

Key contrast Both ask God to clothe the king’s son with justice and righteousness, and both read beyond Solomon to Christ. Arminians underline prayer for righteous rule that must still be exercised; Reformed readers press that judgment and righteousness are bestowed for Christ’s kingdom by God’s gift.

Psalms 72:2

2He will judge your people with righteousness, and your poor with justice.

Arminian

Wesley: the poor especially need the king’s just judgment. People and poor stand together under righteous rule.

Care for the poor is not optional royal garnish. Grace that saves also summons rulers (and the church under Christ) to just judgment of the needy.

Reformed

Calvin: he shall judge thy people with righteousness, and thy poor with judgment. Christ’s reign is marked by equity toward the lowly.

Shared messianic justice—the poor are not forgotten under the true King.

Psalms 72:3

3The mountains shall bring prosperity to the people. The hills bring the fruit of righteousness.

Arminian

Wesley: Mountains—often dry—bring peace/prosperity by righteous government. Hills yield under righteousness.

Shalom rides on right rule. Geography itself is imagined fruitful when justice reigns.

Reformed

Calvin: mountains and hills picture the land’s flourishing under the king’s righteousness—peace as fruit of equity.

Prosperity without righteousness is not this psalm’s hope.

Psalms 72:4

4He will judge the poor of the people. He will save the children of the needy, and will break the oppressor in pieces.

Arminian

Wesley’s trajectory: defend poor, deliver needy children, crush the oppressor. Mercy and severity meet.

Shared seriousness—oppressors are crushed, not negotiated into endless harm. The King’s grace toward the needy includes judgment on predators.

Reformed

Calvin: deliverance of the needy and crushing of oppressors mark the Messiah’s rule. Pity and power are not rivals.

Christ’s kingdom is refuge for the crushed and terror for the crusher.

Psalms 72:5

5They shall fear you while the sun endures; and as long as the moon, throughout all generations.

Arminian

Wesley: Fear—true filial fear; Sun—as long as sun and moon. Generational reverence is the aim.

Shared—fear of God outlasts dynasties. The summons to filial fear remains live for every generation that sees the King’s days.

Reformed

Calvin: enduring fear of God accompanies the King’s long reign—sun and moon measure permanence beyond one Solomon.

The prayer reaches beyond any single reign to the Messiah. Temporary thrones cannot exhaust this while-the-sun prayer.

Psalms 72:6

6He will come down like rain on the mown grass, as showers that water the earth.

Arminian

Wesley: gentle, renewing rule—rain on mown grass, showers on earth. Soft power that refreshes.

The King refreshes rather than only scorches. Grace that waters still invites the land to drink.

Reformed

Calvin: rain and showers image beneficial influence—Christ’s reign restores what cutting left bare.

Messianic refreshment is gift from above, not self-irrigation.

Psalms 72:7

7In his days, the righteous shall flourish, and abundance of peace, until the moon is no more.

Arminian

Wesley: Fear/peace themes continue—righteous flourish; peace abounds while moon lasts.

Righteousness and peace are twin fruits. Flourish is not greed; it is the righteous thriving under the King.

Reformed

Calvin: in his days the righteous flourish; peace until the moon cease. Christ’s days define the climate of justice.

Shared long horizon—till moon be no more points past Solomon to the greater Son.

Psalms 72:8

8He shall have dominion also from sea to sea, from the River to the ends of the earth.

Arminian

Wesley: Dominion—Red Sea to Mediterranean; Ends—of Canaan, yet the wording stretches wider in messianic hearing.

Shared global hope—sea to sea and River to ends outrun a small empire. Christ’s dominion is the agreed fulfillment both camps confess.

Reformed

Calvin: dominion also from sea to sea—Christ’s rule to the ends of the earth. Solomon’s borders were type; the River-to-ends language fits the Messiah.

The king’s dominion is to be universal.

Key contrast Both confess dominion also from sea to sea as reaching beyond Solomon to Christ. Arminians typically join that confession to the ongoing call of the nations; Reformed interpreters stress the Messiah’s settled right to the ends of the earth.

Psalms 72:9

9Those who dwell in the wilderness shall bow before him. His enemies shall lick the dust.

Arminian

Wesley: foes humbled—desert tribes bow; enemies lick dust. Submission language is strong.

Enemies are not imaginary; bowing is the right end. Dust-licking is judgment language both camps take seriously, not cartoon violence.

Reformed

Calvin: enemies lick the dust before the King—forced homage where willing worship is refused.

Christ’s foes do not finally stand tall.

Psalms 72:10

10The kings of Tarshish and of the islands will bring tribute. The kings of Sheba and Seba shall offer gifts.

Arminian

Wesley: Tarshish—Cilician emporium for Mediterranean trade; Sheba—Arabia Felix. Tribute and gifts from distant kings.

Shared gentile homage—nations bring tribute to the King. Mission hope rides this verse in classical reading.

Reformed

Calvin: kings of Tarshish, Sheba, Seba—wide tribute foreshadows nations’ gifts to Christ.

Far kings honor the Anointed; geography does not fence his fame.

Psalms 72:11

11Yes, all kings shall fall down before him. All nations shall serve him.

Arminian

Wesley: universal kneel and serve. All kings, all nations—no exempt throne.

Shared high christology of service due the King. Nations must still serve; the prophecy summons submission.

Reformed

Calvin: all kings fall down; all nations serve him—language too wide for Solomon alone, fulfilled in Christ.

Catholic scope of the Messiah’s claim.

Psalms 72:12

12For he will deliver the needy when he cries; the poor, who has no helper.

Arminian

Wesley: deliverance when the needy calls—poor and helper-less find the King.

Calling is met with deliverance. The needy must still cry; grace answers the cry.

Reformed

Calvin: he will deliver the needy when he crieth—Christ’s royal pity is the for of the nations’ homage.

Empty-handed callers are the King’s specialty.

Psalms 72:13

13He will have pity on the poor and needy. He will save the souls of the needy.

Arminian

Wesley continues pity and life-saving for the weak. Compassion is policy.

Pity is not weakness in the King. Saving lives of the needy is messianic glory both camps praise.

Reformed

Calvin: pity on weak and needy; lives saved—the Messiah’s heart matches his dominion.

The king’s power serves pity for the poor.

Psalms 72:14

14He will redeem their soul from oppression and violence. Their blood will be precious in his sight.

Arminian

Wesley: redeem from oppression and violence; blood precious in his sight. Life valued.

Oppression’s victims are costly to the King. Blood precious rebukes casual cruelty.

Reformed

Calvin: redemption from deceit and violence; their blood precious—God’s King prices the lowly highly.

Precious blood language underwrites justice, not indifference.

Psalms 72:15

15He will live; and Sheba’s gold will be given to him. Men will pray for him continually. They will bless him all day long.

Arminian

Wesley: Live—long and prosperously; Gold—choicest gift; Prayer—continual; Daily—all the day. Prayer for the King is constant.

Shared—the church keeps praying for the King’s cause. Continual prayer is the people’s work under the reign.

Reformed

Calvin: prayer made for him continually; blessings all day—intercession surrounds the throne, fulfilled as the church seeks Christ’s kingdom.

Gold and prayer honor him; neither replaces the other.

Psalms 72:16

16Abundance of grain shall be throughout the land. Its fruit sways like Lebanon. Let it flourish, thriving like the grass of the field.

Arminian

Wesley: abundance even on mountain-tops; fruit like Lebanon; people blossom like grass. Fertility and population thrive.

Shared creational blessing under the King—cities and fields flourish together.

Reformed

Calvin: grain on mountains, fruit like Lebanon, citizens flourishing—pictures of messianic plenty.

The King’s peace is not thin spiritualism; it touches land and city.

Psalms 72:17

17His name endures forever. His name continues as long as the sun. Men shall be blessed by him. All nations will call him blessed.

Arminian

Wesley: Name—honour continued as long as the sun; Blessed—men bless themselves in him; nations call him blessed.

Genesis 12 echo in ‘blessed by him. All nations calling him blessed is messianic mission both camps affirm.

Reformed

Calvin: men shall be blessed by him. All nations will call him blessed—language of the Seed and the Messiah’s everlasting name.

Shared christological center—fame with the sun, blessing in him.

Key contrast Both hear everlasting name and nations blessed in him as reaching Christ. Arminians stress the nations’ calling on that name; Reformed Christians underline the Messiah’s enduring fame secured with the sun.

Psalms 72:18

18Praise be to the LORD God, the God of Israel, who alone does marvelous deeds.

Arminian

Wesley: Amen-frame begins—bless the LORD, God of Israel, who alone does wonders. Doxology to God, not the human throne alone.

Wondrous things are God’s alone. The King’s success is traced upward.

Reformed

Calvin: Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, who only doeth wondrous things—closing praise redirects glory to God.

Shared monergism of wonders—alone does wondrous things.

Psalms 72:19

19Blessed be his glorious name forever! Let the whole earth be filled with his glory! Amen and amen.

Arminian

Wesley: Amen—solemn confirmation of prophecy and prayer; earth filled with God’s glory.

Shared—whole-earth glory is the agreed horizon. Double Amen seals desire and faith.

Reformed

Calvin: glorious name forever; whole earth filled with his glory; Amen and Amen—catholic doxology.

Earth-filling glory is God’s goal confessed by both camps.

Psalms 72:20

20This ends the prayers by David, the son of Jesse.

Arminian

Wesley treats the colophon as closing this prayer-series. David’s prayers ‘ended’ as a collection note.

Editorial pause, not claim that David never prayed again. The book’s shape invites continued praise in Book III and beyond.

Reformed

Calvin: not that David composed no more, but this series of prayers and praises is closed. Colophon of a section.

David son of Jesse named; the greater Son’s reign (vv. 1–19) remains the theological peak of what ‘ended’ here.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Older voices from both traditions on Psalm 72.

Arminian Notable voices

“Judgments—Wherewith thou usest to govern the world, and especially thy people. Righteousness—Not only justice, but all those graces and virtues which are the proper fruits of God’s Spirit. Mountains—Which are commonly dry and barren. Bring peace—prosperity by thy righteous government. Fear—A true and filial fear of God. Sun—As long as the sun and moon endure. Dominion—From the Red Sea to the Mediterranean. Ends—Of the land of Canaan. Tarshish—A city of Cilicia, and an emporium of the Mediterranean trade; and so for the neighbouring places. Sheba—Arabia Felix. Live—Long and prosperously. Gold—The choicest gift which they have. Prayer—They shall make continual prayer for him. Daily—Heb. all the day. Name—His honour and glory. Continued—As long as the sun. Blessed—Men shall bless themselves in him, and wish to be as happy as he is. Filled—With the worship and knowledge and glory of God. Amen—This is added as a solemn confirmation of the foregoing prophecy and prayer.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 72)

Reformed Notable voices

“Solomon prays that God would bestow upon the king the spirit of judgment and righteousness… Yet the kingdom of Christ is principally intended… He shall judge thy people with righteousness, and thy poor with judgment… In his days shall the righteous flourish… He shall have dominion from sea to sea… All kings shall fall down before him… He shall deliver the needy when he crieth… Men shall be blessed in him; all nations shall call him blessed… Blessed be the LORD God, the God of Israel, who only doeth wondrous things; and blessed be his glorious name for ever: and let the whole earth be filled with his glory. Amen, and Amen. The prayers of David the son of Jesse are ended—not that David composed no more, but that this series of prayers and praises is here closed.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 72)
