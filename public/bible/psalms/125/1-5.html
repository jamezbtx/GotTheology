Passage

Psalms 125

Verses 1–5 · ESV · Voices

A Song of Ascents of trust and surround: those who trust in the LORD stand like Mount Zion; as mountains ring Jerusalem, so the LORD rings His people forever. Wicked rule will not rest on the righteous' lot, lest hands stretch to wrong. Prayer seeks good for the upright; peace rests on Israel while the crooked are led away.

Psalms 125:1

1Those who trust in the LORD are like Mount Zion, which cannot be moved, but abides forever.

Arminian

Those who trust in the LORD stand like Mount Zion—unmoved, abiding forever. Trust is the living posture that ties the pilgrim to Zion's stability on the ascent.

Classical Arminians hear a real call to confide in Jehovah. Stand by trusting; the mountain image encourages perseverance in faith, not a shrug that trust no longer matters once grace is named.

Reformed

Those who trust in the LORD are like Mount Zion—cannot be moved, abides forever. The simile locates safety in God-given steadfastness, not in the pilgrim's nerve alone.

Reformed accent: Zion's unmoved abiding pictures the Church under invincible protection. Trust leans on the Rock; stability is His guardianship of those who hope in Him.

Key contrast Both liken trusters in the LORD to unmoved Zion. Arminians typically stress ongoing, willing confidence that keeps the pilgrim standing; Reformed readers typically stress God's invincible protection that will not let His trusting people finally fall.

Psalms 125:2

2As the mountains surround Jerusalem, so the LORD surrounds his people, from this time forth and forevermore.

Arminian

As mountains surround Jerusalem, so the LORD surrounds His people—henceforth forevermore. Near, lasting cover for the community on the road to Zion.

Arminian pastoral: stay within the ring of His care by continued trust. The fortress is impregnable for those who keep confiding; wander outside that trust and you step past the comfort this verse sings.

Reformed

Mountains round Jerusalem mirror the LORD round His people forevermore. Geography catechizes: God's power encompasses the Church as hills fence the city.

Reformed comfort: surround is His continuous custody, not a temporary alliance. From this time forth and forevermore names a span only God can underwrite for His own.

Psalms 125:3

3For the scepter of wickedness shall not rest on the land allotted to the righteous, lest the righteous stretch out their hands to do wrong.

Arminian

Wickedness's scepter shall not rest on the righteous' allotment—lest hands stretch to wrong. Oppression may visit; permanent rule that crushes faith will not.

Classical Arminians urge faithfulness while the rod presses. Fear God more than foes; stay upright so hands do not learn iniquity under prolonged tyranny.

Reformed

The scepter of wickedness shall not rest on the just's lot, lest the righteous stretch hands to iniquity. God limits how long wicked rule bears on His heritage.

Reformed note: He moderates adversity because He knows our weakness. Even the just could stretch toward wrong if the rod never lifted; so He will not let it abide forever.

Key contrast Both hear that wicked rule will not permanently rest on the righteous. Arminians typically emphasize remaining faithful under temporary pressure so hands do not turn to wrong; Reformed readers typically emphasize God's fatherly limit on temptation lest even the just collapse into iniquity.

Psalms 125:4

4Do good, O LORD, to those who are good, and to those who are upright in their hearts!

Arminian

Do good, O LORD, to the good and the upright in heart. Prayer asks for increase of already-shown kindness toward sincere pilgrims.

Arminian reading: bring upright hearts to the throne with real petition. Goodness sought is grace welcomed and lived; integrity of heart is the posture this ask assumes and nurtures.

Reformed

Do good to the good and the upright in heart. After promise comes prayer—confidence without listless inactivity.

Reformed pastoral: what God bids us hope by His word, we seek by earnest asking. Uprightness of heart, not bare outward abstaining from wrong, marks those for whom this good is pleaded.

Psalms 125:5

5But those who turn aside to their crooked ways the LORD will lead away with evildoers! Peace be upon Israel!

Arminian

Turn aside to crooked ways—and the LORD leads them away with evildoers. Peace upon Israel names the purified people, not every empty claim of belonging.

Classical Arminians warn against backsliding into crooked paths. Stay straight in heart; peace rests on true Israel who do not abandon the ascent for crooked detours.

Reformed

Those who turn aside to crooked ways the LORD leads away with workers of iniquity—yet peace upon Israel. Hypocrites are dragged into the light; the upright receive peace.

Reformed accent: peace is not sentimental cover for counterfeit Israelites. God purges the assembly and gathers the upright in heart under His fatherly blessing.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on trust like Zion, the LORD surrounding His people, and the scepter that shall not rest—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 125:1–5.

Arminian Notable voices

“They that trust in the Lord—Every faithful Jew who confides in Jehovah shall stand, in those open and secret attacks of the enemies of God and truth, as unshaken as Mount Zion; and shall not be moved by the power of any adversary. The Lord is round about his people—He is above, beneath, around them; and while they keep within it, their fortress is impregnable. The rod of the wicked shall not rest upon the lot of the righteous—It may come, and stay for a time; but it shall not be permitted to abide. Fear not your enemies, while ye fear God. While you are faithful, the Lord will laugh them to scorn. As for such as turn aside—Who are not faithful; who give way to sin; who backslide, and walk in a crooked way—they shall be led forth to punishment with the common workers of iniquity. Thus thy Church will be purified, and thy peace rest upon thy true Israel.”
Adam Clarke · Commentary on the Bible (on Psalm 125)

Reformed Notable voices

“As the faithful being mingled in this world with the ungodly seem to be exposed to all the ills of life in the same manner as other people, the Prophet, comparing them to Jerusalem, shows that they are defended by an invincible bulwark. Although the world is subject to so many and so sudden changes, and although the faithful are mingled with and placed in the same external condition as others, yet their safety continues steadfast under the invincible protection of God. Not that they are permitted to dwell undisturbed and at ease; but because their safety being under the guardianship of God is assaulted in vain; at least they can never altogether fall, although they may stumble. Whoever desires to be sustained by the hand of God, let him constantly lean upon it. God from his willingness to bear with our weakness moderates our adversities. However vigorous the fear of God may be in our hearts, we are not endowed with adequate strength for enduring to the end, unless the Lord have a regard to our infirmity.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Book of Psalms (on Psalm 125)
