Passage

Psalms 119

Verses 137–144 · ESV · Voices

The Tsadhe stanza confesses the Lord righteous and his rules right; testimonies appointed in righteousness and faithfulness. Zeal consumes because foes forget the words; the promise is well tried and loved; the small and despised still remember precepts. Everlasting righteousness and true law stand; trouble finds the singer yet commandments delight; forever-righteous testimonies lead to the plea for understanding unto life. Both camps rest life on God-given understanding of the word; they differ on how absolute that dependence is when “give me understanding that I may live” is prayed.

Psalms 119:137

137Righteous are you, O Lord, and right are your rules.

Arminian

God righteous; rules right. Classical Arminians hear worship that starts with God’s character—law praised because the Lawgiver is just.

Shared confession: no safer righteousness than his. Do not hunt another standard outside his rules.

Reformed

Calvin: yields praise of righteousness to God and acknowledges it in the law. Judgments here fit commandments more than chastisements. Designating God righteous means that as soon as we depart from him we find no particle of righteousness elsewhere. Adding that evidence is seen in the law: God is robbed of praise if we do not subscribe to all his commandments.

Reformed accent: righteous Lord; right rules. Leave him and righteousness vanishes.

Psalms 119:138

138You have appointed your testimonies in righteousness and in all faithfulness.

Arminian

Testimonies commanded in righteousness and faithfulness. Classical Arminians hear the law as full, perfect rule—God’s appointment, not human craft.

Shared warning: do not invent a new pattern of righteousness beside what the testimonies appoint.

Reformed

Calvin: God has taught in his law full and perfect righteousness and truth. “Greatly” connects with the nouns—God’s design to exhibit a perfect rule. Encomiums so we derive wisdom from the law and devise no other standard of rectitude than what is exhibited there—necessary, since every man would frame a new pattern.

Reformed confession: appointed in righteousness and faithfulness. Perfect rule; refuse homemade standards.

Psalms 119:139

139My zeal consumes me, because my foes forget your words.

Arminian

Consuming zeal over forgotten words. Classical Arminians hear grief for God’s honor outrunning private offense—foes’ amnesia about Scripture burns the heart.

Shared reordering: too tender about our wrongs, too cold about God’s. Ask for zeal that starts with his words.

Reformed

Calvin: persecutors troubled him much; yet not so much private wrongs as violation of the law offended him—consumed with grief on that account, hardly affected by individual troubles. Profitable example: we are too delicate in bearing wrongs—touched with a finger, inflamed with anger, yet cold at grievous offenses against God. Zeal like the prophet’s carries to another sorrow that takes possession of the soul.

Reformed accent: zeal consumes over forgotten words. Holy grief prioritizes God’s law above personal slight.

Psalms 119:140

140Your promise is well tried, and your servant loves it.

Arminian

Wesley: Pure—Without the least mixture of falsehood.

Classical Arminians hear a tested, pure promise that wins love—experience confirms purity. Shared affection: love the refined word because it has proved true.

Reformed

Calvin: cause of zeal was love of heavenly doctrine—displeasure at contempt of truth is hypocrisy unless bound by love. Love not rash or blind; loved because like refined gold or silver, pure from dregs. Metaphor vindicates the word from malicious judgments and expresses true obedience of faith. Few fail to cast some stain by distrust, waywardness, pride, or voluptuousness. Confirms by experience—unfeigned feeling of the heart tasting that purity.

Reformed confession: well-tried promise; servant loves it. Purified word, tasted love, holy zeal.

Psalms 119:141

141I am small and despised, yet I do not forget your precepts.

Arminian

Small and despised, precepts not forgotten. Classical Arminians hear disinterested piety—praise of God without a full belly of benefits.

Shared challenge: many praise when pampered and go silent under roughness. Mean condition is no excuse to forget.

Reformed

Calvin: though tried with poverty and calamities, he persevered in godliness and law-observance; on that account despised by the wicked. Every man praises God in proportion as gorged with benefits; few apply minds to God’s service unless wishes are gratified. Hypocrites lavish praise while full; treated roughly, the blessed name is heard no more. Learn true godliness is disinterested—praise God though afflicted and despised; seek not reward on earth; through heat and cold, poverty and danger, slanders and mockeries, persevere. Christ: ye seek me because ye ate the loaves (John 6:26).

Reformed accent: small and despised, yet precepts remembered. Unmercenary fear of God.

Psalms 119:142

142Your righteousness is righteous forever, and your law is true.

Arminian

Wesley: Everlasting—The same in all ages and places.

Classical Arminians hear unchanging righteousness and true law—stable when human rules fade. Shared contrast: everlasting word versus vanishing human inventions.

Reformed

Calvin: law honored as everlasting righteousness and truth—other rules of life, however attractive, are a shadow that vanishes. Indirectly contrasts doctrine of the law with all human precepts ever delivered, to bring the faithful under it as school of perfect wisdom. More plausibility in men’s refined disquisitions, yet nothing firm or solid as in God’s law.

Reformed confession: forever-righteous righteousness; true law. Human standards fade; the law remains.

Psalms 119:143

143Trouble and anguish have found me out, but your commandments are my delight.

Arminian

Wesley: Trouble—Outward troubles and anguish of spirit.

Classical Arminians hear delight in commandments under pressure—sweetness that meets bitterness. Shared test: profit from the word when sorrow is opposed by consolation from Scripture.

Reformed

Calvin: proves firmness of the law from experience—continual comfort when harassed. True test of profit: oppose to distresses the consolation of the word, that sadness may be effaced. More than v. 141: not only reverent service under rough treatment, but in distress and torment finds in the law most soothing delight mitigating griefs—even seasoning with sweetness. Without this taste, nothing more natural than to be swallowed by sorrow. Form of expression: besieged on all sides, yet remedy in the word’s consolation. Not bare commandments that fill with anxiety, but by synecdoche whole doctrine of the law—elect called to hope of salvation, gate of happiness; law comprehends free adoption and promises.

Reformed accent: trouble found me; commandments delight me. Promise-laden law sweetens anguish.

Psalms 119:144

144Your testimonies are righteous forever; give me understanding that I may live.

Arminian

Forever-righteous testimonies; understanding asked for life. Classical Arminians hear living tied to God-taught grasp of the word—pray for light unto life.

Shared anthropology: we are not made merely to fill the belly; understanding of God’s testimonies is life’s true flourishings. Ask to understand.

Reformed

Calvin: repeats—great dissimilarity between righteousness of God’s testimonies and man’s inventions; the last’s splendor vanishes, the other continues forever. Everlasting righteousness not comprehended elsewhere than in God’s law; clearer definition: righteousness is keeping within the law’s bounds. Last clause with the preceding: desires mind enlightened, yet only by profiting aright in study of the law. Men cannot properly be said to live destitute of heavenly wisdom’s light; created not to stuff bellies like swine or asses but to exercise knowledge and service of God—turning away, life is worse than a thousand deaths. Without meditation on celestial life, men plunge into the grave—living to the world, dead to God. Under life: even if already dead, illuminate my mind with heavenly truth and that grace alone will revive me.

Reformed confession: forever-righteous testimonies; understanding unto life. Illumined by the law’s wisdom is true living; without it, living death.

Key contrast Both tie true life to understanding God’s forever-righteous testimonies and both refuse a merely animal life. Arminians typically hear a sincere plea for illuminating grace so the servant may live in the word’s light. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s severity: without the light of heavenly wisdom one cannot properly be said to live; turning from God’s knowledge and service makes life worse than death—and even the “already dead” ask only that illuminating grace revive them.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on God’s righteousness, tried promise, delight in commandments under anguish, and understanding asked for life—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 119:137–144.

Arminian Notable voices

“Pure—Without the least mixture of falsehood. Everlasting—The same in all ages and places. Trouble—Outward troubles and anguish of spirit.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 119:140, 142–143)

Reformed Notable voices

“O Jehovah, thou art righteous; judgments right—as soon as we depart from him we find no particle of righteousness elsewhere; evidence of righteousness is in the law. My zeal hath consumed me because adversaries forgot thy words—more offended by violation of the law than by private wrongs. Thy word is exceedingly refined; thy servant loves it—love of doctrine, not rash affection; free from dregs like refined gold. Give me understanding and I shall live: men cannot properly be said to live destitute of heavenly wisdom’s light; end of creation is knowledge and service of God; without it life is worse than a thousand deaths.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 119:137, 139–140, 144)
