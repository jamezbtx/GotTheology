Passage

Psalms 119

Verses 25–32 · ESV · Voices

The Daleth stanza begins in the dust: the soul clings low and asks life according to God’s word. Confession of ways, pleas for understanding and strength, removal of false ways, choosing faithfulness, clinging to testimonies, and finally running in the commandments when God enlarges the heart fill the stanza. Both camps pray for quickening by the word; the clear fork is whether the enlarged heart that runs is God’s prior enabling of the unable, or grace replenishing a will that sincerely chooses.

Psalms 119:25

25My soul clings to the dust; give me life according to your word!

Arminian

Wesley: The dust—danger of present death, like one laid in the grave. Quicken—preserve life or raise from the dust. Word—according to thy promise.

Classical Arminians hear desperate prayer answered by promise. Life is asked according to the word—not according to merit. Shared ground: Scripture’s promise as the ground of hope in extremis.

Reformed

Calvin: no more hope of life than if shut up in the tomb. When death stares, petition the God who restores life to the dead. “According to thy word” confesses that apart from the word no hope remains; God has affirmed the life of the faithful is in his hand.

Reformed accent: dust-clinging faith flees to promise. Quicken is God’s prerogative matched to his word.

Psalms 119:26

26When I told of my ways, you answered me; teach me your statutes!

Arminian

Wesley: My ways—my sins; and all my cares, and fears, and troubles.

Classical Arminians hear honest declaration met by God’s answer, then a further plea to be taught. Confession and instruction belong together. Past hearing fuels present teachability.

Reformed

Calvin: he prayed sincerely, not as the proud who trust wisdom, fortitude, or opulence. To declare one’s ways is to attempt nothing without God’s assistance and to commit all plans to his pleasure—honestly, not hypocritically. He was heard, which fosters hope. He holds nothing dearer than true understanding of the law.

Reformed reading: answered prayer leads to “teach me,” not to self-congratulation.

Psalms 119:27

27Make me understand the way of your precepts, and I will meditate on your wondrous works.

Arminian

Wesley: Understand—more thoroughly.

Classical Arminians hear understanding asked so that meditation on God’s works may deepen. Insight is gift; meditation is response. Do not invent mastery of precepts without divine making-to-understand.

Reformed

Calvin: with the law before us we reap little from bare perusal without the Spirit as internal teacher. Make me understand so that I may meditate on thy wondrous works. We have no relish for the law until God sanctifies our minds; from disrelish springs indifference.

Reformed accent: Spirit-given understanding opens meditation. External reading without internal teaching yields little.

Psalms 119:28

28My soul melts away for sorrow; strengthen me according to your word!

Arminian

Wesley: Melteth—like wax before the fire.

Classical Arminians hear grief that dissolves strength, and a plea for word-based strengthening. The same promise that quickens (v. 25) now fortifies. Pastoral realism: sorrow is not denied; the word is the remedy sought.

Reformed

Calvin: the soul melted away with grief—strength poured out like water. He assures himself of remedy if God stretch out his hand. Formerly near death he expected revival; now nearly consumed he hopes for renovated vigor—again according to thy word, for apart from the word God’s power affords little comfort.

Reformed confession: promise-fortified strength under melting sorrow. Word and hand of God restore.

Psalms 119:29

29Put false ways far from me and graciously teach me your law!

Arminian

Wesley: Grant me—vouchsafe an accurate knowledge of thy word, and let me govern myself by it in all things.

Classical Arminians hear twin petitions: remove lying ways, and grant the law graciously. Teaching is gracious gift; self-rule by the word is the aim. Falsehood is not negotiated with.

Reformed

Calvin: knowing how prone man is to vanity and falsehood, he asks sanctification of thoughts lest Satan’s snares lead into error; then to be fortified with the law’s doctrine. Preferable sense of the second clause: freely grant me thy law. “Gratify” marks unmerited kindness. Full of blindness, we are easily deceived; unless God teach by the Spirit of wisdom we are hurried into errors. The means of preservation is instruction in the law—free gift, not merited increase of grace.

Reformed accent: false ways removed and law granted are both mercy. Papal merit for increase of grace falls.

Psalms 119:30

30I have chosen the way of faithfulness; I set your rules before me.

Arminian

Wesley: Laid—or set before me as a mark to aim at; as a rule to direct me.

Classical Arminians hear real choosing under grace—the way of truth adopted, judgments set as aim and rule. Choice is not Pelagian autonomy; it is sincere resolve that still needs enlarging (v. 32).

Reformed

Calvin: he affirms he desires nothing more than to follow righteousness and truth—hence “chosen.” Life is at a fork in every action; he resolved not to relinquish the truth. He was not exempt from temptation but surmounted it by conscientious observance. Setting judgments before him keeps choice fixed; without steady contemplation of the law, eyes wander.

Reformed reading: choosing faithfulness is resolved clinging under temptation, eyes locked on God’s judgments.

Psalms 119:31

31I cling to your testimonies, O Lord; let me not be put to shame!

Arminian

Wesley does not pause here. Clinging to the testimonies and pleading against shame echo Aleph’s concern (v. 6). Classical Arminians hear persevering adhesion under threat of disgrace.

The Lord is addressed; shame’s removal is asked of him while the hand holds the testimonies.

Reformed

Calvin: he combines holy affection for the law with prayer not to be ashamed and enfeebled under the derision of the ungodly. He has so firmly taken hold he cannot be separated. The more sincerely one surrenders to God, the more assailed by vile tongues.

Reformed accent: cling and “let me not be ashamed” are one piety under mockery.

Psalms 119:32

32I will run in the way of your commandments when you enlarge my heart!

Arminian

Wesley: Run—obey with all readiness, fervency, and diligence. Enlarge—when thou shalt replenish my heart with wisdom, and love to thee and thy law.

Classical Arminians hear running as eager obedience when God replenishes heart with wisdom and love. Grace enlarges; the will runs. Choosing the way (v. 30) and asking enlargement belong together—sincere pursuit fulfilled by prevenient and cooperating grace, not native sprinting.

Reformed

Calvin: when God inspires love for his law, he will be vigorous and ready, even steady so as not to faint. Implied admission: inability of men to advance in well-doing until God enlarge their hearts. No sooner does God expand the heart than they are fitted for walking and running. Proper observance is not mere external works but willing obedience; the heart must enlarge—yet not by self-determining power, but when hardness is subdued it moves freely. When God has enlarged, he also furnishes ability.

Reformed confession: enlarge first, then run. Ability and affection are given together.

Key contrast Both confess that running in the commandments depends on God enlarging the heart, and both refuse bare externalism. Arminians typically hear Wesley’s replenishing with wisdom and love enabling sincere, fervent obedience—grace fulfilling a real choosing. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s admission of inability until God enlarges: the heart does not enlarge itself; when God expands it, power to run is given with the affection.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on quickening from the dust, gracious teaching of the law, choosing the way of faithfulness, and running when God enlarges the heart—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 119:25–32.

Arminian Notable voices

“The dust—I am in danger of present death: I am like one laid in the grave. Quicken—preserve my life, or raise me out of the dust. Word—according to thy promise. Run—I will obey thy precepts with all readiness, fervency, and diligence. Enlarge—when thou shalt replenish my heart with wisdom, and love to thee and thy law.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 119:25, 32)

Reformed Notable voices

“He means he had no more hope of life than if shut up in the tomb. By his example he instructs us, when death stares us in the face, to present petitions to God, whose peculiar prerogative it is to restore life to the dead. According to thy word acknowledges that apart from God’s word no hope would be left. The prophet’s meaning is that when God shall inspire him with love for his law he will be vigorous and ready. His words contain an implied admission of the inability of men to make any advancement in well-doing until God enlarge their hearts. No sooner does God expand their hearts than they are fitted not only for walking but for running.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 119:25, 32)
