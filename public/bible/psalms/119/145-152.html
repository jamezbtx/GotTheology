Passage

Psalms 119

Verses 145–152 · ESV · Voices

The Qoph stanza cries with the whole heart for answer and vows to keep statutes; calling for salvation aims at observing testimonies. Help is sought before dawn with hope in the words; eyes wake before the night watches to meditate on the promise. Hearing according to steadfast love and life according to justice are asked; malicious pursuers draw near yet are far from the law, while the Lord is near and all commandments true; from of old the testimonies are known as founded forever. Both camps cry early and hope in the word; they differ on quickening as moment-by-moment dependence on free mercy mediated by the word.

Psalms 119:145

145With my whole heart I cry; answer me, O Lord! I will keep your statutes.

Arminian

Whole-heart cry for answer, vowing to keep statutes. Classical Arminians hear undivided prayer—world distracted by many objects; the servant aims all affection at God.

Shared gratitude: answered prayer should deepen keeping. Distinguish from cold, feigned praise.

Reformed

Calvin: requests God would hear; in gratitude promises to keep commandments. Uses indefinite cry—shows that while the world is distracted by many objects, he directed all heart-affections exclusively to God, depending solely on him. World acknowledges God as author of good, so many formal prayers proceed; he affirms he prayed with his whole heart. Obtaining requests, he proposes God’s glory as end—devote with more ardent affection. Distinguishes from hypocrites who profane the name by cold feigned praises; will give thanks by life and works.

Reformed accent: whole-heart cry; then keep statutes. Undivided dependence, lived gratitude.

Psalms 119:146

146I call to you; save me, that I may observe your testimonies.

Arminian

Call for salvation unto observing testimonies. Classical Arminians hear rescue with a purpose—saved in order to obey.

Shared clarity: whether in safety or near death, welfare is commended to God; continued safety is only under his guard.

Reformed

Calvin: no new statement, more express—cried to God; commended his welfare by prayer. Whether in safety or imminent danger of death, he uniformly reposed upon God—persuaded the only way to continue safe was having him as guardian of his welfare.

Reformed confession: save me, that I may observe. Welfare parked with God; obedience the aim of deliverance.

Psalms 119:147

147I rise before dawn and cry for help; I hope in your words.

Arminian

Before dawn, cry and hope in the words. Classical Arminians hear eager, persevering prayer—early without quitting when answer delays.

Shared manner: earnest desire joined to patient hope. The word before our eyes bridles corrupt haste.

Reformed

Calvin: prevented the dawn by prayers—most eager haste. Cry conveys earnestness—vehemency of mind more than loudness. Haste sets forth perseverance: though prompt, he did not weary like unbelievers who murmur if God does not suddenly grant. Conjoining patience of hope with earnest desire shows true praying (cf. Phil 4:6)—bridle turbulent affections; prayer nourishes hope. Mention of the word not superfluous: only by having the word continually before eyes can we bridle wanton impetuosity of corrupt nature.

Reformed accent: before dawn, cry; hope in words. Prompt prayer plus word-anchored patience.

Psalms 119:148

148My eyes are awake before the watches of the night, that I may meditate on your promise.

Arminian

Wesley: Watches—The middle watch, which was set in the middle of the night; and the morning watch, which was set some hours before the dawning of the day.

Classical Arminians hear night meditation—eyes ahead of the watchmen for the sake of the promise. Shared devotion: lose sleep to muse on what God has said.

Reformed

Calvin: more sedulously intent on meditating on the law than night-watchers on their watch. Meditate more appropriate than discourse—night unseasonable for public teaching; alone, he silently recalled what he learned, passing no part of the night without meditating on the law.

Reformed confession: eyes before the watches for promise-meditation. Secret night musing marks love of the word.

Psalms 119:149

149Hear my voice according to your steadfast love; O Lord, according to your justice give me life.

Arminian

Wesley: Judgment—According to thy word.

Classical Arminians hear mercy-based hearing and life asked by God’s just/worded way—free love first, then quickening. Shared order: approach through promised grace, not desert.

Reformed

Calvin: goodness of God the only ground of hope of being heard. Whatever blessings saints plead, opening argument must be free unmerited grace. Judgments not different—God revealed goodness in his word; from the word derive assurance. Sensible of need of mercy, he betook himself to the word where God sweetly allures and promises grace ready and open for all. Persuaded God will be merciful, learn to entreat him to show himself as promised. Some expound judgments as custom—God’s usual gracious dealing; he prefers judgments as the word. Desires to be quickened: even in the midst of life he is dead except sustained by God’s power. All duly acquainted with infirmity esteem life as nothing and crave to be quickened every moment. God often so exercised him that he might send prayers as from the sepulcher to be restored from death to life.

Reformed confession: hear by lovingkindness; quicken by judgment/word. Free grace opens the plea; life is moment-by-moment divine sustaining.

Key contrast Both ask to be heard by steadfast love and quickened by God’s just/worded way, and both refuse a claim from merit. Arminians typically hear mercy inviting life for the crying servant who hopes in the word. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin’s confession that even in the midst of life we are dead except as God sustains us—so the faithful crave to be quickened every moment, with free unmerited grace as the only opening argument and the word as the place where that grace is promised.

Psalms 119:150

150They draw near who persecute me with evil purpose; they are far from your law.

Arminian

Wesley: Nigh—To me, they are at hand and ready to seize upon me. Are far—They cast away from them all thoughts of thy law.

Classical Arminians hear near danger from those far from Scripture—proximity of malice, distance from the law. Shared comfort: their nearness does not cancel God’s.

Reformed

Calvin: pursuers of malice draw near to do mischief—bent on malice, pursuing close, rushing to indicate they are far from God’s law, casting off uprightness. Wretched to behold enemies who shook off fear of God ready to smite, had not God been near (v. 151).

Reformed accent: near pursuers; far from the law. Malice’s approach exposes lawless hearts.

Psalms 119:151

151But you are near, O Lord, and all your commandments are true.

Arminian

God near; all commandments true. Classical Arminians hear the decisive contrast—enemies near, the Lord nearer; truth of the commandments sure.

Shared courage: God’s nearness and true commands steady the threatened.

Reformed

Calvin: encourages from the consolatory consideration that God, seeing his people sore pressed, comes seasonably (cf. Phil 4:5). Concluding sentence: God never forsakes or disappoints in necessity because true to his promises—assures welfare will always be cared for. Fully persuaded the hand of God is ready to repulse assaults; retain settled belief he does not in vain promise to be guardian of our welfare.

Reformed confession: you are near; commandments are truth. Promise-true nearness guards the pressed.

Psalms 119:152

152Long have I known from your testimonies that you have founded them forever.

Arminian

Wesley: Of old—By long experience. Founded—Thou hast established them upon everlasting foundations.

Classical Arminians hear long-known forever-foundation—experience confirming the testimonies’ permanence. Shared faith: word not faithful for a time only but unchangeable forever, else eternal hope fails.

Reformed

Calvin: prefers—he derived knowledge from the testimonies themselves that they are established forever. Chief point of faith: word not only steadfast for a time but continues unchangeable forever; otherwise it could not include hope of eternal salvation. Inward revelation of the Spirit necessary that assurance of immutability be rooted—until God seals certainty within, belief continually wavers. Yet not without cause he affirms he learned this from the word: when God shines by his Spirit he causes sacred truth that endures forever to shine in the mirror of his word.

Reformed accent: known from the testimonies—founded forever. Spirit seals what the word mirrors: everlasting establishment.
