Passage

Psalms 111

Verses 1–10 · WEB · Voices

An alphabetic hymn of praise: the psalmist gives thanks with the whole heart among the upright; celebrates the LORD’s great works studied by those who delight in them; names splendor, enduring righteousness, remembered wonders, grace and mercy; food to those who fear him and everlasting covenant memory; power shown in giving the nations’ inheritance; faithful and just works and trustworthy precepts established forever; redemption sent and covenant commanded forever; holy and awesome Name; the fear of the LORD as wisdom’s beginning, good understanding for those who practice it, and praise that endures forever.

Psalms 111:1

1Praise the LORD! I will give thanks to the LORD with my whole heart, in the council of the upright, and in the congregation.

Arminian

Praise the LORD! Thanks with my whole heart, in the council of the upright, and in the congregation. Whole-heart praise in public assembly.

Unfeigned thanks among the upright. Grace frees the heart; the singer still gives thanks in the congregation.

Reformed

Calvin: praise with the whole heart—upright, not double—and publicly in the assembly of the just. Secret gratitude should also rise in swelling strains so God is not robbed of half His honor.

Heart first, then public praise. Shared refusal of lip-only worship.

Psalms 111:2

2The LORD’s works are great, pondered by all those who delight in them.

Arminian

Great are the LORD’s works, studied by all those who delight in them. Wesley: Sought—diligently meditated upon.

Delight studies; greatness is not ignored by the indifferent. Shared—meditation is the path into God’s works.

Reformed

Calvin: works are great but few notice them; excellency is known to all who desire it—none ignorant except the willfully blind. Begin study with reverence.

Blindness is culpable indifference. Shared call to seek out the works with pleasure.

Psalms 111:3

3His work is honor and majesty. His righteousness endures forever.

Arminian

Splendor and majesty His work; righteousness endures forever. Wesley: Work—all His works or providence toward His people; Righteousness—justice or faithfulness in performing His word.

God’s work is majestic, and his righteousness lasts forever. Trust the faithfulness that keeps the word.

Reformed

Calvin: every act replete with glorious majesty; justice invitingly conspicuous—not terror only. Commendation against the ungodly’s calumny.

Enduring righteousness answers slander of God’s ways.

Psalms 111:4

4He has caused his wonderful works to be remembered. The LORD is gracious and merciful.

Arminian

Wondrous works remembered; the LORD gracious and merciful. Wesley: Remembered—by their nature and lasting benefits not easily forgotten.

Shared attributes—gracious and merciful. Memory of wonders fuels present trust.

Reformed

Calvin: He causes marvellous works to be remembered; Jehovah is compassionate and merciful—grace the source of the justice that preserves His people.

Memorial of wonders bound to mercy. Shared praise of graciousness.

Psalms 111:5

5He has given food to those who fear him. He always remembers his covenant.

Arminian

Food to those who fear him. He always remembers his covenant forever. Wesley: Meat—all necessary provisions for being and well-being.

Fearers are fed; covenant memory is forever. Shared—provision tied to covenant faithfulness, sincerely experienced by those who fear Him.

Reformed

Calvin: He gives a portion to those who fear Him; He will remember His covenant forever—care that shows the covenant is not void; goodness we receive flows from adoption.

Covenant forever underwrites daily bread for fearers. Shared reliance on His mindfulness.

Key contrast Both tie provision for God-fearers to everlasting covenant remembrance. Arminians emphasize the lived fear and trust that receive food from a faithfully mindful God. Reformed interpreters press that covenant adoption is why He never wearies of showing kindness to His people.

Psalms 111:6

6He has shown his people the power of his works, in giving them the heritage of the nations.

Arminian

Shown His people the power of His works, giving them the heritage of the nations.

Power displayed in gift of inheritance. Shared—conquest of the land (and its gospel analogue) is God’s power, not bare human might.

Reformed

Calvin: He declared the power of His works to give the heritage of the heathen—Holy Land not by mere human power but by miracles; Israel a match for nations only from on high.

God showed his people his power by giving them the nations’ inheritance.

Psalms 111:7

7The works of his hands are truth and justice. All his precepts are sure.

Arminian

Works of His hands truth and justice. All his precepts trustworthy. Wesley: Works—for His people or against enemies; Truth—agreeable to promises and justice; Commandments—laws, especially moral; Sure—constant and unchangeable.

Shared harmony of deeds and precepts—faithful, just, trustworthy. Keep the sure commandments.

Reformed

Calvin: works of His hands are truth and judgment; all His statutes true—beautiful harmony in sayings and doings; He is just and faithful everywhere.

Law and providence agree. Shared trust in sure precepts.

Psalms 111:8

8They are established forever and ever. They are done in truth and uprightness.

Arminian

Established forever and ever, done in truth and uprightness. Wesley: Done—constituted or ordered.

Forever stability; performance in faithfulness. Shared—God’s orders do not expire with fashion.

Reformed

Calvin: established forever, made in truth and rectitude. Without the law’s sacred chain the fruit of redemption would soon be lost.

Eternal statutes in truth. Shared obedience as life’s response.

Psalms 111:9

9He has sent redemption to his people. He has ordained his covenant forever. His name is holy and awesome!

Arminian

He has sent redemption to his people; ordained his covenant forever. His name is holy and awesome is His name! Wesley: Redemption—deliverance from Egypt, type of higher redemption by Christ; Commanded—established by power; Forever—through successive generations; Reverend—terrible to enemies, venerable to His people, holy in all dealings.

Both camps read Egypt as type pointing to Christ.

Reformed

Calvin: He sent redemption; ordained his covenant forever. His name is holy and terrible is His name. Deliverance begins salvation; the law ratifies grace so eternal life’s hope continues in the church. Commandments must not be taken abstractly from promises in Christ.

Reformed accent—adoption sealed so hope does not fail.

Psalms 111:10

10The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom. All those who do his work have a good understanding. His praise endures forever!

Arminian

The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom; all who practice it have good understanding. His praise endures forever! Wesley: The fear—true religion.

Wisdom starts in fear; practice yields understanding; praise forever. Shared—true religion is obedient fear, not craftiness. Arminian readers stress practicing the fear as living godliness grace enables.

Reformed

Calvin: fear of Jehovah is the beginning of wisdom—those who do not obey are brute beasts regarding the first elements; without fear, worldly craft is vanity. Good understanding have those who keep the commandments; fear here is comprehensive true godliness. Spend life celebrating His praises.

Fear as source of wisdom, proved by keeping. Shared—His praise endures forever.

Key contrast Both confess that the fear of the LORD is wisdom’s beginning and that doing His commandments is good understanding. Arminians stress true religion practiced as grace-enabled godliness. Reformed interpreters stress that without this fear all wicked craftiness is empty, and that keeping the law evidences genuine fear.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What earlier interpreters on each side wrote about Psalm 111:1–10.

Arminian Notable voices

“Sought—diligently meditated upon. Work—either all His works, or that eminent branch, His providence towards His people. Righteousness—His justice or faithfulness in performing His word. Remembered—by their own nature and lasting benefits. Meat—all necessary provisions for their being and well-being. The works—all that He doth on behalf of His people or against their enemies. Sure—constant and unchangeable. Redemption—the deliverance out of Egypt, type of that higher redemption by Christ. For ever—through all successive generations of His people. Reverend—terrible to His enemies, venerable in His people’s eyes. The fear—true religion.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Psalm 111)

Reformed Notable voices

“I will praise Jehovah with my whole heart in the congregation of the just—unfeignedly, and publicly. The works of Jehovah are great, sought out of all who have pleasure in them; none are ignorant except the willfully blind. His work is beautiful; His justice endures forever; He has caused His marvellous works to be remembered; merciful and compassionate is Jehovah. He has given a portion to those who fear Him; He will remember His covenant forever. He showed His people the power of His works to give them the heritage of the nations. The works of His hands are truth and judgment; all His statutes are true, established forever. He sent redemption to His people; holy and terrible is His name. The fear of Jehovah is the beginning of wisdom; a good understanding have all who keep His commandments; His praise endureth forever.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Book of Psalms (on Psalm 111)
