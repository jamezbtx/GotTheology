Psalms 48

Verses 1–14 · ESV · Voices

A Song. A Psalm of the Sons of Korah. Great is the LORD and greatly to be praised in the city of our God! His holy mountain, beautiful in elevation, is the joy of all the earth, Mount Zion, in the far north, the city of the great King. Within her citadels God has made himself known as a fortress. For behold, the kings assembled; they came on together. As soon as they saw it, they were astounded; they were in panic; they took to flight. Trembling took hold of them there, anguish as of a woman in labor. By the east wind you shattered the ships of Tarshish. As we have heard, so have we seen in the city of the LORD of hosts, in the city of our God, which God will establish forever. Selah We have thought on your steadfast love, O God, in the midst of your temple. As your name, O God, so your praise reaches to the ends of the earth. Your right hand is filled with righteousness. Let Mount Zion be glad! Let the daughters of Judah rejoice because of your judgments! Walk about Zion, go around her, number her towers, consider well her ramparts, go through her citadels, that you may tell the next generation that this is God, our God forever and ever. He will guide us forever.

Psalms 48:1

1Great is the LORD and greatly to be praised in the city of our God! His holy mountain,

Arminian

Great is the LORD and greatly to be praised in the city of our God! His holy mountain, Wesley: The city—In Jerusalem. Mountain—In his holy mountain. Shared with both camps: the LORD is great and greatly to be praised in his own city and holy mountain.

Arminian pastoral: shared—praise the LORD as great in the city of our God. Join the praise on his holy mountain; refuse a greatness that never gathers his people to worship.

Reformed

Calvin: Great is Jehovah, and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, in the mountain of his holiness—Jerusalem happy because God took charge of defending it; separates the Church from the world; when God selects from the whole human race a small number whom he embraces with fatherly love, an invaluable blessing; his glory manifested chiefly in the Church as in a clearer mirror; holy mountain because the ark was placed there by divine appointment, not by any merits of Jerusalem.

Reformed pastoral: shared—praise the LORD as great where he has set his Church. Receive the holy city as electing gift, not desert; refuse a praise that never distinguishes his people.

Key contrast Both hear the LORD greatly praised in the city of our God on his holy mountain. Arminians typically hear Wesley: the city as Jerusalem and the mountain as his holy mountain—the place of praise. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: God selecting a small number from the human race in fatherly love, separating the Church from the world; the mountain holy because the ark was set there by command, not by Jerusalem's merits.

Psalms 48:2

2beautiful in elevation, is the joy of all the earth, Mount Zion, in the far north, the city of the great King.

Arminian

beautiful in elevation, is the joy of all the earth, Mount Zion, in the far north, the city of the great King. Wesley: The joy—This is spoken prophetically, because the joyful doctrine of the gospel was to go from thence to all nations. The city—Of God, who justly calls himself a great king.

Arminian pastoral: shared—hear Zion's beauty as joy meant for all the earth through the gospel. Honor the great King's city; refuse a Mount Zion whose gladness never reaches the nations.

Reformed

Calvin: Beautiful for situation, the joy of the whole earth, is mount Zion—not mere climate or sweet fruits, a cold speculation; spiritual beauty after the ark abode there; joy of the whole earth because from thence salvation was to issue to the whole world; Christ appeared with his gospel out of Zion to fill the world with true joy; city of the great King—the prerogative rendered the whole city sacred where God chose his seat to rule over all people.

Reformed pastoral: shared—seek Zion's joy as the gospel going out from God's city. Rest gladness on his presence, not on the land's climate; refuse a beauty that never becomes salvation for the earth.

Key contrast Both hear Mount Zion as the joy of all the earth and city of the great King. Arminians typically hear Wesley: joy spoken prophetically, because the joyful doctrine of the gospel was to go from thence to all nations. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: not climate speculation but spiritual beauty after the ark; from Zion salvation issues to the whole world, Christ with his gospel filling the earth with true joy.

Psalms 48:3

3Within her citadels God has made himself known as a fortress.

Arminian

Within her citadels God has made himself known as a fortress. Wesley: Known—By long experience. Palaces—Possibly he may point at the king's palace and the temple, which was the palace of the king of heaven; which two palaces God did in a singular manner protect, and by protecting them, protected the whole city and people.

Arminian pastoral: shared—know God as fortress inside the citadels by long experience. Trust his protection of temple and people; refuse walls that never confess him as the true defense.

Reformed

Calvin: God in her palaces is known for a defense—though the holy city was fortified by strong towers and magnificent houses resembling fortresses, its continued safety was owing to the power and aid of God alone; do not let magnificence shroud the knowledge of his power; worldly wealth tends to make us forget God.

Reformed pastoral: shared—know God alone as fortress within the citadels. Let every palace preach his aid; refuse a defense that dazzles the eye and hides the Guardian.

Psalms 48:4

4For behold, the kings assembled; they came on together.

Arminian

For behold, the kings assembled; they came on together. Wesley: The kings—Either those kings confederate against Jehoshaphat, or the Assyrian princes; whom they vain-gloriously called kings. Passed—In their march towards Jerusalem.

Arminian pastoral: shared—behold assembled kings marching on Zion and still under God's eye. Refuse panic that treats confederate power as final.

Reformed

Calvin: For, behold! the kings assembled; they passed away together—special deliverance; they confederated and conspired to destroy the Church; whether reduced to nothing or undertaking the expedition with one consent, the miracle of their overthrow magnifies God's protecting care.

Reformed pastoral: shared—read assembled kings as assaults God is used to turning. Expect his care when the Church is besieged; refuse despair at confederate strength.

Psalms 48:5

5As soon as they saw it, they were astounded; they were in panic; they took to flight.

Arminian

As soon as they saw it, they were astounded; they were in panic; they took to flight. Wesley: Saw it—They did only look upon it, but not come into it, nor shoot an arrow there—nor cast a bank against it. Marvelled—At the wonderful works wrought by God.

Arminian pastoral: shared—trust that a look at Zion under God can stop the siege before an arrow flies. Marvel at his works; refuse a defense-story that never credits wonder.

Reformed

Calvin: They saw, so they marvelled; they were frightened, they fled precipitately—as soon as they saw the city they marvelled; struck with amazement at the mere sight, as if God had dazzled their eyes with the splendor of his glory; not Caesar's I came, I saw, I conquered, but I came, I saw, I fled.

Reformed pastoral: shared—expect God to undo enemies at the mere sight of his city. Hold the miracle; refuse a courage that never waits for his dazzling.

Psalms 48:6

6Trembling took hold of them there, anguish as of a woman in labor.

Arminian

Trembling took hold of them there, anguish as of a woman in labor. Wesley: Fear—At the tidings of Tirhakah's coming against them, and at that terrible slaughter of their army.

Arminian pastoral: shared—read enemy anguish as God's sudden reverse of pride. Refuse a calm that never trembles when God fights for Zion.

Reformed

Calvin: Fear seized upon them there, and pain as of a woman in travail—sudden change upon the enemies; from the height of secure and presumptuous pride they instantly fell into terror and fled; it is no uncommon thing if the Church is assailed by powerful adversaries—God's usual way to humble his people and then give striking proofs of his power; a nod alone is sufficient to deliver.

Reformed pastoral: shared—trust a nod from God more than matching enemy strength. Endure hard assaults without seeking help in every direction; refuse panic that never waits for his sudden reverse.

Psalms 48:7

7By the east wind you shattered the ships of Tarshish.

Arminian

By the east wind you shattered the ships of Tarshish. Wesley: Breakest—Thou didst no less violently and suddenly destroy these raging enemies of Jerusalem, than sometimes thou destroyest the ships at sea with a fierce and vehement wind, such as the eastern winds were in those parts.

Arminian pastoral: shared—see raging foes shattered as ships in an east wind. Ascribe the sudden ruin to God; refuse explaining away the storm.

Reformed

Calvin: By the east wind thou breakest in pieces the ships of Tarshish—enemies of the Church overthrown and plunged into destruction just as God by suddenly raising storms sinks the strongest ships; not to be wondered at if God, who breaks by winds the strongest vessels, overthrew enemies inflated with presumptuous confidence in their own strength.

Reformed pastoral: shared—measure enemy pride against the God who shatters Tarshish ships. Refuse trust in inflated strength; rest in the storm he raises for his Church.

Psalms 48:8

8As we have heard, so have we seen in the city of the LORD of hosts, in the city of our God, which God will establish forever. Selah

Arminian

As we have heard, so have we seen in the city of the LORD of hosts, in the city of our God, which God will establish forever. Selah. Wesley: Heard—The predictions of the prophets have been verified by the events. Establish—God will defend her in all succeeding ages. And so God would have done, if Jerusalem had not forsaken him, and forfeited his protection.

Arminian pastoral: shared—match what you heard with what you have seen in the city of our God. Hold forever-defense as God's will for a faithful city; refuse a forever claim that never fears forsaking him.

Reformed

Calvin: As we have heard, so have we seen in the city of Jehovah of hosts, in the city of our God: God will establish it for ever—ancient mercy confirmed by present experience, or promises actually performed; Jehovah of armies joined to our God by adoption; city founded by God continues steadfast amidst the world's revolutions; whatever was spoken of that city belongs to the spiritual Jerusalem dispersed through the world; the safety of the Church, though sorely shaken, can never be so weakened as to fall and be involved in ruin.

Reformed pastoral: shared—confess the city God will establish forever. Carry heard-and-seen into the spiritual Jerusalem; refuse a forever that can finally collapse.

Key contrast Both hear and see in the city of our God that God will establish forever. Arminians typically hear Wesley: predictions verified by events; God will defend her in succeeding ages—and so he would have done, if Jerusalem had not forsaken him and forfeited his protection. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: city founded by God continues steadfast; the word belongs to the spiritual Jerusalem; the Church's safety, though sorely shaken, can never be so weakened as to fall.

Psalms 48:9

9We have thought on your steadfast love, O God, in the midst of your temple.

Arminian

We have thought on your steadfast love, O God, in the midst of your temple. Wesley: Thought—It hath been the matter of our serious and deep meditation, when we have been worshipping in thy temple.

Arminian pastoral: shared—make steadfast love the deep meditation of temple worship. Refuse a liturgy that never thinks on hesed.

Reformed

Calvin: O God! we have waited for thy mercy in the midst of thy temple—damam as silent tranquillity of mind; the faithful, though severely afflicted, were not driven from relying upon God's aid; by patience and hope they opened the gate of his grace; temple as seat and pledge of his power and grace, not a fancy of our own inventing.

Reformed pastoral: shared—wait in quiet faith on steadfast love in his temple. Let patience open the gate of grace; refuse restless unbelief that blocks the help laid up for those who wait.

Key contrast Both linger on God's steadfast love in the midst of the temple. Arminians typically hear Wesley: serious and deep meditation on lovingkindness while worshipping in the temple. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: silent waiting—tranquillity of faith that opens the gate of grace; the temple as God-given pledge of presence, not a deliverance invented by fancy.

Psalms 48:10

10As your name, O God, so your praise reaches to the ends of the earth. Your right hand is filled with righteousness.

Arminian

As your name, O God, so your praise reaches to the ends of the earth. Your right hand is filled with righteousness. Wesley: So—Thou art acknowledged to be such an one as thou hast affirmed thyself to be in thy Word, God Almighty, or All-sufficient, the Lord of hosts, and a strong tower to all that trust in thee. Righteousness—Of righteous actions; by which thou discoverest thy holiness.

Arminian pastoral: shared—let praise match the name as far as the earth reaches. Trust the right hand filled with righteous acts; refuse a name praised only where it is already familiar.

Reformed

Calvin: As is thy name, O God! so is thy praise unto the ends of the earth: thy right hand is full of righteousness—wherever the fame of his name is spread, he is worthy of the highest praise; unlike idols renowned without true praise; righteousness as faithfulness in maintaining and defending his people, exhibited completely, not in one specimen only.

Reformed pastoral: shared—carry his praise with his name to the ends of the earth. Touch his righteousness in the defense of his own; refuse empty fame like the idols'.

Psalms 48:11

11Let Mount Zion be glad! Let the daughters of Judah rejoice because of your judgments!

Arminian

Let Mount Zion be glad! Let the daughters of Judah rejoice because of your judgments! Wesley: Judgments—Upon thine and their enemies.

Arminian pastoral: shared—let Zion and Judah's cities rejoice at judgments on the enemies. Refuse a gladness that never thanks God for vindicating his people.

Reformed

Calvin: Mount Zion shall rejoice, the daughters of Judah shall be glad, because of thy judgments—Jerusalem and the other cities of Judea have cause to commend God's righteousness; judgment because God, undertaking the Church's cause, openly showed himself the enemy of her oppressors and repressed their presumption.

Reformed pastoral: shared—rejoice in judgments that prove God the Church's defender. Commend his righteousness; refuse muted Zion when oppressors fall.

Psalms 48:12

12Walk about Zion, go around her, number her towers,

Arminian

Walk about Zion, go around her, number her towers, Wesley: Tell—He bids them mark well her towers, bulwarks, and palaces, with thankfulness to God, when they should find upon enquiry, that not one of them were demolished.

Arminian pastoral: shared—walk and count towers with thankfulness that none fell. Refuse a tour of Zion that never turns into praise.

Reformed

Calvin: Encompass Zion, and go round about her, number her towers—look carefully on every side; estimate her palaces as they deserve, that it may be manifest this is a city chosen of God; not that minds should rest in towers and walls, but that they should set them before us as a mirror in which the character of God may be seen—remembering God in her palaces is known for a fortress.

Reformed pastoral: shared—walk Zion's circuit as a mirror of God's care, not as trust in stone. Number towers to magnify the Chooser; refuse resting the eye in walls alone.

Psalms 48:13

13consider well her ramparts, go through her citadels, that you may tell the next generation

Arminian

consider well her ramparts, go through her citadels, that you may tell the next generation Wesley: Tell it—That they may continue their praises to God for this mercy, by which they hold and enjoy all their blessings.

Arminian pastoral: shared—consider ramparts so the next generation keeps praising the mercy that holds their blessings. Refuse a silence that buries thanksgiving with the present age.

Reformed

Calvin: Set your hearts to her walls… that ye may make report to the generation to come—tacit understanding that the outward city would not always be seen; we belong to that generation to whom these things are reported; since Christ came, the Church has been no less richly adorned with spiritual gifts than Jerusalem under the Law was fortified with walls and towers.

Reformed pastoral: shared—tell the next generation so faith outlasts visible walls. Receive the report as those in the spiritual city; refuse clinging to stone as if the Church's beauty ended there.

Key contrast Both walk Zion's ramparts to tell the next generation. Arminians typically hear Wesley: mark that not one tower was demolished, and tell it so they continue praises for the mercy by which they hold and enjoy all their blessings. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: report because the outward city would not always be seen; we are that generation; the Church is adorned with spiritual gifts no less than old Jerusalem with walls.

Psalms 48:14

14that this is God, our God forever and ever. He will guide us forever.

Arminian

that this is God, our God forever and ever. He will guide us forever. Shared closing with both camps: the God of Zion is our God forever, and he will guide forever—the end of the walk about the city.

Arminian pastoral: shared closing with both camps—tell the next generation that this is God, our God forever. Trust his forever guidance; refuse a Zion tour that never names him as guide.

Reformed

Calvin: For this God is our God for ever and ever: he will be our guide even unto death—demonstrative this distinguishes the only true God from false gods; faith sets a distinct knowledge, not the confusion of superstition; he continues unchangeably steadfast in maintaining his Church; guide and keeper for ever—not abandoning his people in the midst of death.

Reformed pastoral: shared closing with both camps—confess this God as ours forever and our guide unto the end. Hold distinct knowledge against counterfeit deities; refuse a forever that leaves the flock unguided at death.

Key contrast Both confess this God as our God forever and ever, our forever guide. Arminians typically hear the close as the mercy to tell the next generation—our God forever, guiding forever. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: demonstrative this against false gods; distinct certainty of faith; unchangeable maintenance of the Church; guide even unto death, not abandoning his people there.

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the LORD great and greatly to be praised in the city of our God on his holy mountain, Mount Zion beautiful and the joy of all the earth as the city of the great King, God known as a fortress within her citadels, kings assembled then astounded into panic and flight with trembling like a woman in labor, ships of Tarshish shattered by the east wind, heard and seen in the city of the LORD of hosts which God will establish forever, steadfast love thought on in the midst of the temple, name and praise to the ends of the earth with a right hand filled with righteousness, Mount Zion glad and the daughters of Judah rejoicing at his judgments, and the walk about Zion numbering towers and considering ramparts so the next generation may be told that this is God, our God forever and ever, who will guide us forever—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 48:1–14.

Reformed Notable voices

“Great is Jehovah, and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, in the mountain of his holiness—Jerusalem happy because God took charge to defend it; separates the Church from the world; when God selects from the whole human race a small number whom he embraces with fatherly love, an invaluable blessing; his glory manifested chiefly in the Church; holy mountain because the ark was placed there by divine appointment, not by Jerusalem's merits. Beautiful for situation, the joy of the whole earth, is mount Zion—excellencies in which God's glory shone; beauty not mere climate or fruit, but spiritual beauty after the ark abode there; joy of the whole earth because from thence salvation was to issue to the whole world; Christ appeared with his gospel out of Zion to fill the world with true joy; city of the great King—the high prerogative rendered the whole city sacred. God in her palaces is known for a defense—though fortified by towers and magnificent houses, continued safety owed to God's power alone; do not let magnificence shroud his power. Behold the kings assembled; they passed away together—special deliverance; confederated to destroy the Church; upon beholding the city they marvelled, were frightened, fled precipitately, seized with sorrow like a woman in travail; a nod alone on God's part is sufficient to deliver. By the east wind thou breakest the ships of Tarshish—enemies overthrown as God by storms sinks the strongest ships. As we have heard, so have we seen in the city of Jehovah of hosts, in the city of our God: God will establish it for ever—ancient mercy confirmed by present experience, or promises actually performed; Jehovah of armies and our God; city founded by God continues steadfast; whatever was spoken of that city belongs to the spiritual Jerusalem; the safety of the Church, though sorely shaken, can never be so weakened as to fall. O God! we have waited for thy mercy in the midst of thy temple—silent tranquillity of faith opening the gate of grace; temple as pledge of presence. As is thy name, so is thy praise unto the ends of the earth; thy right hand is full of righteousness—wherever his name is heard he is worthy of highest praise; righteousness as faithfulness maintaining his people. Mount Zion shall rejoice… because of thy judgments—God openly the enemy of her oppressors. Encompass Zion… number her towers… that ye may make report to the generation to come—towers and walls as a mirror of God's character; we belong to that generation; the Church no less richly adorned with spiritual gifts than Jerusalem with walls. For this God is our God for ever and ever: he will be our guide even unto death—demonstrative this distinguishes the only true God from false gods; he continues unchangeably steadfast; guide and keeper for ever.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 48
