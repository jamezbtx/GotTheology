Passage

Psalms 51

Verses 1–19 · ESV · Voices

To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David, when Nathan the prophet went to him after Bathsheba. The king pleads for mercy, confesses native and actual sin, asks for a clean heart and the Spirit’s presence, and widens the prayer to Zion.

Psalms 51:1

1Have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love; according to your abundant mercy blot out my transgressions.

Arminian

Have mercy—according to steadfast love and abundant mercy blot out transgressions. Wesley heard lovingkindness and the multitude of tender mercies as the ground of the plea after Nathan exposed David. The king does not bargain; he begs blot-out.

Arminian pastoral: mercy is real and must be sought. Grace that can blot also summons confession. David’s aggravated sin does not cancel the invitation to ask; it deepens the need for rich mercy received.

Reformed

Calvin: David begins by praying for pardon with unwonted earnestness—ordinary kindness would not suffice for so great a sinner. He piles loving-kindness and multitude of compassions because the stain felt deep.

Reformed pastoral: pardon is God’s work according to His compassions, not David’s worth. The plea rests on who God is. Shared: blot out is needed; neither camp softens Bathsheba’s aftermath.

Key contrast Both plead rich mercy to blot out. Arminians typically stress sincere petition that must still be made; Reformed readers typically stress that only the multitude of God’s compassions can cover such guilt.

Psalms 51:2

2Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin!

Arminian

Wash thoroughly; cleanse from sin. Wesley: cleanse me a leprous creature by grace and by the blood signified in ceremonial washings. Filth is inward; washing must go through.

Arminian note: the request is earnest cooperation with grace that washes. David does not scrub himself into pardon; he asks God to wash—and means it.

Reformed

Calvin: multiply to wash me—the stain felt deep, needing multiplied washing. Sin pollutes; remission is aptly compared to washing. The more aggravated the sin, the more earnest the desire for deliverance from terror of conscience.

Reformed accent: God alone washes. Shared: thorough cleansing, not a light rinse.

Psalms 51:3

3For I know my transgressions, and my sin is ever before me.

Arminian

I know my transgressions; my sin is ever before me. Wesley: acknowledge—confession that keeps the sin in view rather than burying it. Honesty before God is the doorway of mercy.

Arminian pastoral: knowledge here is owning, not mere data. Grace enables confession that can still be refused in self-excuse.

Reformed

Calvin: he is subjected to constant anguish that only reconciliation relieves. Prayer did not proceed from dissimulation; many praise grace who never felt bitterness of divine displeasure. He knows inwardly with keenest anguish.

Reformed note: true penitents are wounded and restless until mercy is assured. Shared: sin before the eyes; no shrug.

Psalms 51:4

4Against you, you only, have I sinned and done what is evil in your sight, so that you may be justified in your words and blameless in your judgment.

Arminian

Against you, you only, have I sinned—evil in your sight—so you may be justified. Wesley: comparatively: nothing more grievous than sin against God; severities will glorify His justice, not blemish it.

Arminian weight: guilt before the Judge is primary even when neighbors were wronged. The confession does not erase Uriah; it ranks the offense rightly.

Reformed

Calvin: whether the world pardons or not, God is the Judge; conscience hails him to that bar. Cited in Romans 3: God’s righteousness stands even in human sin. David charges himself and justifies God.

Reformed pastoral: acquittal among men cannot quiet an accusing conscience. Shared: God justified in judgment.

Psalms 51:5

5Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me.

Arminian

Brought forth in iniquity; conceived in sin. Wesley: the filthy stream leads to a corrupt fountain—this crime the fruit of a vile nature still ready to sin as occasion offers.

Arminian reading: original corruption is real and aggravates guilt; it does not excuse the adultery. Nature’s bent is confessed so that grace’s need is plain.

Reformed

Calvin: not one sin only—born in iniquity, nature entirely depraved. He aggravates guilt, not excuses it. Against Pelagius: sin is hereditary disease, not mere imitation. Corruption extends to every part of the soul.

Reformed accent: original sin is confessed to deepen need of new creation. Shared: we are not blank slates.

Key contrast Both confess native corruption behind the crime. Arminians typically stress that inherited bent still leaves the act responsible and grace resistible in response; Reformed readers typically stress total native depravity that only sovereign grace can remake.

Psalms 51:6

6Behold, you delight in truth in the inward being, and you teach me wisdom in the secret heart.

Arminian

Truth in the inward being; wisdom in the secret heart. Wesley: uprightness God requires; original corruption and actual sin both contradict that holy will and the knowledge written in the heart.

Arminian note: God teaches wisdom inwardly—truth is not only outward law. The heart can be taught; neglect of that teaching worsens the fall.

Reformed

Calvin: God desires truth in the inward parts—secret as well as gross sins displease Him. David cannot plead ignorance; wisdom was closely brought home. Privilege aggravated the offense.

Reformed pastoral: inward truth is God’s delight and gift. Shared: heart-deep honesty, not costume piety.

Psalms 51:7

7Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.

Arminian

Purge with hyssop; wash—whiter than snow. Wesley: as lepers were purified by appointed rites, cleanse me by grace and by Christ’s blood those rites signified.

Arminian hope: cleansing is real and available to the contrite who ask. Ceremonial shadow points to blood that actually cleanses.

Reformed

Calvin: he alludes to legal rites as seals of grace, not trust in the symbol. He asks God to accomplish what the rites signified. The Spirit sprinkles the conscience with Christ’s blood.

Reformed accent: atonement is God’s application of Christ’s satisfaction. Shared: whiter than snow is gift.

Psalms 51:8

8Let me hear joy and gladness; let the bones that you have broken rejoice.

Arminian

Joy and gladness; bones you have broken rejoice. Wesley: by the Spirit seal pardon on the conscience; the wounded heart comforted.

Arminian pastoral: joy is restoration of sensed grace, sought and received. Broken bones name real discipline that mercy can reverse into praise.

Reformed

Calvin: joy of the Lord reanimates; it comes by hearing the word that pardons. No solid peace except reposing on God’s promises. Bones broken = extreme grief under displeasure.

Reformed note: assurance grows as God speaks peace. Shared: joy after fracture.

Psalms 51:9

9Hide your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities.

Arminian

Hide your face from my sins; blot out all iniquities. Parallel to v. 1—pardon reiterated. Wesley’s silence on the verse still fits the blot-out theme: remove the record from sight.

Arminian plea: hide and blot are requests faith keeps making until peace settles. Grace invites the ask.

Reformed

Calvin: hide face from sins = pardoning; blot out explains it. Justification as God’s voluntary forgetting of iniquities. Reiteration shows depth of anxiety for a hard-won favor.

Reformed pastoral: gratuitous pardon cleanses. Shared: no leftover ledger with God.

Psalms 51:10

10Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me.

Arminian

Create a clean heart; renew a right spirit. Wesley: work an holy frame that purges inward filth; firm, constant resolution; temper of soul fixed and unmoveable.

Arminian reading: create names God’s work; renew names a spirit that must be kept steadfast. Grace that creates also calls for a fixed will cooperating with the Spirit.

Reformed

Calvin: he moves from pardon to sanctification. Create expresses that nothing less than a miracle reforms; repentance is God’s gift. Sophists who leave a middle free will rob God of glory. Renew a right spirit—restoration after fall is still creation-grace.

Reformed accent: clean heart is new creation, not self-rehab. Shared: God must make the heart clean.

Key contrast Both ask God to create a clean heart. Arminians typically hear creating grace that renews a spirit the believer must keep steadfast; Reformed readers typically hear new-creation grace that leaves no middle free will to share the glory.

Psalms 51:11

11Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your Holy Spirit from me.

Arminian

Cast me not away; take not your Holy Spirit from me. Fear of exile from presence after gross fall. Wesley leaves the verse terse; the plea is retention of the Spirit’s presence.

Arminian pastoral: the Spirit can be grieved and the sense of presence lost; the prayer treats that loss as real danger for a believer who has fallen hard.

Reformed

Calvin: Spirit not altogether taken—gifts obscured, not reprobate mind. Elect may fear loss yet hold that grace is incorruptible seed. Against those who say mortal sin alienates the elect entirely.

Reformed comfort: cast-away language is the saint’s fear; perseverance holds underneath. Shared: dread of Spirit withdrawn is holy.

Key contrast Both fear loss of God’s presence after sin. Arminians typically treat the danger as real for a true believer who may finally fall; Reformed readers typically treat the fear as means while denying final loss of the Spirit in the elect.

Psalms 51:12

12Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with a willing spirit.

Arminian

Restore joy of salvation; uphold with willing spirit. Wesley: comfortable sense of saving grace; free/ingenuous spirit opposed to base servile spirit in his wicked practices—freedom from bondage of sin to run God’s precepts.

Arminian hope: joy restored and a willing spirit upheld—grace that frees for cheerful obedience, not forced drudgery.

Reformed

Calvin: grief remains until peace with God. Free spirit = free and cheerful, not enslaved by guilt; some hear princely courage for kingship. Either way, divine grace alone recovers it.

Reformed accent: joy of salvation is restored gift. Shared: willing spirit is not self-generated pep.

Psalms 51:13

13Then I will teach transgressors your ways, and sinners will return to you.

Arminian

Then teach transgressors; sinners return. Mercy received becomes mission. Wesley’s economy: restored joy overflows into teaching that others may convert.

Arminian pastoral: teaching and return are real aims—grace that saved David aims at others’ turning, which they must still do.

Reformed

Calvin: gratitude constrains helping others into the same grace. He speaks sanguinely of converting others; we forget God can crown such attempts.

Reformed note: recovered saints become instruments. Shared: pardon fuels witness.

Psalms 51:14

14Deliver me from bloodguiltiness, O God, O God of my salvation, and my tongue will sing aloud of your righteousness.

Arminian

Deliver from bloodguiltiness; tongue sings righteousness. Wesley: righteousness = clemency and goodness. Bloodguilt names Uriah and more.

Arminian plea: deliverance from blood-guilt is begged from the God of salvation; praise follows mercy received.

Reformed

Calvin: bloods may mean capital guilt/death-sentence felt; he celebrates God’s righteousness as faithfulness to promise and help. O God of my salvation—trembling yet trusting.

Reformed pastoral: only mercy clears murder’s stain before heaven. Shared: bloodguilt is not minimized.

Psalms 51:15

15O Lord, open my lips, and my mouth will declare your praise.

Arminian

Open my lips; mouth declares praise. Wesley: lips shut with shame and grief. Praise needs God to open what shame closed.

Arminian note: praise is enabled response—God opens; the mouth then actually declares.

Reformed

Calvin: God afford matter of praise; mouth shut until pardon extends. New song after mercy—not mere eloquence tips.

Reformed accent: praise is answer to grace. Shared: shame-closed lips need God.

Psalms 51:16

16For you will not delight in sacrifice, or I would give it; you will not be pleased with a burnt offering.

Arminian

You will not delight in sacrifice—or I would give it. Wesley: not absolute denial of sacrifice, but these crimes not expiated by any ritual offering.

Arminian reading: no bargain of bulls for adultery and murder; mercy and contrition, not payment theater.

Reformed

Calvin: confidence of pardon though he brings no compensation—relies on riches of mercy. Against those who treat sacrifices as purchase price; rites point to Christ, not self-redemption.

Reformed accent: gratuitous reconciliation. Shared: burnt offering cannot buy this clean.

Psalms 51:17

17The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise.

Arminian

Broken spirit; contrite heart God will not despise. Wesley: more value than many sacrifices.

Arminian pastoral: contrition is the offering God receives—real humility that can be presented or withheld; grace softens what pride resists.

Reformed

Calvin: sacrifices of God = broken spirit enough without pretended satisfaction. Broken spirit emptied of vain-glorious confidence; contrite heart has no dealings on exchange. Does not exclude faith; shuts out merit.

Reformed note: penitence is not a wage but the empty hand. Shared: God does not despise the crushed heart.

Key contrast Both prize a broken, contrite heart over ritual. Arminians typically stress sincere contrition grace enables and requires; Reformed readers typically stress contrition as emptied of merit, resting on free mercy alone.

Psalms 51:18

18Do good to Zion in your good pleasure; build up the walls of Jerusalem;

Arminian

Do good to Zion; build Jerusalem’s walls. Wesley: free rich mercy; perfect the city and especially the temple despite my sins.

Arminian note: personal pardon widens into prayer for the people—David’s fall must not ruin Zion; he intercedes.

Reformed

Calvin: from private prayer to the Church—he had nearly ruined it by his fall. Restores by free good pleasure, not others’ righteousness. God builds the spiritual temple.

Reformed accent: Church’s standing is God’s good pleasure. Shared: Zion on the penitent’s lips.

Psalms 51:19

19then will you delight in right sacrifices, in burnt offerings and whole burnt offerings; then bulls will be offered on your altar.

Arminian

Then right sacrifices; bulls on the altar. Wesley: then God pleased with sacrifices of righteousness. Order: mercy and contrition first; public worship gladly follows.

Arminian hope: restored fellowship renews right offering—not bribes, but grateful obedience in the assembly.

Reformed

Calvin: sacrifices of righteousness = warrantable, according to commandment, with reference to their proper end. Not empty form. Thanksgiving joins heart disposition to solemn worship.

Reformed pastoral: accepted offerings flow from prior mercy. Shared: altar after blot-out.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on mercy that blots out, a clean heart created, and a contrite spirit God will not despise—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 51.

Arminian Notable voices

“Create—Work in me an holy frame of heart, whereby my inward filth may be purged away. Right—Heb. firm or constant, that my resolution may be fixed and unmoveable. Spirit—Temper or disposition of soul. The joy—The comfortable sense of thy saving grace, promised and vouchsafed to me, both for my present and everlasting salvation. Free—Or, ingenuous, or liberal, or princely… a spirit, which may free me from the bondage of sin, and enable me chearfully to run the way of God's precepts.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 51:10, 12)

Reformed Notable voices

“Create in me a clean heart, O God! In the previous part of the psalm David has been praying for pardon. He now requests that the grace of the Spirit… might be restored to him… By employing the term create, he expresses his persuasion that nothing less than a miracle could effect his reformation, and emphatically declares that repentance is the gift of God. The Sophists grant the necessity of the aids of the Spirit… but by assigning a middle place to the free will of man, they rob God of a great part of his glory.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 51:10)
