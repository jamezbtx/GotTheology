Passage

Psalms 40

Verses 1–17 · ESV · Voices

To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. The singer waited patiently for the LORD, who inclined and heard his cry; drew him up from the pit of destruction and the miry bog, set his feet on a rock, and made his steps secure; put a new song of praise in his mouth so that many will see and fear and trust in the LORD. Blessed is the one who makes the LORD his trust and does not turn to the proud or those who go astray after a lie. God's wondrous deeds and thoughts toward his people multiply beyond telling. Then the turn shared by Hebrews 10: in sacrifice and offering God has not delighted, but has given an open ear; burnt offering and sin offering he has not required. Behold, I have come; in the scroll of the book it is written of me: I delight to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart. Glad news of deliverance is told in the great congregation without restrained lips; faithfulness, salvation, steadfast love are not concealed. Mercy is not to be restrained; steadfast love and faithfulness preserve. Yet evils encompass beyond number, iniquities overtake so he cannot see, more than the hairs of his head, and the heart fails. Be pleased, O LORD, to deliver; make haste to help; shame those who snatch life and delight in hurt, and who cry Aha, Aha. Let seekers rejoice and lovers of salvation say continually, Great is the LORD. The close: poor and needy, yet the Lord takes thought; help and deliverer—do not delay, O my God.

Psalms 40:1

1I waited patiently for the LORD; he inclined to me and heard my cry.

Arminian

I waited patiently for the LORD; he inclined to me and heard my cry. Long waiting meets an inclined ear; patience is not empty delay but hope that keeps crying until he hears.

Arminian pastoral: shared—wait without quitting the cry. Trust that he inclines; refuse both frantic haste and a silence that has stopped asking.

Reformed

Calvin: In waiting I waited—Hebraism of earnest, constant patience. Though God delayed his help, David's heart did not faint; faith is thoroughly tried only by long endurance. When God seems inactive or asleep, that is totally different from deceit; the fitting season of deliverance will arrive.

Reformed pastoral: shared—endure the trial that proves faith. Do not lose courage when help comes more slowly than desire; wait until he hears.

Key contrast Both prize patient waiting that God finally hears. Arminians typically stress the pastoral cry that keeps asking until he inclines. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's doubled waiting: long anxious suspense that proves faith by calm endurance when God delays.

Psalms 40:2

2He drew me up from the pit of destruction, out of the miry bog, and set my feet upon a rock, making my steps secure.

Arminian

He drew me up from the pit of destruction, out of the miry bog, and set my feet upon a rock, making my steps secure. Wesley: Pit—Desperate dangers and calamities. Rock—A place of strength and safety. Established—Kept me from falling into mischief. Rescue is from miry ruin onto firm footing.

Arminian pastoral: shared—name the pit as real desperation, then the rock as his gift. Stand where he set you; ask him to keep steps from sliding back into the bog.

Reformed

Calvin: roaring pit and miry clay—imminent death as in a gulf of tumultuous waters; weight of calamity nearly overwhelmed him. Then sudden change: feet set upon a rock, steps established—greatness of the grace made manifest.

Reformed pastoral: shared—trace deliverance to his drawing hand, not self-extraction. Remember the miry near-death so praise matches the rescue.

Psalms 40:3

3He put a new song in my mouth, a song of praise to our God. Many will see and fear, and put their trust in the LORD.

Arminian

He put a new song in my mouth, a song of praise to our God. Many will see and fear, and put their trust in the LORD. Wesley: And fear—Shall stand in awe of that God, whom they see to have so great power, either to save or to destroy. New praise becomes public instruction.

Arminian pastoral: shared—sing the new song he put there. Let others see deliverance and learn holy fear and trust; do not pocket praise in private only.

Reformed

Calvin: new song marks consummation of deliverance—exquisite, not ordinary, matching singular grace. Many shall see, fear, and trust: fear and hope joined; none hope in God's favor whose mind is not first imbued with his fear. See means perception of the mind by the Spirit, not bare eyes on events.

Reformed pastoral: shared—let one believer's rescue teach the church. Stir fervent praise proportional to the help; join reverent fear to trust.

Psalms 40:4

4Blessed is the man who makes the LORD his trust, who does not turn to the proud, to those who go astray after a lie!

Arminian

Blessed is the man who makes the LORD his trust, who does not turn to the proud, to those who go astray after a lie! Wesley: The proud—Or, the mighty, the great and proud potentates of the world, to whom most men are apt to look and trust. To lies—lying vanities that promise more than they perform.

Arminian pastoral: shared—plant trust in the LORD alone. Turn from proud props and lying vanities; bless the man who refuses that false turn.

Reformed

Calvin: blessed who set Jehovah for confidence and regard not the proud who turn aside to lying. Not merely the rich or great as props, but those puffed up whose examples allure to apostasy; pride and vanity oppose holy trust in God alone.

Reformed pastoral: shared—guard against imitating the prosperous proud. Refuse vanity that puts fancy in God's place; rest confidence in him alone.

Key contrast Both bless trust in the LORD against proud lies. Arminians typically hear Wesley: the proud as mighty potentates men are apt to look to, and lies as earthly vanities that promise more than they perform. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: not merely relying on the rich, but refusing the alluring examples of the proud who turn aside to vanity—pride itself opposed to simple trust in God.

Psalms 40:5

5You have multiplied, O LORD my God, your wondrous deeds and your thoughts toward us; none can compare with you! I will proclaim and tell of them, yet they are more than can be told.

Arminian

You have multiplied, O LORD my God, your wondrous deeds and your thoughts toward us; none can compare with you! I will proclaim and tell of them, yet they are more than can be told. Wesley: Many—This verse seems to be interposed as a wall of partition, between that which David speaks in his own person, and that which he speaks in the person of the Messiah, in the following verses.

Arminian pastoral: shared—proclaim what you can, confess what exceeds telling. Let multiplied thoughts toward us humble comparison with any rival.

Reformed

Calvin: wonderful works and counsels toward us cannot be reckoned up in order—providence surpasses comprehension. Reverently admire what reason cannot order; yet do not bury mention of his counsels because they are high—meditate according to measure and finish in admiration.

Reformed pastoral: shared—cast cares on this providence rather than earthly fortresses. Refuse both carnal measuring of God and willful ignorance of his works.

Key contrast Both confess deeds and thoughts beyond full telling. Arminians typically hear Wesley's structural note: a wall of partition between David's own voice and the Messiah's in what follows. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: incomprehensible providence toward us that must be admired and modestly rehearsed—not a source-critical fork, but a real difference of emphasis before the shared messianic turn.

Psalms 40:6

6In sacrifice and offering you have not delighted, but you have given me an open ear. Burnt offering and sin offering you have not required.

Arminian

In sacrifice and offering you have not delighted, but you have given me an open ear. Burnt offering and sin offering you have not required. Wesley: in a proper sense these words belong to the person and times of the Messiah—God did not require legal sacrifices for the satisfaction of his justice and the expiation of sins, which only Christ's blood could accomplish. Opened—Heb. bored; devoted to perpetual service as Exod. 21; the Seventy, whom the apostle follows (Heb. 10:5), render a body hast thou prepared me.

Arminian pastoral: shared messianic christology with Hebrews 10—both camps confess Christ over empty sacrifice. Hear the open ear as glad obedience; do not invent a timeline quarrel where Wesley and Calvin agree on the better offering.

Reformed

Calvin: under four kinds of sacrifice David comprehends the law's rites—God takes no pleasure in mere ceremony but requires sincerity, faith, and holiness. Thou hast bored my ears: not the forced Exodus servant custom, but ears cleansed to discern that outward types serve a spiritual end. Hebrews 10: the truth and substance of the figures are in Christ; when they are put in place of true worship they are rejected.

Reformed pastoral: shared messianic christology with Hebrews 10—Christ's offering fulfills what shadows could not. Yield the heart, not only the altar; refuse hypocrisy that mocks God with cold rite.

Key contrast Both, with Hebrews 10, confess Messiah over sacrifice—shared christology, not an Arminian/Reformed quarrel over whether the verse is messianic. Arminians typically hear Wesley's bored ears via Exod. 21 and the LXX body prepared for Christ. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: reject that servant-rite as too forced; ears opened means Spirit-taught discernment that God wants the heart, while still referring the church's obedience to Christ the head in Hebrews 10.

Psalms 40:7

7Then I said, “Behold, I have come; in the scroll of the book it is written of me:

Arminian

Then I said, “Behold, I have come; in the scroll of the book it is written of me: Wesley: These words literally and truly belong to Christ; lo, I offer myself to come into the world, as Heb. 10:5 applies this place expressly to him. Volume—the law of Moses in roll form, pointing to Christ of whom Moses writes much.

Arminian pastoral: shared with Hebrews 10—behold the Son who comes as written. Do not pry a camp wedge where Wesley and Calvin both hear Christ's advent; answer with willing “I have come.”

Reformed

Calvin: Then marks schooling by the Spirit—Lo! I come as willing, not slavish, obedience. In the roll of the book: God writes his own as a general enrolls soldiers; the inward teaching belongs peculiarly to them. Hebrews 10 restricts to Christ the head what belongs to all the elect: referring the whole body of the Church, David must refer us to the head.

Reformed pastoral: shared with Hebrews 10—come willingly under the written will. Offer yourself, not constrained show; see the body's obedience gathered under Christ's coming.

Key contrast Both apply Behold, I have come through Hebrews 10 to Christ—shared messianic confession. Arminians typically hear Wesley: the scroll as Moses' law-volume written concerning Christ. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: enrollment of God's children under the head, with the Apostle referring the church's obedience to Christ without denying David's place in the body.

Psalms 40:8

8I delight to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart.”

Arminian

I delight to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart.” Wesley: eminently true of Christ—heroic obedience that not only resolved but delighted to do the Father's will, even a shameful, painful, cursed death. My heart—receive the law with heartiest love, meditating and obeying.

Arminian pastoral: shared christology—delight, not mere duty, marks the obedient Son. Ask for law written within; refuse a will that only grudges compliance.

Reformed

Calvin: I have delighted to do thy pleasure; law in the midst of my bowels—cordial preference of God's service, Law fixed deep. The Spirit engraves the Law on the heart; God changes the will that naturally inclines to sin so we cordially seek righteousness. True freedom is the heart framed unto obedience.

Reformed pastoral: shared christology—begin obedience at the heart under Christ's delight. Seek the Spirit's engraving; refuse splendid works that lack the living root.

Psalms 40:9

9I have told the glad news of deliverance in the great congregation; behold, I have not restrained my lips, as you know, O LORD.

Arminian

I have told the glad news of deliverance in the great congregation; behold, I have not restrained my lips, as you know, O LORD. Wesley: Righteousness—Thy faithfulness. Great congregation—most public and solemn assemblies, even to the nations. Unrestrained lips publish what God has done.

Arminian pastoral: shared—tell deliverance aloud where the church gathers. Do not restrain lips from faithfulness; let God know you have spoken.

Reformed

Calvin: proclaimed righteousness in the great assembly—the lawfully constituted church, not market noise. Open praise edifies; hiding righteousness in the heart seeks to bury God's memory. He calls God witness against hypocritical loudness without affection.

Reformed pastoral: shared—publish his righteousness for others' building up. Match lip to heart; ask continued favor as a thankful witness.

Psalms 40:10

10I have not hidden your deliverance within my heart; I have spoken of your faithfulness and your salvation; I have not concealed your steadfast love and your faithfulness from the great congregation.

Arminian

I have not hidden your deliverance within my heart; I have spoken of your faithfulness and your salvation; I have not concealed your steadfast love and your faithfulness from the great congregation. Mercy, faithfulness, salvation, and steadfast love are stacked in public speech.

Arminian pastoral: shared—conceal nothing of his steadfast love from the congregation. Speak faithfulness and salvation; refuse a private gospel that never blesses the assembly.

Reformed

Calvin: accumulation of terms—righteousness, truth, salvation, mercy—magnifies goodness. Mercy induces God to regard us; righteousness protects; truth assures constant help; salvation is the effect. Embrace these as held forth in the Word.

Reformed pastoral: shared—rehearse the full chain of his goodness. Hold mercy as the fountain and truth as the confirmation; hide none of it from the great assembly.

Psalms 40:11

11As for you, O LORD, you will not restrain your mercy from me; your steadfast love and your faithfulness will ever preserve me!

Arminian

As for you, O LORD, you will not restrain your mercy from me; your steadfast love and your faithfulness will ever preserve me! Wesley: With-hold not—having been transported to the Messiah's mystery, he is led back to his own case. Thanksgiving turns again to petition for unrestrained mercy.

Arminian pastoral: shared—ask that mercy not be restrained after praise. Lean on steadfast love and faithfulness to preserve; bring your own need without dropping Christ's confession.

Reformed

Calvin: withhold not tender mercies; let goodness and truth always preserve—thankful praise keeps the course of mercy open; perverse silence closes the gate. Confidence of salvation begins at free favor and is confirmed by truth keeping promises.

Reformed pastoral: shared—link gratitude to continued help. Do not stop the fountain by ingratitude; plead mercy and truth as your preservation.

Psalms 40:12

12For evils have encompassed me beyond number; my iniquities have overtaken me, and I cannot see; they are more than the hairs of my head; my heart fails me.

Arminian

For evils have encompassed me beyond number; my iniquities have overtaken me, and I cannot see; they are more than the hairs of my head; my heart fails me. Wesley: Taken hold—as an officer arrests a man. To look—unto God or men with any comfort: ashamed and confounded. Evils and iniquities overwhelm sight and heart.

Arminian pastoral: shared—own sins that overtake like an arrest. Bring failing heart and blinded comfort to mercy; refuse excuses that deny the hold of iniquity.

Reformed

Calvin: innumerable evils compassed on all sides; iniquities have laid hold—he confesses just recompense of transgressions, not unjust severity. Heart fails as to the flesh, yet perseverance in prayer proves faith not wholly extinguished.

Reformed pastoral: shared—set forth sins in magnitude lest you suspect God of undue severity. Groan as a penitent; keep praying when the heart faints.

Key contrast Both feel evils and iniquities beyond number. Arminians typically hear Wesley: sins arrest like an officer, leaving no comforted look to God or man—shame and confusion. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: afflictions as just fruit of transgressions humbly confessed, heart failing in the flesh while faith still prays.

Psalms 40:13

13Be pleased, O LORD, to deliver me! O LORD, make haste to help me!

Arminian

Be pleased, O LORD, to deliver me! O LORD, make haste to help me! Deliverance is asked from free pleasure, and help with holy haste.

Arminian pastoral: shared—plead his good pleasure, not your desert. Ask haste without despairing when he times the help.

Reformed

Calvin: Be pleased—desire help from pure kindness and good-will, free mercy not merit. Make haste: God permits this form even while we must contend against weariness when he delays.

Reformed pastoral: shared—ask free kindness and permitted haste. Refuse both demand as earned wages and a patience that has stopped pleading.

Psalms 40:14

14Let those be put to shame and disappointed altogether who seek to snatch away my life; let those be turned back and brought to dishonor who delight in my hurt!

Arminian

Let those be put to shame and disappointed altogether who seek to snatch away my life; let those be turned back and brought to dishonor who delight in my hurt! Life-snatchers and hurt-lovers are cited before God for shame and turned-back dishonor.

Arminian pastoral: shared—lay violent foes before the Judge. Seek their disappointment without taking private revenge into your own hand.

Reformed

Calvin: cites enemies to God's judgment-seat; the more unjustly they afflict, the more God is disposed to help. Imprecations must rise from zeal of the Spirit, not powerful impulse of the flesh—counterfeit imitation is always to be condemned.

Reformed pastoral: shared—trust recompense to God rather than fleshly passion. Let their cruelty move you to prayer, not to matching malice.

Psalms 40:15

15Let those be appalled because of their shame who say to me, “Aha, Aha!”

Arminian

Let those be appalled because of their shame who say to me, “Aha, Aha!” Wesley: Shame—Their sinful and shameful actions. Mocking Aha meets appalling shame.

Arminian pastoral: shared—bring scorners' taunts to God. Do not answer Aha with Aha; ask that shame fall on sinful mockery.

Reformed

Calvin: destroyed for a reward of their shame—as they sought to overwhelm him with confusion for derision, let similar confusion fall on them. Their wicked triumph poured contempt while he was oppressed.

Reformed pastoral: shared—expect God to reverse cruel scorn. Let that doctrine restrain you toward neighbors; refuse the sport of another's misery.

Psalms 40:16

16But may all who seek you rejoice and be glad in you; may those who love your salvation say continually, “Great is the LORD!”

Arminian

But may all who seek you rejoice and be glad in you; may those who love your salvation say continually, “Great is the LORD!” One man's deliverance becomes common joy for seekers and lovers of salvation.

Arminian pastoral: shared—seek him and love his salvation aloud. Make Great is the LORD your continual word; refuse salvation invented apart from him.

Reformed

Calvin: asks nothing individual that does not pertain to the whole church—gladness for all saints when God proves his readiness to help. Those who love thy salvation expect preservation from God alone; seekers of God contrast with seekers of the psalmist's life; Aha yields to Jehovah be magnified.

Reformed pastoral: shared—apply one deliverance as common proof of his goodness. Renounce vain hopes; magnify the LORD with those who love his salvation.

Psalms 40:17

17As for me, I am poor and needy, but the Lord takes thought for me. You are my help and my deliverer; do not delay, O my God!

Arminian

As for me, I am poor and needy, but the Lord takes thought for me. You are my help and my deliverer; do not delay, O my God! Poverty and need meet the Lord's thought; help and deliverer are named against delay.

Arminian pastoral: shared—confess poor and needy without despair. Trust that he takes thought; ask your help and deliverer not to delay.

Reformed

Calvin: poor and needy, yet Jehovah hath regarded—grace enhanced because he stretched his hand to one despised and destitute. Thou art my help and deliverer; make no delay—courage from experience that help hitherto given will not fail.

Reformed pastoral: shared—do not read misery as God's weariness of you. Approach without wavering; plead help and deliverer from proven mercy.

Key contrast Both end poor and needy under the Lord's care. Arminians typically stress the pastoral comfort that he takes thought and must not delay. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin: regard shown precisely to the despised poor, courage drawn from prior deliverance so the plea make no delay rests on experience, not presumption.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on patient waiting heard at last, drawing from pit and miry bog onto a rock with secure steps, a new song that teaches many to fear and trust, blessing on those who make the LORD their trust and refuse the proud and the lie, wondrous deeds and thoughts beyond telling, open ear over sacrifice and offering, Behold I have come written in the scroll, delight to do God's will with the law within the heart, glad news of deliverance in the great congregation, mercy not restrained, evils and iniquities that overwhelm and fail the heart, haste for deliverance, shame on life-snatchers and Aha-criers, joy for those who seek and love salvation, and the poor and needy held in the Lord's thought as help and deliverer without delay—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 40:1–17.

Arminian Notable voices

“Pit—Desperate dangers and calamities. Rock—A place of strength and safety. Established—Kept me from falling into mischief. And fear—Shall stand in awe of that God, whom they see to have so great power, either to save or to destroy. The proud—Or, the mighty, the great and proud potentates of the world, to whom most men are apt to look and trust. Turn—From God, in whom alone they ought to trust. To lies—To lying vanities, such as worldly power and wisdom, and riches, and all other earthly things, or persons, in which men are prone to trust: which are called lies, because they promise more than they perform. Many—This verse seems to be interposed as a wall of partition, between that which David speaks in his own person, and that which he speaks in the person of the Messiah, in the following verses. Sacrifice—These and the following words may in an improper sense belong to the time of David; when God might be said, not to desire or require legal sacrifices comparatively. Thou didst desire obedience rather than sacrifices, but in a proper sense, they belong only to the person and times of the Messiah—God did not desire or require them for the satisfaction of his own justice and the expiation of men's sins, which could not possibly be done by the blood of bulls or goats, but only by the blood of Christ. Opened—Heb. bored. I have devoted myself to thy perpetual service, and thou hast accepted of me as such, and signified so much by the boring of mine ears, according to the law and custom, Exod. 21:5,6. The seventy Jewish interpreters, whom the apostle follows, Heb. 10:5, translate these words, a body hast thou prepared me. Them—These words literally and truly belong to Christ: seeing thou requirest a better sacrifice than those of the law, lo, I offer myself to come into the world, as Heb. 10:5 applies this place expressly to Christ. Volume—The law of Moses, made up in the form of a roll or volume; this place manifestly points to Christ, concerning whom much is said in the books of Moses. I delight—This is eminently true of Christ, and is here observed as an act of heroic obedience, that he not only resolved to do, but delighted in doing the will of God, even to die a most shameful, painful, and cursed death. My heart—I do not only understand it, but receive it with heartiest love, delighting both to meditate of it, and to yield obedience to it. Righteousness—Thy faithfulness. Great congregation—In the most public and solemn assemblies: not only to the Jews, but also to all nations. With-hold not—David, having been transported by the spirit of God to the commemoration of the great mystery of the Messiah, he now seems to be led back by the same spirit to the consideration of his own case. Taken hold—Men's sins are figuratively said to take hold of them, as an officer takes hold of a man whom he arrests. To look—Unto God or men, with any comfort: I am ashamed and confounded. Shame—Their sinful and shameful actions.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 40

Reformed Notable voices

“In waiting I waited for Jehovah—not merely waited, but by repetition showed long anxious suspense; though God delayed, David's heart did not faint; faith is thoroughly tried only by long endurance. Drew me from the roaring pit and miry clay—imminent death as in a gulf roaring with waters; then sudden change: feet on a rock, steps established. New song—consummation of deliverance; singular grace furnishes exquisite praise, and many shall see, fear, and trust—fear joined to hope, for none hope in God whose mind is not first imbued with his fear. Blessed who set Jehovah for confidence and regard not the proud who turn aside to lying—not merely the rich, but those puffed up whose vanity opposes holy trust in God alone. Wonderful works and counsels toward us more than can be told—providence surpasses comprehension; admire what reason cannot order, yet meditate according to measure. Sacrifice and offering thou hast not taken pleasure; thou hast bored my ears—not forced Exodus servant-rite, but ears cleansed so he discerns that God requires sincerity, faith, and holiness, not mere ceremonies; then Lo! I come; in the roll of the book it is written of me; I delight to do thy will; thy law in the midst of my bowels—willing obedience from the heart, Law engraved by the Spirit. Hebrews 10 restricts what belongs to all the elect to Christ the head: David speaks not in his own name only but shows what belongs to God's children, and referring the whole body of the Church he must refer us to the head; legal sacrifices give way because their truth and substance are in Christ. Proclaimed righteousness in the great assembly—open praise that God may continue goodness; not hidden in the heart. Withhold not tender mercies; let goodness and truth preserve—thankful lips keep the fountain open. Innumerable evils; iniquities have laid hold—just recompense confessed, not unjust severity charged; heart fails in the flesh yet prayer continues. Be pleased to deliver; make haste—free kindness, not desert. Shame those who seek my life and cry Aha—imprecation from zeal of the Spirit, not fleshly passion. Let seekers rejoice; lovers of thy salvation say, Jehovah be magnified. Poor and needy, yet Jehovah hath regarded me; my help and deliverer—make no delay.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 40
