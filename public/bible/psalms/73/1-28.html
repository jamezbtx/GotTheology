Passage

Psalms 73

Verses 1–28 · ESV · Voices

A Psalm of Asaph. Truly God is good to Israel—to the pure in heart—yet Asaph’s feet almost slipped when he envied the arrogant and the prosperity of the wicked: sleek, untroubled, proud, violent, blasphemous, always at ease. He nearly called clean hands vain under daily rebuke, until the sanctuary of God: there he discerned their end—slippery places, sudden terrors, dream-phantoms. Embittered and beastlike, he is nevertheless held by the right hand, guided by counsel, received to glory; God is his portion forever. Those far from God perish; for him it is good to be near God, the Lord GOD his refuge, that he may tell of all God’s works.

Psalms 73:1

1Truly God is good to Israel, to those who are pure in heart.

Arminian

Wesley: Clean heart—true Israelites who love God and serve in spirit and truth. God is good to Israel opens the psalm as confession before complaint.

Arminian pastoral: shared—goodness to the pure in heart is affirmed first. Grace invites purity of heart as living response, not a claim that merit earned the good.

Reformed

Calvin: Yet God is good to Israel—to the right of heart. After agitation, faith breaks out: God is gracious to genuine saints, not every name on the roll.

Reformed accent: shared—Israel is limited to the upright in heart; hypocrites are cut off. Providence is confessed against the flesh’s misgiving.

Psalms 73:2

2But as for me, my feet had almost stumbled, my steps had nearly slipped.

Arminian

Wesley: Feet almost gone—faith in providence nearly overthrown; steps nearly slipped.

Arminian hearing: honesty about near-fall. Shared: saints can almost stumble without God casting them off; the confession itself is already returning.

Reformed

Calvin: And I—even the well-taught almost slipped. Temptation that affronts God’s honor assails even those schooled in godliness.

Reformed pastoral: shared warning—all are in danger of falling unless upheld by God’s hand.

Psalms 73:3

3For I was envious of the arrogant when I saw the prosperity of the wicked.

Arminian

Wesley: Envy of the arrogant at the wicked’s prosperity—the temptation named.

Arminian note: envy is confessed as sin’s edge, not excused. Shared: prosperity of the wicked wounds faith; grace still calls envy what it is.

Reformed

Calvin: I envied the foolish when I saw the wicked prosper—temptation to join their way and doubt God’s ordering.

Reformed accent: shared—envy of the ungodly is perilous folly opposite the fear of God.

Key contrast Both name envy of the arrogant as the slip’s cause. Arminians typically stress the free turning from envy back toward God; Reformed readers typically stress how close even the godly come without God’s upholding hand.

Psalms 73:4

4For they have no pangs until death; their bodies are fat and sleek.

Arminian

Wesley: No bands—they are not dragged to death by sentence they deserve; strength firm.

Arminian pastoral: easy death and sleek bodies fuel the scandal. Shared realism—outward ease is not denied.

Reformed

Calvin: no bands to their death; strength vigorous—they seem exempt from the diseases that bind others to God’s yoke.

Reformed note: shared—God sometimes places the wicked on a stage of ease to try faith.

Psalms 73:5

5They are not in trouble as others are; they are not stricken like the rest of mankind.

Arminian

Wesley: Not in trouble as other (good) men frequently are; not plagued like others.

Arminian hearing: the comparison stings the righteous sufferer. Shared: exemption from common trouble is the temptation’s fuel.

Reformed

Calvin: not in the trouble common to man—special exemption that looks like privilege.

Reformed pastoral: shared—beholding despisers at ease while the godly labor is a sore trial.

Psalms 73:6

6Therefore pride is their necklace; violence covers them as a garment.

Arminian

Wesley: Pride as necklace; violence as garment—ornament and clothing of the prosperous wicked.

Arminian note: pride and violence worn openly. Shared moral clarity—prosperity without repentance hardens into display.

Reformed

Calvin: pride compasses as a chain; violence covers as raiment—they glory in audacity; pride is violence’s mother.

Reformed accent: shared—abuse of kindness meets divine forbearance that faith must interpret aright.

Psalms 73:7

7Their eyes swell out through fatness; their hearts overflow with follies.

Arminian

Wesley: Eyes swelling with fatness; hearts overflowing with follies—pampered excess.

Arminian pastoral: abundance without God breeds folly. Shared: fatness of eye images insolent prosperity.

Reformed

Calvin: eye going out for fatness; thoughts exceeding bounds—glutted prosperity projects pride.

Reformed note: shared diagnosis—ease intoxicates until imagination mounts above ordinary bounds.

Psalms 73:8

8They scoff and speak with malice; loftily they threaten oppression.

Arminian

Wesley: Speak—boasting of oppressions; loftily threatening.

Arminian hearing: malice spoken from height. Shared: oppression is not quiet; it advertises itself.

Reformed

Calvin: insolent talk of extortion; they speak from on high as if little gods.

Reformed pastoral: shared—unbridled speech reveals hearts that forget they are men.

Psalms 73:9

9They set their mouths against the heavens, and their tongue struts through the earth.

Arminian

Wesley: Mouth against the heavens—blaspheming God and deriding providence; tongue walking through the earth—liberty to reproach all.

Arminian note: heaven and earth both targeted. Shared: strutting tongue is rebellion, not mere manners.

Reformed

Calvin: mouth set against the heavens; tongue walking through the earth—contumely against God and men.

Reformed accent: shared—presumption imagines heaven and earth subject to it.

Psalms 73:10

10Therefore his people turn back to them, and find no fault in them.

Arminian

Wesley: Turn—to the wicked company; Waters—full cup of prosperity wrung out as if all blessings poured on them.

Arminian pastoral: people drawn toward ease’s cup. Shared grief—prosperity can pull even the visible people aside.

Reformed

Calvin: his people return hither; waters of a full cup wrung out—many swayed by the temptation, vexed when religion seems to yield no advantage.

Reformed note: shared—the scandal of ease can shipwreck or shake those counted among God’s people.

Psalms 73:11

11And they say, "How can God know? Is there knowledge in the Most High?"

Arminian

Wesley: The wicked (and the tempted) ask whether God knows—knowledge in the Most High doubted.

Arminian hearing: atheism of practice voiced as question. Shared: ‘How can God know?’ is the heart’s dark suggestion under envy.

Reformed

Calvin: How doth God know?—even believers can be shaken to doubt providence when disorder dazzles; they must disburden into God’s bosom.

Reformed accent: shared—not only the profane deny care; true believers are tempted unless wonderfully preserved.

Psalms 73:12

12Behold, these are the wicked; always at ease, they increase in riches.

Arminian

Wesley: Behold—psalmist summing up: ungodly prosper, increase in riches.

Arminian pastoral: the summary presses the ‘why’. Shared: ease and increasing wealth of the wicked is stated without varnish.

Reformed

Calvin: Behold these ungodly—yet at ease forever, heaping riches; long duration wears the righteous’ patience.

Reformed note: shared pictorial envy—wicked cherished while the upright are scourged.

Psalms 73:13

13All in vain have I kept my heart clean and washed my hands in innocence.

Arminian

Wesley: In vain—tempted to think religion unprofitable; clean heart and washed hands describe true religion’s parts.

Arminian note: cleansing heart and hands is real piety felt as wasted. Shared: the temptation is not against ritual only but against sincere holiness.

Reformed

Calvin: purified heart and washed hands in vain—daily scourge makes innocence seem unrewarded.

Reformed pastoral: shared—purity of heart joined to upright conduct; the complaint is the trial’s language, not final dogma.

Psalms 73:14

14For all the day long I have been stricken and rebuked every morning.

Arminian

Wesley: Stricken all day; rebuked every morning—continual chastening while the wicked rest.

Arminian pastoral: morning-by-morning rebuke vs. their ease. Shared solidarity with sufferers who keep integrity under stripe.

Reformed

Calvin: scourged daily; chastisement every morning—no end of calamity for the upright in his feeling.

Reformed accent: shared—the contrast with v. 12 fuels the crisis that sanctuary will answer.

Psalms 73:15

15If I had said, "I will speak thus," I would have betrayed the generation of your children.

Arminian

Wesley: Offend—speaking thus would grieve, discourage, and tempt God’s children to revolt.

Arminian hearing: love for the generation restrains bitter speech. Shared: envy’s conclusion must not be preached to the children.

Reformed

Calvin: If I speak thus I have transgressed—behold, the generation of thy children remains; God still reserves a people.

Reformed note: shared bridle—doubts are not published as settled truth against the church.

Psalms 73:16

16But when I thought how to understand this, it seemed to me a wearisome task,

Arminian

Wesley: To know—to find out the reason of this providence; too painful / wearisome.

Arminian pastoral: honest weariness before clarity. Shared: unaided reasoning cannot settle the scandal of prosperity.

Reformed

Calvin: applied mind to know—yet trouble/unprofitable labor until the sanctuaries; carnal reason sinks under the confusion.

Reformed accent: shared—natural understanding fails; illumination must come from elsewhere than ourselves.

Psalms 73:17

17until I went into the sanctuary of God; then I discerned their end.

Arminian

Wesley: Until—consulted the word of God; sanctuary and oracle in hard cases. Their end—prosperity short.

Arminian hope: entering God’s presence and Word turns envy to discernment. Shared hinge—sanctuary clarity, not private cleverness, ends the wearisome task.

Reformed

Calvin: until I entered the sanctuaries—school of God (Word and Spirit). End = God’s judgments delayed yet sure, not mere exit from life.

Reformed pastoral: shared—eyes must be opened; Word given due authority; last day may still await visible punishment.

Key contrast Both locate the turn in the sanctuary. Arminians typically stress freely entering Word and ordinance for clarifying light; Reformed readers typically stress that God must school us—without his Word and Spirit, reason stays blind to the wicked’s true end.

Psalms 73:18

18Truly you set them in slippery places; you make them fall to ruin.

Arminian

Wesley: Slippery places; cast down to ruin—footing of the prosperous is ice.

Arminian note: after sanctuary, judgment’s footing is seen. Shared: ease was never solid ground.

Reformed

Calvin: set in slippery places; cast into destruction—short lift so the fall is heavier; secret providence manages what looked random.

Reformed accent: shared watchtower view—faith now sees the ice under the wicked’s dance.

Psalms 73:19

19How they are destroyed in a moment, swept away utterly by terrors!

Arminian

Wesley: Terrors—God’s dreadful judgments unexpectedly seizing; desolation in a moment.

Arminian pastoral: sudden consumption awakens wonder. Shared: moment-destruction answers ‘forever’ ease.

Reformed

Calvin: destroyed in a moment; consumed with terrors—suddenness corrects our dullness about divine judgment.

Reformed note: shared—unlooked-for ruin teaches what prosperity hid.

Psalms 73:20

20Like a dream when one awakes, O Lord, when you rouse yourself, you despise them as phantoms.

Arminian

Wesley: Dream on waking; despise their image—felicity a shadow, raised to shame.

Arminian hearing: glory exposed as phantom. Shared: waking under God ends the pleasant dream of vain life.

Reformed

Calvin: dream after awaking; God makes their image despised—outward show is appearance, not substance.

Reformed pastoral: shared—illuminated minds are less dazzled; phantoms vanish when God rouses.

Psalms 73:21

21When my soul was embittered, when I was pricked in heart,

Arminian

Wesley: Pricked—deeply wounded with disquieting thoughts; heart grieved.

Arminian pastoral: confession of embitterment without excuse. Shared: envy left a wound God heals by truth.

Reformed

Calvin: heart in a ferment; pierced in reins—soured envy and emulation against God’s ways.

Reformed accent: shared—believers’ disquiet is sharper because they know God is Judge.

Psalms 73:22

22I was brutish and ignorant; I was like a beast toward you.

Arminian

Wesley: Foolish and ignorant; as a beast before God—self-rebuke for brutish reasoning.

Arminian note: honesty about folly opens the door to ‘nevertheless.’ Shared: envy made the psalmist beastlike in judgment.

Reformed

Calvin: foolish, ignorant, as a brute with thee—perverse envy from ignorance; blame on himself, not on providence.

Reformed pastoral: shared—when we quarrel with providence, our understanding is the perverse part.

Psalms 73:23

23Nevertheless, I am continually with you; you hold my right hand.

Arminian

Wesley: Nevertheless—though just cause to cast off, kindness continued; held—that faith might not fail.

Arminian hope: held right hand = sustaining care through the almost-slip. Shared miracle—continual with-ness after beastly folly.

Reformed

Calvin: nevertheless continually with thee; held my right hand—secret grace kept him from open blasphemy and total apostasy.

Reformed accent: shared—God’s fatherly eye and hand uphold when we barely know it.

Psalms 73:24

24You guide me with your counsel, and afterward you will receive me to glory.

Arminian

Wesley: Guide with counsel; afterward receive to glory—present leading and final reception.

Arminian pastoral: counsel now, glory afterward—path of trust. Shared: guidance is personal and forward-looking.

Reformed

Calvin: guide with counsel; at length receive into glory—future confidence after restoration; glory spans earthly foretaste to heaven.

Reformed note: shared—wisdom from God, not self-reliance, and grace unto glory.

Psalms 73:25

25Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you.

Arminian

Wesley: Heaven and earth—none desired besides God; exclusive satisfaction.

Arminian hearing: desire recentered after envy of riches. Shared: God alone fills what prosperity falsely promised.

Reformed

Calvin: whom in heaven but thee; none on earth besides—satisfied with God alone; unlawful to desire another object beside him.

Reformed pastoral: shared—sanctuary profit is exclusive cleaving; creatures must not share the throne of desire.

Psalms 73:26

26My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.

Arminian

Wesley: Flesh and heart failing; God strength and portion forever—rock when self collapses.

Arminian pastoral: portion forever outlasts sleek bodies of vv. 4–12. Shared climax—God himself is the lot of the godly.

Reformed

Calvin: flesh and heart failed; God strength of heart and portion forever—nothingness without God; portion = abundant sufficiency and perfection of happiness.

Reformed accent: shared—cast wholly on God when powers decay; portion language excludes rival trusts.

Key contrast Both confess God as strength and forever portion when flesh fails. Arminians typically stress choosing God as portion over worldly ease; Reformed readers typically stress that only the God who holds and portions us can be our happiness—self has no sufficiency.

Psalms 73:27

27For behold, those who are far from you shall perish; you put an end to everyone who is unfaithful to you.

Arminian

Wesley: Far from thee perish; go a whoring—revolt to wickedness, spiritual whoredom.

Arminian note: distance and unfaithfulness meet an end. Shared: nearness vs. farness is the moral geography of the psalm’s close.

Reformed

Calvin: who depart perish; destroyed those who go a whoring—scattering hope among many objects; spiritual adultery vs. exclusive faith.

Reformed pastoral: shared—apostates who will not fix on God alone are named adulterers in heart.

Psalms 73:28

28But for me it is good to be near God; I have made the Lord GOD my refuge, that I may tell of all your works.

Arminian

Wesley: Good to be near God; Lord GOD as refuge; telling his works—nearness ends envy’s story in praise.

Arminian hope: drawing near is good and freely claimed as refuge for witness. Shared telos—nearness, not riches, is the good; testimony follows trust.

Reformed

Calvin: good to draw near; trust in the Lord Jehovah to recount his works—steadfast communion even if all others wander; confidence fixed so the hand may hold.

Reformed accent: shared—gather affections from evil examples; hope in him never wants matter for praise.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on envy at the wicked’s prosperity, the sanctuary where their end is discerned, God as portion, and the good of nearness to God—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 73.

Arminian Notable voices

“It is probable Asaph was the author. He begins with a general principle, confesses his temptation to envy the wicked, then tells how he gained the victory and advantage by it. Clean heart—true Israelites who love God and serve in spirit and truth. Feet almost gone—faith in providence nearly overthrown. In vain—tempted to think religion unprofitable; true religion is cleansing of heart and hands. Until—till he consulted the word of God; in hard cases they resorted to the sanctuary and the oracle therein. Their end—there he learned their prosperity was short. Nevertheless—though he gave just cause to cast him off, kindness continued; held—that faith might not fail. Far from thee perish; go a whoring—those who revolt to wickedness. It is good to draw near to God.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 73)

Reformed Notable voices

“The psalmist, contending against carnal sense, extols God’s goodness, then confesses that the wicked’s wealth and the righteous’ troubles almost disposed him to cast off the fear of God. He reproves his folly in judging from the present only, and shows that if we leave providence to God’s secret purpose, the righteous are not defrauded nor the wicked escape. Until he entered the sanctuaries—the school of God in Word and Spirit—he could not discern their end: not mere death, but judgments delayed yet sure. God holds his right hand; counsel guides; afterward glory. Whom have I in heaven but thee? Flesh and heart fail, but God is strength and portion forever. It is good to draw near to God and trust in the Lord Jehovah, that we may recount all his works.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 73)
