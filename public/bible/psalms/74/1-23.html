Passage

Psalms 74

Verses 1–23 · ESV · Voices

A Maskil of Asaph. The sanctuary is smashed: why cast off forever? Enemies roar in the meeting place, hack carved work, burn the dwelling, and burn every meeting place of God in the land. No signs, no prophet. Yet God is King from of old who split sea and crushed Leviathan; he is asked to remember the covenant, not forget the life of the poor, and rise against foolish taunt.

Psalms 74:1

1O God, why do you cast us off forever? Why does your anger smoke against the sheep of your pasture?

Arminian

Wesley: Smoke—be angry; Sheep—whom thou wast wont to guard. Forever and smoking anger feel total.

Arminian pastoral: the question is prayer, not atheism. Sheep still address the Shepherd; grace invites the why without licensing despair’s final word.

Reformed

Calvin: faithful complain God has cast off his people—anger like smoke against the flock. The forever is the wound’s language.

Reformed accent: shared—cast-off dread is brought to God. Sheep of pasture remain covenant identity under wrath’s feel.

Psalms 74:2

2Remember your congregation, which you have purchased of old, which you have redeemed to be the tribe of your heritage! Remember Mount Zion, where you have dwelt.

Arminian

Wesley: Remember relation; Heritage—chosen; Purchased by mighty hand; Mount Zion—special presence.

Arminian note: purchase, redeem, heritage, Zion—history is the plea. Shared: God is asked to remember what he did, while the people still cry remember.

Reformed

Calvin: remember the congregation purchased of old, redeemed as heritage, Zion where he dwelt. Covenant memory is faith’s argument.

Reformed pastoral: shared—purchase and dwelling are God’s acts; merit is not the ground.

Psalms 74:3

3Direct your steps to the perpetual ruins; the enemy has destroyed everything in the sanctuary!

Arminian

Wesley: lift/steps toward perpetual ruins; enemy destroyed all in the sanctuary.

Arminian hearing: ruins are ‘perpetual’ to the eye—prayer asks God to walk the wreck. Shared seriousness about sacred space violated.

Reformed

Calvin: perpetual desolations—the enemy has destroyed everything in the sanctuary. God is begged to inspect the ruin.

Reformed note: shared—sanctuary wreck is not minimized.

Psalms 74:4

4Your foes have roared in the midst of your meeting place; they set up their own signs for signs.

Arminian

Wesley: foes roar in the meeting place; set their signs for signs—pagan markers replace God’s.

Arminian pastoral: shared—sacrilege is loud. Roaring in God’s assembly is blasphemy both camps name as such.

Reformed

Calvin: enemies roar in the assemblies; set up their ensigns for signs—profane triumph inside holy space.

Reformed accent: shared outrage at signs displacing God’s.

Psalms 74:5

5They were like those who swing axes in a forest of trees.

Arminian

Wesley: Lift up—destroy as men lift ax to hew trees. Temple beauty treated as timber.

Arminian note: violence is workmanlike and pitiless. Shared: carved worship space meets lumberjacks.

Reformed

Calvin: ax-men in a thicket—systematic hacking of holy woodwork.

Reformed pastoral: shared—destruction is deliberate craft of hate.

Psalms 74:6

6And all its carved work they broke down with hatchets and hammers.

Arminian

Wesley: Carved work—beautiful ornaments of the temple—broken with hatchets and hammers.

Arminian hearing: art offered to God is smashed. Shared grief—beauty dedicated is not spared by the foe.

Reformed

Calvin: carved work broken down—nothing ornate left. Desecration completes robbery.

Reformed note: shared—hammers preach contempt for God’s house.

Psalms 74:7

7They set your sanctuary on fire; they profaned the dwelling place of your name, bringing it down to the ground.

Arminian

Wesley: sanctuary fired; dwelling of the Name profaned to the ground.

Arminian pastoral: Name’s dwelling burned—shared horror. Prayer after arson still addresses the Name.

Reformed

Calvin: sanctuary set on fire; dwelling place of the name cast to the ground. Profanation is total in appearance.

Reformed accent: shared—fire against the Name’s house is assault on God.

Psalms 74:8

8They said to themselves, “We will utterly subdue them”; they burned all the meeting places of God in the land.

Arminian

Wesley: Sanctuaries—temple and synagogues burned across the land. Utter subdue is the foe’s intent.

Arminian note: shared—every meeting place targeted. Corporate worship’s erasure is the enemy’s strategy.

Reformed

Calvin: burn all meeting places of God in the land—sweeping policy against worship.

Reformed pastoral: shared—subdue-them ideology meets the God who still hears.

Psalms 74:9

9We do not see our signs; there is no longer any prophet, and there is none among us who knows how long.

Arminian

Wesley: Signs—tokens of presence; Prophet—to tell how long; How long—desolation’s duration unknown.

Arminian pastoral: absence of sign and prophet is confessed honestly. Shared: not knowing how long still leaves room to ask the King of v. 12.

Reformed

Calvin: no signs; no prophet; none knows how long. Extreme desolation includes prophetic silence.

Reformed accent: shared—silence is part of the trial; faith will argue from old works next.

Psalms 74:10

10How long, O God, is the foe to scoff? Is the enemy to revile your name forever?

Arminian

Wesley: How long shall desolation and scoff continue? Name reviled forever is intolerable.

Arminian hearing: how-long prayer resists forever-scoff. Shared: God’s name, not only Israel’s comfort, is at stake.

Reformed

Calvin: how long shall the adversary reproach and blaspheme the name forever? The cause is God’s honor.

Reformed note: shared—scoff against the Name fuels intercession.

Psalms 74:11

11Why do you hold back your hand, why do you keep your right hand in your chest?

Arminian

Wesley: why is the right hand withheld, kept in the bosom—power unused to the eye.

Arminian pastoral: bold complaint asks for action. Grace allows why without dethroning God.

Reformed

Calvin: why withdraw the hand—keep the right hand in the bosom? Faith pleads for the arm once known in Exodus deeds.

Reformed accent: shared—anthropomorphism of withheld hand is prayer, not dogma that God cannot act.

Psalms 74:12

12Yet God my King is from of old, working salvation in the midst of the earth.

Arminian

Wesley: King—thou hast been our King and wilt be; from of old, working salvation mid-earth.

Arminian hope: Yet turns the psalm. Shared confession—God is King from of old; salvation history is the ground of fresh asking.

Reformed

Calvin: yet God is my King of old, working salvation in the midst of the earth. Memory of kingship steadies complaint.

Reformed pastoral: shared—Yet God is the hinge; not yet we.

Psalms 74:13

13You divided the sea by your might; you broke the heads of the sea monsters on the waters.

Arminian

Wesley: Divided by power; Dragons—large fishes/crocodiles—heads broken.

Arminian note: exodus might recalled. Shared: creation-combat and Red Sea memory underwrite hope in ruin.

Reformed

Calvin: divided the sea; broke dragons’ heads on the waters—former salvation works.

Reformed accent: shared—might once shown is argued back to God.

Psalms 74:14

14You crushed the heads of Leviathan; you gave him as food for the creatures of the wilderness.

Arminian

Wesley: Leviathan—whale or great fish; Meat—food (for desert scavengers / people in the figure).

Arminian pastoral: crushed chaos-monster becomes food—total victory image. Shared: God, not Israel, crushed Leviathan.

Reformed

Calvin: Leviathan’s heads broken; given for meat in the wilderness—triumph complete.

Reformed note: shared mythic-scale victory language for the King’s past acts.

Psalms 74:15

15You split open springs and brooks; you dried up ever-flowing streams.

Arminian

Wesley: springs opened; ever-flowing streams dried—water mastery both ways.

Arminian hearing: shared—God commands water for salvation purposes (rock-streams; Jordan/Reed Sea).

Reformed

Calvin: cleave fountain and flood; dry up mighty rivers—sovereign hydrography of redemption.

Reformed pastoral: shared—creation’s water answers its King.

Psalms 74:16

16Yours is the day, yours also the night; you have established the heavenly lights and the sun.

Arminian

Wesley: Day—the sun; Night—moon and stars established. Time itself is God’s.

Arminian note: shared—day and night ownership rebukes the foe’s ‘forever’ scoff.

Reformed

Calvin: day and night are his; he prepared light and sun. Cosmic order is covenant God’s.

Reformed accent: shared—lights established by the same King who owns Zion’s ruin prayer.

Psalms 74:17

17You have fixed all the boundaries of the earth; you have made summer and winter.

Arminian

Wesley: boundaries fixed; summer and winter made. Seasons and borders are gifts.

Arminian pastoral: shared providence—geography and calendar confess the Creator while temples burn.

Reformed

Calvin: set all borders of earth; made summer and winter—fixed orders.

Reformed note: shared—ruin of sanctuary does not unmake the Maker of seasons.

Psalms 74:18

18Remember this, O LORD, how the enemy scoffs, and a foolish people reviles your name.

Arminian

Wesley: Fool—pretends wisdom, is fool; Name reviled. Remember the scoff.

Arminian hearing: foolish people = blasphemers. Shared: remember is plea for God’s honor.

Reformed

Calvin: remember the enemy’s reproach; foolish people blaspheme the name. Argument from God’s glory.

Reformed pastoral: shared—folly of reviling the Name.

Psalms 74:19

19Do not deliver the soul of your dove to the wild beasts; do not forget the life of your poor forever.

Arminian

Wesley: dove imagery—fragile congregation; poor’s life not forgotten forever.

Arminian pastoral: dove and poor need delivering remembrance. Shared tenderness—God’s poor are not beast-food by decree of the enemy.

Reformed

Calvin: deliver not the soul of thy turtledove; forget not the life of thy poor forever. Tender names in hard prayer.

Reformed accent: shared—dove/poor identity under threat.

Psalms 74:20

20Have regard for the covenant, for the dark places of the land are full of the habitations of violence.

Arminian

Wesley: Covenant with Abraham; Dark places—cruelty’s dens across the land.

Arminian note: covenant regard is the ask. Shared: violence’s habitations do not erase the covenant; they occasion pleading it.

Reformed

Calvin: have respect unto the covenant—dark places full of violence’s habitations. Faith argues covenant, not merit.

Reformed pastoral: shared—covenant is the legal mercy ground in desolation.

Key contrast Both plead the covenant amid land-wide violence. Arminians typically stress that covenant mercy must still be sought in prayer; Reformed readers typically stress that regard for the covenant rests on God’s faithfulness, not the people’s desert.

Psalms 74:21

21Let not the downtrodden turn back in shame! Let the poor and needy praise your name!

Arminian

Wesley: downtrodden not turned back ashamed; poor and needy praising the Name—outcome desired.

Arminian hope: shame reversed into praise. Shared: poor praise is the public answer to violence.

Reformed

Calvin: oppressed not return ashamed; poor and needy praise the name—doxology from the rescued.

Reformed note: shared telos—Name praised by the needy.

Psalms 74:22

22Arise, O God, defend your cause; remember how the foolish scoff at you all the day!

Arminian

Wesley: Arise—exert power; Cause—plead for us; Foolish—impious blasphemy all day.

Arminian pastoral: arise and defend your cause—God’s cause and the people’s overlap. Shared urgency.

Reformed

Calvin: arise, plead thine own cause; remember foolish reproach all day. God is asked to vindicate himself.

Reformed accent: shared—thine own cause centers the plea beyond private revenge.

Psalms 74:23

23Do not forget the clamor of your foes, the uproar of those who rise up against you, which goes up continually!

Arminian

Wesley: continual uproar of foes rising against God—do not forget the noise.

Arminian note: clamor goes up continually; prayer asks God not to ignore it. Shared: enemies against God, not only against Asaph.

Reformed

Calvin: forget not the voice of adversaries; tumult of those who rise against God ascends continually. Closing pressure on divine remembrance.

Reformed pastoral: shared—continual uproar meets the King from of old (v. 12).

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on the ruined sanctuary, God as King from of old who crushed the sea-monsters, and the plea to remember the covenant—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 74.

Arminian Notable voices

“Smoke—That is, be angry. Sheep—whom thou wast wont to guard and feed as a shepherd. Remember—consider thy relation to them. Heritage—whom thou didst choose and set apart for thyself. Rod—the tribe of Judah, from which the sceptre was not to depart. Purchased—by a mighty hand. Congregation—the people of Israel. Mount Zion—the place of thy special presence. Lift up—destroy them, as men lift up an ax to hew down trees. Carved work—the beautiful works and ornaments of the temple. Sanctuaries—the temple and synagogues. Signs—tokens of God’s presence. Prophet—to tell us how long. How long—shall this desolation continue. King—thou hast been our King, and wilt be so still. Divided—by thy power. Dragons—large fishes or crocodiles. Leviathan—the whale or some other great fish. Meat—to be food for the people of Israel. Day—the sun. Night—the moon and stars. Covenant—made with Abraham. Dark places—of the land, where the enemies exercised their cruelty. Fool—who though he pretend to be wise, yet in truth is a fool. Cause—plead for us. Arise—exert thy power. Foolish—impious and blasphemous.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament (on Ps 74)

Reformed Notable voices

“The faithful complain that God has cast off his people, that the sanctuary is defiled and burned, that no signs or prophets remain… Yet they recall that God is their King of old, who divided the sea, broke the heads of the dragons, and gave Leviathan for meat… They plead the covenant, the oppression of the poor, and the reproach of the foolish enemy, praying that God would arise and plead his own cause. In extreme desolation faith still argues from God’s former works and from his covenant faithfulness, not from the people’s merit.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms (on Ps 74)
