Passage

Psalms 98

Verses 1–9 · ESV · Voices

A Psalm. Sing to the Lord a new song—he has done marvelous things; his right hand and holy arm have worked salvation. He has made known his salvation and revealed his righteousness to the nations; he has remembered steadfast love and faithfulness to Israel; all the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God. Make a joyful noise, all the earth; break forth in joyous song. Sing with lyre and melody; with trumpets and horn make a joyful noise before the King, the Lord. Let sea and world roar; rivers clap; hills sing for joy together before the Lord, for he comes to judge the earth—he will judge the world with righteousness and the peoples with equity.

Psalms 98:1

1Oh sing to the Lord a new song, for he has done marvelous things! His right hand and his holy arm have worked salvation for him.

Arminian

Sing a new song—marvelous things; right hand and holy arm worked salvation. Shared: new song; marvelous deeds; God's own arm.

Arminian pastoral: marvels justify newness. Salvation by his arm keeps human boasting out.

Reformed

Calvin: new song for marvelous things; his own right hand and holy arm got the victory—not by ordinary means; God himself author of singular salvation. Isaiah 59:16 echoes the arm alone.

Reformed pastoral: shared—new song; marvelous; holy arm. Extraordinary mercy demands extraordinary praise.

Psalms 98:2

2The Lord has made known his salvation; he has revealed his righteousness in the sight of the nations.

Arminian

The Lord has made known his salvation; revealed his righteousness in the sight of the nations. Wesley: Salvation—world's redemption by the Messiah, long a secret among the Jews; Righteousness—faithfulness in keeping this great promise. Shared: publicly known salvation; righteousness before nations.

Arminian pastoral: what was reserved is now shown. Nations see faithfulness kept in Messianic redemption.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah made known his salvation; revealed righteousness in the heathen's sight—open publication of saving righteousness beyond Israel.

Reformed pastoral: shared—made known; revealed to nations. Hidden mercy becomes international news.

Key contrast Both celebrate salvation made known and righteousness revealed to the nations. Arminians, with Wesley, typically stress Messianic redemption once largely reserved among Israel and now shown in promise-keeping faithfulness. Reformed readers, with Calvin, typically stress God's own arm publishing saving righteousness openly before the heathen. Shared: public gospel; nations see.

Psalms 98:3

3He has remembered his steadfast love and faithfulness to the house of Israel. All the ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God.

Arminian

He remembered steadfast love and faithfulness to Israel; all ends of the earth have seen our God's salvation. Shared: covenant memory; earth-wide sight.

Arminian pastoral: Israel remembered; earth sees. Particular love becomes universal vision.

Reformed

Calvin: he remembered goodness and truth toward Israel; all ends of the earth seen the salvation of our God—covenant fidelity overflowed to the world's edges.

Reformed pastoral: shared—remembered love and faithfulness; ends of the earth see. Zion's mercy is not Zion's private stash forever.

Psalms 98:4

4Make a joyful noise to the Lord, all the earth; break forth into joyous song and sing praises!

Arminian

Joyful noise, all the earth; break forth into joyous song and sing praises. Shared: all-earth noise; break forth; praises.

Arminian pastoral: restraint yields to outbreak. All the earth is the choir roll.

Reformed

Calvin: exult before Jehovah, all the earth; make a loud noise, rejoice, sing praise—volume matches marvel.

Reformed pastoral: shared—joyful noise; all the earth; break forth. Quiet gratitude is good; this verse asks more.

Psalms 98:5

5Sing praises to the Lord with the lyre, with the lyre and the sound of melody!

Arminian

Sing praises with the lyre, with lyre and sound of melody. Shared: lyre; melody.

Arminian pastoral: instruments serve the Name. Melody is theology sung.

Reformed

Calvin: sing to Jehovah with the harp, with harp and voice of a psalm—ordered music under the King.

Reformed pastoral: shared—lyre; melody. Crafted praise answers crafted salvation (v. 1).

Psalms 98:6

6With trumpets and the sound of the horn make a joyful noise before the King, the Lord!

Arminian

With trumpets and horn make a joyful noise before the King, the Lord. Shared: brass before the King.

Arminian pastoral: King titles the Lord. Noise is court ceremony for the true Sovereign.

Reformed

Calvin: with trumpets and sound of cornet sing before Jehovah the King—royal acclaim.

Reformed pastoral: shared—trumpets; horn; before the King. Worship is audience with reigning majesty.

Psalms 98:7

7Let the sea roar, and all that fills it; the world and those who dwell in it!

Arminian

Let the sea roar and its fullness; the world and its dwellers. Shared: sea and inhabited world.

Arminian pastoral: roar is invited praise. Dwellers and depths share one summons.

Reformed

Calvin: let the sea roar and its fullness; the world and those who dwell therein—creation and population together.

Reformed pastoral: shared—sea roar; world and dwellers. No province of earth is mute by permission.

Psalms 98:8

8Let the rivers clap their hands; let the hills sing for joy together

Arminian

Let rivers clap; hills sing for joy together. Shared: clapping rivers; singing hills.

Arminian pastoral: metaphor teaches gladness of the order. Nature rehearses the verdict's goodness.

Reformed

Calvin: floods clap hands; hills joyful together—figures of united celebration.

Reformed pastoral: shared—rivers clap; hills sing together. Equity's approach (v. 9) makes landscape cheer.

Psalms 98:9

9before the Lord, for he comes to judge the earth. He will judge the world with righteousness, and the peoples with equity.

Arminian

Before the Lord—he comes to judge the earth; world with righteousness, peoples with equity. Shared: coming Judge; righteousness; equity.

Arminian pastoral: judgment is the song's why. Equity means fair dealing for peoples—hope, not dread only, for those who love his reign.

Reformed

Calvin: before Jehovah—he comes to judge the earth with righteousness and equity. Coming judgment is creation's cue to rejoice.

Reformed pastoral: shared—comes to judge; righteousness; equity. Closing note matches Psalm 96:13's faithful judgment.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on a new song for marvelous salvation by God's own arm, righteousness shown to the nations, covenant love remembered, earth's joyful noise before the King, and creation clapping as he comes to judge with equity—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 98:1–9.

Arminian Notable voices

“Salvation—the redemption of the world by the Messiah, long kept as a secret among the Jews, and not thoroughly known even by most of them. Righteousness—his faithfulness in accomplishing this great promise.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 98

Reformed Notable voices

“Sing to Jehovah a new song—for he has done marvelous things; his right hand and arm of holiness have gotten him the victory. Jehovah has made known his salvation; revealed his righteousness in the sight of the heathen. He has remembered his goodness and truth toward the house of Israel; all ends of the earth have seen the salvation of our God. New song marks extraordinary, not common, praise—God procured salvation not by ordinary means but by his own arm, himself the author of a singular deliverance. Exult, all the earth; sing with harp and voice of psalm; trumpets and cornet before Jehovah the King. Let sea roar and its fullness; world and dwellers; floods clap hands; hills joyful together before Jehovah—for he comes to judge the earth with righteousness and equity.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 98
