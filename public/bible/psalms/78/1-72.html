Passage

Psalms 78

Verses 1–72 · WEB · Voices

A Maskil of Asaph. Give ear to teaching and dark sayings of old: fathers must tell the coming generation the LORD's glorious deeds, that children set their hope in God and not be a stubborn generation. Ephraim turned back in battle; Israel forgot wonders in Egypt and Zoan—sea, cloud, rock-water—yet rebelled in the desert, tested God for craving, and didn’t trust in his saving power. Manna and quail, wrath on the strong, flattery after slaughter, compassion that atoned and restrained anger: still they rebelled and limited the Holy One. He struck Egypt's firstborn, led his flock to the holy land, then forsook Shiloh when high places provoked him; power and glory went captive until he shamed the foe. He rejected Joseph's tent, chose Judah and Mount Zion which he loved, built his sanctuary, and chose David from the sheepfolds to shepherd Israel with upright heart and skillful hand.

Psalms 78:1

1Hear my teaching, my people. Turn your ears to the words of my mouth.

Arminian

Hear my teaching, my people, to my teaching. Wesley: My law—the doctrine about to be delivered. The maskil opens as catechesis for the covenant people.

Shared—teaching the next generation begins with hearing. Incline the ear; the narrative of mercies and sins that follows is meant to form hope and obedience.

Reformed

Calvin: the writer addresses the Jews as teacher, calling attention because even true believers are often backward to receive instruction. Torah here is instruction—words of his mouth.

Shared—solemn call to hear. The majesty of what follows should stir sluggish disciples; the gospel likewise demands earnest attention.

Psalms 78:2

2I will open my mouth in a parable. I will utter dark sayings of old,

Arminian

I will open my mouth in a parable; utter dark sayings of old. Wesley: Parable—weighty sentences; Dark sayings—not hard words but prodigious things: God's goodness, Israel's ingratitude, stupid insensibleness under such teaching.

Shared—ancient history pressed as parable. The darkness is moral wonder, not genre fog.

Reformed

Calvin: mashal denotes grave striking sentences; chidoth pointed sayings worthy of notice—not ambiguous wrap. Design: stimulate hearers to weigh God's benefits and the people's ingratitude.

Shared—weighty history, not riddle for its own sake. Matthew 13 applies the pattern to Christ teaching with prophetic majesty.

Psalms 78:3

3which we have heard and known, and our fathers have told us.

Arminian

Things heard and known, and our fathers told us. Shared transmission: domestic memory of God's works.

Wesley is silent here; the plain sense holds—fathers tell, children know. Faith receives public history, not private speculation.

Reformed

Calvin: common subject transmitted father to son, yet illustrated with majesty. Heard and known—not fables; fathers are the chosen people entrusted with divine truth.

Shared—apostolic chain of memory. Sublime truth reaches even babes who are teachable.

Psalms 78:4

4We will not hide them from their children, telling to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, his strength, and his wondrous deeds that he has done.

Arminian

We won’t hide them from their children; we will tell the generation to come the praises of the LORD, his strength, and his wondrous works. Praise published to the next age.

Teaching is refusal to hide. Wesley’s tradition hears sincere duty to declare deeds so hope may be set (v. 7).

Reformed

Calvin: we will not conceal from posterity what ancestors taught—truth published age to age. End: celebrate Jehovah's praises, power, and wonderful works.

Shared—no concealment. Church and household shoulder transmission so praise does not die.

Psalms 78:5

5For he established a covenant in Jacob, and appointed a teaching in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children;

Arminian

He established a covenant in Jacob, and appointed a teaching in Israel, commanded fathers to teach children. Wesley: Established—chief of mercies; testimony—law as witness of duties and blessings.

Shared—law and testimony are mercy first. Fathers teach so children may answer God.

Reformed

Calvin: God is author; fathers instruct by commandment, not mere impulse. Statute and law seal grace so knowledge of free adoption is not obliterated.

Shared—commanded pedagogy. Written law ratifies the covenant people as gathered for holy obedience.

Psalms 78:6

6that the generation to come might know, even the children who should be born; who should arise and tell their children,

Arminian

That the generation to come might know, children yet unborn arise and tell their children.

Wesley silent; the aim is knowing that spreads. Teach so the unborn may teach.

Reformed

Calvin: law given not for one age; fathers transmit as inheritance that it never be lost. Each father diligently instructs; preachers of truth should be as numerous as those born.

Shared—continual succession of teaching. Church as pillar serves this transmission.

Psalms 78:7

7that they might set their hope in God, and not forget God’s deeds, but keep his commandments,

Arminian

So they might set their hope in God, not forget his works, but keep his commandments. Shared triad: hope, memory, obedience.

Hope first—then remembrance that fuels keeping. Wesleyan Arminians urge trust that issues in kept commands.

Reformed

Calvin: trust first; remembrance of benefits confirms faith; then devoted obedience. True service begins with faith; distrust breeds murmuring.

Shared—hope in God, not forget, keep. Heavenly wisdom: hearts fixed on God, then holy obedience.

Psalms 78:8

8and might not be as their fathers— a stubborn and rebellious generation, a generation that didn’t make their hearts loyal, whose spirit was not steadfast with God.

Arminian

Not like their fathers—stubborn and rebellious, heart not steadfast, spirit not faithful. Shared warning against ancestral pattern.

The generation is blamed for unset heart and unfaithful spirit. Arminians hear culpability and a sincere call not to imitate—without making the Spirit's converting work unnecessary.

Reformed

Calvin: Jews no better than fathers—treacherous, rebellious, crooked. Special work of God to turn hearts by the Spirit; yet men are not exempt from blame when lust draws them away. Papist inference of autonomous heart-bending does not stand.

Shared—do not copy the fathers' revolt. Blame and need of Spirit stand together.

Key contrast Both confess a stubborn generation whose heart was not steadfast and spirit not faithful. Arminians stress culpable refusal and the call to a different obedience. Reformed readers commonly follow Calvin: God alone turns the heart by the Spirit, yet the stubborn remain blameworthy—no autonomous free-will proof from the charge.

Psalms 78:9

9The children of Ephraim, being armed and carrying bows, turned back in the day of battle.

Arminian

Ephraimites armed and carrying bows, turned back in the day of battle. Wesley: Ephraim put for all Israel; may refer to 1 Sam 4; bows for all arms; discourse of causes runs to Shiloh's fall and move to Judah/Zion.

Shared—armed yet fleeing.

Reformed

Calvin: example of unfaithfulness; skillful archers fleeing proves divine displeasure and effeminate hearts. Ephraim named as leaders into revolt (calves), warning against empty church title without holy fruit.

Shared—bow and flight. Deprived of God's aid, martial pride collapses.

Psalms 78:10

10They didn’t keep God’s covenant, and refused to walk in his law.

Arminian

They didn’t keep God’s covenant, refused to walk in his law.

Refusal is willful. Arminian interpreters hear rejection of known law.

Reformed

Calvin: reason aid was withheld—perfidy despising law and covenant. Covenant first (trust in promises), then law; refuse implies deliberate obstinacy, not mere giddiness.

Shared—covenant not kept. Unbelief toward promises underwrites the walk of refusal.

Psalms 78:11

11They forgot his doings, his wondrous deeds that he had shown them.

Arminian

They forgot his doings, his wondrous deeds he had shown them. Both hear: forgetfulness of wonders.

Wesley silent; ingratitude is the hinge. Remembering wonders should restrain sin.

Reformed

Calvin: shameful impiety from ingratitude—wickedly buried deliverances. Monstrous stupidity; they shut eyes lest God's glory restrain them.

Shared—forgot works and wonders. Memory of grace is a means God uses to keep a people.

Psalms 78:12

12He did marvelous things in the sight of their fathers, in the land of Egypt, in the field of Zoan.

Arminian

Wonders in Egypt, in the field of Zoan, in fathers' sight. Wesley: Field—territory; Zoan—ancient eminent Egyptian city.

Shared—public miracles before the fathers. Zoan names the scene; history, not myth.

Reformed

Calvin: transition to first ancestors—whole race perverse from the origin. Miracles before departure; Zoan recalls the place.

Shared—wonders in Egypt/Zoan. Forgetting such signs aggravates later revolt.

Psalms 78:13

13He split the sea, and caused them to pass through. He made the waters stand as a heap.

Arminian

He divided the sea; waters stood like a heap.

Wesley silent; heap of waters is the LORD's act. Pass through as gift, not technique.

Reformed

Calvin: order of nature reversed—waters heaped. Passage and cloud (v. 14) are pledges toward the heavenly inheritance; Paul reads baptismal pattern (1 Cor 10).

Shared—divided sea, heap of waters. Deliverance that stretches toward everlasting salvation.

Psalms 78:14

14In the daytime he also led them with a cloud, and all night with a light of fire.

Arminian

Daytime cloud; night fiery light. Shared guidance.

Pillar leads continually. Trust the Guide in desert heat and dark.

Reformed

Calvin: after the sea, God still guides; cloud shades and pledges protection until the inheritance.

Shared—cloud and fire. Guidance is fatherly presence, not bare navigation.

Psalms 78:15

15He split rocks in the wilderness, and gave them drink abundantly as out of the depths.

Arminian

He split rocks; drink abundantly as out of the depths. Wesley: Wilderness—Rephidim and again Kadesh.

Shared—rock water in named places. Abundance from the dry rock.

Reformed

Calvin: not mere spring opened—water from profound deeps, as from the earth's center. Fatherly care in miracle.

Shared—clave the rock. Ordinary nature changed to supply the flock.

Psalms 78:16

16He brought streams also out of the rock, and caused waters to run down like rivers.

Arminian

Wesley: Streams miraculously followed even to Canaan's borders.

Both traditions see God’s ongoing supply here. Grace that travels with the camp.

Reformed

Calvin: where no drop was, a mighty river—abundance that silences cavil.

Shared—rivers from rock. Copious gift exposes later craving as lust, not need.

Psalms 78:17

17Yet they still went on to sin against him, to rebel against the Most High in the desert.

Arminian

Yet they still went on sinning against him, provoking the Most High in the wilderness. Wesley: Wilderness—where singular obligations to obedience; great aggravation.

Shared—desert sin against near mercies. Obligation intensifies guilt.

Reformed

Calvin: after succession of love, they rebelled as was natural to them. Provoke—heinous; place aggravates: fresh deliverance, tokens of presence, need itself should have restrained.

Shared—sinned still more in the desert. Mercies abused become witnesses against.

Psalms 78:18

18They tempted God in their heart by asking food according to their desire.

Arminian

They tempted God in their heart by asking food they craved. Wesley: Tempted—desired proof of power; Lust—not necessary subsistence but luxurious appetite.

Shared—heart-test by craving. Ask within God's limits; do not demand proof for lust.

Reformed

Calvin: tempting is overpassing bounds—not asking food when hungry, but loathing manna and craving for the soul/lust. Separate power from will and you rend God.

Shared—tested in heart by craving. Impose restraint; unchecked flesh murmurs for more than lawful.

Psalms 78:19

19Yes, they spoke against God. They said, “Can God prepare a table in the wilderness?

Arminian

They spoke against God: Can God prepare a table in the wilderness? Shared open blasphemy of doubt.

Mouth publishes the heart's test. Classical Arminians hear sincere invitation still to trust the table-God.

Reformed

Calvin: not ashamed to utter impiety conceived within; malignity possessed the heart. Table means dainty Egypt-fare—abundance and variety demanded.

Shared—speech against God. Lust develops from consent into public question of God's power.

Psalms 78:20

20Behold, he struck the rock, so that waters gushed out, and streams overflowed. Can he give bread also? Will he provide meat for his people?”

Arminian

He struck the rock—water gushed; can he give bread or meat? Shared irony of unbelief after water.

Wesley silent; water miracle should have silenced the sneer. Faith argues from gift to Giver.

Reformed

Calvin: bitter irony—reciting as from their mouth what stood before their eyes. Unbelief grants the rock yet denies bread and flesh.

Shared—water conceded, bread doubted. Past mercy does not automatically produce trust.

Psalms 78:21

21Therefore the LORD heard, and was angry. A fire was kindled against Jacob, anger also went up against Israel,

Arminian

The LORD heard; wrath, fire against Jacob, anger against Israel.

Hearing is full knowledge before judgment. Anger rises against covenant names Jacob/Israel.

Reformed

Calvin: hearing implies perfect knowledge—figure from earthly judges; God does not punish unacquainted. Wrath answered unbelief (v. 22).

Shared—LORD heard and was wroth. Fire in Jacob is holy response to distrust.

Psalms 78:22

22because they didn’t believe in God, and didn’t trust in his salvation.

Arminian

Because they didn’t believe in God, and didn’t trust in his saving power. Wesley: Trusted not—that he could and would save from feared famine.

Shared—unbelief and distrust of salvation. Classical Arminians urge trust in saving power sincerely offered in the narrative of care.

Reformed

Calvin: enormity—believed not, trusted not in his salvation. Faith hopes every blessing from God and yields obedience; distrust murmurs and rebels.

Shared—no belief, no trust in saving power. Faith is root of piety; pretenses without hope in God's salvation are empty.

Psalms 78:23

23Yet he commanded the skies above, and opened the doors of heaven.

Arminian

Yet he commanded the skies and opened the doors of heaven. Wesley: Heaven—storehouse whose doors God shuts or opens.

Shared—sky under command. Doors open at his word despite unbelief.

Reformed

Calvin: not bare history—censure: though fed with manna they still lusted. Heaven's doors magnify the gift they scorned.

For the reader: Shared—commanded skies. Fatherly storehouse opened; later flesh-gift comes in wrath.

Psalms 78:24

24He rained down manna on them to eat, and gave them food from the sky.

Arminian

Rained manna; grain of heaven. Shared corn from above.

Manna as gift to eat. Receive heavenly grain with thanks, not loathing.

Reformed

Calvin: corn of heaven—ingratitude twofold if they despise common food and angels' bread. Verbs recall past mercy against present lust.

Shared—manna rained. Spiritual meat in Paul's sense points beyond the wilderness loaf.

Psalms 78:25

25Man ate the bread of angels. He sent them food to the full.

Arminian

Man ate bread of the angels; food in abundance. Wesley: Angels' food—manna by ministry of angels.

Both traditions see abundance in the bread of angels. Full supply removes excuse for craving Egypt.

Reformed

Calvin: bread of the mighty/angels—high celebration to make impiety more detestable. Sent to the full.

Shared—angels' bread, abundance. Scorning such provision is monstrous.

Psalms 78:26

26He caused the east wind to blow in the sky. By his power he guided the south wind.

Arminian

East wind; by his power the south wind. Wesley: First eastern, then southern wind.

Shared—winds as servants. Power leads the weather for judgment-gift.

Reformed

Calvin: flesh granted differently from manna—fatherly manna vs gluttony's flesh. Winds obey; elements execute his command.

Shared—east and south by power. Distance cannot bar his will.

Psalms 78:27

27He also rained meat on them as the dust, winged birds as the sand of the seas.

Arminian

Rained meat like dust; winged birds like sand. Wesley: Fowl—God took away use of wings; fell to Israel.

Shared—quail deluge. Miracle of quantity and fall.

Reformed

Calvin: flesh as dust, birds as sand—undoubted proof of power they malignantly denied.

Shared—meat rained. Lavish answer to lust is distinct from fatherly manna.

Psalms 78:28

28He let them fall in the middle of their camp, around their habitations.

Arminian

Fell in midst of camp, around dwellings. Shared placement of the catch.

Food at the tent door. Proximity of gift and later wrath (vv. 30–31).

Reformed

Calvin: fall in his camp/tabernacles—Israel's camp as God's. Provision into the heart of the people.

Shared—midst of camp. No scavenging hunt required; judgment arrives mid-feast.

Psalms 78:29

29So they ate, and were well filled. He gave them their own desire.

Arminian

They ate and were well filled; he gave what they craved.

Desire granted. Filling is not the same as blessing when lust leads.

Reformed

Calvin: ate and filled—not mere supply but ungovernable lust proved. God fulfills desire of those who fear him (Ps 145) differently; here he yields to perverse desire in wrath.

Shared—filled with craving. Deadly burden can be the answer to unbridled want.

Psalms 78:30

30They didn’t turn from their cravings. Their food was yet in their mouths,

Arminian

Before craving satisfied, food still in mouths. Shared unfinished lust.

Wesley silent; meat between teeth when judgment hits. Desire outruns gratitude.

Reformed

Calvin: not estranged from lust—still burning while meat in mouth. Present felt pleasure when vengeance struck.

Shared—food in mouth, lust unslaked. Abundance does not quench depraved appetite.

Psalms 78:31

31when the anger of God went up against them, killed some of their strongest, and struck down the young men of Israel.

Arminian

Anger of God rose; killed strongest; laid low young men. Wesley: Mightiest—most healthy and strong, likely most eager feeders.

Shared—elite cut down. Strength is no shield against holy anger.

Reformed

Calvin: wrath ascended—sudden rising to judgment. Fat ones and chosen named so the robust who trust strength are conspicuously judged.

For the church: Shared—strongest fall. Pride of vigor meets the plague mid-banquet.

Psalms 78:32

32For all this they still sinned, and didn’t believe in his wondrous works.

Arminian

For all this they still sinned and did not believe in his wondrous works. Shared hardened unbelief after plague.

Rod should teach; continued sin after wonders is willful. Classical Arminians urge repentance while wonders still preach.

Reformed

Calvin: fools wise under the rod—yet these unreformed; monstrous obduracy. Wondrous works include the plague; disbelief of word and despising miracles; plagues increased.

Shared—still sinned, did not believe. Obstinacy after severe spectacle is near despair of reform.

Psalms 78:33

33Therefore he consumed their days in vanity, and their years in terror.

Arminian

Days vanish like a breath; years in terror. Wesley: Vanity—tedious fruitless marches; Trouble—diseases, dangers, perplexities.

Shared—wasted years under curse. Wilderness chronology as judgment.

Reformed

Calvin: whole people consumed—forty years confirmed. Days in vanity like smoke; years in haste/tumult—strength proud suddenly withered.

Shared—breath-days, terror-years. Righteous retribution for obstinacy.

Psalms 78:34

34When he killed them, then they inquired after him. They returned and sought God earnestly.

Arminian

When he killed them, then they inquired after him; repented and sought earnestly. Wesley: Returned—from idols; Enquired—speedily sought ease and safety.

Shared—death awakens seeking. Yet later verses expose flattery; seek in sincerity.

Reformed

Calvin: aggravation—convinced yet not sincerely humbled; mocked God with false pretenses. Severe killing opened eyes to discover desperate wickedness.

Shared—when slain, they sought. Fear-seeking without upright heart (vv. 36–37) is hollow.

Psalms 78:35

35They remembered that God was their rock, the Most High God, their redeemer.

Arminian

Remembered God their rock, Most High their redeemer. Wesley: Redeemer—God alone preserved and alone could help.

Shared—rock and redeemer confessed. Memory of redemption should stick.

Reformed

Calvin: remembered Rock and Redeemer—yet from fallen redemption; eyes opened to strip excuse of ignorance.

Shared—Rock, Redeemer recalled. Right titles on the tongue can still mask a wrong heart.

Psalms 78:36

36But they flattered him with their mouth, and lied to him with their tongue.

Arminian

Flattered with mouths; lied with tongues. Wesley: Lied—false protestations of sincere future obedience.

Shared—mouth religion. Arminians demand unfeigned confession, not fear-flattery.

Reformed

Calvin: perfidy—no sincere confession; constrained slavish flattery while wishing truth extinguished. Guard hidden hypocrisy as well as gross.

Shared—flattery and lies. Assent without pure affection is double heart.

Psalms 78:37

37For their heart was not right with him, neither were they faithful in his covenant.

Arminian

Heart not steadfast; not faithful to his covenant. Shared unstable heart and covenant breach.

Steadfastness required. Faithfulness is free loyalty, not momentary panic piety.

Reformed

Calvin: source—not steadfast/faithful; what lacks unfeigned purity is lying before God. Covenant-breakers toward faithfulness of divine promises.

Shared—heart and covenant. Mutual relation: God's faithful covenant answered by unfeigned faith.

Psalms 78:38

38But he, being merciful, forgave iniquity, and didn’t destroy them. Yes, many times he turned his anger away, and didn’t stir up all his wrath.

Arminian

Yet compassionate, atoned for iniquity, did not destroy; restrained anger often, not all wrath.

Compassion leads God to restrain his anger again and again. Classical Arminians celebrate mercy that spares and atones while still calling for true return—God's resolve to show mercy is firm, not hesitant.

Reformed

Calvin: merciful, expiated iniquity, did not destroy; multiplied turning away anger; mitigated chastisements. Mercy essential attribute—not induced by desert; punishments moderate versus guilt.

Shared—compassion, atonement, restrained wrath. Underserved goodness sustains a remnant seed.

Psalms 78:39

39He remembered that they were but flesh, a wind that passes away, and doesn’t come again.

Arminian

Remembered they were flesh; wind that passes away, and doesn’t come again. Shared frailty moved pity.

Flesh and passing wind—ground of compassion. Humility fits creatures who do not return to earthly life.

Reformed

Calvin: remembered flesh—spirit/breath fleeting. Nothing in us but misery moves pity; pure undeserved goodness sustains. Not excluding resurrection; earthly state alone viewed.

Shared—flesh, passing wind. Compassion rests on our frailty and his goodness, not our merit.

Psalms 78:40

40How often they rebelled against him in the wilderness, and grieved him in the desert!

Arminian

How often rebelled in wilderness; grieved him in the desert.

The words “How often” pile up the people’s sins. Desert grief of God is relational language without denying his settled holiness.

Reformed

Calvin: confirms—provoked so often they must have perished thousand times but for mercy. Wilderness includes place and time; fresh benefits and short span aggravate.

Shared—often rebelled and grieved. Interrogative heightens continual sinning.

Psalms 78:41

41They turned again and tempted God, and provoked the Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

Tested again and again; provoked the Holy One of Israel.

Wesley's "limited" (AV)—fencing God's power by unbelief. Do not shrink the Holy One to your measure.

Reformed

Calvin: returned—continued course of sinning; tempted and limited—metaphor of marking out, shutting God within bars when operations are capped by human fancy.

Shared—tested and provoked the Holy One. Unbelief that measures God by small capacity pulls him from his throne.

Psalms 78:42

42They didn’t remember his hand, nor the day when he redeemed them from the adversary;

Arminian

Did not remember his hand, nor the day he redeemed them from the adversary. Wesley: Hand—glorious works; Enemy—day delivered from Pharaoh.

Shared—forgotten redemption day. Memory of the foe's defeat should curb new tests.

Reformed

Calvin: remembered not his hand in the day of deliverance from the oppressor. Forgetfulness of unusual stretched-out hand leaves no excuse.

Shared—power and redemption day forgotten. Wilful amnesia feeds waywardness.

Psalms 78:43

43how he set his signs in Egypt, his wonders in the field of Zoan,

Arminian

Signs in Egypt; marvels in fields of Zoan. Shared reprise of exodus signs.

Zoan again—public marvels. Teach children these signs (vv. 4–6).

Reformed

Calvin: signs then miracles—Egypt stricken with terror that should not have vanished. Both terms heighten what forgetfulness buried.

Shared—signs and marvels in Zoan. Rehearsal serves accusation of ingratitude.

Psalms 78:44

44he turned their rivers into blood, and their streams, so that they could not drink.

Arminian

Rivers to blood; could not drink streams.

Nile turned—judgment on Egypt's life-water.

Reformed

Calvin: enumerates enough to open wickedness—not full order. Extraordinary plagues, previously unheard-of.

Both traditions remember the rivers turned to blood. Accumulation of judgments should have stuck in Israel's mind.

Psalms 78:45

45He sent among them swarms of flies, which devoured them; and frogs, which destroyed them.

Arminian

Swarms of flies; frogs destroyed them. Wesley: Flies extraordinary in nature and hurt; likewise frogs.

Shared—devouring swarms. Created pests as rods of God.

Reformed

Calvin: mixture/flies and frog—destroying agents. Variety of terms enhances vengeance.

Shared—flies and frogs. Nature armed against oppressors.

Psalms 78:46

46He also gave their increase to the caterpillar, and their labor to the locust.

Arminian

Crops to destroying locust; fruit of labor to the locust. Wesley: Labour—herbs by their care.

Shared—labor's fruit eaten. Toil without divine favor fails.

Reformed

Calvin: produce to caterpillar/locust—harvest seized. Labor lost under judgment.

Shared—locust on crops and labor. Egypt's economy under the LORD's hand.

Psalms 78:47

47He destroyed their vines with hail, their sycamore fig trees with frost.

Arminian

Wesley: Under vines and sycamores all trees; hail/frost destroy fruit and sometimes trees.

Shared—vineyard and sycamore struck. Food trees fail at his word.

Reformed

Calvin: vines and wild figs/sycamores with hailstones—severe loss on valued Egyptian produce.

Shared—hail and frost on trees. Agricultural pride leveled.

Psalms 78:48

48He also gave over their livestock to the hail, and their flocks to hot thunderbolts.

Arminian

Cattle to hail; flocks to thunderbolts. Shared livestock plague.

Herds and flocks given over. Wealth on the hoof is not secure against God.

Reformed

Calvin: cattle to hail, flocks to thunderbolts—continuing the cascade of wrath.

Shared—cattle and flocks. Animate wealth under the same sentence.

Psalms 78:49

49He threw on them the fierceness of his anger, wrath, indignation, and trouble, and a band of angels of evil.

Arminian

Burning anger, wrath, indignation, distress; company of destroying angels. Wesley: Evil angels—employed in producing plagues.

Shared—angels as instruments of judgment. Fierceness piled in synonyms.

Reformed

Calvin: fierceness, fury, anger, affliction; evil/hurtful angels—ministers of calamity (elect or fallen as instruments differ). Accumulation awakens dull minds.

Shared—destroying angels. God executes wrath by appointed messengers.

Psalms 78:50

50He made a path for his anger. He didn’t spare their soul from death, but gave their life over to the pestilence,

Arminian

Made a path for his anger; spared not from death; lives over to plague. Shared cleared way for wrath.

Path for anger—judgment unimpeded. Death and pestilence as sentenced.

Reformed

Calvin: leveled a path to anger—no impediment; kept not soul from death; cattle/life to pestilence. Overflowing torrent of wrath.

Shared—path for anger, plague. Final Egyptian strokes approach.

Psalms 78:51

51and struck all the firstborn in Egypt, the chief of their strength in the tents of Ham.

Arminian

Struck every firstborn; firstfruits of strength in tents of Ham. Wesley: Ham—Egyptians, posterity of Ham.

Practically: Shared—firstborn struck. Tents of Ham name the judged line.

Reformed

Calvin: firstborn as firstfruits of strength; tents of Ham—Misraim son of Ham. Free love toward Shem's seed preferred without intrinsic desert.

Shared—firstborn in Ham's tents. Election-mercy to Israel stands out against Egypt's loss.

Psalms 78:52

52But he led out his own people like sheep, and guided them in the wilderness like a flock.

Arminian

Led out his own people like sheep; guided in wilderness like a flock.

Sheep and flock—tender guidance after plagues. People go forth under care.

Reformed

Calvin: fatherly love—people as sheep; no wisdom or power of their own; God condescends as shepherd.

Shared—sheep led, flock guided. Mean servile Israel shepherded against warlike foes.

Psalms 78:53

53He led them safely, so that they weren’t afraid, but the sea overwhelmed their enemies.

Arminian

Led in safety, not afraid; sea overwhelmed enemies. Shared safe conduct vs drowned foe.

Safety and fearlessness as gift. Enemies under the sea.

Reformed

Calvin: conducted in safety—not that they always felt confident, but with God as guardian they had no just cause to fear; unbelief bred panic questions.

Shared—safety; sea covers enemies. Objective protection stands even when hearts flutter.

Psalms 78:54

54He brought them to the border of his sanctuary, to this mountain, which his right hand had taken.

Arminian

Brought to his holy land; mountain his right hand won. Wesley: Holy place—Canaan separated; Mountain—mountainous country.

Shared—holy land, won mountain. Gifted geography of presence.

Reformed

Calvin: holy border; mountain his right hand acquired—inheritance secured by his power.

Shared—holy land by his right hand. Arrival is purchased grace, not Israelite prowess.

Psalms 78:55

55He also drove out the nations before them, allotted them for an inheritance by line, and made the tribes of Israel to dwell in their tents.

Arminian

Drove out nations; apportioned possession; settled tribes in tents.

Nations expelled; lots assigned. Tribes dwell by gift.

Reformed

Calvin: expelled heathen; inheritance by line; Israel in their tents—settled after driving out.

Shared—nations out, tribes in. Possession is apportioned under God.

Psalms 78:56

56Yet they tempted and rebelled against the Most High God, and didn’t keep his testimonies,

Arminian

Yet tested and rebelled against Most High; did not keep testimonies. Shared relapse in the land.

Land-gift does not automate fidelity. Testimonies still bind.

Reformed

Calvin: after settlement they tempted and provoked; kept not testimonies—ingratitude in the inheritance.

Shared—tested, rebelled, testimonies broken. New stage, same revolt pattern.

Psalms 78:57

57but turned back, and dealt treacherously like their fathers. They were twisted like a deceitful bow.

Arminian

Wesley: Deceitful bow—breaks when drawn or shoots awry, frustrates archer.

Shared—treachery and useless bow. Promise of aim without true flight.

Reformed

Calvin: turned back, treacherous like fathers; deceitful bow—appears ready, fails in action.

Shared—fathers' pattern, deceitful bow. Covenant people who will not shoot straight.

Psalms 78:58

58For they provoked him to anger with their high places, and moved him to jealousy with their engraved images.

Arminian

Provoked with high places; jealousy with idols. Shared cultic provocation.

High places and graven images. Jealousy language is holy love offended.

Reformed

Calvin: high places and graven images moved him to anger/jealousy—idolatry as covenant adultery.

Shared—high places, idols. Worship rivals awaken divine jealousy.

Psalms 78:59

59When God heard this, he was angry, and greatly abhorred Israel,

Arminian

When God heard, full of wrath, utterly rejected Israel. Wesley: Heard—perceived after manner of men.

Shared—wrath and rejection.

Reformed

Calvin: heard and wroth—long-suffering abused; cry ascended; weight of punishment shows offense. Abhorred Israel when ark departed as sign of farewell.

Shared—heard, wrath, rejected. Patient God finally exhibits severe judgment.

Psalms 78:60

60so that he abandoned the tent of Shiloh, the tent which he placed among men,

Arminian

Forsook dwelling at Shiloh, tent among mankind. Wesley: Shiloh; Among men—condescension and folly of despising the privilege.

Takeaway: Shared—Shiloh forsaken. Presence withdrawn as judgment on scorned nearness.

Reformed

Calvin: bereft of presence—only true comfort; ark carried testifies departure. Shiloh habitation among men—God near by power and grace; wickedness constrained forsaking.

Shared—Shiloh tent forsaken. Warning to all who flatter themselves on false grounds of presence (Jer 7).

Psalms 78:61

61and delivered his strength into captivity, his glory into the adversary’s hand.

Arminian

Delivered his strength into captivity, glory to the foe. Wesley: Strength/glory—the ark; Enemies—Philistines.

Shared—ark as strength and glory captured. Sign and pledge removed.

Reformed

Calvin: strength into captivity, beauty/glory into enemy hand—not that Philistines imprisoned God, but protection withdrawn as if the general taken.

Shared—power and glory delivered. Hyperbole aggravates Israel's heinous offense.

Psalms 78:62

62He also gave his people over to the sword, and was angry with his inheritance.

Arminian

Gave people over to sword; vented wrath on his heritage.

Sword and heritage—covenant people not immune. Wrath on what he claims.

Reformed

Calvin: shut up to the sword; wroth with inheritance—flower of people consumed; fire metaphor for sudden slaughter (1 Sam 4).

Shared—sword on the people, wrath on heritage. Own inheritance can taste the rod.

Psalms 78:63

63Fire devoured their young men. Their virgins had no wedding song.

Arminian

Fire devoured young men; young women no marriage song.

No nuptial joy—demographic judgment. War silences wedding songs.

Reformed

Calvin: fire devouring chosen—metaphor; maidens not applauded—nuptial songs cease for want of men.

Shared—youth dead, no marriage song. Calamity heaped in domestic silence.

Psalms 78:64

64Their priests fell by the sword, and their widows couldn’t weep.

Arminian

Priests fell by sword; widows made no lamentation. Wesley: Priests—Hophni and Phinehas; No lamentation—prevented by death or invasion.

Both traditions remember the fall of Eli’s house here. Even priests fall; mourning disrupted.

Reformed

Calvin: priests under special protection perish; widows make no lamentation—sorrow-death or captivity forbids rites.

Shared—priests by sword, widows silent. Every kind of calamity heaped.

Psalms 78:65

65Then the Lord awakened as one out of sleep, like a mighty man who shouts by reason of wine.

Arminian

Lord awakened as one out of sleep; mighty man who shouts by reason of wine.

Asleep figure—not literal slumber. Sudden arousal for defense and shame of foes.

Reformed

Calvin: awoke as asleep—made drunk by people's sins so watch failed; yet not long sunk; heathen mockery of majesty awoke him like a giant after wine.

Shared—awoke as from sleep. Figure teaches delayed then decisive action for his glory.

Psalms 78:66

66He struck his adversaries backward. He put them to a perpetual reproach.

Arminian

Put adversaries to rout; everlasting shame. Wesley: Smote—with piles; Reproach—golden emerods with returned ark as monuments of shame.

For the reader: Shared—Philistine shame. Everlasting reproach on ark-mockers.

Reformed

Calvin: smote enemies behind; everlasting disgrace—awful vengeance shows covenant not disannulled even while Israel still bears traces of chastisement.

Shared—adversaries routed, everlasting shame. God avenges his glory on the uncircumcised.

Psalms 78:67

67Moreover he rejected the tent of Joseph, and didn’t choose the tribe of Ephraim,

Arminian

Rejected tent of Joseph; did not choose tribe of Ephraim. Wesley: Refused—ark no longer in Shiloh's tabernacle in Joseph/Ephraim.

Shared historic transfer of the ark's seat. Rejection of Joseph's tent is cultic relocation under judgment—not spite of whole genealogy forever.

Reformed

Calvin: rejection—ark's removal deprives honor; Ephraim rejected as ark-residence (not fully severed from the church body). Traces of punishment remain even after Philistines struck; scornful note as revolt later cuts ten tribes.

Shared—Joseph's tent rejected, Ephraim not chosen for the dwelling. Judicial reshaping of where God sets his name.

Key contrast Both confess God rejected Joseph's tent and did not choose Ephraim for the ark's seat. Arminians typically follow Wesley's historical reading: Shiloh forsaken, ark removed from Ephraim's bounds under judgment. Reformed readers side with Calvin's sharper edge: free judicial rejection of that dwelling privilege as lasting monument of wrath, while still not treating every Ephraimite as finally cut from the church.

Psalms 78:68

68But chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion which he loved.

Arminian

Chose tribe of Judah, Mount Zion which he loved. Wesley: Chose—for seat of ark and worship.

Shared—Judah and beloved Zion as historic worship seat. Love language marks place of name without turning the verse into abstract metaphysics.

Reformed

Calvin: preferred Judah; chose Zion—sanctuary there solely from free unmerited love, not place's worthiness. Relative as causal: because he loved. Second restitution as free as Abrahamic adoption; church gathered by pure grace in all ages.

Shared—Judah chosen, Zion loved. Unconditioned love of the place for the people's good.

Key contrast Both confess God chose Judah and Mount Zion which he loved as the ark's seat. Arminians side with Wesley's focus: chosen for worship and ark residence in redemptive history. Reformed commentators typically emphasize Calvin: Zion enriched by free good pleasure alone—no intrinsic merit of place or people; church always gathered by pure grace.

Psalms 78:69

69He built his sanctuary like the heights, like the earth which he has established forever.

Arminian

Built sanctuary like high heavens, like earth founded forever. Wesley: Sanctuary—Solomon's temple; Palaces—magnificent; Established—fixed, not moved like tabernacle.

Shared—temple stability versus mobile tent. Forever language pledges settled worship.

Reformed

Calvin: sanctuary like high places—conspicuous; like earth established forever—not temporary palace. Perpetuity in Christ the truth of the figure; Chaldean ruin answered by the antitype.

Shared—sanctuary founded forever. Stability celebrated is finally Christ-ward.

Psalms 78:70

70He also chose David his servant, and took him from the sheepfolds;

Arminian

Chose David his servant; took him from the sheepfolds.

Choice of David from folds—historic elevation of the servant. Vocational election to rule Jacob.

Reformed

Calvin: after temple, kingdom—chief signs of favor; David made king from sheepfold, peasant to throne. Grace not limited to his person; worth of Abraham's children flows from mercy. Christ our king and priest typified; lowly start matches the type.

Shared—David chosen from sheepfolds. Pure good pleasure elevates the servant.

Key contrast Both confess God chose David his servant from the sheepfolds. Arminians typically emphasize vocational election of the shepherd to feed Israel in history. Reformed readers typically follow Calvin: exaltation from peasant cot displays free grace; kingdom and priesthood as unmerited favor, fulfilled in Christ's lowly then royal work.

Psalms 78:71

71from following the ewes that have their young, he brought him to be the shepherd of Jacob, his people, and Israel, his inheritance.

Arminian

From nursing ewes to be the shepherd of Jacob, his people, and Israel, his inheritance. Shared transfer of flock-care.

Ewes to Israel—same shepherd skill redirected. People and inheritance named as God's.

Reformed

Calvin: grace furthered—keeper of sheep made shepherd of chosen heritage. Allusion teaches lawful kings must be like shepherds, not tyrants.

Shared—from ewes to Jacob/Israel. Shepherd-king pattern under God's claim on the flock.

Psalms 78:72

72So he was their shepherd according to the integrity of his heart, and guided them by the skillfulness of his hands.

Arminian

With upright heart he shepherded; guided with skillful hand. Shared integrity and skill of David.

Upright heart and skillful hand—model rule. Close calls the people to grateful fidelity under such care.

Reformed

Calvin: fed in uprightness; guided by prudence of hands—Spirit-governed success. Indirect rebuke of later ingratitude that shook off the holy order and yoke.

Shared—upright heart, skillful hand. David's fidelity magnifies the people's later perverseness; Christ the greater shepherd remains.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What earlier interpreters on each side wrote about Psalm 78:1–72.

Arminian Notable voices

“My law—The doctrine which I am about to deliver. Parable—Weighty sentences. Dark sayings—Not that the words are hard to be understood, but the things, God's transcendent goodness, their unparalleled ingratitude. Testimony—His law, a witness between God and men. Ephraim—Put for all Israel; may refer to the overthrow of 1 Samuel 4; the psalmist then relates causes unto Shiloh forsaken and removal to Judah and Zion. Tempted—Desired a proof of God's power; lust—not necessary subsistence but luxurious appetite. Angels' food—Manna by ministry of angels. Vanity—Tedious fruitless marches; trouble—diseases and dangers. Lied—False protestations of future obedience. Deceitful bow—Breaks when drawn or shoots awry. Shiloh—Tent among men; condescension despised. Strength and glory—The ark. Refused—Ark no longer in Joseph/Ephraim; chose Judah for seat of ark and worship; sanctuary—Solomon's temple, fixed forever.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 78

Reformed Notable voices

“Hearken to my instruction: even believers are backward to learn; mashal and pointed sayings stir attention to benefits and ingratitude. Fathers must transmit the law as inheritance; trust first, remembrance of works, then obedience. The fathers a rebellious generation—God turns hearts by his Spirit, yet the stubborn remain blameworthy. Ephraim armed yet fled; they kept not the covenant and forgot wonders. In the desert they provoked the Most High, tempted God by craving, believed not nor trusted his salvation; manna was fatherly, flesh was lust answered in wrath. When he slew them they sought him yet flattered and lied; he, merciful, expiated iniquity and restrained anger, remembering they were flesh. They limited the Holy One; he forsook Shiloh and delivered his strength to captivity, then awoke and shamed the foe. He rejected Joseph's tent, chose Judah and Zion which he loved of free unmerited love, built a sanctuary founded forever in Christ the truth of the figure, and chose David from the sheepfolds to shepherd Israel with upright heart and prudent hand.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 78
