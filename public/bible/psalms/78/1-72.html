Passage

Psalms 78

Verses 1–72 · ESV · Voices

A Maskil of Asaph. Give ear to teaching and dark sayings of old: fathers must tell the coming generation the LORD's glorious deeds, that children set their hope in God and not be a stubborn generation. Ephraim turned back in battle; Israel forgot wonders in Egypt and Zoan—sea, cloud, rock-water—yet rebelled in the desert, tested God for craving, and did not trust his saving power. Manna and quail, wrath on the strong, flattery after slaughter, compassion that atoned and restrained anger: still they rebelled and limited the Holy One. He struck Egypt's firstborn, led his flock to the holy land, then forsook Shiloh when high places provoked him; power and glory went captive until he shamed the foe. He rejected Joseph's tent, chose Judah and Mount Zion which he loves, built his sanctuary, and chose David from the sheepfolds to shepherd Israel with upright heart and skillful hand.

Psalms 78:1

1Give ear, O my people, to my teaching; incline your ears to the words of my mouth!

Arminian

Give ear, O my people, to my teaching. Wesley: My law—the doctrine about to be delivered. The maskil opens as catechesis for the covenant people.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—teaching the next generation begins with hearing. Incline the ear; the narrative of mercies and sins that follows is meant to form hope and obedience.

Reformed

Calvin: the writer addresses the Jews as teacher, calling attention because even true believers are often backward to receive instruction. Torah here is instruction—words of his mouth.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—solemn call to hear. The majesty of what follows should stir sluggish disciples; the gospel likewise demands earnest attention.

Psalms 78:2

2I will open my mouth in a parable; I will utter dark sayings from of old,

Arminian

I will open my mouth in a parable; utter dark sayings from of old. Wesley: Parable—weighty sentences; Dark sayings—not hard words but prodigious things: God's goodness, Israel's ingratitude, stupid insensibleness under such teaching.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—ancient history pressed as parable. The darkness is moral wonder, not genre fog.

Reformed

Calvin: mashal denotes grave striking sentences; chidoth pointed sayings worthy of notice—not ambiguous wrap. Design: stimulate hearers to weigh God's benefits and the people's ingratitude.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—weighty history, not riddle for its own sake. Matthew 13 applies the pattern to Christ teaching with prophetic majesty.

Psalms 78:3

3things that we have heard and known, that our fathers have told us.

Arminian

Things heard and known, that our fathers told us. Shared transmission: domestic memory of God's works.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley is silent here; the plain sense holds—fathers tell, children know. Faith receives public history, not private speculation.

Reformed

Calvin: common subject transmitted father to son, yet illustrated with majesty. Heard and known—not fables; fathers are the chosen people entrusted with divine truth.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—apostolic chain of memory. Sublime truth reaches even babes who are teachable.

Psalms 78:4

4We will not hide them from their children, but tell to the coming generation the glorious deeds of the LORD, and his might, and the wonders that he has done.

Arminian

We will not hide them from their children, but tell the coming generation the glorious deeds of the LORD, his might, and wonders. Shared: praise published to the next age.

Arminian pastoral: Teaching is refusal to hide. Classical Arminians hear sincere duty to declare deeds so hope may be set (v. 7).

Reformed

Calvin: we will not conceal from posterity what ancestors taught—truth published age to age. End: celebrate Jehovah's praises, power, and wonderful works.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—no concealment. Church and household shoulder transmission so praise does not die.

Psalms 78:5

5He established a testimony in Jacob and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers to teach to their children,

Arminian

He established a testimony in Jacob and appointed a law in Israel, commanded fathers to teach children. Wesley: Established—chief of mercies; testimony—law as witness of duties and blessings.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—law and testimony are mercy first. Fathers teach so children may answer God.

Reformed

Calvin: God is author; fathers instruct by commandment, not mere impulse. Statute and law seal grace so knowledge of free adoption is not obliterated.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—commanded pedagogy. Written law ratifies the covenant people as gathered for holy obedience.

Psalms 78:6

6that the next generation might know them, the children yet unborn, and arise and tell them to their children,

Arminian

That the next generation might know, children yet unborn arise and tell their children. Shared multi-generational chain.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley silent; the aim is knowing that spreads. Teach so the unborn may teach.

Reformed

Calvin: law given not for one age; fathers transmit as inheritance that it never be lost. Each father diligently instructs; preachers of truth should be as numerous as those born.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—continual succession of teaching. Church as pillar serves this transmission.

Psalms 78:7

7so that they should set their hope in God and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments;

Arminian

So they should set their hope in God, not forget his works, but keep his commandments. Shared triad: hope, memory, obedience.

Arminian pastoral: Hope first—then remembrance that fuels keeping. Classical Arminians urge real trust that issues in kept commands.

Reformed

Calvin: trust first; remembrance of benefits confirms faith; then devoted obedience. True service begins with faith; distrust breeds murmuring.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—hope in God, not forget, keep. Heavenly wisdom: hearts fixed on God, then holy obedience.

Psalms 78:8

8and that they should not be like their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation, a generation whose heart was not steadfast, whose spirit was not faithful to God.

Arminian

Not like their fathers—stubborn and rebellious, heart not steadfast, spirit not faithful. Shared warning against ancestral pattern.

Arminian pastoral: The generation is blamed for unset heart and unfaithful spirit. Classical Arminians hear real culpability and a sincere call not to imitate—without making the Spirit's converting work unnecessary.

Reformed

Calvin: Jews no better than fathers—treacherous, rebellious, crooked. Special work of God to turn hearts by the Spirit; yet men are not exempt from blame when lust draws them away. Papist inference of autonomous heart-bending does not stand.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—do not copy the fathers' revolt. Blame and need of Spirit stand together.

Key contrast Both confess a stubborn generation whose heart was not steadfast and spirit not faithful. Arminians typically stress culpable refusal and the call to a different obedience. Reformed readers typically follow Calvin: God alone turns the heart by the Spirit, yet the stubborn remain blameworthy—no autonomous free-will proof from the charge.

Psalms 78:9

9The Ephraimites, armed with the bow, turned back on the day of battle.

Arminian

Ephraimites armed with the bow turned back on the day of battle. Wesley: Ephraim put for all Israel; may refer to 1 Sam 4; bows for all arms; discourse of causes runs to Shiloh's fall and move to Judah/Zion.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—armed yet fleeing. Historic flight under judgment, not a tribal cartoon.

Reformed

Calvin: example of unfaithfulness; skillful archers fleeing proves divine displeasure and effeminate hearts. Ephraim named as leaders into revolt (calves), warning against empty church title without holy fruit.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—bow and flight. Deprived of God's aid, martial pride collapses.

Psalms 78:10

10They did not keep God's covenant, but refused to walk according to his law.

Arminian

They did not keep God's covenant, refused to walk according to his law. Shared: covenant-break and refusal.

Arminian pastoral: Refusal is willful. Classical Arminians hear real rejection of known law.

Reformed

Calvin: reason aid was withheld—perfidy despising law and covenant. Covenant first (trust in promises), then law; refuse implies deliberate obstinacy, not mere giddiness.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—covenant not kept. Unbelief toward promises underwrites the walk of refusal.

Psalms 78:11

11They forgot his works and the wonders that he had shown them.

Arminian

They forgot his works and the wonders he had shown them. Shared: forgetfulness of wonders.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley silent; ingratitude is the hinge. Remembering wonders should restrain sin.

Reformed

Calvin: shameful impiety from ingratitude—wickedly buried deliverances. Monstrous stupidity; they shut eyes lest God's glory restrain them.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—forgot works and wonders. Memory of grace is a means God uses to keep a people.

Psalms 78:12

12In the sight of their fathers he performed wonders in the land of Egypt, in the fields of Zoan.

Arminian

Wonders in Egypt, in the fields of Zoan, in fathers' sight. Wesley: Field—territory; Zoan—ancient eminent Egyptian city.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—public miracles before the fathers. Zoan names the scene; history, not myth.

Reformed

Calvin: transition to first ancestors—whole race perverse from the origin. Miracles before departure; Zoan recalls the place.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—wonders in Egypt/Zoan. Forgetting such signs aggravates later revolt.

Psalms 78:13

13He divided the sea and let them pass through it, and made the waters stand like a heap.

Arminian

He divided the sea; waters stood like a heap. Shared exodus sea.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley silent; heap of waters is the LORD's act. Pass through as gift, not technique.

Reformed

Calvin: order of nature reversed—waters heaped. Passage and cloud (v. 14) are pledges toward the heavenly inheritance; Paul reads baptismal pattern (1 Cor 10).

Reformed pastoral: Shared—divided sea, heap of waters. Deliverance that stretches toward everlasting salvation.

Psalms 78:14

14In the daytime he led them with a cloud, and all the night with a fiery light.

Arminian

Daytime cloud; night fiery light. Shared guidance.

Arminian pastoral: Pillar leads continually. Trust the Guide in desert heat and dark.

Reformed

Calvin: after the sea, God still guides; cloud shades and pledges protection until the inheritance.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—cloud and fire. Guidance is fatherly presence, not bare navigation.

Psalms 78:15

15He split rocks in the wilderness and gave them drink abundantly as from the deep.

Arminian

He split rocks; drink abundantly as from the deep. Wesley: Wilderness—Rephidim and again Kadesh.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—rock water in named places. Abundance from the dry rock.

Reformed

Calvin: not mere spring opened—water from profound deeps, as from the earth's center. Fatherly care in miracle.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—clave the rock. Ordinary nature changed to supply the flock.

Psalms 78:16

16He made streams come out of the rock and caused waters to flow down like rivers.

Arminian

Streams from the rock; waters like rivers. Wesley: Streams miraculously followed even to Canaan's borders.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—ongoing supply. Grace that travels with the camp.

Reformed

Calvin: where no drop was, a mighty river—abundance that silences cavil.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—rivers from rock. Copious gift exposes later craving as lust, not need.

Psalms 78:17

17Yet they sinned still more against him, rebelling against the Most High in the desert.

Arminian

Yet they sinned still more, rebelling against the Most High in the desert. Wesley: Wilderness—where singular obligations to obedience; great aggravation.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—desert sin against near mercies. Obligation intensifies guilt.

Reformed

Calvin: after succession of love, they rebelled as was natural to them. Provoke—heinous; place aggravates: fresh deliverance, tokens of presence, need itself should have restrained.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—sinned still more in the desert. Mercies abused become witnesses against.

Psalms 78:18

18They tested God in their heart by demanding the food they craved.

Arminian

They tested God in their heart by demanding the food they craved. Wesley: Tempted—desired proof of power; Lust—not necessary subsistence but luxurious appetite.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—heart-test by craving. Ask within God's limits; do not demand proof for lust.

Reformed

Calvin: tempting is overpassing bounds—not asking food when hungry, but loathing manna and craving for the soul/lust. Separate power from will and you rend God.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—tested in heart by craving. Impose restraint; unchecked flesh murmurs for more than lawful.

Psalms 78:19

19They spoke against God, saying, "Can God spread a table in the wilderness?

Arminian

They spoke against God: Can God spread a table in the wilderness? Shared open blasphemy of doubt.

Arminian pastoral: Mouth publishes the heart's test. Classical Arminians hear sincere invitation still to trust the table-God.

Reformed

Calvin: not ashamed to utter impiety conceived within; malignity possessed the heart. Table means dainty Egypt-fare—abundance and variety demanded.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—speech against God. Lust develops from consent into public question of God's power.

Psalms 78:20

20He struck the rock so that water gushed out and streams overflowed. Can he also give bread or provide meat for his people?"

Arminian

He struck the rock—water gushed; can he give bread or meat? Shared irony of unbelief after water.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley silent; water miracle should have silenced the sneer. Faith argues from gift to Giver.

Reformed

Calvin: bitter irony—reciting as from their mouth what stood before their eyes. Unbelief grants the rock yet denies bread and flesh.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—water conceded, bread doubted. Past mercy does not automatically produce trust.

Psalms 78:21

21Therefore, when the LORD heard, he was full of wrath; a fire was kindled against Jacob; his anger rose against Israel,

Arminian

The LORD heard; wrath, fire against Jacob, anger against Israel. Shared judicial hearing.

Arminian pastoral: Hearing is full knowledge before judgment. Anger rises against covenant names Jacob/Israel.

Reformed

Calvin: hearing implies perfect knowledge—figure from earthly judges; God does not punish unacquainted. Wrath answered unbelief (v. 22).

Reformed pastoral: Shared—LORD heard and was wroth. Fire in Jacob is holy response to distrust.

Psalms 78:22

22because they did not believe in God and did not trust his saving power.

Arminian

Because they did not believe in God and did not trust his saving power. Wesley: Trusted not—that he could and would save from feared famine.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—unbelief and distrust of salvation. Classical Arminians urge real trust in saving power sincerely offered in the narrative of care.

Reformed

Calvin: enormity—believed not, trusted not in his salvation. Faith hopes every blessing from God and yields obedience; distrust murmurs and rebels.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—no belief, no trust in saving power. Faith is root of piety; pretenses without hope in God's salvation are empty.

Psalms 78:23

23Yet he commanded the skies above and opened the doors of heaven,

Arminian

Yet he commanded the skies and opened the doors of heaven. Wesley: Heaven—storehouse whose doors God shuts or opens.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—sky under command. Doors open at his word despite unbelief.

Reformed

Calvin: not bare history—censure: though fed with manna they still lusted. Heaven's doors magnify the gift they scorned.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—commanded skies. Fatherly storehouse opened; later flesh-gift comes in wrath.

Psalms 78:24

24and he rained down on them manna to eat and gave them the grain of heaven.

Arminian

Rained manna; grain of heaven. Shared corn from above.

Arminian pastoral: Manna as gift to eat. Receive heavenly grain with thanks, not loathing.

Reformed

Calvin: corn of heaven—ingratitude twofold if they despise common food and angels' bread. Verbs recall past mercy against present lust.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—manna rained. Spiritual meat in Paul's sense points beyond the wilderness loaf.

Psalms 78:25

25Man ate of the bread of the angels; he sent them food in abundance.

Arminian

Man ate bread of the angels; food in abundance. Wesley: Angels' food—manna by ministry of angels.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—abundant angel-bread. Full supply removes excuse for craving Egypt.

Reformed

Calvin: bread of the mighty/angels—high celebration to make impiety more detestable. Sent to the full.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—angels' bread, abundance. Scorning such provision is monstrous.

Psalms 78:26

26He caused the east wind to blow in the heavens, and by his power he led out the south wind;

Arminian

East wind; by his power the south wind. Wesley: First eastern, then southern wind.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—winds as servants. Power leads the weather for judgment-gift.

Reformed

Calvin: flesh granted differently from manna—fatherly manna vs gluttony's flesh. Winds obey; elements execute his command.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—east and south by power. Distance cannot bar his will.

Psalms 78:27

27he rained meat on them like dust, winged birds like the sand of the seas;

Arminian

Rained meat like dust; winged birds like sand. Wesley: Fowl—God took away use of wings; fell to Israel.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—quail deluge. Miracle of quantity and fall.

Reformed

Calvin: flesh as dust, birds as sand—undoubted proof of power they malignantly denied.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—meat rained. Lavish answer to lust is not the same as fatherly manna.

Psalms 78:28

28he let them fall in the midst of their camp, all around their dwellings.

Arminian

Fell in midst of camp, around dwellings. Shared placement of the catch.

Arminian pastoral: Food at the tent door. Proximity of gift and later wrath (vv. 30–31).

Reformed

Calvin: fall in his camp/tabernacles—Israel's camp as God's. Provision into the heart of the people.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—midst of camp. No scavenging hunt required; judgment arrives mid-feast.

Psalms 78:29

29And they ate and were well filled, for he gave them what they craved.

Arminian

They ate and were well filled; he gave what they craved. Shared filled craving.

Arminian pastoral: Desire granted. Filling is not the same as blessing when lust leads.

Reformed

Calvin: ate and filled—not mere supply but ungovernable lust proved. God fulfills desire of those who fear him (Ps 145) differently; here he yields to perverse desire in wrath.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—filled with craving. Deadly burden can be the answer to unbridled want.

Psalms 78:30

30But before they had satisfied their craving, while the food was still in their mouths,

Arminian

Before craving satisfied, food still in mouths. Shared unfinished lust.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley silent; meat between teeth when judgment hits. Desire outruns gratitude.

Reformed

Calvin: not estranged from lust—still burning while meat in mouth. Present felt pleasure when vengeance struck.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—food in mouth, lust unslaked. Abundance does not quench depraved appetite.

Psalms 78:31

31the anger of God rose against them, and he killed the strongest of them and laid low the young men of Israel.

Arminian

Anger of God rose; killed strongest; laid low young men. Wesley: Mightiest—most healthy and strong, likely most eager feeders.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—elite cut down. Strength is no shield against holy anger.

Reformed

Calvin: wrath ascended—sudden rising to judgment. Fat ones and chosen named so the robust who trust strength are conspicuously judged.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—strongest fall. Pride of vigor meets the plague mid-banquet.

Psalms 78:32

32In spite of all this, they still sinned; despite his wonders, they did not believe.

Arminian

In spite of all this they still sinned; despite wonders, did not believe. Shared hardened unbelief after plague.

Arminian pastoral: Rod should teach; continued sin after wonders is willful. Classical Arminians urge repentance while wonders still preach.

Reformed

Calvin: fools wise under the rod—yet these unreformed; monstrous obduracy. Wondrous works include the plague; disbelief of word and despising miracles; plagues increased.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—still sinned, did not believe. Obstinacy after severe spectacle is near despair of reform.

Psalms 78:33

33So he made their days vanish like a breath, and their years in terror.

Arminian

Days vanish like a breath; years in terror. Wesley: Vanity—tedious fruitless marches; Trouble—diseases, dangers, perplexities.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—wasted years under curse. Wilderness chronology as judgment.

Reformed

Calvin: whole people consumed—forty years confirmed. Days in vanity like smoke; years in haste/tumult—strength proud suddenly withered.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—breath-days, terror-years. Righteous retribution for obstinacy.

Psalms 78:34

34When he killed them, they sought him; they repented and sought God earnestly.

Arminian

When he killed them, they sought him; repented and sought earnestly. Wesley: Returned—from idols; Enquired—speedily sought ease and safety.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—death awakens seeking. Yet later verses expose flattery; seek in sincerity.

Reformed

Calvin: aggravation—convinced yet not sincerely humbled; mocked God with false pretenses. Severe killing opened eyes to discover desperate wickedness.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—when slain, they sought. Fear-seeking without upright heart (vv. 36–37) is hollow.

Psalms 78:35

35They remembered that God was their rock, the Most High God their redeemer.

Arminian

Remembered God their rock, Most High their redeemer. Wesley: Redeemer—God alone preserved and alone could help.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—rock and redeemer confessed. Memory of redemption should stick.

Reformed

Calvin: remembered Rock and Redeemer—yet from fallen redemption; eyes opened to strip excuse of ignorance.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—Rock, Redeemer recalled. Right titles on the tongue can still mask a wrong heart.

Psalms 78:36

36But they flattered him with their mouths; they lied to him with their tongues.

Arminian

Flattered with mouths; lied with tongues. Wesley: Lied—false protestations of sincere future obedience.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—mouth religion. Classical Arminians demand unfeigned confession, not fear-flattery.

Reformed

Calvin: perfidy—no sincere confession; constrained slavish flattery while wishing truth extinguished. Guard hidden hypocrisy as well as gross.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—flattery and lies. Assent without pure affection is double heart.

Psalms 78:37

37Their heart was not steadfast toward him; they were not faithful to his covenant.

Arminian

Heart not steadfast; not faithful to his covenant. Shared unstable heart and covenant breach.

Arminian pastoral: Steadfastness required. Faithfulness is real loyalty, not momentary panic piety.

Reformed

Calvin: source—not steadfast/faithful; what lacks unfeigned purity is lying before God. Covenant-breakers toward faithfulness of divine promises.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—heart and covenant. Mutual relation: God's faithful covenant answered by unfeigned faith.

Psalms 78:38

38Yet he, being compassionate, atoned for their iniquity and did not destroy them; he restrained his anger often and did not stir up all his wrath.

Arminian

Yet compassionate, atoned for iniquity, did not destroy; restrained anger often, not all wrath. Shared patience and forbearance.

Arminian pastoral: Compassion multiplies restraint. Classical Arminians celebrate mercy that spares and atones while still calling for true return—God's resolve to show mercy is firm, not hesitant.

Reformed

Calvin: merciful, expiated iniquity, did not destroy; multiplied turning away anger; mitigated chastisements. Mercy essential attribute—not induced by desert; punishments moderate versus guilt.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—compassion, atonement, restrained wrath. Underserved goodness sustains a remnant seed.

Psalms 78:39

39He remembered that they were but flesh, a wind that passes and comes not again.

Arminian

Remembered they were flesh; wind that passes and comes not again. Shared frailty moved pity.

Arminian pastoral: Flesh and passing wind—ground of compassion. Humility fits creatures who do not return to earthly life.

Reformed

Calvin: remembered flesh—spirit/breath fleeting. Nothing in us but misery moves pity; pure undeserved goodness sustains. Not excluding resurrection; earthly state alone viewed.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—flesh, passing wind. Compassion rests on our frailty and his goodness, not our merit.

Psalms 78:40

40How often they rebelled against him in the wilderness and grieved him in the desert!

Arminian

How often rebelled in wilderness; grieved him in the desert. Shared frequent provocation.

Arminian pastoral: "How often" piles sins. Desert grief of God is real relational language without denying his settled holiness.

Reformed

Calvin: confirms—provoked so often they must have perished thousand times but for mercy. Wilderness includes place and time; fresh benefits and short span aggravate.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—often rebelled and grieved. Interrogative heightens continual sinning.

Psalms 78:41

41They tested God again and again and provoked the Holy One of Israel.

Arminian

Tested again and again; provoked the Holy One of Israel. Shared repeated testing.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley's "limited" (AV)—fencing God's power by unbelief. Do not shrink the Holy One to your measure.

Reformed

Calvin: returned—continued course of sinning; tempted and limited—metaphor of marking out, shutting God within bars when operations are capped by human fancy.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—tested and provoked the Holy One. Unbelief that measures God by small capacity pulls him from his throne.

Psalms 78:42

42They did not remember his power or the day when he redeemed them from the foe,

Arminian

Did not remember his power or the day he redeemed them from the foe. Wesley: Hand—glorious works; Enemy—day delivered from Pharaoh.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—forgotten redemption day. Memory of the foe's defeat should curb new tests.

Reformed

Calvin: remembered not his hand in the day of deliverance from the oppressor. Forgetfulness of unusual stretched-out hand leaves no excuse.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—power and redemption day forgotten. Wilful amnesia feeds waywardness.

Psalms 78:43

43when he performed his signs in Egypt and his marvels in the fields of Zoan.

Arminian

Signs in Egypt; marvels in fields of Zoan. Shared reprise of exodus signs.

Arminian pastoral: Zoan again—public marvels. Teach children these signs (vv. 4–6).

Reformed

Calvin: signs then miracles—Egypt stricken with terror that should not have vanished. Both terms heighten what forgetfulness buried.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—signs and marvels in Zoan. Rehearsal serves accusation of ingratitude.

Psalms 78:44

44He turned their rivers to blood, so that they could not drink of their streams.

Arminian

Rivers to blood; could not drink streams. Shared first-plague memory.

Arminian pastoral: Nile turned—judgment on Egypt's life-water. Historic plague, not allegory only.

Reformed

Calvin: enumerates enough to open wickedness—not full order. Extraordinary plagues, previously unheard-of.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—blood rivers. Accumulation of judgments should have stuck in Israel's mind.

Psalms 78:45

Arminian

Swarms of flies; frogs destroyed them. Wesley: Flies extraordinary in nature and hurt; likewise frogs.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—devouring swarms. Created pests as rods of God.

Reformed

Calvin: mixture/flies and frog—destroying agents. Variety of terms enhances vengeance.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—flies and frogs. Nature armed against oppressors.

Crops to destroying locust; fruit of labor to the locust. Wesley: Labour—herbs by their care.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—labor's fruit eaten. Toil without divine favor fails.

Reformed

Psalms 78:47

Vines with hail; sycamores with frost. Wesley: Under vines and sycamores all trees; hail/frost destroy fruit and sometimes trees.

Reformed

Calvin: vines and wild figs/sycamores with hailstones—severe loss on valued Egyptian produce.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—hail and frost on trees. Agricultural pride leveled.

Psalms 78:48

Cattle to hail; flocks to thunderbolts. Shared livestock plague.

Arminian pastoral: Herds and flocks given over. Wealth on the hoof is not secure against God.

Reformed

Calvin: cattle to hail, flocks to thunderbolts—continuing the cascade of wrath.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—cattle and flocks. Animate wealth under the same sentence.

Psalms 78:50

Psalms 78:51

Struck every firstborn; firstfruits of strength in tents of Ham. Wesley: Ham—Egyptians, posterity of Ham.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—firstborn struck. Tents of Ham name the judged line.

Reformed

Calvin: firstborn as firstfruits of strength; tents of Ham—Misraim son of Ham. Free love toward Shem's seed preferred without intrinsic desert.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—firstborn in Ham's tents. Election-mercy to Israel stands out against Egypt's loss.

Psalms 78:52

52Then he led out his people like sheep and guided them in the wilderness like a flock.

Arminian

Led out his people like sheep; guided in wilderness like a flock. Shared shepherd exodus.

Arminian pastoral: Sheep and flock—tender guidance after plagues. People go forth under care.

Reformed

Calvin: fatherly love—people as sheep; no wisdom or power of their own; God condescends as shepherd.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—sheep led, flock guided. Mean servile Israel shepherded against warlike foes.

Psalms 78:53

53He led them in safety, so that they were not afraid, but the sea overwhelmed their enemies.

Arminian

Led in safety, not afraid; sea overwhelmed enemies. Shared safe conduct vs drowned foe.

Arminian pastoral: Safety and fearlessness as gift. Enemies under the sea.

Reformed

Calvin: conducted in safety—not that they always felt confident, but with God as guardian they had no just cause to fear; unbelief bred panic questions.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—safety; sea covers enemies. Objective protection stands even when hearts flutter.

Psalms 78:54

54And he brought them to his holy land, to the mountain which his right hand had won.

Arminian

Brought to his holy land; mountain his right hand won. Wesley: Holy place—Canaan separated; Mountain—mountainous country.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—holy land, won mountain. Gifted geography of presence.

Reformed

Calvin: holy border; mountain his right hand acquired—inheritance secured by his power.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—holy land by his right hand. Arrival is purchased grace, not Israelite prowess.

Psalms 78:55

55He drove out nations before them; he apportioned them for a possession and settled the tribes of Israel in their tents.

Arminian

Drove out nations; apportioned possession; settled tribes in tents. Shared conquest allotment.

Arminian pastoral: Nations expelled; lots assigned. Tribes dwell by gift.

Reformed

Calvin: expelled heathen; inheritance by line; Israel in their tents—settled after driving out.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—nations out, tribes in. Possession is apportioned under God.

Psalms 78:56

56Yet they tested and rebelled against the Most High God and did not keep his testimonies,

Arminian

Yet tested and rebelled against Most High; did not keep testimonies. Shared relapse in the land.

Arminian pastoral: Land-gift does not automate fidelity. Testimonies still bind.

Reformed

Calvin: after settlement they tempted and provoked; kept not testimonies—ingratitude in the inheritance.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—tested, rebelled, testimonies broken. New stage, same revolt pattern.

Psalms 78:57

57but turned away and acted treacherously like their fathers; they twisted like a deceitful bow.

Arminian

Turned away; treacherous like fathers; twisted like a deceitful bow. Wesley: Deceitful bow—breaks when drawn or shoots awry, frustrates archer.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—treachery and useless bow. Promise of aim without true flight.

Reformed

Calvin: turned back, treacherous like fathers; deceitful bow—appears ready, fails in action.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—fathers' pattern, deceitful bow. Covenant people who will not shoot straight.

Psalms 78:58

58For they provoked him to anger with their high places; they moved him to jealousy with their idols.

Arminian

Provoked with high places; jealousy with idols. Shared cultic provocation.

Arminian pastoral: High places and graven images. Jealousy language is holy love offended.

Reformed

Calvin: high places and graven images moved him to anger/jealousy—idolatry as covenant adultery.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—high places, idols. Worship rivals awaken divine jealousy.

Psalms 78:59

59When God heard, he was full of wrath, and he utterly rejected Israel.

Arminian

When God heard, full of wrath, utterly rejected Israel. Wesley: Heard—perceived after manner of men.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—wrath and rejection. Historic sentence on persistent idolatry—not casual mood.

Reformed

Calvin: heard and wroth—long-suffering abused; cry ascended; weight of punishment shows offense. Abhorred Israel when ark departed as sign of farewell.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—heard, wrath, rejected. Patient God finally exhibits severe judgment.

Psalms 78:60

60He forsook his dwelling at Shiloh, the tent where he dwelt among mankind,

Arminian

Forsook dwelling at Shiloh, tent among mankind. Wesley: Shiloh; Among men—condescension and folly of despising the privilege.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—Shiloh forsaken. Presence withdrawn as judgment on scorned nearness.

Reformed

Calvin: bereft of presence—only true comfort; ark carried testifies departure. Shiloh habitation among men—God near by power and grace; wickedness constrained forsaking.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—Shiloh tent forsaken. Warning to all who flatter themselves on false grounds of presence (Jer 7).

Psalms 78:61

61and delivered his power to captivity, his glory to the hand of the foe.

Arminian

Delivered his power to captivity, glory to the foe. Wesley: Strength/glory—the ark; Enemies—Philistines.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—ark as strength and glory captured. Sign and pledge removed.

Reformed

Calvin: strength into captivity, beauty/glory into enemy hand—not that Philistines imprisoned God, but protection withdrawn as if the general taken.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—power and glory delivered. Hyperbole aggravates Israel's heinous offense.

Psalms 78:62

62He gave his people over to the sword and vented his wrath on his heritage.

Arminian

Gave people over to sword; vented wrath on his heritage. Shared heritage under wrath.

Arminian pastoral: Sword and heritage—covenant people not immune. Wrath on what he claims.

Reformed

Calvin: shut up to the sword; wroth with inheritance—flower of people consumed; fire metaphor for sudden slaughter (1 Sam 4).

Reformed pastoral: Shared—sword on the people, wrath on heritage. Own inheritance can taste the rod.

Psalms 78:63

63Fire devoured their young men, and their young women had no marriage song.

Arminian

Fire devoured young men; young women no marriage song. Shared social desolation.

Arminian pastoral: No nuptial joy—demographic judgment. War silences wedding songs.

Reformed

Calvin: fire devouring chosen—metaphor; maidens not applauded—nuptial songs cease for want of men.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—youth dead, no marriage song. Calamity heaped in domestic silence.

Psalms 78:64

64Their priests fell by the sword, and their widows made no lamentation.

Arminian

Priests fell by sword; widows made no lamentation. Wesley: Priests—Hophni and Phinehas; No lamentation—prevented by death or invasion.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—Eli's house. Even priests fall; mourning disrupted.

Reformed

Calvin: priests under special protection perish; widows make no lamentation—sorrow-death or captivity forbids rites.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—priests by sword, widows silent. Every kind of calamity heaped.

Psalms 78:65

65Then the Lord awoke as from sleep, like a strong man shouting because of wine.

Arminian

LORD awoke as from sleep; strong man shouting because of wine. Shared anthropomorphic waking.

Arminian pastoral: Asleep figure—not literal slumber. Sudden arousal for defense and shame of foes.

Reformed

Calvin: awoke as asleep—made drunk by people's sins so watch failed; yet not long sunk; heathen mockery of majesty awoke him like a giant after wine.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—awoke as from sleep. Figure teaches delayed then decisive action for his glory.

Psalms 78:66

66And he put his adversaries to rout; he put them to everlasting shame.

Arminian

Put adversaries to rout; everlasting shame. Wesley: Smote—with piles; Reproach—golden emerods with returned ark as monuments of shame.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—Philistine shame. Everlasting reproach on ark-mockers.

Reformed

Calvin: smote enemies behind; everlasting disgrace—awful vengeance shows covenant not disannulled even while Israel still bears traces of chastisement.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—adversaries routed, everlasting shame. God avenges his glory on the uncircumcised.

Psalms 78:67

67He rejected the tent of Joseph; he did not choose the tribe of Ephraim,

Arminian

Rejected tent of Joseph; did not choose tribe of Ephraim. Wesley: Refused—ark no longer in Shiloh's tabernacle in Joseph/Ephraim.

Arminian pastoral: Shared historic transfer of the ark's seat. Rejection of Joseph's tent is cultic relocation under judgment—not spite of whole genealogy forever.

Reformed

Calvin: rejection—ark's removal deprives honor; Ephraim rejected as ark-residence (not fully severed from the church body). Traces of punishment remain even after Philistines struck; scornful note as revolt later cuts ten tribes.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—Joseph's tent rejected, Ephraim not chosen for the dwelling. Judicial reshaping of where God sets his name.

Key contrast Both confess God rejected Joseph's tent and did not choose Ephraim for the ark's seat. Arminians typically follow Wesley's historical reading: Shiloh forsaken, ark removed from Ephraim's bounds under judgment. Reformed readers typically hear Calvin's sharper edge: free judicial rejection of that dwelling privilege as lasting monument of wrath, while still not treating every Ephraimite as finally cut from the church.

Psalms 78:68

68but he chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion, which he loves.

Arminian

Chose tribe of Judah, Mount Zion which he loves. Wesley: Chose—for seat of ark and worship.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—Judah and beloved Zion as historic worship seat. Love language marks place of name without turning the verse into abstract metaphysics.

Reformed

Calvin: preferred Judah; chose Zion—sanctuary there solely from free unmerited love, not place's worthiness. Relative as causal: because he loved. Second restitution as free as Abrahamic adoption; church gathered by pure grace in all ages.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—Judah chosen, Zion loved. Unconditioned love of the place for the people's good.

Key contrast Both confess God chose Judah and Mount Zion which he loves as the ark's seat. Arminians typically hear Wesley's focus: chosen for worship and ark residence in redemptive history. Reformed readers typically stress Calvin: Zion enriched by free good pleasure alone—no intrinsic merit of place or people; church always gathered by pure grace.

Psalms 78:69

69He built his sanctuary like the high heavens, like the earth, which he has founded forever.

Arminian

Built sanctuary like high heavens, like earth founded forever. Wesley: Sanctuary—Solomon's temple; Palaces—magnificent; Established—fixed, not moved like tabernacle.

Arminian pastoral: Shared—temple stability versus mobile tent. Forever language pledges settled worship.

Reformed

Calvin: sanctuary like high places—conspicuous; like earth established forever—not temporary palace. Perpetuity in Christ the truth of the figure; Chaldean ruin answered by the antitype.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—sanctuary founded forever. Stability celebrated is finally Christ-ward.

Psalms 78:70

70He chose David his servant and took him from the sheepfolds;

Arminian

Chose David his servant; took him from the sheepfolds. Shared shepherd-to-king.

Arminian pastoral: Choice of David from folds—historic elevation of the servant. Vocational election to rule Jacob.

Reformed

Calvin: after temple, kingdom—chief signs of favor; David made king from sheepfold, peasant to throne. Grace not limited to his person; worth of Abraham's children flows from mercy. Christ our king and priest typified; lowly start matches the type.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—David chosen from sheepfolds. Pure good pleasure elevates the servant.

Key contrast Both confess God chose David his servant from the sheepfolds. Arminians typically emphasize vocational election of the shepherd to feed Israel in history. Reformed readers typically follow Calvin: exaltation from peasant cot displays free grace; kingdom and priesthood as unmerited favor, fulfilled in Christ's lowly then royal work.

Psalms 78:71

71from following the nursing ewes he brought him to shepherd Jacob his people, Israel his inheritance.

Arminian

From nursing ewes to shepherd Jacob his people, Israel his inheritance. Shared transfer of flock-care.

Arminian pastoral: Ewes to Israel—same shepherd skill redirected. People and inheritance named as God's.

Reformed

Calvin: grace furthered—keeper of sheep made shepherd of chosen heritage. Allusion teaches lawful kings must be like shepherds, not tyrants.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—from ewes to Jacob/Israel. Shepherd-king pattern under God's claim on the flock.

Psalms 78:72

72With upright heart he shepherded them and guided them with his skillful hand.

Arminian

With upright heart he shepherded; guided with skillful hand. Shared integrity and skill of David.

Arminian pastoral: Upright heart and skillful hand—model rule. Close calls the people to grateful fidelity under such care.

Reformed

Calvin: fed in uprightness; guided by prudence of hands—Spirit-governed success. Indirect rebuke of later ingratitude that shook off the holy order and yoke.

Reformed pastoral: Shared—upright heart, skillful hand. David's fidelity magnifies the people's later perverseness; Christ the greater shepherd remains.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on teaching the next generation, Ephraim's flight, Egypt and wilderness mercies, patience and judgment, Shiloh forsaken, and the choice of Judah, Zion, and David—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 78:1–72.

Arminian Notable voices

“My law—The doctrine which I am about to deliver. Parable—Weighty sentences. Dark sayings—Not that the words are hard to be understood, but the things, God's transcendent goodness, their unparalleled ingratitude. Testimony—His law, a witness between God and men. Ephraim—Put for all Israel; may refer to the overthrow of 1 Samuel 4; the psalmist then relates causes unto Shiloh forsaken and removal to Judah and Zion. Tempted—Desired a proof of God's power; lust—not necessary subsistence but luxurious appetite. Angels' food—Manna by ministry of angels. Vanity—Tedious fruitless marches; trouble—diseases and dangers. Lied—False protestations of future obedience. Deceitful bow—Breaks when drawn or shoots awry. Shiloh—Tent among men; condescension despised. Strength and glory—The ark. Refused—Ark no longer in Joseph/Ephraim; chose Judah for seat of ark and worship; sanctuary—Solomon's temple, fixed forever.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 78

Reformed Notable voices

“Hearken to my instruction: even believers are backward to learn; mashal and pointed sayings stir attention to benefits and ingratitude. Fathers must transmit the law as inheritance; trust first, remembrance of works, then obedience. The fathers a rebellious generation—God turns hearts by his Spirit, yet the stubborn remain blameworthy. Ephraim armed yet fled; they kept not the covenant and forgot wonders. In the desert they provoked the Most High, tempted God by craving, believed not nor trusted his salvation; manna was fatherly, flesh was lust answered in wrath. When he slew them they sought him yet flattered and lied; he, merciful, expiated iniquity and restrained anger, remembering they were flesh. They limited the Holy One; he forsook Shiloh and delivered his strength to captivity, then awoke and shamed the foe. He rejected Joseph's tent, chose Judah and Zion which he loved of free unmerited love, built a sanctuary founded forever in Christ the truth of the figure, and chose David from the sheepfolds to shepherd Israel with upright heart and prudent hand.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 78
