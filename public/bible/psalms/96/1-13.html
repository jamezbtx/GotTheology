Passage

Psalms 96

Verses 1–13 · WEB · Voices

Sing to the Lord a new song, all the earth; bless his name; tell his salvation day by day; declare his glory and marvelous works among the nations. Great is the Lord, feared above all gods—for the peoples' gods are idols, but the LORD made the heavens. Honor and majesty are before him. Strength and beauty in his sanctuary. Families of nations, ascribe glory and strength; bring an offering into his courts; worship in holy array. Tremble, all the earth. Say among the nations, The Lord reigns—the world stands firm; he judges the peoples with equity. Let heavens, earth, sea, field, and forest rejoice before the Lord, for he comes to judge the earth in righteousness and faithfulness.

Psalms 96:1

1Sing to the LORD a new song! Sing to the LORD, all the earth.

Arminian

Sing to the LORD a new song; sing to the LORD, all the earth! Wesley: O sing—upon this new and great occasion, not the ark's removal, but the coming of the Messiah. Both note: new song; all the earth.

The occasion is Messianic greatness. All-earth scope matches the newness of the song.

Reformed

Calvin: sing a new song to Jehovah, all the earth—whole world summoned; new song marks extraordinary mercy when the gospel gathers the nations.

New song; all the earth. Universal praise answers unprecedented salvation.

Key contrast Both hear a new song for all the earth. Arminians, with Wesley, typically stress the occasion as the Messiah's coming rather than a mere ark procession. Calvin’s heirs stress the whole world's call and the gospel-era mercy that makes the song "new." Unprecedented praise; earth-wide summons.

Psalms 96:2

2Sing to the LORD! Bless his name! Proclaim his salvation from day to day!

Arminian

Proclaim his salvation from day to day. Common confession: blessing the name; daily telling.

Salvation is news for every day, not a one-time shout. Blessing the name keeps the tongue busy with God.

Reformed

Calvin: sing; bless his name; show forth his salvation from day to day—continuous publication, not a momentary flash.

Bless his name; salvation day to day. Persevering witness matches persevering mercy.

Psalms 96:3

3Declare his glory among the nations, his marvelous works among all the peoples.

Arminian

Declare his glory among the nations, his marvelous works among all the peoples.

Missions begin in declaration. Marvels of God are for all peoples' ears.

Reformed

Calvin: declare his glory among the heathen; his wonders among all people—the nations must hear what Zion already knows.

Glory among nations; works among peoples. Particular mercy becomes public report.

Psalms 96:4

4For the LORD is great, and greatly to be praised! He is to be feared above all gods.

Arminian

Great is the Lord, greatly to be praised; feared above all gods. Wesley: Gods—gods of the nations (v. 5). Both readings hold: greatness; praise; fear above gods.

Fear relative to rivals. Greatness fuels both praise and holy dread.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah is great and greatly to be praised; terrible above all gods—whatever is or is called deity cannot match him.

Doxology rests on comparison that ends all rivalry.

Psalms 96:5

5For all the gods of the peoples are idols, but the LORD made the heavens.

Arminian

All the gods of the peoples are idols, but the LORD made the heavens. Both agree: idols empty; Creator of heavens.

Worthless is the verdict. Maker of heavens unmasks carved nothings.

Reformed

Calvin: all gods of the nations are vanities; Jehovah made the heavens—creation unmasks idolatry.

Worthless idols; Lord made the heavens. Cosmic making is the idol-breaker.

Psalms 96:6

6Honor and majesty are before him. Strength and beauty are in his sanctuary.

Arminian

Splendor and majesty before him; strength and beauty in his sanctuary. Wesley: Before him—in his presence. Agreed: royal presence; sanctuary glory.

Beauty is not decoration only—it is God-near. Approach the sanctuary as strength's house.

Reformed

Calvin: strength and honor before him; power and glory in his sanctuary—presence language for worship's theater.

Splendor, majesty, strength, and beauty surround him. Sanctuary displays what presence holds.

Psalms 96:7

7Ascribe to the LORD, you families of nations, ascribe to the LORD glory and strength.

Arminian

Ascribe to the Lord, families of the peoples, glory and strength. Both hear: family-nations; ascribe glory and strength.

Peoples come by families. Ascribe means give what is already his.

Reformed

Calvin: give to Jehovah, assemblies of peoples, glory and strength—Gentiles summoned into the praise once centered in Zion; Christ's advent opens that door.

Families of peoples; glory and strength. The courts widen without shrinking God's due.

Psalms 96:8

8Ascribe to the LORD the glory due to his name. Bring an offering, and come into his courts.

Arminian

Ascribe the glory due to his name. Bring an offering; come into his courts.

The glory given to God is a debt owed to his name. Offering and entry are glad duty, not bribes.

Reformed

Calvin: give the glory of his name; bring an offering; come into his courts—worship with gift and near approach.

The call is to give the glory due his name, bring an offering, and enter his courts. Name-honor is paid in drawn-near praise.

Psalms 96:9

9Worship the LORD in holy array. Tremble before him, all the earth.

Arminian

Wesley: Beauty—clothed with gifts and graces needful in God's worship. Both see: holy splendor; earth-tremble.

Holiness is attire for worshipers. Trembling is reverence, not terror without hope.

Reformed

Calvin: worship in the beauty of the sanctuary; whole earth tremble before his face—holy beauty and holy fear together.

Splendor of holiness; tremble, all the earth. Beauty does not undo awe.

Psalms 96:10

10Say among the nations, “The LORD reigns.” The world is also established. It can’t be moved. He will judge the peoples with equity.

Arminian

Say among the nations, The Lord reigns! World established, never moved; he judges peoples with equity. Wesley: Reigneth—God hath now set up his kingdom; Established—nations enjoy lasting peace by it. Undisputed: reign; established world; equitable judgment.

Proclaim the reign as present kingdom news. Equity is the nations' hope under his rule.

Reformed

Calvin: say among the heathen, Jehovah reigns; world established, not moved; he judges peoples in righteousness—reign means righteous government that remakes crooked lives.

Lord reigns; world established; judge with equity. Announcement to nations is the Church's errand.

Psalms 96:11

11Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice. Let the sea roar, and its fullness!

Arminian

Let heavens be glad, earth rejoice; sea roar and its fullness. No dispute here: cosmic gladness; sea's roar as praise.

Creation joins the choir. Roar can be worship when the King reigns.

Reformed

Calvin: heavens rejoice, earth glad, sea thunder with its fullness—nature summoned to celebrate the reign.

The reign is too large for human voices alone.

Psalms 96:12

12Let the field and all that is in it exult! Then all the trees of the woods shall sing for joy

Arminian

Let the field exult and all in it; trees of the woods shall sing for joy. Both affirm: field and forest in praise.

Agriculture and woodland find a voice. Joy is the forest's sermon under the coming Judge.

Reformed

Calvin: field joyful and all in it; trees of the wood rejoice—creation's lower registers answer the King.

Field exults; trees sing. Judgment's approach is good news for a renewed world.

Psalms 96:13

13before the LORD; for he comes, for he comes to judge the earth. He will judge the world with righteousness, the peoples with his truth.

Arminian

Before the Lord—he comes to judge the earth; world in righteousness, peoples in faithfulness. Wesley: Before—at presence of Lord and Maker; Cometh—to set up his throne among all nations. Double "comes"; righteous, faithful judgment.

Coming is throne-setting among the nations. Faithfulness is the peoples' comfort in the verdict.

Reformed

Calvin: before Jehovah—he comes to judge the earth with righteousness and truth. Coming judgment is the reason creation sings.

He comes; judge in righteousness and faithfulness. Equity (v. 10) is sworn in again at the close.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

What earlier interpreters on each side wrote about Psalm 96:1–13.

Arminian Notable voices

“O sing—upon this new and great occasion, not the removal of the ark, but the coming of the Messiah. Gods—gods of the nations, as the next verse expounds. Before him—in his presence. Beauty—clothed with all gifts and graces needful in God's worship. Reigneth—God hath now set up his kingdom in the world. Established—nations shall by means of it enjoy established and lasting peace. Before—at the presence and approach of their Lord and Maker. Cometh—to set up his throne among all the nations of the earth.”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 96

Reformed Notable voices

“Sing to Jehovah a new song; sing all the earth—exhorting the whole world; new song for extraordinary mercy, the gospel gathering of the nations. Bless his name; show forth salvation day to day; declare glory among the heathen. Jehovah is great and greatly to be praised; terrible above all gods—for all gods of the nations are vanities, but Jehovah made the heavens. Strength and honor before him; power and glory in his sanctuary. Give glory and strength, assemblies of peoples; bring an offering into his courts; worship in the beauty of the sanctuary; whole earth tremble. Say among the heathen, Jehovah reigns; the world established, shall not be moved; he shall judge peoples in righteousness. Heavens rejoice, earth glad, sea thunder; field joyful; trees of the wood rejoice before Jehovah—for he comes to judge the earth with righteousness and truth. Advent of Christ opens the courts to the nations as one body with the ancient people.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 96
