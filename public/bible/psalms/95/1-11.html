Passage

Psalms 95

Verses 1–11 · WEB · Voices

Oh come, sing to the Lord, the rock of our salvation; enter his presence with thanksgiving and songs. He is a great God and King above all gods; depths and heights, sea and dry land are his. Come, worship and kneel before the LORD, our Maker, for he is our God. We the people of his pasture, the sheep in his care. Today, oh that you would hear his voice! Don’t harden your heart, as at Meribah and Massah, when the fathers tested him though they had seen his work. Forty years he loathed that generation—astray in heart, ignorant of his ways—and swore they would not enter his rest.

Psalms 95:1

1Oh come, let’s sing to the LORD. Let’s shout aloud to the rock of our salvation!

Arminian

Oh come, let’s sing to the LORD; let’s shout aloud to the rock of our salvation. Where they meet: invitation; rock of salvation.

The plain call stands—come and sing. Salvation's rock is reason enough for noise that is joy, not noise for its own sake.

Reformed

Calvin: come, rejoice before Jehovah; shout aloud to the Rock of our salvation. Praise recounts benefits; the Rock titles God as sure help.

Come; sing; rock of our salvation. Begin the psalm with gathered gladness, not private muttering only.

Psalms 95:2

2Let’s come before his presence with thanksgiving. Let’s extol him with songs!

Arminian

Come before his presence with thanksgiving; joyful noise with songs of praise. Both traditions hear: presence; thanksgiving; praise-songs.

Thanksgiving is the door into presence. Songs carry gratitude, not self-display.

Reformed

Calvin: come before his face with praise; in psalms shout for joy. Face/presence means near approach in worship.

The people come into God’s presence with thanksgiving and praise. Nearness is answered with thanks, not presumption.

Psalms 95:3

3For the LORD is a great God, a great King above all gods.

Arminian

For the LORD is a great God, a great King above all gods. Wesley: Gods—above all called gods: angels, earthly potentates, especially heathen false gods. Greatness; kingship above rivals.

Name the rivals without granting them deity. Great King relativizes every lesser throne.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah is a great God and great King above all gods—whatever is or is accounted deity; idols are nothing; angels only reflect sparks. Convince the world who alone is to be feared.

Great God; King above all gods. Worship's ground is unmatched deity.

Psalms 95:4

4In his hand are the deep places of the earth. The heights of the mountains are also his.

Arminian

In his hand are the deep places of the earth. The heights of the mountains are also his also. Wesley: Hand—under his government; Strength—strongest or highest mountains. Common reading: depths and heights owned.

For the church: geography preaches providence. Nothing too deep or high to escape his hand.

Reformed

Calvin: deep places of the earth and heights of mountains are in his hand—creation's extremes under government.

The depths and the heights both belong to God. The King's domain is the whole vertical range.

Psalms 95:5

5The sea is his, and he made it. His hands formed the dry land.

Arminian

The sea is his, and he made it; his hands formed the dry land. Maker-rights over sea and land.

God owns the sea and the land because he made them. Creator hands invite creature praise (vv. 1–2).

Reformed

Calvin: sea is his, and he made it; hands formed dry land—maker's claim seals the call to worship.

Sea his; dry land formed. Creation underwrites the doxology.

Psalms 95:6

6Oh come, let’s worship and bow down. Let’s kneel before the LORD, our Maker,

Arminian

Oh come, worship and bow down; kneel before the Lord, our Maker. Triple posture—worship, bow, kneel; Maker.

Body joins heart. Maker names why kneeling is fitting, not optional flattery.

Reformed

Calvin: come, worship, bow down, kneel before Jehovah our Maker—language grows vehement as he turns to the chosen people's gratitude for free favor and spiritual distinction above the nations.

The call is to worship, bow, and kneel before our Maker. Entire devotion answers electing kindness.

Psalms 95:7

7for he is our God. We are the people of his pasture, and the sheep in his care. Today, oh that you would hear his voice!

Arminian

For he is our God; we are people of his pasture, sheep in his care. Today, oh that you would hear his voice— Wesley: Pasture—feeds and keeps in his own pasture/land; Sheep—special care; Today—forthwith, presently. Both confess: covenant flock; urgent today.

Being his flock is the reason to listen. Today means now—do not postpone the Shepherd's voice. Hebrews 3–4 takes the same today as living warning.

Reformed

Calvin: he is our God; people of his pastures; flock in his care. Today, oh that you will hear his voice—Law as crook showing him Shepherd; today marks the Jews' privilege of near revelation. Hebrews presses the same today upon the church.

Privilege intensifies the summons; both camps read Hebrews 3–4 as shared pastoral warning, not a timetable quarrel.

Psalms 95:8

8Don’t harden your heart, as at Meribah, as in the day of Massah in the wilderness,

Arminian

Do not harden your heart, as at Meribah, as in the day of Massah in the wilderness. Wesley: Harden not—by obstinate unbelief; Provocation—bold contest with God; Temptation—day you tempted me. Meribah/Massah pattern; soft heart commanded.

Hardness is obstinate unbelief. The wilderness sites are moral memory, not antiquarian trivia. Hebrews warns the same way: do not repeat the fathers' contest.

Reformed

Calvin: harden not your heart as in Meribah/Massah—Shepherd's kindness becomes call to pliable submission; fathers' obstinacy is the foil. Hebrews carries the warning forward to hearers of the gospel.

Do not harden; Meribah; Massah. Softness of heart is the flock's duty under the crook.

Psalms 95:9

9when your fathers tempted me, tested me, and saw my work.

Arminian

When fathers tempted me, tested me, and proof, though they had seen my work. Wesley: Works—both of mercy and of justice. Either reading: testing despite seen works.

Sight did not produce trust. Seen mercy and justice still met provocation—beware repeating that contradiction.

Reformed

Calvin: fathers tempted and proved me though they had seen my work—aggravation of unbelief after visible deeds. The warning assumes remembered history.

Tested; proved; seen work. Revelation resisted is worse than ignorance.

Psalms 95:10

10Forty long years I was grieved with that generation, and said, “They are a people who err in their heart. They have not known my ways.”

Arminian

For Forty long years I was grieved with that generation—astray in heart; have not known my ways. Wesley: Do err—insincere hearts bent to backsliding; Not known—after teaching and discoveries, they did not know or consider great things wrought for them. Both sides agree: long grief; heart-error; ways unknown.

Forty years names prolonged patience met by prolonged stray. Heart-error is the root; ignorance here is culpable inattention.

Reformed

Calvin: forty years I strove with this generation—they err in heart and have not known my ways. Long contention exposes an erring heart, not a brief lapse.

Duration teaches how seriously God takes unbelief in his flock.

Psalms 95:11

11Therefore I swore in my wrath, “They won’t enter into my rest.”

Arminian

Therefore I swore in my wrath, "They won’t enter into my rest." Wesley: My rest—the promised land, called the rest (Deut 12:9). Both traditions see: oath; wrath; barred rest. Hebrews 3–4 applies the same exclusion as warning to persevering faith.

Rest is gift that unbelief forfeits.

Reformed

Calvin: I swore in my wrath, They won’t enter into my rest—oath seals exclusion. Hebrews takes up the same rest-bar as living admonition for those who hear today.

Swore; wrath; shall not enter my rest. Both camps stand under Hebrews 3–4: today-hearing versus wilderness hardness.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Classic Arminian and Reformed comment on Psalm 95:1–11, in the writers’ own words.

Arminian Notable voices

“Gods—above all called gods: angels, earthly potentates, and especially heathen false gods. Hand—under his government; Strength—strongest or highest mountains. Pasture—whom he feeds and keeps in his own pasture, or land appropriated to himself. Sheep—under his special care. Today—forthwith or presently. Harden not—by obstinate unbelief. Provocation—bold and wicked contest with God in the wilderness. Temptation—day in which you tempted me. Works—both of mercy and of justice. Do err—hearts insincere and bent to backsliding. Not known—after all teaching and discoveries, they did not know or consider the great things wrought for them. My rest—the promised land, called the rest (Deut 12:9).”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 95

Reformed Notable voices

“Come, rejoice before Jehovah; joyful noise to the Rock of our salvation. Come before his face with praise. Jehovah is a great God and King above all gods; deep places and mountain heights in his hand; sea and dry land his making. Come, worship, bow, kneel before Jehovah our Maker—because he is our God, we the people of his pastures and flock of his hand; today, if you will hear his voice. Harden not your heart as in Meribah, as in Massah in the wilderness, when fathers tempted and proved me though they had seen my work. Forty years I strove with this generation—they err in heart and have not known my ways. I swore in my wrath they shall not enter my rest. The kindness of the Shepherd becomes the call to pliable submission; obstinacy of the fathers is held up as warning. Hebrews takes up the same today and the same barred rest.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 95
