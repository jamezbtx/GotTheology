Passage

Psalms 95

Verses 1–11 · ESV · Voices

Oh come, sing to the Lord, the rock of our salvation; enter his presence with thanksgiving and songs. He is a great God and King above all gods; depths and heights, sea and dry land are his. Come, worship and kneel before the Lord our Maker—for he is our God, and we the people of his pasture, the sheep of his hand. Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as at Meribah and Massah, when the fathers tested him though they had seen his work. Forty years he loathed that generation—astray in heart, ignorant of his ways—and swore they would not enter his rest.

Psalms 95:1

1Oh come, let us sing to the Lord; let us make a joyful noise to the rock of our salvation!

Arminian

Oh come, sing to the Lord; joyful noise to the rock of our salvation. Shared: invitation; rock of salvation.

Arminian pastoral: Wesley has no extra lemma here; the plain call stands—come and sing. Salvation's rock is reason enough for noise that is joy, not noise for its own sake.

Reformed

Calvin: come, rejoice before Jehovah; joyful noise to the Rock of our salvation. Praise recounts benefits; the Rock titles God as sure help.

Reformed pastoral: shared—come; sing; rock of our salvation. Begin the psalm with gathered gladness, not private muttering only.

Psalms 95:2

2Let us come into his presence with thanksgiving; let us make a joyful noise to him with songs of praise!

Arminian

Come into his presence with thanksgiving; joyful noise with songs of praise. Shared: presence; thanksgiving; praise-songs.

Arminian pastoral: thanksgiving is the door into presence. Songs carry gratitude, not self-display.

Reformed

Calvin: come before his face with praise; in psalms shout for joy. Face/presence means near approach in worship.

Reformed pastoral: shared—presence; thanksgiving; praise. Nearness is answered with thanks, not presumption.

Psalms 95:3

3For the Lord is a great God, and a great King above all gods.

Arminian

For the Lord is a great God, and a great King above all gods. Wesley: Gods—above all called gods: angels, earthly potentates, especially heathen false gods. Shared: greatness; kingship above rivals.

Arminian pastoral: name the rivals without granting them deity. Great King relativizes every lesser throne.

Reformed

Calvin: Jehovah is a great God and great King above all gods—whatever is or is accounted deity; idols are nothing; angels only reflect sparks. Convince the world who alone is to be feared.

Reformed pastoral: shared—great God; King above all gods. Worship's ground is unmatched deity.

Psalms 95:4

4In his hand are the depths of the earth; the heights of the mountains are his also.

Arminian

In his hand are the depths of the earth; the heights of the mountains are his also. Wesley: Hand—under his government; Strength—strongest or highest mountains. Shared: depths and heights owned.

Arminian pastoral: geography preaches providence. Nothing too deep or high to escape his hand.

Reformed

Calvin: deep places of the earth and heights of mountains are in his hand—creation's extremes under government.

Reformed pastoral: shared—depths; heights; his. The King's domain is the whole vertical range.

Psalms 95:5

5The sea is his, for he made it, and his hands formed the dry land.

Arminian

The sea is his, for he made it; his hands formed the dry land. Shared: maker-rights over sea and land.

Arminian pastoral: making grounds owning. Creator hands invite creature praise (vv. 1–2).

Reformed

Calvin: sea is his for he made it; hands formed dry land—maker's claim seals the call to worship.

Reformed pastoral: shared—sea his; dry land formed. Creation underwrites the doxology.

Psalms 95:6

6Oh come, let us worship and bow down; let us kneel before the Lord, our Maker!

Arminian

Oh come, worship and bow down; kneel before the Lord, our Maker. Shared: triple posture—worship, bow, kneel; Maker.

Arminian pastoral: body joins heart. Maker names why kneeling is fitting, not optional flattery.

Reformed

Calvin: come, worship, bow down, kneel before Jehovah our Maker—language grows vehement as he turns to the chosen people's gratitude for free favor and spiritual distinction above the nations.

Reformed pastoral: shared—worship; bow; kneel; Maker. Entire devotion answers electing kindness.

Psalms 95:7

7For he is our God, and we are the people of his pasture, and the sheep of his hand. Today, if you hear his voice,

Arminian

For he is our God; we are people of his pasture, sheep of his hand. Today, if you hear his voice— Wesley: Pasture—feeds and keeps in his own pasture/land; Sheep—special care; Today—forthwith, presently. Shared: covenant flock; urgent today.

Arminian pastoral: flock identity fuels hearing. Today means now—do not postpone the Shepherd's voice. Hebrews 3–4 takes the same today as living warning.

Reformed

Calvin: he is our God; people of his pastures; flock of his hand; today if you will hear his voice—Law as crook showing him Shepherd; today marks the Jews' privilege of near revelation. Hebrews presses the same today upon the church.

Reformed pastoral: shared—our God; pasture people; sheep of his hand; today hear. Privilege intensifies the summons; both camps read Hebrews 3–4 as shared pastoral warning, not a timetable quarrel.

Psalms 95:8

8do not harden your hearts, as at Meribah, as on the day at Massah in the wilderness,

Arminian

Do not harden your hearts, as at Meribah, as on the day at Massah in the wilderness. Wesley: Harden not—by obstinate unbelief; Provocation—bold contest with God; Temptation—day you tempted me. Shared: Meribah/Massah pattern; soft heart commanded.

Arminian pastoral: hardness is obstinate unbelief. The wilderness sites are moral memory, not antiquarian trivia. Hebrews warns the same way: do not repeat the fathers' contest.

Reformed

Calvin: harden not your heart as in Meribah/Massah—Shepherd's kindness becomes call to pliable submission; fathers' obstinacy is the foil. Hebrews carries the warning forward to hearers of the gospel.

Reformed pastoral: shared—do not harden; Meribah; Massah. Softness of heart is the flock's duty under the crook.

Psalms 95:9

9when your fathers put me to the test and put me to the proof, though they had seen my work.

Arminian

When fathers put me to the test and proof, though they had seen my work. Wesley: Works—both of mercy and of justice. Shared: testing despite seen works.

Arminian pastoral: sight did not produce trust. Seen mercy and justice still met provocation—beware repeating that contradiction.

Reformed

Calvin: fathers tempted and proved me though they had seen my work—aggravation of unbelief after visible deeds. The warning assumes remembered history.

Reformed pastoral: shared—tested; proved; seen work. Revelation resisted is worse than ignorance.

Psalms 95:10

10For forty years I loathed that generation and said, “They are a people who go astray in their heart, and they have not known my ways.”

Arminian

For forty years I loathed that generation—astray in heart; have not known my ways. Wesley: Do err—insincere hearts bent to backsliding; Not known—after teaching and discoveries, they did not know or consider great things wrought for them. Shared: long grief; heart-error; ways unknown.

Arminian pastoral: forty years names prolonged patience met by prolonged stray. Heart-error is the root; ignorance here is culpable inattention.

Reformed

Calvin: forty years I strove with this generation—they err in heart and have not known my ways. Long contention exposes an erring heart, not a brief lapse.

Reformed pastoral: shared—forty years; astray in heart; ways not known. Duration teaches how seriously God takes unbelief in his flock.

Psalms 95:11

11Therefore I swore in my wrath, “They shall not enter my rest.”

Arminian

Therefore I swore in my wrath, "They shall not enter my rest." Wesley: My rest—the promised land, called the rest (Deut 12:9). Shared: oath; wrath; barred rest. Hebrews 3–4 applies the same exclusion as warning to persevering faith.

Arminian pastoral: rest is gift that unbelief forfeits. Do not invent a camp fight over end-time charts here—hear the shared apostolic warning: soft hearts enter; hard hearts stay out.

Reformed

Calvin: I swore in my wrath they shall not enter my rest—oath seals exclusion. Hebrews takes up the same rest-bar as living admonition for those who hear today.

Reformed pastoral: shared—swore; wrath; shall not enter my rest. Both camps stand under Hebrews 3–4: today-hearing versus wilderness hardness.

Historic quotes

Voices from both camps

Historic words on joyful noise to the rock of salvation, the great King above all gods, kneeling before our Maker, sheep of his hand, and the today-warning against Meribah hardness that bars the rest—so each camp speaks in its own voice beside Psalm 95:1–11.

Arminian Notable voices

“Gods—above all called gods: angels, earthly potentates, and especially heathen false gods. Hand—under his government; Strength—strongest or highest mountains. Pasture—whom he feeds and keeps in his own pasture, or land appropriated to himself. Sheep—under his special care. Today—forthwith or presently. Harden not—by obstinate unbelief. Provocation—bold and wicked contest with God in the wilderness. Temptation—day in which you tempted me. Works—both of mercy and of justice. Do err—hearts insincere and bent to backsliding. Not known—after all teaching and discoveries, they did not know or consider the great things wrought for them. My rest—the promised land, called the rest (Deut 12:9).”
John Wesley · Explanatory Notes upon the Old Testament, Psalm 95

Reformed Notable voices

“Come, rejoice before Jehovah; joyful noise to the Rock of our salvation. Come before his face with praise. Jehovah is a great God and King above all gods; deep places and mountain heights in his hand; sea and dry land his making. Come, worship, bow, kneel before Jehovah our Maker—because he is our God, we the people of his pastures and flock of his hand; today, if you will hear his voice. Harden not your heart as in Meribah, as in Massah in the wilderness, when fathers tempted and proved me though they had seen my work. Forty years I strove with this generation—they err in heart and have not known my ways. I swore in my wrath they shall not enter my rest. The kindness of the Shepherd becomes the call to pliable submission; obstinacy of the fathers is held up as warning. Hebrews takes up the same today and the same barred rest.”
John Calvin · Commentary on the Psalms, Psalm 95

Quotations lightly abridged for length; wording follows standard English translations of the sources named.
