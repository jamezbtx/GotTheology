Passage

Deuteronomy 31

Verses 1–8 · WEB

Now one hundred twenty years old and barred from crossing the Jordan, Moses assures Israel that the LORD himself will go before them and that Joshua will lead. He charges the people and then Joshua, “Be strong and courageous,” because God “will not fail you nor forsake you.” The passage grounds courage in God’s presence rather than in any human leader.

Deuteronomy 31:1

1Moses went and spoke these words to all Israel.

Arminian

Moses goes and speaks to all Israel as his leadership nears its end. Arminian readers see a faithful servant using his final days to prepare the people for life without him.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see Moses completing the ministry God gave him. The verse opens a chapter about transition, in which God provides for his people’s future beyond any single leader.

Deuteronomy 31:2

2He said to them, “I am one hundred twenty years old today. I can no more go out and come in. The LORD has said to me, ‘You shall not go over this Jordan.’

Arminian

Moses says he is one hundred twenty years old and recalls that the LORD told him, “You shall not go over this Jordan.” Arminian readers see Moses accepting the consequence of Meribah with humility.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see Moses submitting to God’s word about his own life. His honest acknowledgment of limits sets the stage for pointing Israel to God rather than to himself.

Deuteronomy 31:3

3The LORD your God himself will go over before you. He will destroy these nations from before you, and you shall dispossess them. Joshua will go over before you, as the LORD has spoken.

Arminian

The LORD himself will go over before Israel, destroy the nations, and Joshua will lead. Arminian readers see God’s presence as the decisive assurance, with Joshua as his chosen instrument.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God as the true leader of the conquest, working through Joshua. The verse places God first and the human leader second, guarding against misplaced confidence.

Deuteronomy 31:4

4The LORD will do to them as he did to Sihon and to Og, the kings of the Amorites, and to their land, when he destroyed them.

Arminian

God will do to the nations as he did to Sihon and Og. Arminian readers see recent victories offered as evidence that encourages trust for what lies ahead.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see past acts of God as pledges of his future faithfulness. Sihon and Og were defeated on the east side of the Jordan, as Deuteronomy 2–3 recounts.

Deuteronomy 31:5

5The LORD will deliver them up before you, and you shall do to them according to all the commandment which I have commanded you.

Arminian

God will deliver the nations, and Israel must act according to all the commandment given. Arminian readers see God’s gift and Israel’s obedience held together in the conquest.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see divine deliverance calling for obedient action. The verse ties Israel’s conduct in the land to the commands already laid out in Deuteronomy.

Deuteronomy 31:6

6Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid or scared of them, for the LORD your God himself is who goes with you. He will not fail you nor forsake you.”

Arminian

Moses urges the people, “Be strong and courageous,” for God “will not fail you nor forsake you.” Arminian readers see courage grounded in God’s faithful presence rather than human strength.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s promise as the basis of confidence. Hebrews 13:5 echoes this assurance in the words “I will in no way leave you, neither will I in any way forsake you.”

Deuteronomy 31:7

7Moses called to Joshua, and said to him in the sight of all Israel, “Be strong and courageous, for you shall go with this people into the land which the LORD has sworn to their fathers to give them; and you shall cause them to inherit it.

Arminian

Moses tells Joshua before all Israel to be strong and courageous, for he will cause them to inherit the land. Arminian readers see a public commissioning that confirms Joshua’s role to the people.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s appointed leader recognized publicly. The same charge recurs in Joshua 1:6, where God says, “Be strong and courageous; for you shall cause this people to inherit the land which I swore to their fathers to give them.”

Deuteronomy 31:8

8The LORD himself is who goes before you. He will be with you. He will not fail you nor forsake you. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be discouraged.”

Arminian

Moses assures Joshua that the LORD goes before him and “will not fail you nor forsake you.” Arminian readers see personal encouragement for a leader facing an enormous task.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s presence pledged to his servant. The closing words “Don’t be afraid. Don’t be discouraged” address the fears any new leader might feel.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
