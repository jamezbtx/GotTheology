Passage

Deuteronomy 15

Verses 19–23 · WEB

Deuteronomy 15:19–23 deals with the firstborn males of herd and flock. They are set apart to the LORD, not worked or sheared, and eaten by the household before the LORD each year at the chosen place. A firstborn animal with a defect is not sacrificed but eaten at home like game, as long as its blood is poured out. Interpreters harmonize this with Numbers 18:15–18, which gives firstborn meat to the priests, in different ways. Both traditions agree that the law teaches that firstfruits belong to God.

Deuteronomy 15:19

19You shall dedicate all the firstborn males that are born of your herd and of your flock to the LORD your God. You shall do no work with the firstborn of your herd, nor shear the firstborn of your flock.

Arminian

Firstborn males of herd and flock are dedicated to the LORD, not worked or sheared. Arminians see the firstborn as a standing reminder that all increase comes from God.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and connect the law with God’s claim on the firstborn at the exodus (Exodus 13:2, 11–16). Both traditions agree on the verse.

Deuteronomy 15:20

20You shall eat it before the LORD your God year by year in the place which the LORD shall choose, you and your household.

Arminian

The household eats it yearly before the LORD at the chosen place. Arminians see worship as a joyful family meal in God’s presence.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree. Both traditions note that Numbers 18:15–18 gives firstborn meat to the priests and recognize that interpreters harmonize the two laws in different ways.

Deuteronomy 15:21

21If it has any defect—is lame or blind, or has any defect whatever, you shall not sacrifice it to the LORD your God.

Arminian

A firstborn with any defect is not to be sacrificed. Arminians see God deserving the best, not leftovers or damaged gifts.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and connect the requirement with Malachi 1:8 and with Christ as the unblemished offering (1 Peter 1:19). Both traditions agree on the verse.

Deuteronomy 15:22

22You shall eat it within your gates. The unclean and the clean shall eat it alike, as the gazelle and as the deer.

Arminian

A defective firstborn may be eaten at home by clean and unclean alike, like gazelle or deer. Arminians see God’s practical provision so that nothing is wasted.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that the animal becomes ordinary food (compare 12:15, 22). Both traditions read the verse as procedural and agree on its meaning.

Deuteronomy 15:23

23Only you shall not eat its blood. You shall pour it out on the ground like water.

Arminian

Do not eat the blood but pour it out like water. Arminians see continued respect for the life God gives, even in ordinary eating.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and connect the rule with Leviticus 17:11 and Genesis 9:4. Both traditions agree on the meaning of the verse for Israel.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
