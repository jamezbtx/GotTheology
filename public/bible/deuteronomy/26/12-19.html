Passage

Deuteronomy 26

Verses 12–19 · WEB

Every third year, the tithe is given locally to the Levite, the foreigner, the orphan, and the widow. The worshiper declares before the LORD that he has given it as commanded and asks God to bless Israel and its land. Moses closes the law section by describing a mutual declaration: Israel affirms the LORD as its God, and the LORD affirms Israel as his own possession.

Deuteronomy 26:12

12When you have finished tithing all the tithe of your increase in the third year, which is the year of tithing, then you shall give it to the Levite, to the foreigner, to the fatherless, and to the widow, that they may eat within your gates and be filled.

Arminian

In the third year, the tithe goes to the Levite, foreigner, orphan, and widow so they can eat and be filled (compare Deuteronomy 14:28–29). Worship includes care for the needy.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same concern and the same link with Deuteronomy 14:28–29. The third-year tithe turns worship toward filling the hungry within the gates. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 26:13

13You shall say before the LORD your God, “I have put away the holy things out of my house, and also have given them to the Levite, to the foreigner, to the fatherless, and to the widow, according to all your commandment which you have commanded me. I have not transgressed any of your commandments, neither have I forgotten them.

Arminian

The worshiper declares that he has given the holy portion as commanded and has not forgotten God’s commandments. This is a statement of faithfulness in this specific duty, not a claim of sinless perfection.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise read the declaration as sincere covenant obedience in a particular matter, offered as a response to grace rather than as merit. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 26:14

14I have not eaten of it in my mourning, neither have I removed any of it while I was unclean, nor given of it for the dead. I have listened to the LORD my God’s voice. I have done according to all that you have commanded me.

Arminian

He has not eaten the tithe while mourning or unclean, nor given any for the dead. Interpreters often connect “for the dead” with rites honoring the dead, though the exact practice is uncertain.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same uncertainty about what “for the dead” refers to, and they likewise refuse to press a firm identification. The main point is that the tithe was kept holy and used as God commanded. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 26:15

15Look down from your holy habitation, from heaven, and bless your people Israel, and the ground which you have given us, as you swore to our fathers, a land flowing with milk and honey.”

Arminian

“Look down from your holy habitation, from heaven, and bless your people Israel.” The obedient worshiper still depends on God for blessing.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that blessing remains God’s gift, grounded in his oath to the fathers, even after a confession of obedience. The worshiper still asks God to look down and bless. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 26:16

16Today the LORD your God commands you to do these statutes and ordinances. You shall therefore keep and do them with all your heart and with all your soul.

Arminian

Today God commands Israel to keep his statutes with all their heart and soul. Obedience is meant to be wholehearted, not mechanical (compare 6:5).

Reformed

Reformed readers emphasize the same inward obedience: the statutes are to be kept with all the heart and soul, not only in outward form. This is shared ground, echoing 6:5.

Deuteronomy 26:17

17You have declared today that the LORD is your God, and that you would walk in his ways, keep his statutes, his commandments, and his ordinances, and listen to his voice.

Arminian

Israel declares that the LORD is its God and that it will walk in his ways. Covenant involves a real commitment from the people.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same human commitment within the covenant: Israel declares that the LORD is its God and promises to walk in his ways. The declaration is real, and it answers God’s prior grace. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 26:18

18The LORD has declared today that you are a people for his own possession, as he has promised you, and that you should keep all his commandments.

Arminian

The LORD declares Israel to be his own possession, as he promised. Exodus 19:5–6 and 1 Peter 2:9 use similar language for God’s people.

Reformed

Reformed readers draw the same links. Both traditions see the covenant declarations as mutual, with God’s promise as the foundation. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 26:19

19He will make you high above all nations that he has made, in praise, in name, and in honor, and that you may be a holy people to the LORD your God, as he has spoken.

Arminian

God will set Israel above all nations in praise, name, and honor, so they may be a holy people. Israel’s honor serves God’s glory.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that Israel’s being set high among the nations serves God’s praise, name, and holiness, as he has spoken. Election is unto holiness, not mere privilege. This is shared ground.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
