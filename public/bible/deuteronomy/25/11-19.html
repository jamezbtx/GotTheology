Passage

Deuteronomy 25

Verses 11–19 · WEB

Further laws follow: a severe penalty for a woman who seizes a man’s genitals during a fight, a ban on dishonest weights and measures, and a command to remember Amalek’s attack on Israel’s weak and weary stragglers after the exodus and, once Israel has rest, to blot out Amalek’s memory.

Deuteronomy 25:11

11When men strive against each other, and the wife of one draws near to deliver her husband out of the hand of him who strikes him, and puts out her hand, and grabs him by his private parts,

Arminian

The case concerns a woman who intervenes in a fight by seizing the man’s genitals. The law protects the ability to have children, closely tied to the levirate law just before it.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same connection to preserving family lines and fruitfulness, which the preceding verses have just protected. Both traditions treat the case as part of Israel’s concern for the family name. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 25:12

12then you shall cut off her hand. Your eye shall have no pity.

Arminian

Her hand is to be cut off without pity. Interpreters debate whether it was meant literally or understood in some other way, and both traditions acknowledge its severity.

Reformed

Reformed readers acknowledge the same difficulty and the same interpretive debate. Both traditions read it within Israel’s civil law rather than as a rule for today.

Deuteronomy 25:13

13You shall not have in your bag diverse weights, one heavy and one light.

Arminian

No two sets of weights, one heavy and one light, may be kept for buying and selling. Cheating in trade is condemned as a sin, not merely a sharp practice.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Proverbs 11:1 calls a false balance an abomination to the LORD, so dishonest trade is an offense against God, not only against a neighbor. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 25:14

14You shall not have in your house diverse measures, one large and one small.

Arminian

No two sets of measures may be kept in the house either. Honesty applies at home as well as in the marketplace; private stock cannot hide a public cheat.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the ban extending from the trader’s bag to the household store. God requires integrity where no buyer is watching. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 25:15

15You shall have a perfect and just weight. You shall have a perfect and just measure, that your days may be long in the land which the LORD your God gives you.

Arminian

Weights and measures must be perfect and just, with the promise of long life in the land. Honesty is tied to God’s blessing.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same link between just weights and long life in the land God gives. Honest dealing is part of covenant faithfulness, not a lesser matter. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 25:16

16For all who do such things, all who do unrighteously, are an abomination to the LORD your God.

Arminian

Everyone who deals dishonestly is an abomination to the LORD. Economic injustice is a sin against God, not only against neighbors.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that dishonest dealing is an abomination to the LORD. The language matches the seriousness given to idolatry and other grave sins. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 25:17

17Remember what Amalek did to you by the way as you came out of Egypt,

Arminian

Israel must remember what Amalek did as they came out of Egypt (Exodus 17:8–16). Memory is a covenant duty, keeping the offense from being forgotten before the judgment in verse 19.

Reformed

Reformed readers draw the same link to Exodus 17, where Amalek first attacked Israel in the wilderness. Remembering God’s past deliverance and the enemy’s cruelty belong together. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 25:18

18how he met you by the way, and struck the rearmost of you, all who were feeble behind you, when you were faint and weary; and he didn’t fear God.

Arminian

Amalek attacked the weak and weary at the rear and “didn’t fear God.” The sin is cruelty toward the vulnerable and contempt for God. This detail is not in Exodus 17 and adds to the account.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same added detail: Amalek struck the feeble at the rear and did not fear God. Cruelty to the weak is here named as contempt for God. This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 25:19

19Therefore it shall be, when the LORD your God has given you rest from all your enemies all around, in the land which the LORD your God gives you for an inheritance to possess it, that you shall blot out the memory of Amalek from under the sky. You shall not forget.

Arminian

Once Israel has rest, they must blot out Amalek’s memory. The command is carried out later, in part, in 1 Samuel 15. Both traditions read it as a specific judgment on Amalek, not a license for ethnic hostility.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise read it as God’s judgment on a particular people’s sin. Neither tradition applies it to any present-day group. Both note the tension between remembering and blotting out: Israel must not forget to carry it out.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
