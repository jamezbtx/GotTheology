Passage

Deuteronomy 32

Verses 44–52 · WEB

Moses and Joshua recite the Song, and Moses urges Israel to set their hearts on these words, “because it is your life.” That same day God commands Moses to climb Mount Nebo, view Canaan, and die there as Aaron did on Mount Hor, because at Meribah he “didn’t uphold my holiness among the children of Israel.”

Deuteronomy 32:44

44Moses came and spoke all the words of this song in the ears of the people, he and Joshua the son of Nun.

Arminian

Moses and Joshua speak all the words of the Song to the people. Arminian readers see Joshua joining Moses, signaling continuity of leadership. The Song is shared by both leaders.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the Song delivered with Joshua present. The verse marks the completion of the Song’s public recitation. The presence of Joshua links the Song to the next generation of leadership.

Deuteronomy 32:45

45Moses finished reciting all these words to all Israel.

Arminian

Moses finishes reciting all these words to Israel. Arminian readers see a faithful messenger completing his task. Nothing of the Song is left unspoken, and the people have heard every word.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see Moses fulfilling God’s command. The verse closes the Song’s recitation. The task God assigned in 31:19 has been carried through to the end without omission.

Deuteronomy 32:46

46He said to them, “Set your heart to all the words which I testify to you today, which you shall command your children to observe to do, all the words of this law.

Arminian

Moses says, “Set your heart to all the words which I testify to you today.” Arminian readers see a call to wholehearted commitment and teaching the next generation.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the necessity of internalizing God’s word. The command includes teaching children to obey. The heart must be engaged, and the words must be passed on to children.

Deuteronomy 32:47

47For it is no vain thing for you, because it is your life, and through this thing you shall prolong your days in the land, where you go over the Jordan to possess it.”

Arminian

Moses says, “For it is no vain thing for you, because it is your life.” Arminian readers see God’s word as the source of life in the land.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the vital importance of God’s word. The verse ties obedience to prolonged days in the land. Life is not found apart from God’s word.

Deuteronomy 32:48

48The LORD spoke to Moses that same day, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses that same day. Arminian readers see God addressing Moses personally at the close of his ministry. The time has come.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s timing in this final word. The verse transitions from the Song to Moses’ own end. The day of the Song is also the day of Moses’ final instructions.

Deuteronomy 32:49

49“Go up into this mountain of Abarim, to Mount Nebo, which is in the land of Moab, that is across from Jericho; and see the land of Canaan, which I give to the children of Israel for a possession.

Arminian

God tells Moses to climb Mount Nebo and see Canaan. Arminian readers see grace in allowing Moses to view the land he cannot enter. Moses sees what he cannot enter.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s kindness in showing Moses the promised land. The location across from Jericho anticipates the crossing to come. He sees the land God gives.

Deuteronomy 32:50

50Die on the mountain where you go up, and be gathered to your people, as Aaron your brother died on Mount Hor, and was gathered to his people;

Arminian

Moses will die on the mountain and be gathered to his people, as Aaron did on Mount Hor. Arminian readers see the parallel fates of the two brothers.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s word fulfilled for both leaders. The comparison recalls Numbers 20:22–29. The brothers who led Israel together share a similar end outside the land.

Deuteronomy 32:51

51because you trespassed against me among the children of Israel at the waters of Meribah of Kadesh, in the wilderness of Zin; because you didn’t uphold my holiness among the children of Israel.

Arminian

The reason is that Moses and Aaron trespassed at Meribah and “didn’t uphold my holiness among the children of Israel.” Arminian readers see accountability for public failure.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see the cause stated plainly in God’s own words. The verse repeats the explanation given in Numbers 20:12. The failure was public, and its consequence is likewise stated publicly.

Deuteronomy 32:52

52For you shall see the land from a distance; but you shall not go there into the land which I give the children of Israel.”

Arminian

Moses will see the land from a distance but not enter. Arminian readers see both mercy and consequence in God’s final word to Moses. The vision is a gift.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s justice and kindness together. The chapter ends with Moses looking toward the promise he helped Israel approach. God’s promise to Israel stands, even though Moses will not enter.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
