Passage

Deuteronomy 16

Verses 18–22 · WEB

Deuteronomy 16:18–22 opens a new section on Israel’s officials (16:18–18:22) with the appointment of judges and officers in every town. They must judge rightly, show no partiality, and take no bribes, and Israel must pursue ‘that which is altogether just’. Verses 21–22 then forbid an Asherah pole or sacred stone beside the LORD’s altar. Arminian and Reformed readers agree that just judgment and pure worship belong together as the marks of a people living under God.

Deuteronomy 16:18

18You shall make judges and officers in all your gates, which the LORD your God gives you, according to your tribes; and they shall judge the people with righteous judgment.

Arminian

Appoint judges and officers in every town to judge with righteous judgment. Arminians see God caring for justice in everyday community life.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that civil authority is established under God for justice (compare Romans 13:1–4). Both traditions agree on the verse.

Deuteronomy 16:19

19You shall not pervert justice. You shall not show partiality. You shall not take a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and perverts the words of the righteous.

Arminian

Do not pervert justice, show partiality, or take a bribe, for a bribe blinds the wise. Arminians see the moral corruption that comes from self-interest.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree and note that the verse echoes Exodus 23:6–8. Both traditions read it as a lasting standard for anyone who judges.

Deuteronomy 16:20

20You shall follow that which is altogether just, that you may live and inherit the land which the LORD your God gives you.

Arminian

Follow what is ‘altogether just’ so that you may live and inherit the land. Arminians see justice tied to Israel’s continued enjoyment of God’s gift.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree that justice is required of God’s covenant people. Both traditions read the verse as tying life in the land to faithfulness.

Deuteronomy 16:21

21You shall not plant for yourselves an Asherah of any kind of tree beside the LORD your God’s altar, which you shall make for yourselves.

Arminian

Do not plant an Asherah beside the LORD’s altar. Arminians see God forbidding syncretism, mixing his worship with that of other gods.

Reformed

Reformed interpreters agree that worship must be undivided. Both traditions note that Asherah was associated with a Canaanite goddess and her cult objects, and they agree on the verse.

Deuteronomy 16:22

22Neither shall you set yourself up a sacred stone which the LORD your God hates.

Arminian

Do not set up a sacred stone, which the LORD hates. Arminians see God rejecting forms of worship tied to idolatry. The ban on standing stones also appears in Leviticus 26:1.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree and connect the verse with the second commandment. Both traditions note that standing stones were used in Canaanite worship and agree on the verse.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
