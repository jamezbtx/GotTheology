Passage

Deuteronomy 2

Verses 16–25 · WEB

Once the old generation has died, the LORD tells Israel to pass Moab and to leave Ammon alone, since he has given Ammon its land too. Another parenthetical note recalls peoples displaced by Ammon, Edom, and the Caphtorim. Then the LORD commands Israel to cross the Arnon and fight Sihon, promising to put dread of Israel on the nations.

Deuteronomy 2:16

16So, when all the men of war were consumed and dead from among the people,

Arminian

Only after the fighting men of the old generation had all died does the new stage begin. The timing is tied to God’s judgment.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same link. Only when all the men of war were consumed and dead from among the people does the next word come; the new stage of the journey is timed to the completed judgment.

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:17

17the LORD spoke to me, saying,

Arminian

The LORD speaks to Moses again. God’s word drives each step of the journey; after the death of the old generation of fighting men, a fresh command opens the way forward.

Reformed

Reformed readers read it the same way. The speech that follows about Ammon and Sihon is introduced as the LORD’s own word to Moses, so the next borders and battles are not Moses’ initiative.

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:18

18“You are to pass over Ar, the border of Moab, today.

Arminian

Israel is to pass over Ar, the border of Moab, that day. The time of waiting is over; the command is dated to “today,” and the next movements will follow at once.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. Passing Ar, Moab’s border, “today” marks the end of delay and the beginning of the approach to the lands where God will either forbid battle or command it.

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:19

19When you come near the border of the children of Ammon, don’t bother them, nor contend with them; for I will not give you any of the land of the children of Ammon for a possession, because I have given it to the children of Lot for a possession.”

Arminian

Israel must not fight Ammon, because God has given its land to Lot’s descendants. As with Edom and Moab, God sets limits on Israel’s inheritance.

Reformed

Reformed readers likewise see God’s sovereign allotment of nations. Israel must not bother or contend with Ammon, because God has given that land to the children of Lot for a possession, as he had with Moab; Israel’s inheritance stops at a line God has already drawn.

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:20

20(That also is considered a land of Rephaim. Rephaim lived there in the past, but the Ammonites call them Zamzummim,

Arminian

Another parenthetical note: Ammon’s land was also considered a land of Rephaim. Rephaim had lived there in the past, but the Ammonites called them Zamzummim. The old inhabitants, however named, were a people Israel might have feared, had God not already dealt with them.

Reformed

Reformed readers read the note the same way. The Rephaim, known to the Ammonites as Zamzummim, belong to the same class of great peoples already compared with the Anakim, so the encouragement of the earlier notes is extended to this border as well.

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:21

21a great people, many, and tall, as the Anakim; but the LORD destroyed them from before Israel, and they succeeded them, and lived in their place,

Arminian

The LORD destroyed this great, tall people before Ammon. The note implies that God can do for Israel what he did for Ammon.

Reformed

Reformed readers draw the same encouragement. The verse as printed says the LORD destroyed this great, tall people “from before Israel”; read with verses 20 and 22, many take the sense to be that God cleared the Rephaim so the Ammonites could live in their place, though the wording is compact and discussed.

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:22

22as he did for the children of Esau who dwell in Seir, when he destroyed the Horites from before them; and they succeeded them, and lived in their place even to this day.

Arminian

God did the same for the children of Esau in Seir, destroying the Horites from before them, so that they have lived in their place “even to this day.” The repeated pattern stresses God’s rule over more than one nation’s history.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree. What God did for Esau’s descendants against the Horites is set beside what he did in Ammon’s land, so Israel is taught to see one providence at work in the older displacements around them.

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:23

23Then the Avvim, who lived in villages as far as Gaza: the Caphtorim, who came out of Caphtor, destroyed them and lived in their place.)

Arminian

The Caphtorim from Caphtor displaced the Avvim near Gaza. Caphtor is often identified with Crete, and Amos 9:7 links the Philistines with Caphtor, though details are uncertain.

Reformed

Reformed readers note the same identification and uncertainty. The Caphtorim from Caphtor displaced the Avvim who had lived in villages as far as Gaza. Caphtor is often identified with Crete, and Amos 9:7 links the Philistines with Caphtor, though the details remain uncertain.

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:24

24“Rise up, take your journey, and pass over the valley of the Arnon. Behold, I have given into your hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land; begin to possess it, and contend with him in battle.

Arminian

God commands Israel to cross the Arnon and says he has given Sihon into their hand. The gift is declared before the battle begins.

Reformed

Reformed readers emphasize that the outcome is certain because God has promised it. Israel is to cross the Arnon and begin to possess Sihon’s land, for God has already said, “I have given into your hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land.”

This is shared ground.

Deuteronomy 2:25

25Today I will begin to put the dread of you and the fear of you on the peoples who are under the whole sky, who shall hear the report of you, and shall tremble and be in anguish because of you.”

Arminian

God will put dread of Israel on all peoples who hear of them. The nations’ fear will come from God’s action, not Israel’s strength (compare Joshua 2:9–11).

Reformed

Reformed readers read it the same way. Today God will begin to put the dread and fear of Israel on peoples under the whole sky, who will tremble at the report. The nations’ fear will come from God’s action, not from Israel’s strength (compare Joshua 2:9–11).

This is shared ground.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
