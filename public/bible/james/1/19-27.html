Passage

James 1

Verses 19–27 · WEB

Because God has given new birth through the word, believers must receive that word humbly and obey it. Hearing without doing is self-deception, like glancing in a mirror and forgetting your face. True religion shows itself in a controlled tongue, care for orphans and widows, and a life kept unstained by the world. Both traditions agree that genuine faith obeys; they differ in how they speak about the word’s saving work and the law of freedom.

James 1:19

19So, then, my beloved brothers, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger;

Arminian

Be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger. Arminian readers see practical discipleship flowing from new birth. The word must be heard with humility and patience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see these commands as the fruit of being born by the word. Those reborn should be eager listeners, restrained speakers, and slow to anger.

James 1:20

20for the anger of man doesn’t produce the righteousness of God.

Arminian

Human anger does not produce God’s righteousness. Arminian readers stress that righteous living is not achieved through outbursts of temper, however justified they feel.

Reformed

Reformed readers read God’s righteousness here as the upright life God requires, not justification. Human anger works against the righteousness God desires in His people.

James 1:21

21Therefore, putting away all filthiness and overflowing of wickedness, receive with humility the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.

Arminian

Put away filth and receive the implanted word, which can save your souls. Arminian readers stress the ongoing need to welcome the word humbly. Salvation is held out to those who receive and continue in it.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the implanted word as the gospel God has already planted in believers. They receive it continually, and through it God brings salvation to completion.

James 1:22

22But be doers of the word, and not only hearers, deluding your own selves.

Arminian

Be doers of the word, not hearers only. Arminian readers see a clear call to obedience. Hearing without doing deceives the heart.

Reformed

Reformed readers agree: true hearing produces obedience. James warns against hearing that never changes the life.

James 1:23

23For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man looking at his natural face in a mirror;

Arminian

A hearer who does not act is like a man looking at his face in a mirror. Arminian readers see the word as a mirror revealing who we are and what needs to change.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same mirror image. Scripture shows us ourselves, and genuine hearing leads to action.

James 1:24

24for he sees himself, and goes away, and immediately forgets what kind of man he was.

Arminian

He looks, walks away, and forgets what he looked like. Arminian readers see the danger of a passing glance at Scripture without lasting change.

Reformed

Reformed readers note how quickly forgotten hearing fails to transform. The problem is not looking but forgetting.

James 1:25

25But he who looks into the perfect law of freedom and continues, not being a hearer who forgets but a doer of the work, this man will be blessed in what he does.

Arminian

The one who looks into the perfect law of freedom and continues will be blessed in his doing. Arminian readers stress continuing in the word. Freedom comes through grace-enabled obedience.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the law of freedom as God’s will written on renewed hearts, not a burden for earning acceptance. Blessing comes to those who keep looking and obeying, as the fruit of grace.

James 1:26

26If anyone among you thinks himself to be religious while he doesn’t bridle his tongue, but deceives his heart, this man’s religion is worthless.

Arminian

Religion is worthless if the tongue is unbridled. Arminian readers see a practical test of real faith: speech reveals the heart.

Reformed

Reformed readers see the same test. Religion that leaves the tongue unchecked deceives the heart and is empty.

James 1:27

27Pure religion and undefiled before our God and Father is this: to visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained by the world.

Arminian

Pure religion cares for orphans and widows and keeps oneself unstained by the world. Arminian readers emphasize practical holiness and compassion as the fruit of faith.

Reformed

Reformed readers see true religion as both mercy and holiness. Caring for the vulnerable and resisting worldliness reflect God’s own character and the life of grace.

About these notes. Commentary here is pastoral and educational. It is not a substitute for reading primary sources, confessions, or trusted teachers in either tradition. Both Arminian and Reformed families contain internal diversity (Wesleyan, classical Remonstrant, confessional Calvinist, and more). GotTheology aims for fair representation—not a final verdict.
