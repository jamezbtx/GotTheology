Attributes of God

Who God is—holy, loving, sovereign—read first in the verses, then in Arminian and Reformed notes side by side.

Also: Doctrines · Bible

  • HolinessGod is the Holy One—utterly pure, set apart, and worthy of fear and love—and both camps call his people to be holy as he is holy.
  • LoveGod is love, and he has loved in the sending of the Son—both camps refuse a loveless God; they part over whether that love, in the design of salvation, is for every person or particularly for the elect.
  • JusticeGod is righteous—wrath against sin is real, and he will by no means clear the guilty—and both camps confess it; they part over how that justice orders mercy, not over whether judgment is a mood.
  • MercyMercy is God’s pity toward the miserable—not a second name for the love hub—and both camps confess it; they part over Romans 9’s “I will have mercy,” whether it names whom he wills or a sincere general mercy with particular application.
  • FaithfulnessGod keeps covenant—if we are faithless, he remains faithful—and both camps confess it; they do not disagree that his word cannot fail, though they may differ over whom the promises finally hold.
  • OmnipotenceGod can do all that is consistent with his nature—both camps confess the Almighty, and neither treats power as a magic that lies, un-Gods himself, or authors sin.
  • OmniscienceGod knows all things—himself, the possible, and the actual—and classical Arminians are not open theists; both camps confess exhaustive knowledge and part over how that knowledge relates to the decree.
  • OmnipresenceGod is wholly present everywhere—not spread thin, not locked in a shrine—and both camps confess it; they do not disagree that the Father, the risen Christ, and the Spirit are with the church.
  • ImmutabilityGod does not change in being or character—both camps confess it, and both read “relented” texts as a change of dealing, not a mutable essence; prayer is not wasted.
  • EternityGod is without beginning or end—the King of the ages—and both camps confess it; they do not disagree that the Word was in the beginning, or that immortality belongs to God.
  • SovereigntyGod rules; nothing is finally outside his hand—both camps confess a King, and they part over whether that rule includes an unconditional decree of who will believe.
